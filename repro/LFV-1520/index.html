AI-assisted bug hunt in Apache Lucene

CaseFoldingFilter writes a term with an unpaired surrogate for a word that contains U+1DF95: the Unicode 18 fold table maps this supplementary letter to U+00DF (ß), and the filter's in-place fold cannot make the term shorter, so the term keeps the letter's second UTF-16 unit, the index stores U+FFFD in its place, and behind WhitespaceTokenizer the queries Ma?e, /ma.e/ and Mate~1 no longer find the word 'Ma' + U+1DF95 + 'e', which they find on 10.6.0 (main and branch_11x, both unreleased, since the fold table regenerated by #17010)

What it shows

You analyze text with CaseFoldingFilter behind a tokenizer that keeps the letter U+1DF95 (LATIN SMALL LIGATURE LONG S WITH DESCENDER S, new in Unicode 18) inside a token, for example the word 'Ma' + U+1DF95 + 'e' with WhitespaceTokenizer. You expect the folded term 'maße', because the filter promises Unicode simple case folding and the fold table of main and branch_11x maps U+1DF95 to ß (U+00DF), as Unicode 18 does. Instead the term is 'maß' followed by the second half of the old letter (its low surrogate) and 'e', which is not valid UTF-16, and the index stores it as 'maß' + U+FFFD + 'e': the word is still not found under its sharp-s spelling 'Maße' (it was not found before either, when the letter was not folded at all), and it now shares a term with text that really contains U+FFFD at that place.

Expected vs actual

  • Expected: The term is the fold of U+1DF95: ß (U+00DF), one UTF-16 unit.
  • Actual: The term is ß followed by the unpaired low surrogate U+DF95, two units: term 0 expected:<ß[]> but was:<ß[?]>.

What the test does: a one-token stream whose text is the one letter U+1DF95 goes through CaseFoldingFilter, and the term must be UnicodeUtil.foldCase of the letter, taken from the build's own table. On a build with the Unicode 18 table that is ß (U+00DF), and the test fails with 'term 0 expected:<ß[]> but was:<ß[?]>': the term is ß followed by the letter's second UTF-16 unit, which the console prints as '?'. On a build whose table leaves the letter alone the test checks that the filter leaves it alone, and passes. The test shows the term only: the three queries that the title names (Ma?e, /ma.e/ and Mate~1 behind WhitespaceTokenizer) are not part of it; they were run with separate programs, and the finding page has their counts under Who is affected. The test's input has no randomness; the seed only names the run.
