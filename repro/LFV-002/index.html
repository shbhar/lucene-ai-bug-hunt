Merge doc-values carry-over (#16570) can lose an acknowledged update or close the writer on a duplicate delGen

Finding LFV-002Area: index / IndexWriter mergePriority: mediumImpact: data corruptionNovelty: new

Finding page for LFV-002

AI-assisted finding, confirmed by automated checks. Upstream status: Fix in progress, by Jim Ferenczi (Lucene PMC; open PR #16988) on 2026-11-06. Open PR #16988 (jimczi, opened 2026-11-06; milestone 10.6.1; open as read on 2026-11-21) says it was found via this list and names LFV-002. It clamps the delGen of the carried-over on-disk doc-values value in IndexWriter's merge to min(minResidual - 1, completedDelGen) and adds our minimal repro as TestMergeDocValuesCarryOverRace with one added setting (setMaxDocValuesOverlays(16)). Our minimal repro fails on main 781025d6fe32 and passes there with the PR's change applied, with and without assertions (runs of 2026-11-14). With the same change of one expression applied to IndexWriter of branch_11x 5d7ca54ba503 it passes with the overlays setting at its default, at 0, at 1 and at 8, with and without assertions (runs of 2026-11-19). Source. This site files nothing upstream.

What it shows

While a merge runs, a document in a merging segment gets an older doc-values update that is still being applied and a newer update to the same segment and field that finishes first. You expect the newest value to win on the merged segment. Instead the merge gives the carried-over on-disk value a delGen at or above the in-flight packet's, so the older value silently wins and survives commit and reopen, or, with assertions on and equal gens, a duplicate-delGen assertion closes the writer and discards uncommitted changes.

The test shows that during a merge, a doc-values update whose packet is still being applied loses to an older value carried over from disk, when a newer packet on the same segment and field finishes first (#16570 carry-over path, default config).

Expected vs actual

Expected the k0 document's last update n=2 after the merge, commit and reopen; actual: the older n=1 (expected:<2> but was:<1>), with -ea and -da.
