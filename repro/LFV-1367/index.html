AI-assisted bug hunt in Apache Lucene

Classic Highlighter: SimpleFragmenter and SimpleSpanFragmenter move their fragment boundary only one fragment size per break, so after a long token or a long gap between tokens (a run of spaces, stripped markup) every following word becomes its own fragment and getBestFragment can return just the highlighted word (every release since 1.9)

Finding LFV-1367Area: highlighter / classic Highlighter fragmenting (SimpleFragmenter.isNewFragment and SimpleSpanFragmenter.isNewFragment fragment counter)Priority: lowImpact: minorNovelty: known outside Lucene

AI-assisted finding, confirmed by automated checks, not yet triaged by a human Lucene committer. This site files nothing upstream.

What it shows

You highlight a text with the classic Highlighter and SimpleFragmenter, whose javadoc promises 'same-size fragments' of fragmentSize characters each, and the text has a long token or a long stretch without tokens (60 x's before 'alpha beta gamma ...' with SimpleFragmenter(20), or 300 spaces before a sentence with the default Highlighter and StandardAnalyzer). You expect the words after that stretch to be grouped into fragments of about fragmentSize characters again, so that the best fragment for 'gamma' is 'alpha beta <B>gamma</B>', as it is without the long token. Instead isNewFragment moves its boundary only one fragment size per break, so the boundary stays behind the text, every following word starts its own fragment until it catches up, and getBestFragment returns just ' <B>gamma</B>' (SimpleSpanFragmenter behaves the same).

Expected vs actual

  • Expected: You expect the words after that stretch to be grouped into fragments of about fragmentSize characters again, so that the best fragment for 'gamma' is 'alpha beta <B>gamma</B>', as it is without the long token.
  • Actual: isNewFragment moves its boundary only one fragment size per break, so the boundary stays behind the text, every following word starts its own fragment until it catches up, and getBestFragment returns just ' <B>gamma</B>' (SimpleSpanFragmenter behaves the same).
