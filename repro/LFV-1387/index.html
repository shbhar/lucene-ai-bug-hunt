AI-assisted bug hunt in Apache Lucene

HindiAnalyzer runs HindiNormalizationFilter before HindiStemFilter, so the 20 of HindiStemmer's 67 suffix rules that are spelled with a long vowel or candrabindu (-ती, -ाती, -ेगी, -ोगी, -ूंगा, -ाई, -नी, ...) can never fire, and feminine and future verb forms keep part of their suffix: 'खाती' is indexed as 'खात' while 'खाता' and 'खाते' give 'खा'

Finding LFV-1387Area: analysis-common / Hindi analyzer (HindiAnalyzer filter chain; HindiStemmer suffix table versus HindiNormalizer)Priority: lowImpact: minorNovelty: new

AI-assisted finding, confirmed by automated checks, not yet triaged by a human Lucene committer. This site files nothing upstream.

What it shows

You index Hindi text with the stock HindiAnalyzer; one document says 'वह रोज़ खाती है' ('she eats every day') and a query says 'खाता' (the masculine form of 'eats'). You expect both forms to stem to 'खा', because HindiStemmer implements Ramanathan and Rao's light stemmer, whose suffix list contains -ती, and Lucene's own TestHindiStemmer pins खाती -> खा next to खाता -> खा. Instead HindiAnalyzer runs HindiNormalizationFilter first, which folds the long vowel signs ी and ू, the long vowels आ, ई and ऊ and the candrabindu ँ to their short or plain forms, so the 20 suffix rules spelled with those characters can never match: 'खाती' is indexed as 'खात' and the query finds nothing, with no error.

Expected vs actual

  • Expected: खाती (*khātī*) is analyzed to खा (*khā*), as HindiStemFilter alone and TestHindiStemmer give.
  • Actual: term 0 expected:<खा[]> but was:<खा[त]>: the analyzer gives खात (*khāt*), with -ea and -da.
