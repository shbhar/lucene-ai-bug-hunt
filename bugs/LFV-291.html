Intervals.notContainedBy over an OR keeps matches that lie inside one of the OR's clauses but not another

Intervals.notContainedBy(york, or("new york", "york city")) should keep only a york in neither phrase, but Lucene rewrites it into "not in new york, or not in york city", so a york inside one phrase still matches: 3 hits instead of 1. Silent, default settings, 8.1.0 to 10.5.2 and main.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

An interval is the range of positions a source matches. notContainedBy(small, big) keeps the intervals of small that lie inside no interval of big. By default Lucene pulls an OR out of such operators: one copy per clause, ORed. That is right for "contained by", but "not inside (B1 or B2)" means not inside B1 and not inside B2.

  1. Index three documents: 0 "i love new york", 1 "york city is big", 2 "york is here". Search notContainedBy(york, or(phrase(new, york), phrase(york, city))), meaning "york, but not as part of new york or york city".
  2. You expect 1 hit, doc 2: doc 0's york (position 3) lies inside "new york" (positions 2-3), and doc 1's york (position 0) inside "york city" (positions 0-1). Chaining two filters, or using or(false, ...), returns exactly doc 2.
  3. Instead you get 3 hits. By default Lucene splits the OR and builds or(notContainedBy(york, "new york"), notContainedBy(york, "york city")). The first filter keeps docs 1 and 2, the second keeps docs 0 and 2, and their OR keeps all three.
  4. So a york inside one phrase survives the other phrase's filter, and adding a phrase adds hits: notContainedBy(a, "c a") on "c a" gives 0 hits, notContainedBy(a, or("c a", b)) gives 1. No error, with assertions on or off.

Who is affected

#

Anyone who excludes with notContainedBy over an OR of two or more distinct clauses, one longer than one word: a phrase, ordered, extend, a multi-word synonym that keeps the original token, or a parsed fn:or(...). No configuration needed; two or three documents suffice; 8.1.0 to 10.5.2 and main. Silent over-matching, never beyond the small side's hits.

Mechanism, terms and numbers675 words

Intervals.notContainedBy(small, big) returns the intervals of small that do not appear within intervals of big. When big is an Intervals.or(...) that splits into two or more groups (in practice at least two distinct clauses, one of them longer than one position: a phrase, ordered, extend, a multi-word synonym that keeps the original token), the default disjunction rewrite of LUCENE-8477 pulls the OR out of the excluded side and ORs one filter per clause. That computes "not inside B1, or not inside B2" where the operator means "not inside B1 and not inside B2", so an interval inside one clause is still returned by the other clause's filter. "york, but not in new york or york city" returns 3 documents instead of 1, and adding a clause to the exclusion adds hits. Chained filters, or or(false, ...), give the documented answer. It is silent, needs no configuration, is reachable from the flexible query parser and through a multi-word synonym that keeps the original token, with no user-written OR. It is in every release from 8.1.0 to 10.5.2 and on main (8 of them run, the rest by git ancestry); 8.0.0 gives the documented answer.

Terms

#
  • Interval: the range of positions [start, end] where an IntervalsSource matches in a document. york in "i love new york" is [3,3]; phrase(new, york) is [2,3].
  • notContainedBy(small, big): keeps each interval of small that does not lie inside any interval of big. Its javadoc: "Returns intervals from the small IntervalsSource that do not appear within intervals from the big IntervalsSource" (Intervals.java:539-540).
  • Disjunction rewrite (LUCENE-8477, on by default): operators that are sensitive to internal gaps pull an OR among their sources to the top, FUNC(a, or(x, y)) => or(FUNC(a, x), FUNC(a, y)) (Disjunctions.java:28-31, class note Intervals.java:47-54), so that a longer clause is not "minimized away". It is exact where OR distributes: containedBy(small, or(B1, B2)) is containedBy(small, B1) or containedBy(small, B2). Intervals.or(false, ...) turns it off.

The bug

#
  1. Intervals.notContainedBy calls NotContainedByIntervalsSource.build (Intervals.java:545-547), which returns Intervals.or(Disjunctions.pullUp(subtrahend, s -> new NotContainedByIntervalsSource(minuend, s))) (NotContainedByIntervalsSource.java:27-30).
  2. Disjunctions.pullUp (Disjunctions.java:66-73) splits the excluded OR with splitDisjunctions (:79-95): all clauses with minExtent() == 1 go into one group, every other clause (in practice a longer one) into its own group. An OR with a single distinct clause has already been collapsed to it (DisjunctionIntervalsSource.java:45-49). With two or more groups, build makes one filter per group and ORs them.
  3. An OR returns an interval when any branch returns it. A branch filters against one group only. When that group does not occur in the document, RelativeIterator.reset marks it absent (RelativeIterator.java:58-61) and the filter returns the small source's intervals unchanged (NotContainedByIntervalsSource.java:73-75); when it has no iterator in the segment at all, DifferenceIntervalsSource.java:44-47 does the same. So doc 0's york, removed by "not inside new york", comes back through "not inside york city".
  4. The operator needs the intersection of the filters (for clauses that do not nest, De Morgan: not (inside B1 or inside B2) = not inside B1 and not inside B2); the built query computes their union.
  5. The other containment operators pull an OR up only from positive positions, where OR distributes: containedBy and containing from their big side, notContaining from its minuend, the side it keeps (ContainedByIntervalsSource.java:28-30, ContainingIntervalsSource.java:31-33, NotContainingIntervalsSource.java:27-30). notContainedBy alone pulls one out of the side it subtracts.

Exposure and likelihood393 words
  • The natural use: "term X, but not as part of phrase P1 or P2". A Stack Overflow question asks for exactly this with span queries.
  • Executed trigger paths (main, also 10.5.2): a user-written Intervals.or of phrases; the flexible StandardQueryParser strings fn:notContainedBy(b fn:or("a b" "b c")), parsed to or(NOT_CONTAINED_BY(b,BLOCK(a,b)),NOT_CONTAINED_BY(b,BLOCK(b,c))), and fn:notContainedBy(york fn:or("new york" "york city")); with no user-written OR, Intervals.analyzedText("nyc", ...) with a query-time multi-word synonym that keeps the original token (nyc => nyc, new york city), which builds or(BLOCK(new,york,city), nyc); an OR of single terms wrapped in extend(..., 0, 1). A replace-only synonym (nyc => new york city) builds BLOCK(new,york,city) with no OR and is correct.
  • Not affected: an OR of single terms only (one group); Intervals.notWithin (by code reading: no pull-up, Intervals.java:491-495); containedBy, containing and notContaining.
  • Downstream, read only (nothing run): Elasticsearch and OpenSearch map the intervals query's not_contained_by filter to Intervals.notContainedBy and any_of to the rewriting Intervals.or; the Solr 10.1 reference guide documents an intervals parser with any_of and not_contained_by.
  • Default vs opt-in: no configuration needed. The plain-Java diagram probe uses the default codec, IndexWriterConfig and IndexSearcher; the JUnit fails under the test framework's randomized codecs; assertions on or off. The intervals API itself is opt-in and less used than boolean and phrase queries, and its package is marked experimental (package-info.java:21).
  • Scale: no minimum; two or three short documents suffice (every run used one segment; multi-segment indexes were not run). A document is wrongly returned when every occurrence of the small term lies inside at least one group and at least one occurrence lies outside at least one group. With two phrases, a document is excluded only where every occurrence lies inside both phrases at once; how much that changes real result sets was not measured.
  • Damage: silent over-matching and inflated counts; adding a clause to the exclusion adds hits. Every branch returns only intervals of the small source, so the extra hits stay within the small source's hits. The same source drives matches(), so highlighting sees the same extra intervals (by code reading, not tested). No exception, crash or index damage.
  • How likely: plausible (a judgement, not measured): ordinary text triggers it, but notContainedBy over an OR of phrases is a niche query shape.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The javadoc: notContainedBy "Returns intervals from the small IntervalsSource that do not appear within intervals from the big IntervalsSource"; no exception for an OR.
  • The docs call the rewrite the accurate mode, or(false, ...) the less accurate one; here or(false, ...) is right.
  • 8.0.0 gives the repro's documented answer; notContainedBy's only rewrite test checks the query's shape, not its matches.
The full argument264 words
  • Contracts. Intervals.notContainedBy (Intervals.java:539-540); the package doc, "Returns intervals that are not contained by another interval" (package-info.java:60-61); the flexible parser doc, "Matches intervals of the source that are not contained by intervals of the reference" (queryparser/.../nodes/intervalfn/package-info.java:596). None makes an exception for an OR.
  • The rewrite is the accuracy mode. "if you care more about speed than about accuracy you can use the or(boolean, IntervalsSource...) factory method to prevent rewriting" (Intervals.java:53-54); or(false, ...) "may run more efficiently, but can miss valid hits due to minimization" (Intervals.java:130-131); CHANGES for LUCENE-8477: "Interval disjunctions could miss valid hits ... This behaviour can be disabled if users are more interested in speed rather than accuracy of matching" (lucene/CHANGES.txt:6486-6490).
  • Wrong interval matches are bugs: LUCENE-10140, "Fix cases where minimizing interval iterators could return incorrect matches" (lucene/CHANGES.txt:4974-4975).
  • Tests. TestDisjunctionRewrites#testNotContainedBy (TestDisjunctionRewrites.java:201-213) asserts the OR-of-filters structure only, with the comment "the 'big' interval should not be minimized, the 'small' one should be". No upstream test checks the matches of notContainedBy with an OR as the big source (TestIntervalQuery#testNotContainedByQuery uses an unordered big source).
  • History. 7.4.0-8.0.0 filtered against the whole OR and give the documented answer. LUCENE-8477 aimed at matches missed through minimization (#9243 asked for "not losing matches by adding disjunctions"); its issue and PR never discuss the negated side. The rewrite's author later said intervals rewrite disjunctions "so that queries behave as your or I would expect them to" (#10244).
  • Monotonicity. Adding a clause to an exclusion can only remove hits; here it adds them.
The devil's-advocate check164 words

The strongest case for "intended" (rated moderate by the refute review and the "works as designed" review, weak by the devil's-advocate verifier): the pull-up is deliberate LUCENE-8477 code, TestDisjunctionRewrites#testNotContainedBy pins exactly this OR shape, the package is experimental, and users can opt out with or(false, ...).

Why it fails: the test checks structure, not matches, and its stated goal (do not minimize the big side) is also met by chaining the same groups. The "pull up the big side" rule is sound for notContaining, where the big side is the one kept, and wrong for notContainedBy, where it is subtracted. No javadoc, CHANGES, MIGRATE entry or discussion gives notContainedBy over an OR the meaning "outside any one clause". "Experimental" allows API changes, not wrong results. The opt-out is documented as the less accurate mode, and here it is the accurate one. The repro's clauses do not nest, so nothing is minimized away and 8.0.0 gave the expected answer. Verdict: a defect.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Chain one NotContainedBy filter per pulled-up clause (recommended)

In NotContainedByIntervalsSource.build, chain one filter per pulled-up clause, notContainedBy(notContainedBy(small, B1), B2), instead of OR-ing them (+9/-2), and update the structural test. The repro, 195 tests in the interval, flexible-parser and highlighter suites, the whole queries module and the static checks pass. Dropping the pull-up instead breaks nested clauses.

static IntervalsSource build(IntervalsSource minuend, IntervalsSource subtrahend) {
  // An interval of the minuend is excluded if it lies inside any disjunct of the subtrahend, so
  // chain one filter per disjunct: OR-ing the filters would keep an interval that lies outside
  // just one of them.
  IntervalsSource result = minuend;
  for (IntervalsSource disjunct : Disjunctions.pullUp(subtrahend, Function.identity())) {
    result = new NotContainedByIntervalsSource(result, disjunct);
  }
  return result;
}

Trade-offs

  • The built query's structure, toString and equals change. An OR of filters compared its branches as a set (DisjunctionIntervalsSource.java:108-118); a chain compares in order, and that order comes from a hash set, so two queries that differ only in clause order might not be equal. That matters to anything that relies on equals and hashCode (the query cache, deduplication, structural tests). Not tested.
  • Cost: one filter per group as before, but the small source is iterated once through nested filters instead of once per OR branch. No benchmark was run.
  • Zero groups (by code reading, not tested): notContainedBy(small, or()) with an empty rewriting OR gives no matches today and would return small unchanged with the fix.
  • Every validation run used a single segment.
  • Not reviewed by a committer.

Document the current meaning

#

Say in the javadoc that notContainedBy over a split OR means "outside at least one clause".

Trade-offs

Downstream note

#

Downstream: used as an allow or visibility rule, it shows a document whose term sits inside only one excluded phrase, and anyone who can write documents can place it so (4 visible instead of 2 in the probe). Bounded by the small side's hits; as a MUST_NOT deny rule it hides more instead. No exploit or such deployment is known.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal. While writing an independent reference semantics for interval operators, an AI tool suspected the rewrite. A bounded-exhaustive harness comparing Lucene's real operators with that reference on every tiny document then failed 300,120 of 1,436,292 negated-OR checks on main and 0 of 248,076 in a smaller run with the fix. A deterministic public-API JUnit followed.

The method in full177 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation (the hunt's semi-formal tier; no model checker, SMT solver or prover).

  • In a hunt unit (spans and intervals, 2026-10-26) the finder first suspected the defect by reading the callers of pullUpDisjunctions while writing IvRef, an exact reference semantics for interval operators.
  • The harness IvTV runs the real interval sources of the main jars against IvRef over every document of up to 6 positions over {a, b, c} (and up to 4 positions with stacked tokens), for every interval expression of 1-2 operator levels: 1,401,702,324 checks on separate build machines.
  • Its rewrite check for a negated filter over an OR (the reference takes the excluded side as the plain union of the clauses) failed 300,120 of 1,436,292 checks on stock main and 0 of 248,076 with the fix shadowed, in a smaller run (287,753,697 checks in all). That count also includes nesting shapes, so it is the signal, not a measure of the regression.
  • The finder then wrote a deterministic public-API JUnit; the independent checks, history sweep, prior-report search and security screen followed.

History

#

Introduced by LUCENE-8477 (merged 2019-03-27, first released in 8.1.0), which replaced one filter against the whole OR with an OR of per-clause filters. 8.0.0 gives the documented answer; 8.1.0, 8.1.1, 8.2.0, 8.11.4, 9.0.0, 9.12.3, 10.0.0, 10.5.2 and main are wrong (executed), and git ancestry puts every release in between in the range. A regression; not fixed upstream.

Versions tested6 rows

Introduced by LUCENE-8477 "Automatically rewrite disjunctions when internal gaps matter" (apache/lucene-solr#620, issue #9523, by @romseygeek, approved by @jimczi, merged 2019-03-27): f1782d0dd11 on the main line (first release 9.0.0) and the backport 2571bf355f8 (first release 8.1.0). Before it, notContainedBy built one difference filter against the whole big source. Later commits only moved the package to the queries module (8.2.0), reformatted it and made code cleanups. No commit-level bisection; the 8.0.0/8.1.0 jars bracket it and the built query's shape changes there. There is no 10.6.0 release yet, but the unreleased branch_10x and branch_10_6 carry the same build(), so a fix should go there too. Upstream main 49483e82b357 (2026-10-26) has no intervals change since ea57360912ed.

Scope: for an OR whose clauses nest, such as or("x y z", y), 8.0.0 and or(false, ...) were already wrong through minimization; the regression claim is for clauses that do not nest.

Bug record

LFV-291, snapshot of 2026-10-27

Registry title
Intervals.notContainedBy(small, or(B1, B2)) returns small intervals that lie inside B1 or B2, because the default disjunction rewrite turns it into an OR of per-clause filters
Area
queries / intervals (Intervals.notContainedBy: NotContainedByIntervalsSource disjunction rewrite, LUCENE-8477)
Priority
Medium, rank 35: Since 8.1, notContainedBy over an OR containing a phrase silently over-matches on ordinary text, and Elasticsearch and OpenSearch filters expose it, so it heads the intervals rows.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that call Intervals.notContainedBy with an OR as the big (excluded) source that the default rewrite splits into more than one group, which needs at least one clause of minExtent > 1 such as a phrase (written directly, through StandardQueryParser's fn:notContainedBy with fn:or [exec], through a multi-word query-time synonym in Intervals.analyzedText with no user-written OR [exec], or through the Elasticsearch and OpenSearch not_contained_by filter over any_of [read]), get silently over-matching results with the default codec, IndexWriterConfig and searcher, at any index size, with -ea and -da, in every release from 8.1.0 through 10.5.2 and on main: a small interval is kept when it lies outside at least one clause instead of outside all of them, so the exclusion is weakened (3 hits instead of 1 in the repro), with no error, crash or index damage; an OR of single terms only is grouped into one clause and is correct.
Summary
You index 'i love new york', 'york city is big' and 'york is here' and search notContainedBy(york, or(phrase(new, york), phrase(york, city))), meaning 'york that is not part of new york or york city'; you expect only the third document, which is what or(false, ...) and the nested form notContainedBy(notContainedBy(york, 'new york'), 'york city') return. Instead all three documents match: the default disjunction rewrite (LUCENE-8477) pulls the OR out of the excluded operand and builds or(NOT_CONTAINED_BY(york, 'new york'), NOT_CONTAINED_BY(york, 'york city')), which computes 'not inside B1 or not inside B2' where the javadoc promises 'not inside (B1 or B2)'. So adding a clause to the exclusion adds hits: notContainedBy(a, or(phrase(c, a), b)) matches 'c a' although notContainedBy(a, phrase(c, a)) does not.
