Hunspell spell() ignores KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG on COMPOUNDRULE compound parts

When a Hunspell dictionary builds a compound with a COMPOUNDRULE, Lucene's spell checker ignores the restrictions on the compound's parts and accepts words the dictionary forbids and C Hunspell rejects, such as the lower-case Swedish street name tolvkronorsgatan. It fails on every release tested since 8.9.0; stemming is not affected.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

Hunspell dictionaries build compounds with compound flags or with COMPOUNDRULE patterns. Entries can also carry restrictions such as KEEPCASE or FORCEUCASE. Lucene enforces those restrictions on flag compounds and skips them on rule compounds.

  1. You load the public Swedish dictionary sv_SE and call spell("tolvkronorsgatan"). A compound rule builds it from tolv (twelve), kronors and gatan ("the street"), and the dictionary marks gatan FORCEUCASE: a compound that ends in it is a street name and must be capitalized.
  2. You expect false: C Hunspell 1.3.2 and 1.7.2 reject it and accept the capitalized Tolvkronorsgatan, and Lucene itself rejects storgatan, which the dictionary builds with compound flags instead of a rule.
  3. Instead spell returns true. Lucene checks a rule compound's parts only against the rule: the first and middle parts are raw dictionary lookups (Hunspell.java:445), and the last part never gets the FORCEUCASE check (:475-493) that flag compounds get (:279).
  4. The same on tiny dictionaries with COMPOUNDRULE GH and bar/H: with foo/GK (KEEPCASE) Lucene accepts Foobar and FOOBAR; with foo/GN (NEEDAFFIX), foo/GW (FORBIDDENWORD) or foo/GF (COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG) it accepts foobar. C Hunspell 1.7.2 rejects all of them, and so does Lucene when the same words are joined by COMPOUNDFLAG.
  5. No error, with assertions on or off; it fails on every release tested from 8.9.0 to 10.5.2 (7 releases) and on main. suggest() offers such words too: Foobar for the typo Foobarr, where C Hunspell suggests foobar.

Who is affected

#

Applications that use Lucene's opt-in Hunspell spell checker (Hunspell.spell, suggest) with a COMPOUNDRULE dictionary whose rule entries also carry one of these flags. On the Swedish sv_SE dictionary, lower-case rule compounds that must be capitalized are accepted (run for words ending in gatan, sverige and afrika); the other flags show on synthetic dictionaries only. Stemming, indexing and search are unchanged.

Mechanism, terms and numbers897 words

A Hunspell dictionary can build compound words with compound flags (COMPOUNDFLAG, COMPOUNDBEGIN, ...) or with COMPOUNDRULE patterns over flags. Its entries can also carry restrictions: KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG. Lucene's Hunspell.spell applies these restrictions to the parts of flag compounds but not to the parts of rule compounds: checkCompoundRules (Hunspell.java:437-464 on main a9779ef3c193) takes every non-final part straight from dictionary.lookupWord (:445), and checkLastCompoundPart (:475-493) never applies the FORCEUCASE check. So spell() accepts compounds that C Hunspell 1.7.2 rejects (1.3.2 too, except the COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG case): on the public Swedish dictionary sv_SE, lower-case rule compounds such as tolvkronorsgatan; on small dictionaries, a KEEPCASE first part in capitalized or upper case and a NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD or COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG entry as a first part (NEEDAFFIX also as a middle part). suggest() offers such words as corrections (run for the KEEPCASE and FORCEUCASE cases). Nothing throws, assertions on and off give the same result, and it fails on all 7 releases run from 8.9.0, the first with the spell-checking API, to 10.5.2; the main-line history shows only refactors of this code in between. Stemming (HunspellStemFilter, getRoots) is not.

Terms

#
  • Flag compound: a compound accepted through COMPOUNDFLAG or COMPOUNDBEGIN / COMPOUNDMIDDLE / COMPOUNDEND: every part carries a compound flag.
  • Rule compound: a compound accepted because a COMPOUNDRULE pattern over flags matches its parts, for example COMPOUNDRULE GH (a G word, then an H word) or GH* (then any number of H words).
  • Per-entry flags (hunspell(5) of C Hunspell 1.7.2): KEEPCASE "Forbid uppercased and capitalized forms of words signed with KEEPCASE flags"; FORCEUCASE "Last word part of a compound with flag FORCEUCASE forces capitalization of the whole compound word" (the manual's example is the Dutch "straat", street); NEEDAFFIX "virtual stems in the dictionary, words only valid when affixed. Except, if the dictionary word has a homonym or a zero affix"; FORBIDDENWORD "forbidden word form"; COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG "Dictionary words with this flag are removed from the beginning and middle of compound words".

The bug, step by step (spell("Foobar") with KEEPCASE K, COMPOUNDRULE GH, foo/GK, bar/H)

Exposure and likelihood256 words
  • Who is hit. Code that calls Hunspell.spell or Hunspell.suggest (lucene-analysis-common) with a dictionary that uses COMPOUNDRULE and whose rule-flagged entries also carry KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD or COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG.   • What they get. spell says "correct" for words the dictionary forbids; suggest can offer them as corrections (Foobarr gives [Foobar], C 1.7.2 suggests foobar; tolvkronorsgaatan gives [tolvkronorsgatan], C suggests nothing). Lower-case numeral rule compounds ending in three of them (gatan, sverige, afrika) were run and are accepted; the other ten were not run. How often such words occur in real text was not measured. Two more public dictionaries declare some of these flags next to COMPOUNDRULE; whether their rule entries carry them was not checked: Galician gl_ES (LibreOffice collection: NEEDAFFIX, KEEPCASE, 3 rules) and Dutch nl (wooorm/dictionaries collection: KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, FORBIDDENWORD, 37 numeral rules; the LibreOffice nl_NL copy has no COMPOUNDRULE).

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • hunspell(5) defines KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG with no compound-flag-only exception; C Hunspell enforces them on rule compounds.
  • Lucene says Hunspell "can be used in place of native Hunspell for many languages" and enforces these flags on flag compounds (keepcase.wrong).
  • The rule path contradicts itself: it rejects a NEEDAFFIX entry as the last part, accepts it in the middle.
The full argument406 words
  • The format. hunspell(5) of C Hunspell 1.7.2 (man/hunspell.5): KEEPCASE (:561-566) "Forbid uppercased and capitalized forms of words signed with KEEPCASE flags"; FORCEUCASE (:496-500) "Last word part of a compound with flag FORCEUCASE forces capitalization of the whole compound word"; NEEDAFFIX (:582-584) "words only valid when affixed. Except, if the dictionary word has a homonym or a zero affix" (an unflagged homonym is a valid word; C 1.7.2, doStem and the fix skip only the flagged homonym); COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG (:452-455) "Dictionary words with this flag are removed from the beginning and middle of compound words". None is limited to flag compounds. FORBIDDENWORD (:552-556, "This flag signs forbidden word form. Because affixed forms are also forbidden, we can subtract a subset from set of the accepted affixed and compound words") is the weakest: the text forbids whole word forms, compounds included, but does not name compound parts, so it rests on C's behaviour and Lucene's own flag path.
  • Lucene's documentation (Hunspell-rule semantics, broad compatibility). "This class can be used in place of native Hunspell for many languages for spell-checking and suggesting purposes" (Hunspell.java:35-36); its list of unsupported cases (Hungarian, non-BMP characters, PHONE; :39-44) does not cover this. spell returns "whether the given word's spelling is considered correct according to Hunspell rules" (:72).
  • C Hunspell. Two 1.7.2 builds reject every claimed word on the same dictionaries, and 1.3.2 every one except the COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG case, which 1.3.2 ignores on dictionary words in both kinds of compound (executed). In 1.7.2's compound_check, rule mode skips NEEDAFFIX homonyms, breaks on a FORBIDDENWORD or NEEDAFFIX first word, skips COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG words and checks FORCEUCASE before returning a rule match; spell() applies KEEPCASE to the returned first part (read). Nuspell's check_compound_with_rules has the same checks (read only).
  • Lucene's own tests. keepcase.wrong lists Unitvv and UNITVV (a KEEPCASE first part of a flag compound) and forceucase.wrong lists foobaz. No fixture combines COMPOUNDRULE with KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE or NEEDAFFIX.
  • History. LUCENE-9684 (d7968130c3f, apache/lucene-solr#2228) added the raw lookup; its PR says "I've reimplemented Hunspell's logic". LUCENE-9710 (9d45dfe776a) implemented FORCEUCASE on the flag path only; LUCENE-9776 (4b3fb1e0659) moved only the last rule part to doStem, for inflection; LUCENE-9785 (31a64927a46, "Like Hunspell does") kept the case check on the first part of flag compounds. No commit, test or comment says rule compounds skip these checks.
The devil's-advocate check171 words

Strongest case, rated weak by every reviewer: hunspell(5) Note III says "COMPOUNDRULE flags work completely separately from the compounding mechanisms using COMPOUNDFLAG, COMPOUNDBEGIN, etc. compound flags. (Use these flags on different entries for words)." Lucene documents compatibility only "for many languages", no fixture pins this behaviour, and sv_SE reuses its COMPOUNDMIDDLE/COMPOUNDEND flags as rule flags against that note.

Why it fails: Note III is about which compound flags a rule matches, not about per-entry flags such as KEEPCASE or NEEDAFFIX, and FORCEUCASE is defined for any compound. C Hunspell 1.7.2 (and 1.3.2 for all but COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG) and Nuspell apply the checks in rule mode, Lucene's rule path already applies NEEDAFFIX to the last part, and the synthetic repros reuse no flags and still fail, so it is not a misuse. A note in the manual's BREAK entry ("COMPOUNDRULE handles only the suffixation of the last word part") explains why non-final parts are not affix-stripped, not why their entry flags are ignored; the recommended fix keeps the raw lookup. Verdict: a defect.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Give the rule parts the flag path's entry checks

Give rule-compound parts the checks flag-compound parts already get: pass the original case into checkCompoundRules, drop NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG entries from first and middle parts, case-check the first part (KEEPCASE), and skip last-part readings with a FORCEUCASE problem. Validated: the repro passes and the analysis-common tests run 1,915 tests with 0 failures (18 skipped).

Give the rule parts the flag path's entry checks (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

Trade-offs

  • One extra pass over the homonyms of each candidate part and a small IntsRef per matched part; no benchmark was run.
  • Homonyms need a stated rule. The fix filters every entry on its own. C 1.7.2 decides by the homonym listed first in the .dic (affixmgr.cxx:1671-1696, :1747-1756) and Lucene's flag path by the first homonym in Lucene's sorted entry order (Stemmer.java:237-240, Hunspell.java:163-184, :232). In a probe on 32 small dictionaries (foo/G plus a flagged foo), the fix accepts foobar/Foobar with a COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG, FORBIDDENWORD or KEEPCASE homonym in every order; C rejects them when the flagged homonym is listed first, and the flag path when it sorts first. NEEDAFFIX homonyms are skipped one by one everywhere. Homonym order already separates Lucene from C on plain words (FORBIDDENWORD foo), so the PR should state its rule and add a homonym test; fix.diff's helper javadoc holds for single entries only.
  • acceptCase also drops Lucene's hidden case-variant entries (the capitalized copies Lucene adds for mixed-case words and for all-caps words with flags, Dictionary.java:1062-1064, like Hunspell's ONLYUPCASE entries) from middle rule parts, and from the first part unless the word is title or upper case, as the flag path already does (Hunspell.java:166, :186-198); no test covers that case.
  • Forbidden compounds stop being accepted and suggested. A dictionary that relied on the old behaviour relied on something C Hunspell does not do.
  • For the PR, the repro can become fixture files in Lucene's style (.aff / .dic / .good / .wrong, run by TestSpellChecking), one per flag, plus a .sug case.

Not run. doStem also strips affixes, so first and middle parts would start matching inflected forms, which hunspell(5) says COMPOUNDRULE does not handle ("handles only the suffixation of the last word part"); it would need a roots-only mode and still needs the case and FORBIDDENWORD checks.

Downstream note

#

Downstream token filters built on HunspellStemFilter are not affected; only code that calls Lucene's spell-checking API (Hunspell.spell, suggest) gets the wrong verdicts.

How it was found

#

Differential testing of Lucene's Hunspell.spell against C Hunspell 1.7.2 on generated compounding dictionaries; minimizing the disagreeing dictionaries gave KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG clusters on one code path, checkCompoundRules. A public-API JUnit then reproduced it on the stock jar.

The method in full192 words

Method: cross-implementation differential testing (the hunt's non-formal tier).

  • Inputs: seeded, grammar-generated synthetic dictionaries with one or two compounding directives (COMPOUNDFLAG, COMPOUNDBEGIN/END, COMPOUNDRULE) plus modifiers such as KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG, 1-4 short roots, and every short word over the roots' letters plus 2-3-part concatenations.
  • Lucene's spell() and getRoots() (public API, stock jar) were compared with C Hunspell 1.7.2 built from the pristine source; every disagreement was re-checked on a Linux distribution build of 1.7.2.
  • Greedy one-element-at-a-time minimization of the disagreeing dictionaries gave KEEPCASE (4 dictionaries), FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD and COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG (4) clusters, all on the same code path; the root cause was then read in Hunspell.checkCompoundRules.
  • Found 2026-10-27 on main a9779ef3c193; then checked by an independent reproducer, an intended-behaviour devil's advocate (which showed that hunspell(5) documents COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG for dictionary words, making it the fifth asserted manifestation, and found the sv_SE case), two executed reviews by a model from another family (OpenAI GPT-5.6), an adjudication, a history and prior-report follow-up, a security screen and a credibility audit. This doc added the compact JUnit, the gradle runs, the fix validation, C runs on the repro's dictionaries and the suggest() probe.

History

#

Present since COMPOUNDRULE support landed, LUCENE-9684 (d7968130c3f, 2021); the FORCEUCASE form since LUCENE-9710 (9d45dfe776a), both before any release. First released in 8.9.0 through the 8.x backport; fails on every release tested (7, from 8.9.0 to 10.5.2) and on main; the releases in between by code history. Not fixed upstream.

Bug record

#

LFV-731, snapshot of 2026-10-30

Registry title
Hunspell spell() skips the per-entry checks on COMPOUNDRULE compound parts, so it accepts compounds that C Hunspell rejects: a KEEPCASE first part in capitalized or upper case, a FORCEUCASE last part in lower case, and a NEEDAFFIX or FORBIDDENWORD entry as a first or middle part
Area
analysis / hunspell (Hunspell.spell compound checking, COMPOUNDRULE)
Priority
Low, rank 399: The opt-in Hunspell spell-checker accepts COMPOUNDRULE compounds that C Hunspell rejects, a spelling-verdict error that one real public dictionary (sv_SE) hits.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Only applications that call the opt-in Hunspell spell-checker (Hunspell.spell; suggest() validates its candidates with the same check but was not executed) with a COMPOUNDRULE dictionary whose rule-flagged entries also carry KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD or COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG are hit: spell() returns true for misspelled compounds that C Hunspell rejects, with no exception, the same with -ea and -da and with no scale needed; the public sv_SE dictionary shows it for FORCEUCASE (lower-case numeral rule compounds that end in one of its 13 FORCEUCASE entries, such as tolvkronorsgatan), the other flags are shown only on synthetic dictionaries, stemming and analysis (HunspellStemFilter, getRoots) are not affected, and every release from 8.9.0 to 10.5.2 has it.
Summary
You load a Hunspell dictionary that joins foo/G and bar/H by 'COMPOUNDRULE GH', give foo or bar one of the per-entry flags KEEPCASE, FORCEUCASE, NEEDAFFIX or FORBIDDENWORD, call Hunspell.spell, and expect the answer C Hunspell 1.3.2 and 1.7.2 give (and Lucene itself gives when the same parts are joined by COMPOUNDFLAG): Foobar and FOOBAR rejected when foo is KEEPCASE, foobar rejected when bar is FORCEUCASE, and foobar rejected when foo is a NEEDAFFIX virtual stem or a FORBIDDENWORD entry. Instead spell() returns true in all four cases, with and without -ea, in every release since 8.9.0; on the public Swedish dictionary sv_SE it accepts lower-case rule compounds such as tolvkronorsgatan that the dictionary's FORCEUCASE flag and C Hunspell reject. The cause: checkCompoundRules takes every non-final part as a raw dictionary.lookupWord result, without the NEEDAFFIX, FORBIDDENWORD, COMPOUNDFORBIDFLAG and first-part case (KEEPCASE) filters, and checkLastCompoundPart never applies the FORCEUCASE check (hasForceUCaseProblem) that the COMPOUNDFLAG path uses.
Introduced
d7968130c3f5, LUCENE-9684 'Hunspell: support COMPOUNDRULE' (apache/lucene-solr#2228, by donnerpeter; issue apache/lucene#10723): it added checkCompoundRules with raw dictionary.lookupWord lookups for the parts and no case argument; git blame on main still points Hunspell.java:445 to it [read: git blame and git show in the history clone] [exec: commit-level probe, the hunspell package of each commit compiled over the 9.0.0 jars: the parent cf5db8d6513 has no COMPOUNDRULE support; d7968130c3f accepts the KEEPCASE-first, NEEDAFFIX-first, NEEDAFFIX-last and FORBIDDENWORD-first rule compounds while its simple-word KEEPCASE/NEEDAFFIX/FORBIDDENWORD controls are correct]. The FORCEUCASE form starts at 9d45dfe776a (LUCENE-9710, apache/lucene-solr#2272), which implemented FORCEUCASE on the COMPOUNDFLAG path only (before it Lucene ignored FORCEUCASE everywhere); 4b3fb1e0659 (LUCENE-9776, apache/lucene-solr#2397) moved only the last rule part to Stemmer.doStem, which fixed NEEDAFFIX for the last part only [exec: commit-level probe]. Reached 8.x through the backport c6b964a1157 (apache/lucene-solr#2499, LUCENE-9687, merged 2021-05-28). Present since the feature's first commit, before any release; not a regression (2021-01-22 (main line, d7968130c3f); 2021-02-01 for the FORCEUCASE form (9d45dfe776a); 2021-05-28 (8.x backport))
