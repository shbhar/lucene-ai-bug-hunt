FastVectorHighlighter matchedFields: merging overlapping matches drops the first match's term infos, so WeightedFragListBuilder can return the wrong best fragment

When FastVectorHighlighter merges overlapping matches from several matchedFields, it keeps their summed boost and offsets but drops the first match's terms, so WeightedFragListBuilder underweights that passage: 0.61 instead of 2.13 in the repro, and a weaker passage is returned. In every release since 4.6.0.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

WeightedPhraseInfo(Collection), the constructor that merges overlapping matches from several fields, sums every member's boost and joins their offsets, but starts its term list empty and adds terms only from the second member on. WeightedFieldFragList scores a fragment from that term list, so the merged passage loses the first match's terms.

Background: matchedFields and weighted fragments

#
  1. FastVectorHighlighter can highlight one stored field using the matches of several indexed fields of the same text (matchedFields), for example a stemmed field and an unstemmed copy.
  2. For each field it lists the matches: a phrase or term with its query boost, its offsets and its terms, each with an IDF weight. Matches from different fields that overlap in the text are merged into one.
  3. A FragListBuilder then cuts the text into fragments and scores each one. SimpleFragListBuilder, the default, adds up the boosts; WeightedFragListBuilder adds IDF weight x boost over the distinct terms and scales by the square root of the number of terms.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Index one document with the same text, "running fast xx xx xx swim slow", in fields a and b (term vectors with offsets). Query a:"running fast" OR b:fast OR a:"swim slow" and call getBestFragments with matchedFields = {a, b}, fragment size 20, WeightedFragListBuilder and ScoreOrderFragmentsBuilder.
  2. You expect <b>running fast</b> xx xx xx: two clauses match it, one matches "swim slow". With field a alone both passages weigh 0.8679 and it wins the tie on position; under the documented formula b's extra match inside it raises that passage's weight.
  3. Instead you get xx <b>swim slow</b>. The overlapping matches a:"running fast" (offsets 0-12) and b:fast (8-12) are merged into one: boost 2.0 and offsets 0-12 are right, but its terms are just [fast], so running is gone.
  4. The weighted score is built from those terms, so the passage weighs 0.6137, below "swim slow" at 0.8679, with no error. With a one-line fix the merge keeps [running, fast, fast] and the passage weighs 2.1259.

Who is affected

#

Callers of the matchedFields overload of getBestFragments that pass the non-default WeightedFragListBuilder: terms are lost whenever two matched fields match overlapping text, and the snippet changes when that drops the passage below a competing one. Hits, scores and highlight spans are right; the Simple and Single builders are unaffected. The recorded search found no affected downstream default.

Mechanism, terms and numbers707 words

FastVectorHighlighter can highlight a stored field from the matches of several indexed fields (matchedFields). It collects each field's matches and merges matches that overlap into one WeightedPhraseInfo. The merging constructor WeightedPhraseInfo(Collection) says "While we are here merge the boosts and termInfos" and does sum every member's boost and join every member's offsets, but it starts its term list empty and adds terms only from the second member on, so the first member's terms are lost. WeightedFragListBuilder scores a fragment from those terms (the sum of the IDF weights of distinct terms times the boost, scaled by the square root of the number of terms), so a passage that two fields match counts for less than it should and a passage with fewer matches can be returned as the best snippet. In the one-document repro the passage matched by a:"running fast" and b:fast weighs 0.6137, less than the 0.8679 it gets with field a alone, and "swim slow" (0.8679) wins; with the one-line fix termsInfos = new ArrayList<>(first.termsInfos) it weighs 2.1259 and wins. The visible effect is the snippet choice: hits, scores and highlight spans are right, and SimpleFragListBuilder and SingleFragListBuilder are unaffected (only the debug-only getText() text of the merged match also lacks the first match's terms). It is in every release since 4.6.0 (2013).

Exposure and likelihood323 words
  • Who is hit. Callers of the matchedFields overload (FastVectorHighlighter.java:244-262) that pass WeightedFragListBuilder; the highlighter's default builder is SimpleFragListBuilder (:49). The two merging constructors are also public, but this overload is the in-tree path to them. The terms are lost whenever two matched fields match overlapping text, the normal case for the set-up the overload is for: several fields indexed from the same text with different analysis (its javadoc advises that "all matchedFields share the same source as storedField", :224-226), such as a stemmed field and an unstemmed copy. The returned snippet changes only when the lower weight drops that fragment below a competing one, as in the repro.
  • What changes. Which fragment is returned first; no exception, and hits, scores and highlight spans are right. The debug-only getText()/toString() text of the merged match also leaves out the first match's terms.
  • How often. Deterministic, with assertions on and off. A bounded-exhaustive check of the merge (the real classes against a handwritten expected merge, distinct synthetic term texts; 1 to 3 matched fields, up to 2 matches each over 4 positions, boosts 1 or 2: 305,319 configurations) found that every one of the 490,792 merged groups lacks exactly the first member's terms, and every configuration with a merge gets a fragment weight different from the documented formula; 0 mismatches with the fix.
  • Order dependence. On a full tie of start offset, end offset and boost, the set order decides which member loses its terms (MergedIterator breaks ties by input index; its javadoc leaves that order undefined): an independent reproducer's test returned "Red ..." with {body, body_cs} and "blue" with {body_cs, body}. That is an instability, not a broken documented guarantee.
  • Downstream. The recorded search found no downstream default that reaches the combination: Elasticsearch's matched_fields uses SimpleFragListBuilder or SingleFragListBuilder, and Solr's FVH path calls only the single-field getBestFragments. It found no report since 4.6.0.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • Its own comment says "merge the boosts and termInfos"; its javadoc notes only that it "just grabs seqnum from the first info".
  • The single-field merge addIfNoOverlap keeps both matches' terms; the package docs define the weight from the IDF weights of distinct terms.
  • An extra match lowers the passage's computed weight: 0.8679 with a alone, 0.6137 with a and b.
The full argument189 words
  • Its own comment and javadoc. "While we are here merge the boosts and termInfos" (FieldPhraseList.java:263); the javadoc names one shortcut only: "Note that this just grabs seqnum from the first info" (:259). The code treats first specially for seqnum, boost and offsets (:272-274), not for its terms.
  • The documented weight. WeightedFragListBuilder is the "sum-of-distinct-weights-approach. The totalBoost is calculated by summarizing the IDF-weights of distinct terms" (package-info.java:189-190); the merge feeds it every member's boost but only some members' terms.
  • The sibling merge keeps both. addIfNoOverlap, the single-field merge, adds the other match's terms and boost (FieldPhraseList.java:178-179).
  • No test or discussion endorses it. The upstream matchedFields tests use SimpleFragListBuilder only (TestFastVectorHighlighter.java:917) and no test asserts merged terms. The introducing thread (#6338, LUCENE-5274, 31 comments) discusses MergedIterator, immutability and the debug-only text, never dropping terms.
  • Precedent. #15635 ("Fix FVH boost issue (#15442)", merged 2026-02-04) fixed lost scoring data in the sibling merge; its CHANGES entry, filed under the issue number GITHUB#15442, reads "Preserve boost values when merging overlapping phrases in FastVectorHighlighter so highlight scoring fully reflects query boosts".
The devil's-advocate check216 words

Strongest case for "intended": fragment ranking is a heuristic, and no contract beyond the package docs' formula says which fragment must win; WeightedFragListBuilder is opt-in; the FastVectorHighlighter is legacy (on #15442 a maintainer called it old/legacy and suggested the UnifiedHighlighter, and a 2025-11-21 dev@ thread proposed deprecating it); the set-order effect rests on a tie order MergedIterator does not document; fix A changes existing fragment weights; and with the fix a stemmed field plus its plain copy count both "run" and "running", which arguably counts one word twice.

Why it fails: the weight is a documented formula computed from these terms, and the merge keeps the other members' data but drops the first member's for no stated reason, against its own comment; the result depends on sort and set order, and an extra match can lower a passage. The highlighter is not deprecated (no @Deprecated in the package at 5adff78bb168) and its scoring fixes are still merged (#15635). Counting both "run" and "running" is what addIfNoOverlap already does, and repeated texts are counted once. It does justify priority Low. The independent reproducer, the cross-model reviews and the devil's advocate rated the intended-behaviour case weak; the adjudication agreed. The later cross-model fact check of this doc rated it moderate: a maintainer could reasonably decline the change.

A. Start the merged term infos from the first member's (recommended; validated)

#

FieldPhraseList.java, +1/-1: the merging constructor starts termsInfos as a copy of first.termsInfos instead of an empty list; the loop then adds members 2..n as before.

-      termsInfos = new ArrayList<>();
+      termsInfos = new ArrayList<>(first.termsInfos);
       seqnum = first.seqnum;
       boost = first.boost;

Trade-offs

  • It accumulates term infos the way the single-field merge addIfNoOverlap does, a plain addAll (FieldPhraseList.java:178).
  • When two fields match the same term at the same offsets, the merged match now holds two terms for it, so the length norm becomes sqrt(2) instead of 1: existing fragment weights change in that case, a behaviour change worth a line in CHANGES. It follows the documented formula, and no upstream test changes.
  • Unchanged and not claimed: WeightedFieldFragList keeps the first occurrence's weight per term text, so the same text with different IDF in two fields still depends on order.

What the checks validated

  • Without the fix the repro fails with assertions on and off (gradle, seed DEADBEEF).
  • With the fix: the repro passes with assertions on and off; :lucene:highlighter:test passes with assertions on and off, 723 tests each, among them TestFastVectorHighlighter, TestFieldPhraseList, TestWeightedFragListBuilder, TestSimpleFragListBuilder and TestSingleFragListBuilder; an independent re-run with assertions on counted 35 classes, 723 tests, 0 failures, 0 skipped; :lucene:highlighter:checkGoogleJavaFormat passes.
  • One seed; nightly tests and the full suite were not run. The other checks' own tests pass with the same line, and 4.6.0 passes with only FieldPhraseList replaced by it.

B. Merge all members' term infos, skipping exact duplicates (same text and offsets)

#

Keeps the norm at 1 when two fields match the same term at the same offsets.

Trade-offs

More code, and it departs from addIfNoOverlap, which does a plain addAll. TermInfo.equals compares positions only (FieldTermStack.java:238-249), so it cannot be the dedup key across fields. The repro passes under either option (with B the passage would weigh 1.7358 > 0.8679). Not validated.

Downstream note

#

The recorded search found no affected downstream default (Elasticsearch's matched_fields uses the Simple or Single builder, Solr's FVH path the single-field call); an application on this overload avoids the loss with SimpleFragListBuilder, which scores by the correctly merged boost, a different weighting.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal differential checking: the matchedFields path of FastVectorHighlighter was compared with its single-field path and with WeightedFieldFragList's documented weight formula, guided by the merging constructor's own comment. A bounded-exhaustive check of the merge (305,319 configurations) and a one-test public-API JUnit confirmed it.

The method in full109 words

A bounded comparison of the matchedFields path against the single-field path and against the weight formula, guided by the comment "merge the boosts and termInfos", showed the merged match missing the first member's terms. It was reduced to one public-API JUnit test and backed by a bounded-exhaustive differential check of the real merge against a handwritten expected merge written from the comments, with distinct synthetic term texts (1 to 3 matched fields, up to 2 matches each over 4 positions, boosts 1 or 2: 305,319 configurations, 490,792 merged groups): every merged group lacked exactly the first member's terms, with boost and offsets right, and the fix gave 0 mismatches.

Priority

minor

Summary
You index one document whose fields a and b both hold 'running fast xx xx xx swim slow' (stored, with term vectors, positions and offsets) and highlight field a with FastVectorHighlighter.getBestFragments(..., matchedFields = {a, b}, 20, 1, new WeightedFragListBuilder(), new ScoreOrderFragmentsBuilder(), ...) for a:"running fast" OR b:fast OR a:"swim slow". You expect the passage that a:"running fast" and b:fast both match to be weighed with the term infos of both matches, as the merging constructor's comment ('merge the boosts and termInfos') and WeightedFieldFragList's sum-of-distinct-IDF-weights formula say, so the best fragment is '<b>running fast</b> xx xx xx' (weight 2.13 against 0.87). Instead the merged match keeps the summed boost and both offsets but only the second match's term infos ('running' is lost), the passage weighs 0.61, and the best fragment returned is 'xx <b>swim slow</b>', with no error.
