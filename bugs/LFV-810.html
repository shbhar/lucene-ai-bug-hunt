A nested Intervals.ordered(a, a) or unordered(a, a) passes its inner gap to its parent, so maxgaps misses matches

When an interval query nests a whole ordered(a, a) or unordered(a, a) in another ordered or unordered, Lucene counts the gap inside the pair as the parent's own: maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, a), a)) misses a x a a, while (a, b) finds a x b a. Silent, since 8.5.0.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

Interval queries compute a parent's gaps as its span minus the widths of its children. A whole ordered(a, a) or unordered(a, a) is built as an internal repeat source whose width is computed per copy (1 + 1 = 2 for two one-position terms), not over its span, so a parent counts the gap inside the pair as its own.

Background in three steps

#
  1. An interval query returns position ranges: ordered(a, b) on a x b returns [0,2]. Its width() is normally the number of positions it spans, here 3.
  2. gaps() of an ordered or unordered interval is its span minus the widths of its immediate children: ordered(ordered(a, b), a) on a x b a gives 4 - (3 + 1) = 0. Gaps inside a child do not count.
  3. maxgaps(n, ...) keeps only intervals with at most n gaps. Since 10.1.0 (#13819) nested ordered and unordered sources stay separate children of their parent, so their inner gaps stay hidden.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Index one document, a x a a (positions 0-3), and search maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, a), a)): the pair "a then a", followed by a, with no gap between those two parts.
  2. You expect 1 hit: the pair matches [0,2] and the last a sits at 3, right after it. IntervalIterator#gaps counts only the gaps between a parent's immediate parts, not inside them, and ordered(ordered(a, b), a) on a x b a does give 1 hit.
  3. Instead you get 0 hits. Two equal arguments become one internal repeat source whose width() is 1 + 1 = 2 instead of its 3-position span, so the parent computes gaps = 4 - (2 + 1) = 1 and maxgaps(0) drops the document.
  4. The same happens for ordered(unordered(a, a), b) on a x a b (since 8.5.0), unordered(ordered(a, a), b) and ordered(x, ordered(a, a)), also from fn: query strings. A no-op maxwidth(100, ...) around the pair brings the hit back; used under MUST_NOT, the document is wrongly kept.

Who is affected

#

Anyone who nests a whole ordered or unordered of identical sources (ordered(a, a), unordered(a, a), unorderedNoOverlaps(a, a), or analyzedText of a phrase with a repeated word and no gap limit) inside another ordered or unordered, with maxgaps above it, on documents where the repeats are not adjacent. Default settings.

Mechanism, terms and numbers699 words

Intervals.ordered and Intervals.unordered fold equal sub-sources into an internal RepeatingIntervalsSource. When every argument is equal (ordered(a, a), unordered(a, a)), the whole operator is replaced by that repeat source, whose iterators compute width() per copy (1 + 1 = 2 for the two one-position copies in a x a) instead of over the interval's span (3). Counting per copy is right for a run inside a larger operator (ordered(a, a, b)), but when the repeat stands for a whole operator nested in another ordered or unordered, the parent, which computes its gaps as its span minus its children's widths, counts the child's inner gap as its own. maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, a), a)) therefore misses a x a a while maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, b), a)) matches a x b a; ordered(unordered(a, a), b) misses a x a b in every release since 8.5.0. Weight#matches agrees with the scorer, used as an exclusion the query keeps documents it should remove, and a no-op maxwidth(100, ...) around the pair restores the hit. Not fixed upstream; a role-aware width() plus role-aware equals/hashCode in one class fixes it.

The mechanism (main 5adff78bb168, lucene/queries/.../intervals/)

#
  1. The collapse. OrderedIntervalsSource.build passes its arguments through deduplicate (OrderedIntervalsSource.java:29-38), which turns each run of equal sub-sources into RepeatingIntervalsSource.build(source, count) (:40-57). When everything collapses into one repeat source, it gets ris.setName("ORDERED") (:53-55) and is returned as the whole operator (:34-35). UnorderedIntervalsSource does the same with a count map (UnorderedIntervalsSource.java:33-57, setName("UNORDERED") at :53-55). The name is read only by toString() (RepeatingIntervalsSource.java:123-125).
  2. The summed width. The class javadoc: the iterators have "a specialized IntervalIterator#width() implementation that sums up the widths of the individual sub-iterators, rather than just returning the full span of the iterator" (RepeatingIntervalsSource.java:38-40). DuplicateIntervalIterator.width() adds one term per copy (:156-164): cache[pos * 2] - cache[pos * 2 + 1] + 1 (:161), that is start - end + 1, which is the copy's width only for a one-position copy such as a term (for longer copies the sign is wrong: the separate LFV-306; the repro uses terms, so its numbers do not depend on it). DuplicateMatchesIterator.width() sums end - start + 1 per copy for Weight#matches (:315-322); neither looks at the name.
  3. The parent reads child widths. The ordered iterator starts from end - start + 1 and subtracts every child's width() (OrderedIntervalsSource.java:166-172); gaps() returns the result (:185-186). The unordered iterator does the same (UnorderedIntervalsSource.java:158-161), and so does atLeast (MinimumShouldMatchIntervalsSource.java:235-238, from reading only).
  4. Equality hides the nesting too. hashCode/equals ignore the name (RepeatingIntervalsSource.java:103-114): ordered(x, ordered(a, a)).equals(ordered(x, a, a)) is true, while the (a, b) pair is not equal. A query cache cannot tell the nested query from the flat one.
  5. Why wrappers hide it. IntervalFilter (behind maxwidth and maxgaps) does not override width(), so a filtered child reports its span. or() and extend() hide it too: their interval iterators (DisjunctionIntervalIterator, DisjunctionIntervalsSource.java:154, and ExtendedIntervalIterator) do not override width() either, so they report the span, and maxgaps(0, ordered(or(ordered(a, a), z), a)) and maxgaps(0, ordered(extend(ordered(a, a), 0, 0), a)) both find a x a a (executed). Only the matches iterator of extend() passes the child's width through (ExtendedIntervalsSource.java:70).

Exposure and likelihood235 words
  • Trigger paths (executed). The Java factories (ordered(ordered(a, a), a), ordered(x, ordered(a, a)), ordered(unordered(a, a), b), unordered(ordered(a, a), b)); StandardQueryParser strings such as fn:maxgaps(0 fn:ordered(fn:ordered(a a) a)), which misses a x a a; Intervals.analyzedText of a phrase with a repeated word and maxGaps -1, which returns a bare ORDERED(bora,bora) (IntervalBuilder.java:143-146), so maxgaps(0, ordered(analyzedText("bora bora", ..., -1, true), island)) misses bora x bora island while the stricter maxGaps 5 finds it; Intervals.unorderedNoOverlaps(a, a), which builds ORDERED(a,a) (Intervals.java:445-447).
  • Default settings. Default codec, analyzer and searcher, assertions on and off (the runs used a one-document in-memory index). Nothing is written to the index; only query results change.
  • Silent and consistent. No exception; Weight#matches returns null like the scorer, which is consistent with match-based highlighters also seeing no match (no highlighter was run).
  • Both directions. As a filter it loses documents; as an exclusion it keeps them: MatchAll MUST plus IntervalQuery(maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, a), a))) MUST_NOT, and Intervals.notContainedBy(x, maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, a), a))), return 1 hit on a x a a instead of 0.
  • How likely. Rare: it needs a nested whole repeat, a gap limit above it and non-adjacent repeats (on a a b there is no inner gap to leak). The recorded searches found no report of it, although it has shipped since 8.5.0 (2020). For such a query and document the miss happens every time.

Minimal repro

#

One JUnit test, one method, public API: one document a x a b, core StandardAnalyzer, a plain IndexSearcher. It uses ordered(unordered(a, a), b), the shape that fails on every release since 8.5.0 and does not depend on how ordered deduplicates. It fails with assertions on and off and passes with the fix.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • IntervalIterator#gaps excludes "the gaps inside those sub-intervals" of a parent's immediate parts.
  • #13819 (10.1.0) made nested ordered and unordered sources hide their inner gaps; with unequal terms, or a no-op maxwidth(100, ...) around the pair, the query matches.
  • LUCENE-9099, which added this code, rejected nesting duplicates as ORDERED(a, a) because that "breaks MAXGAPS filtering".
The full argument271 words
  • Contract. "Note that this returns the number of gaps between the immediate sub-intervals of this interval, and does not include the gaps inside those sub-intervals." (IntervalIterator.java:65-66). Package docs: maxgaps "Filters out intervals that have more than a set number of gaps between their constituent sub-intervals" (package-info.java:55-57).
  • Design text. LUCENE-8597 (#9643), which added gaps(): ordered/unordered combinations "would return the number of positions separating their immediate children. Note that this does not include the gaps within any of those children themselves."
  • Maintainer intent for nesting. #13819 (jimczi, merged 2024-10-03): "ordered(\"a\", ordered(\"b\", \"c\")) should result in a different gap compared to ordered(\"a\", \"b\", \"c\")"; "The flattening that is currently applied prevent some valid queries to match". Its tests testOrderedWithNoGap and testUnorderedWithNoGap (TestIntervalQuery.java:375-385, :341-351) use distinct terms; TestIntervals#testNestedMaxGaps (TestIntervals.java:751-768) treats a nested ordered as one sub-interval of its full span.
  • LUCENE-9099's own issue text (#10141): rewriting UNORDERED(a, b, c, a, b) to UNORDERED(ORDERED(a, a), ORDERED(b, b), c) "then breaks MAXGAPS filtering", i.e. a nested ORDERED(a, a) hides its inner gaps from the parent.
  • No consistent semantics explains the current answers. The unequal twin matches; a filter that removes nothing (maxwidth(100, ...), maxgaps(1000, ...)) around the pair flips 0 hits to 1; a stricter analyzedText (maxGaps 5) finds a document the unrestricted one (-1) misses; and whether two arguments compare equal changes the result.
  • No upstream test pins the current value. The repeat tests (TestIntervals.java:729-749, TestSimplifications.java:41-68) cover runs inside a larger operator and top-level repeats; all 1,768 tests of the queries, queryparser and highlighter modules pass with the fix.
The devil's-advocate check248 words

The strongest case for "intended": the RepeatingIntervalsSource javadoc sums the widths on purpose, and the LUCENE-9099 commit says this "preserves maxgaps filtering on ordered and unordered sources that contain repeats"; when LUCENE-9099 landed, nested ordered sources were flattened anyway, so ordered(ordered(a, a), a) meaning ordered(a, a, a) was the semantics of the day; the package is "experimental" (package-info.java:21); #13819's tests use only distinct terms; and a user could write ordered(a, a, a).

Why it fails: the javadoc is on a package-private class and describes the run role (ordered(a, a, b), where each copy is an immediate sub-interval of the parent); the class cannot tell that role from the whole-operator role, whose only marker, the name, nothing reads. #13819 replaced the flattening semantics with exactly the rule this violates. Flattening never crossed operator types, so ordered(unordered(a, a), b) leaking while ordered(unordered(a, c), b) did not was inconsistent even before 10.1.0, and that query has no flat equivalent. "Experimental" is about API stability, not wrong hit counts. The reviews did not all agree in form: the devil's-advocate verifier, which ran its own probes, rejected the argument (real, high confidence that it is not intended); read-only reviews by an independent reviewer from another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6), which could not run code, both rated the argument moderate. The intended-behaviour review rated the bug real (medium); the refute review returned "not real" (low) because no test could run, and noted that the static evidence favours a defect.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Role-aware width and equality in RepeatingIntervalsSource (validated)

Let a repeat source that stands for a whole operator report its full span from width(), keep gaps() as span minus the copies' widths, and make equals/hashCode tell the two roles apart (+31/-12 in RepeatingIntervalsSource). The repro passes with assertions on and off, and so do all 1,768 queries, queryparser and highlighter tests.

@Override
public int width() {
  return wholeSource ? super.width() : copyWidths(); // copyWidths(): the old summing loop
}

@Override
public int gaps() {
  return super.width() - copyWidths();
}
All options and trade-offs3 options · 574 words

Three options. A was built and validated on main 5adff78bb168; B was not built; C (the sibling LFV-807's fix) was run and fixes only part of this bug. Any fix should also update the RepeatingIntervalsSource class javadoc, and it composes with LFV-306's one-line sign fix in the same width() loop (that fix alone leaves this bug).

A. Role-aware width and equality in RepeatingIntervalsSource (validated) (recommended)

#

A repeat source with a name (it stands for a whole ordered/unordered) passes wholeSource = true to its iterators: DuplicateIntervalIterator.width() returns super.width() (the span) and gaps() keeps subtracting the copies' widths; DuplicateMatchesIterator.width() returns endPosition() - startPosition() + 1. equals adds (this.name != null) == (o.name != null) and hashCode adds name != null. The class javadoc explains the two roles. One file, +31/-12.

@Override
public int width() {
  return wholeSource ? super.width() : copyWidths(); // copyWidths(): the old summing loop
}

@Override
public int gaps() {
  return super.width() - copyWidths();
}

Trade-offs

  • The equality part is needed: with a width-only change and the test framework's query cache on, ordered(x, ordered(a, a)) was served the cached result of the equal flat ordered(x, a, a) and still had 0 hits.
  • Keeps the collapse and what TestSimplifications asserts (ordered(a, a) still equals unorderedNoOverlaps(a, a)); relies on the name, set after construction, as the role marker.
  • A top-level ordered(a, a) now reports width() 3 for [0,2] instead of 2. width() is read only by parent operators (ordered, unordered, atLeast) and by matches iterators (every .width() call in the intervals package); scoring and maxwidth use end - start + 1.
  • What the validation does not show: the runs show that the change makes the repro pass and breaks none of the module tests; they say nothing about how maintainers will judge the approach (a role flag set after construction), about API compatibility beyond these tests, or about performance (no benchmark was run).

What the checks validated

  • Gradle in a scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168, OpenJDK 25.0.3, seed DEADBEEF: stock, the repro fails with assertions on and off; with the fix it passes, on and off.
  • All tests of the queries, queryparser and highlighter modules pass with the fix and the repro in place: 116 classes, 1,768 tests, 0 failures, 6 skipped.
  • checkGoogleJavaFormat, ecjLint, forbiddenApisMain and renderJavadoc of lucene/queries pass.
  • The independent reproducer's 11-test probe passes 11 of 11 with the same approach applied.

B. Do not return the bare repeat source for a whole operator (not built)

#

When deduplicate returns a single repeat source, wrap it in an OrderedIntervalsSource / UnorderedIntervalsSource with that one child instead of returning it, so the wrapper reports its span and the repeat its inner gaps.

Trade-offs

No role flag, and ordered(x, ordered(a, a)) stops equalling ordered(x, a, a) without touching equals. But it adds an iterator layer per nested repeat, the one-child conjunction path is untested, and the toString/equality assertions in TestSimplifications need checking.

C. Drop deduplication from ordered only (LFV-807's fix; partial) (validated by the checks)

#

Removing deduplicate from OrderedIntervalsSource.build (proposed for LFV-807) removes the ordered(a, a)-as-child shapes, but unordered must keep deduplicating so that unordered(a, a) does not match a single a.

Trade-offs

Fixes LFV-807 and part of this bug; use it together with A, not instead of it.

What the checks validated

Run on 5adff78bb168: the repro still fails with C alone and passes with A and C together.

Downstream note

#

Products that build interval queries from user rules inherit this when a rule with a repeated word and no gap limit is nested under a rule with a gap limit, and an application that uses such a query as an exclusion lets the document through; no downstream product was run for this doc.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal. A bounded-exhaustive comparison ran Lucene's interval iterators against an independently written reference semantics: every document of up to 4 positions over 3 terms and 6,856 query expressions (1,100,295 checks). Gap mismatches for nested repeated pairs pointed at this code; a public-API JUnit test on the stock jar confirmed it.

The method in full127 words

Method: semi-formal (bounded-exhaustive translation validation). A reference semantics for intervals was written from the javadoc, independently of Lucene's code. A test program enumerated every document of 1 to 4 positions over {a, b, U+10428} and 6,856 expressions of depth up to 3 over term, ordered, unordered, or, phrase, maxgaps(0|1) and atLeast(2 of 3), with repeated arguments, and compared exact minimal intervals, gaps, IntervalQuery hits and Weight#matches with the stock main jar: 1,100,295 checks.

  • The mismatches were then attributed with switchable fixes: with the fixes of the related repeat bugs applied, 13,469 mismatches remained; adding this fix brought them down to 3,596 (the rest belong to other defects).
  • The case was reduced to a public-API JUnit test on the stock jar, which failed with assertions on and off.

History

#

Introduced in 8.5.0 by LUCENE-9099 (7c1ba1aebee, branch_8x aa916bac3c3, 2020-02-06), which added the summed width and the collapse. The repro fails on every release run from 8.5.0 to 10.6.0 and on main; 8.4.1 passes. The same-operator shapes count only from 10.1.0. Not fixed upstream.

Versions tested6 rows

LFV-810, snapshot of 2026-11-09

Registry title
A nested Intervals.ordered(a, a) or unordered(a, a) passes its inner gap up to its parent, so maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, a), a)) misses 'a x a a' while maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, b), a)) matches 'a x b a'
Area
queries / intervals (a whole ordered/unordered of equal sub-sources nested in another operator: RepeatingIntervalsSource width(), Ordered/UnorderedIntervalsSource.deduplicate)
Priority
Medium, rank 202: Silent missed interval matches since 8.5.0 in the same repeat family as LFV-807, LFV-306 and LFV-809, but it needs a nested whole repeat under maxgaps with non-adjacent repeats.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Anyone whose interval query nests a whole ordered or unordered of identical sub-sources (ordered(a, a), unordered(a, a), unorderedNoOverlaps(a, a), or analyzedText of a phrase with a repeated word and no gap limit) inside another ordered or unordered (atLeast too, by code reading) and filters with maxgaps above it, through the Java factories or StandardQueryParser fn: strings, with default writer, codec, analyzer and searcher settings (the runs used a one-document index), with -ea and -da, in every release since 8.5.0 (all four shapes since 10.1.0), silently loses documents that the gaps() contract says match, and keeps them wrongly when that query is used as an exclusion (MUST_NOT, notContainedBy); Weight#matches agrees with the scorer, scores and maxwidth are unaffected, and nothing is written to the index.
Summary
You nest a repeated pair inside another interval operator, for example ordered(ordered(a, a), a), and filter it with maxgaps(0, ...): the IntervalIterator#gaps javadoc counts only the gaps between the parent's immediate sub-intervals, so 'a x a a' (sub-intervals [0,2] and [3,3]) should match, just as maxgaps(0, ordered(ordered(a, b), a)) matches 'a x b a'. Instead, because the two arguments of the inner ordered(a, a) are equal, Lucene builds it as a RepeatingIntervalsSource whose width() is the sum of its two copies (2) rather than its span (3), so the parent counts the inner 'x' as its own gap (gaps 1) and maxgaps(0) silently drops the document. The same happens for ordered(unordered(a, a), b), unordered(ordered(a, a), b) and ordered(x, ordered(a, a)), while wrapping the inner pair in a no-op filter such as maxwidth(100, ...) brings the hit back.
Introduced
7c1ba1aebeea540b67ae304deee60162baee2e12 (master, first released in 9.0.0) and its branch_8x backport aa916bac3c3369a461afa06e384e070657c32973 (the same day; first released in 8.5.0): 'LUCENE-9099: Correctly handle repeats in ORDERED and UNORDERED intervals' (apache/lucene-solr#1097 by romseygeek, one approving review by jimczi; the JIRA issue is now apache/lucene#10141). It changed ordered/unordered gaps() to span minus the sum of the children's width(), added IntervalIterator#width() and RepeatingIntervalsSource with a summed width(), and made deduplicate() replace a whole ordered(a, a)/unordered(a, a) by a RepeatingIntervalsSource that only setName("ORDERED"/"UNORDERED") marks [read: git blame on main, git log -S] [exec: the 8.4.1 jars plus only the intervals package of aa916bac3c33 flip unordered(ordered(a, a), b) from pass to fail, and the package of its parent 3246b2605869 behaves like 8.4.1].
