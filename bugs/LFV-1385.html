GreekStemmer tests the suffix -ομαστε (-omaste) before -ιομαστε (-iomaste), so αγαπιόμαστε (agapiómaste, "we are loved") is stemmed to αγαπι (agapi), not αγαπ (agap) like the verb's other forms

The finding in 30 seconds

#

A stemmer cuts word endings so that all forms of a word share one index term. Lucene's Greek stemmer applies numbered suffix rules; rule 11 handles the verb ending -ομαστε (-omaste) and its longer form -ιομαστε (-iomaste). It tests the shorter one first, so the longer one is never removed.

  1. Index one document, αγαπιόμαστε (agapiómaste, "we are loved"), with the stock new GreekAnalyzer(), then search for the analyzed αγαπιέμαι (agapiémai, "I am loved").
  2. You expect both to become αγαπ (agap): GreekStemmer cites Ntais's thesis, whose rule-set 11 removes -ομαστε (-omaste) or -ιομαστε (-iomaste) and gives ΑΓΑΠ + ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ → ΑΓΑΠ (AGAP + IOMASTE → AGAP) as its own example. The verb's other forms, such as αγαπιέται (agapiétai) and αγαπιόμουν (agapiómoun), do give αγαπ (agap).
  3. But rule 11 tests -ομαστε (-omaste, 6 letters) first, and every word ending in -ιομαστε (-iomaste, 7 letters) also ends in -ομαστε. So it cuts 6 letters, not 7: αγαπιομαστε (agapiomaste, 11 letters, accents removed) becomes αγαπι (agapi). The -ιομαστε branch below it never runs.
  4. The search finds 0 of 1 documents, with no error; with the two branches swapped it finds 1 of 1. ρωτιόμαστε (rotiómaste, "we are asked") gives ρωτι (roti) the same way; κοιμόμαστε (koimómaste, "we sleep"), without the ι, is not affected.

Who is affected

#

Everyone who indexes or searches Greek text with GreekAnalyzer (default constructor), GreekStemFilter or the greekStem factory. Only words ending in -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) are hit: the "we" form of verbs like αγαπιέμαι (agapiémai, "I am loved"). They silently miss the verb's other forms; the exact form still matches. How often such words occur was not measured.

Mechanism, terms and numbers834 words

GreekStemmer says it stems "according to" Georgios Ntais's thesis Development of a Stemmer for the Greek Language (GreekStemmer.java:22-24). The thesis's rule-set 11 removes the verb ending ΟΜΑΣΤΕ (OMASTE) or ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ (IOMASTE), gives ΑΓΑΠ + ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ → ΑΓΑΠ (AGAP + IOMASTE → AGAP) as its example, and its section 3.1 says the rules always apply to the longest possible suffix. Lucene's rule11 (:474-492) tests ομαστε (omaste) first (:475) and ιομαστε (iomaste) only in the else if (:480). Every word ending in ιομαστε also ends in ομαστε, and len > 7 implies len > 6, so the first branch always wins: it removes 6 letters instead of 7, the ι (i) stays, and the ιομαστε branch with its own add-back can never run. Because the length changed, stem() skips the long list rule21 (:70) and nothing later removes the ι. Through the stock new GreekAnalyzer(), αγαπιόμαστε (agapiómaste, "we are loved") is indexed as αγαπι (agapi) while αγαπιέμαι (agapiémai), αγαπιέται (agapiétai) and αγαπιόμουν (agapiómoun) give αγαπ (agap), so a one-document search for the analyzed αγαπιέμαι finds 0 of 1 documents, with no error. ρωτιόμαστε (rotiómaste), κρατιόμαστε (kratiómaste), βαριόμαστε (variómaste) and στενοχωριόμαστε (stenochoriómaste) behave the same; -όμαστε (-ómaste) verbs without the ι (κοιμόμαστε (koimómaste), ερχόμαστε (erchómaste)) are not affected. The order came with the class (LUCENE-2463, 2010), so every release from 3.1.0 to 10.6.0 and main has it. Swapping the two branches fixes it; it changes indexed terms, so the CHANGES entry needs a reindex note.

The intended stem comes from the algorithm the class cites (rule-set 11 and its own worked example), backed by the code's own unreachable branch and the verb's other tested forms. Lucene documents no exact stems, so this is strong evidence of the intended output, not a documented guarantee. No native speaker of Greek checked the words, and the English glosses are approximate.

Terms

#
  • Stemming: cutting endings so that αγαπιέμαι (agapiémai, "I am loved"), αγαπιέται (agapiétai, "he/she is loved"), αγαπιόμουν (agapiómoun, "I was loved") and αγαπιόμαστε (agapiómaste, "we are loved") all get the index term αγαπ (agap).
  • Before the stemmer: GreekAnalyzer lower-cases and removes accents with GreekLowerCaseFilter, so the stemmer sees αγαπιομαστε (agapiomaste, 11 letters).
  • Rule-set 11 of the thesis the class cites (Ntais, 2006): "if ( word ends on ΟΜΑΣΤΕ |ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ ){ remove the suffix; if ( remaining part is ΟΝ ){ add \"ΟΜΑΣΤ\"; } }", with the example ΑΓΑΠ + ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ → ΑΓΑΠ (AGAP + IOMASTE → AGAP); ΟΜΑΣΤΕ, ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ, ΟΝ and ΟΜΑΣΤ read OMASTE, IOMASTE, ON and OMAST. Section 3.1: "the rules, generic or specific, are applied each time for the longest possible suffix in the list."

The bug

#
  1. GreekStemmer.stem (GreekStemmer.java:42-73) runs the "short rules" rule0 to rule20, then the "long list" rule21 only if no short rule changed the length (:70), then rule22.
  2. rule11 (:474-492) tests len > 6 && endsWith(s, len, "ομαστε") (omaste) first (:475) and removes 6 letters (:476); only in the else if does it test len > 7 && endsWith(s, len, "ιομαστε") (iomaste, :480) and remove 7 (:481).
  3. ιομαστε ends with ομαστε, and len > 7 implies len > 6, so any word that would pass the second test already passed the first: the else if branch, with its own ον (on) add-back (:482-489), can never run. A z3 string-theory query over unbounded strings proves it (UNSAT).
  4. For αγαπιομαστε (agapiomaste) the first branch cuts 6 letters: αγαπι (agapi). The length changed, so rule21 is skipped, and no later rule removes the ι (i). The verb's other forms end as αγαπ (agap) through other rules (a rule-by-rule trace): αγαπιέμαι (agapiémai), αγαπιέσαι (agapiésai) and αγαπιέται (agapiétai) through rule21's ιεμαι, ιεσαι, ιεται (-iemai, -iesai, -ietai; :819-821); αγαπιούνται (agapioúntai) and αγαπιόμουν (agapiómoun) through rule21's ιουνται (-iountai, :797) and ιομουν (-iomoun, :805); αγαπιέστε (agapiéste) through rule12's ιεστε (-ieste, :502-503), which skips rule21.

Exposure and likelihood280 words
  • Who is exposed. Applications that analyze Greek text with the stock GreekAnalyzer (GreekAnalyzer.java:77, the default constructor included), GreekStemFilter directly, or the greekStem factory (for example in a CustomAnalyzer). The stemmer has no option: GreekStemFilter takes none and its factory rejects any parameter (GreekStemFilterFactory.java:44-48). GreekAnalyzer's only option is its stopword set (GreekAnalyzer.java:89), which can drop a word but does not change its stem. Tokens marked as keywords before GreekStemFilter (for example by SetKeywordMarkerFilter or StemmerOverrideFilter) are not stemmed (GreekStemFilter.java:29-31, :51).
  • What triggers it. Ordinary words that end in -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) after lower-casing and accent removal: the first person plural present of the -ιέμαι / -ιόμουν (-iémai / -iómoun) passive class (αγαπιόμαστε (agapiómaste), ρωτιόμαστε (rotiómaste), κρατιόμαστε (kratiómaste), βαριόμαστε (variómaste), στενοχωριόμαστε (stenochoriómaste)). Deterministic, at index time and, when the query is analyzed the same way, at query time; no size or setting needed.
  • Not affected. -όμαστε (-ómaste) verbs without the ι (κοιμόμαστε (koimómaste), ερχόμαστε (erchómaste)) and ονομάστε (onomáste). The verb's other forms tested above give the bare root, and this bug does not touch any form that does not end in -ιομαστε (-iomaste) after accent removal.
  • The damage. Missed matches between the -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) form and the verb's other forms, in both directions, with no error. The exact form typed still matches itself.
  • Scale, honestly. One paradigm cell of one verb class. Its frequency in real Greek text was not measured: the likelihood "rare" and the priority Low are a judgement, not a measurement.
  • Existing indexes keep αγαπι-style (agapi) terms until reindexed; after a fix, new query terms and old documents disagree for these words until the field is reindexed.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • GreekStemmer cites Ntais's thesis: rule-set 11 removes -ομαστε or -ιομαστε (-omaste, -iomaste), longest suffix first, with the example ΑΓΑΠ + ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ → ΑΓΑΠ (AGAP + IOMASTE → AGAP).
  • The -ιομαστε branch never runs; Lucene's tests stem the class's other forms to the bare root.
  • Snowball's Greek stemmer gives αγαπ (agap). The expected stem is the cited algorithm's; no native-speaker check.
The full argument353 words
  • The class's contract. GreekStemmer.java:22-24: "A stemmer for Greek words, according to: <i>Development of a Stemmer for the Greek Language.</i> Georgios Ntais". :26-28: input is expected lower-cased with diacritics removed, which GreekAnalyzer does.
  • The thesis (G. Ntais, MSc thesis, Stockholm University / KTH, 2006), rule-set 11 (printed pp. 24-25): "if ( word ends on ΟΜΑΣΤΕ |ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ ){ remove the suffix; if ( remaining part is ΟΝ ){ add \"ΟΜΑΣΤ\"; } }" and "The rule removes the suffix ΟΜΑΣΤΕ and ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ for a group of words. Example: ... ΑΓΑΠ ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ ΑΓΑΠ" (OMASTE, IOMASTE, ON, OMAST; AGAP + IOMASTE → AGAP). Section 3.1 (printed p. 19): "the rules, generic or specific, are applied each time for the longest possible suffix in the list." (The line ΑΓΑΠ + Ω (AGAP + O) above the rule-11 example is not a rule-11 case.)
  • The code's own intent (an inference). The 7-letter ιομαστε (iomaste) branch, with its own ον (on) add-back that writes back ομαστ (omast) (:480-489), strongly suggests it was meant to run; it could also be a leftover, but nothing in the history says so.
  • Tests. TestGreekStemmer.testVerbs pins this verb class to the bare root in other persons: περνιέμαι (perniémai), περνιόμουν (perniómoun) → περν (pern) (:394-395); φοριέμαι (foriémai), φοριόμουν (foriómoun), φοριέσαι (foriésai) → φορ (for) (:456-458); κρατιέμαι (kratiémai), κρατιόμουν (kratiómoun) → κρατ (krat) (:488, :491). No Greek test has an -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) word, so nothing pins αγαπι (agapi).
  • Siblings. Lucene's Snowball Greek stemmer lists both suffixes in one longest-match table (org/tartarus/snowball/ext/GreekStemmer.java:778-781) and gives αγαπ (agap). Independent implementations of the thesis (a PHP version credited to Ntais, Joomla's com_finder, a test table ["αγαπιομαστε", "αγαπ"] in Yoast SEO) also give αγαπ (agap) for αγαπιομαστε (agapiomaste).
  • History. rule11 came with the class in LUCENE-2463 "Improve Greek analysis" (#3537); its thread (3 comments, read on GitHub) says nothing about rule 11, and no later commit message, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry in the git history mentions it.
  • No native-speaker check. The expected stem is taken from the cited algorithm and the evidence above, not from a Greek speaker's judgement.
The devil's-advocate check265 words

The strongest case for "works as designed" (rated moderate by the cross-model reviewer): stemming is heuristic and Lucene promises no exact stems; GreekStemmer and GreekStemFilter are @lucene.experimental (GreekStemmer.java:30, GreekStemFilter.java:37); the class only says it stems "according to" the thesis (GreekStemmer.java:23-24), which need not mean rule for rule; Lucene's own tests accept forms that do not conflate: TestGreekStemmer.java has 14 "doesn't conflate" notes, among them :336 "past/present verb stems will not conflate (from the paper)" and, right next to the lines cited above, :489 (κρατούμουν (kratoúmoun) → κρατουμ (kratoum), :490) and :492 (κρατείσαι (krateísai) → κρατεισα (krateisa), :493); and the output has been stable since 2010 with no report found, while a fix costs a reindex.

Why it fails: none of this explains a branch that no input can reach. The "doesn't conflate" notes show that Lucene accepts imperfect conflation and pins it on purpose; none of them is about a dead branch, and no test pins an -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) form, so they do not show that αγαπι (agapi) was chosen. "According to" does not explain why the code has a dedicated branch for the longer suffix that can never run, when the thesis's own rule-set 11 example is this very word and its stated principle is the longest suffix. The upstream tests conflate the verb's other tested forms to the bare root, and the Snowball sibling gives αγαπ (agap). GreekAnalyzer itself is not experimental. The reindex cost is a compatibility decision for maintainers, not evidence that αγαπι is the intended output: a maintainer may still choose "won't fix".

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Test the longer suffix first (recommended)

Recommended: in GreekStemmer.rule11, test ιομαστε (iomaste) before ομαστε (omaste) (swap the two branches; one file, +6/-6). Validated on 5adff78bb168: the repro passes and the module's check passes (1,910 tests). It changes the indexed terms of -ιόμαστε words, so the CHANGES entry needs a reindex note.

// GreekStemmer.rule11: the longer suffix first
if (len > 7 && endsWith(s, len, "ιομαστε")) {
  len -= 7;
  ... // unchanged 'ον' add-back
} else if (len > 6 && endsWith(s, len, "ομαστε")) {
  len -= 6;
  ... // unchanged 'ον' add-back
}
All options and trade-offs2 options · 456 words

Every option that fixes the stem changes indexed terms for -ιόμαστε words, so the CHANGES entry should say that fields analyzed with GreekAnalyzer, GreekStemFilter or greekStem may need a reindex. Precedent: LUCENE-6742 (5.4.0, "Changes in Backwards Compatibility Policy"): "If you have indexed text using those stemmers you may need to reindex."

Workaround until a fix ships (not executed): a custom analyzer can put a StemmerOverrideFilter that maps the affected words to their bare roots (for example αγαπιομαστε (agapiomaste) → αγαπ (agap)) after GreekLowerCaseFilter and before GreekStemFilter, because its keys must be lower-cased and accent-free, as the stemmer sees them. It marks overridden tokens as keywords (StemmerOverrideFilter.java:83), which GreekStemFilter skips (GreekStemFilter.java:51).

Trade-offs

Changes the indexed term of every -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) word (αγαπι (agapi) becomes αγαπ (agap)): reindex note needed. ονομάστε (onomáste) still gives ονομαστ (onomast): the ον (on) add-back in the ομαστε branch keeps working. Edge case: the swap makes the formerly dead ον add-back of the ιομαστε branch (:482-489) reachable, so the accent-free input ονιομαστε (oniomaste) gives ονι (oni) on stock and ονομαστ (onomast) with the fix. That is what rule-set 11 says (remaining part ΟΝ (ON): add ΟΜΑΣΤ (OMAST)); a test could pin it.

Downstream note

#

Search products that build their Greek analysis on GreekAnalyzer or GreekStemFilter get the same stems, and the fix changes their indexed terms too.

How it was found

#

A scan of Lucene's 21 hand-written stemmers for else-if chains whose later suffix test can only match when an earlier one already did flagged rule 11. A z3 string-theory query proved the -ιομαστε (-iomaste) branch unreachable, and running the stock analyzer on the thesis's own example confirmed it.

The method in full162 words
  • Scan. A syntactic scan of the 21 hand-written stemmers under lucene/analysis looked for if/else-if chains in which a shorter suffix is tested before a longer suffix that ends with it. It flagged GreekStemmer.rule11.
  • Proof. A z3 string-theory query over unbounded strings: no word satisfies the :480 guard (len > 7, ends with ιομαστε, iomaste) without satisfying the :475 guard (len > 6, ends with ομαστε, omaste) (UNSAT), and every -ιομαστε word keeps a trailing ι (i) after the :476 cut (UNSAT). Vacuity checks and a check that the branch becomes reachable after the swap are satisfiable.
  • Execution. The stock jars were run on the thesis's rule-set 11 example (ΑΓΑΠ + ΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ → ΑΓΑΠ, AGAP + IOMASTE → AGAP) and a 9-word rule-11 table (4 of 9 wrong on stock, 0 of 9 with the swap), then reduced to the one-assertion public-API JUnit.
  • Method class: semi-formal (a syntactic scan plus an SMT proof, confirmed by execution).

LFV-1385, snapshot of 2026-11-08

Registry title
GreekStemmer rule11 tests the suffix 'ομαστε' (omaste) before 'ιομαστε' (iomaste), so its 'ιομαστε' branch can never run and -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) verb forms keep a trailing ι (i): GreekAnalyzer stems 'αγαπιόμαστε' (agapiómaste) to 'αγαπι' (agapi) instead of 'αγαπ' (agap), so it no longer matches the other forms of the same verb
Area
analysis-common / Greek stemmer (GreekStemmer.rule11; GreekStemFilter, GreekAnalyzer and the 'greekStem' factory)
Priority
Low, rank 305: In every release since 3.1.0 the stock GreekAnalyzer stems -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) verb forms with a trailing ι (i) because of a dead branch, so they silently miss the other forms of the same verb. It is Low because only one paradigm cell of one passive verb class is hit, the exact form typed still matches, and a fix changes indexed terms.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Missed matches only, with no error: every application that indexes or queries Greek text through GreekAnalyzer (default constructor included), GreekStemFilter or the 'greekStem' factory, on every release from 3.1.0 through 10.6.0 and on main, gets a stem with a trailing ι (i) for words that end in -ιόμαστε (-iómaste) after lower-casing and accent removal, the first person plural present of the -ιέμαι/-ιόμουν (-iémai/-iómoun) passive verb class (αγαπιόμαστε agapiómaste, ρωτιόμαστε rotiómaste, κρατιόμαστε kratiómaste, βαριόμαστε variómaste, στενοχωριόμαστε stenochoriómaste give αγαπι agapi, ρωτι roti, κρατι krati, βαρι vari, στενοχωρι stenochori, while the other forms of these verbs give αγαπ agap, ρωτ rot, κρατ krat, βαρ var, στενοχωρ stenochor), so a search for another form of the verb misses documents that hold only the -ιόμαστε form and the other way round; -όμαστε (-ómaste) verbs without the ι (κοιμόμαστε koimómaste, ερχόμαστε erchómaste) are not affected, no scale or setting is needed, -ea and -da behave the same, and because a fix changes indexed terms, affected fields need reindexing after it.
Summary
You index Greek text with the stock GreekAnalyzer; one document says 'αγαπιόμαστε' (agapiómaste, 'we are loved') and a query says 'αγαπιέμαι' (agapiémai, 'I am loved'). You expect both to stem to 'αγαπ' (agap), because GreekStemmer says it follows Ntais's thesis, whose rule-set 11 removes both -ομαστε (-omaste) and -ιομαστε (-iomaste), gives ΑΓΑΠΙΟΜΑΣΤΕ (AGAPIOMASTE) -> ΑΓΑΠ (AGAP) as its own example and always applies the longest suffix, and the class even has a dedicated branch for 'ιομαστε' (iomaste). Instead rule11 tests 'ομαστε' (omaste) first (GreekStemmer.java:475), which every -ιομαστε word also ends with, so it removes only 6 characters and the 'ιομαστε' branch at :480 never runs: 'αγαπιόμαστε' is indexed as 'αγαπι' (agapi) and the query finds nothing, with no error.
