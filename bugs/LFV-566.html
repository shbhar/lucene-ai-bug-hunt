SpellChecker with SuggestWordFrequencyComparator can drop the most frequent suggestion once its queue is full

With SuggestWordFrequencyComparator, SpellChecker drops a more frequent suggestion once its queue is full: for recieve, asking for 2 gives [receive, relieve], but asking for 1 gives [relieve], although receive is in 50 documents and relieve in 1. Since 3.1.0; the default comparator is not affected.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

This is the index-based n-gram SpellChecker of lucene-suggest, used with a reader and a field; DirectSpellChecker is a different class.

  1. Index receive in 50 documents and relieve in 1. Build a SpellChecker with LevenshteinDistance and SuggestWordFrequencyComparator ("Frequency first, then score") and ask for suggestions for recieve, restricted to that field.
  2. Ask for 2: you get [receive, relieve], frequency first. Both pass the default accuracy 0.5: receive scores 0.714, relieve 0.857.
  3. Ask for 1: you expect [receive], the first entry of that list. You get [relieve], with no error.
  4. Why: the n-gram search returns relieve first. Once the 1-slot queue is full, the minimum score rises to the score of the queue's weakest word, 0.857. That is a safe bound for a score-first order, but here the weakest word is the least frequent one, so receive (0.714) is rejected before its 50 documents are counted.
  5. It also happens at the recommended numSug=5: five of six rare one-edit neighbours of abcdefg fill the queue (the sixth ties and is not kept) and abcdfeg (50 documents) is missing. On an English test corpus 65-76% of lookups return a set other than the comparator's top numSug, but the intended word is lost in only 0.2-0.4%.

Who is affected

#

Applications that use the index-based SpellChecker with SuggestWordFrequencyComparator and pass a reader and field (a user-written comparator that is not score-first may be hit without them). The default comparator, DirectSpellChecker, and the frequency comparator with SUGGEST_ALWAYS or a null reader or field are not affected. It shows even in tiny indexes; the visible harm is mild.

Mechanism, terms and numbers689 words

The index-based n-gram SpellChecker of lucene-suggest can rank suggestions with a pluggable comparator; the shipped SuggestWordFrequencyComparator orders "Frequency first, then score". Once suggestSimilar has collected numSug words, it raises its accuracy cutoff to the score of the queue's head, the word the queue evicts when a better one arrives. That shortcut is a valid lower bound only for the default score-first order. Under the frequency order the head is the least frequent word, so a later word that is more frequent but scores a little lower is rejected before its frequency is read, although the queue would have kept it.

In the smallest case, receive (50 documents) and relieve (1 document) for the typo recieve: asking for 2 returns [receive, relieve], asking for 1 returns [relieve]. At the javadoc-recommended numSug=5 a frequent word can be left out entirely. No error is raised, and the returned list is still sorted by the comparator: what goes wrong is which words get into it. With the frequency comparator it needs a reader and field (restricted mode) and a full queue; a user-written comparator that is not score-first may not need restricted mode. Every release from 3.1.0 to 10.5.2 and main is affected; the default comparator, DirectSpellChecker, and the frequency comparator with SUGGEST_ALWAYS are not. Measured on an English test corpus, the user-visible harm is mild: the set differs from the comparator's top numSug in most lookups, but the typo's intended word is lost in only 0.2-0.4%.

Terms

#
  • SpellChecker: lucene-suggest's index-based spell checker. It keeps a separate spell index of words split into n-grams. suggestSimilar runs an n-gram query against it, fetches up to 10 * numSug hits, scores each with a StringDistance (here LevenshteinDistance: 1 - edits / longer length) and keeps the best numSug in a bounded queue.
  • Restricted mode: pass the user index's reader and a field (with SUGGEST_WHEN_NOT_IN_INDEX or SUGGEST_MORE_POPULAR). Each candidate's document frequency (freq) is then read from that field.
  • Comparator: the order of the queue, set through the constructor or setComparator. The default, SuggestWordScoreComparator, is score, then frequency; SuggestWordFrequencyComparator is frequency, then score.
  • Accuracy: the minimum score a word needs (default 0.5).

The bug

#
  1. For each hit, suggestSimilar computes the score and skips the word if score < accuracy, before it reads the word's frequency (SpellChecker.java:382-385).
  2. After each insertion, once the queue holds numSug words, it sets accuracy = sugQueue.top().score (:396-399, comment "if queue full, maintain the minScore score").
  3. top() is the smallest element under the configured comparator (SuggestWordQueue.java:52). With the default order that is the lowest score, and any later word scoring below it would lose to it anyway: a valid shortcut. With the frequency order it is the least frequent word; its score says nothing about which later words the queue would keep.
  4. So a later word that is more frequent but scores lower than that word is rejected at step 1, unread. The returned list is still sorted by the comparator; the defect is which words get into it.

The numbers

#

receive in 50 documents, relieve in 1, query recieve: scores 1 - 2/7 = 0.714 and 1 - 1/7 = 0.857. The n-gram search returns relieve first. With numSug=1 the queue is full after relieve, accuracy becomes 0.857 and receive is skipped: [relieve]. With numSug=2 both get in: [receive, relieve]. With the default comparator the answers are [relieve] and [relieve, receive], consistent with each other.

At numSug=5: abcdfeg in 50 documents (two edits from abcdefg, 0.714) and six one-edit words with 1 document each (0.857). The six are reached first: five fill the queue (the sixth ties the head and is not kept), the cutoff rises to 0.857, and abcdfeg is dropped: [abcdefx, abcdefy, abcdefz, abcdexg, abcdeyg]. It fails for numSug 1-6 and is right from 7, when abcdfeg arrives before the queue is full.

The outcome depends on n-gram hit order: adding retrieve and reprieve (1 document each) to the first index makes the search reach receive first, and numSug=1 then returns [receive].

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The comparator is "Frequency first, then score"; LUCENE-2479 added it so users could "sort by freq first".
  • If the comparator selects, the top 1 must head the top 2: here 2 gives [receive, relieve], 1 gives [relieve]. The default comparator never does this.
  • The cutoff is from 2004, when score-first was the only order; LUCENE-2479 left it unchanged.
The full argument316 words
  • Contract. Constructor javadoc (SpellChecker.java:129-132): "the given java.util.Comparator for sorting the results"; getComparator (:178): "the comparator in use for ranking suggestions"; SuggestWordFrequencyComparator.java:21: "Frequency first, then score."; lucene/CHANGES.txt:16164-16165: "Added ability to provide a sort comparator for spelling suggestions along with two implementations. The existing comparator (score, then frequency) is the default". None of these says outright that the comparator chooses which words are returned; that reading rests on the comparator's own rule, "ranking", the request below, and the fact that the comparator-ordered queue is what selects (SpellChecker.java:366, 395).
  • Intent. The LUCENE-2479 (#3553) request: "there are situations where it would be useful to sort by freq first, instead of the current 'sort by edit distance, and then subsort by freq if edit distance is equal'". In the same thread the author did not fold frequency into the score because "this would mean having to look up the freq. for every suggestion, which I think would be pretty bad performance-wise": about the scoring, not the cutoff, but avoiding docFreq lookups was part of the design.
  • Prefix check. If the comparator picks the top numSug, then with every candidate retrieved and passing the filters, numSug=k must be the first k of the full list under any fixed order. The frequency comparator breaks it ([relieve] vs [receive, relieve]; an independent case returns [abcdefghijkl, abcdefgx] for 2 but [abcdefghijkl, abxdefyh, abcdefgx] for 3); the default comparator never does.
  • History. The 2004 sandbox import wrote min=((SuggestWord) sugqueue.top()).score when SuggestWord.compareTo (score, then frequency) was the only order, so it was correct. LUCENE-2479 (2010) built the queue with the comparator and left the cutoff; later commits only renamed or reformatted it.
  • Tests. testComparator (TestSpellChecker.java:153-171) uses "five and fvei", which "have the same score", so it cannot reach the cutoff. Nothing pins the current behaviour: with either fix every suite in the module passes.
The devil's-advocate check344 words

The strongest case for "works as designed" (a cross-model reviewer with the intended lens, the independent reproducer, the devil's advocate, the adjudication): the comparator is "for sorting the results" and the 6-argument @return says "first criteria: the edit distance, second criteria (only if restricted mode): the popularity", so the comparator may only order a set chosen by score; the javadoc warns "if numSug == 1, don't count on that suggestion being the best one ... set this value to at least 5"; DirectSpellChecker also builds a bounded pool by score before it applies the comparator; LUCENE-2479's author wanted to avoid a frequency lookup for every suggestion; and the measured harm is small.

Why it fails: the javadoc caveat names its own cause, n-gram retrieval ("the Lucene similarity that is used to fetch the most relevant n-grammed terms is not the same as the edit distance"), while here every candidate is retrieved and then rejected by the cutoff, and numSug=5 fails too. Today's output follows neither reading: the receive case contradicts "the comparator selects", and an order probe contradicts "score selects" (query abcdefg: abcdefgxy, 50 documents, 0.778, beats abcdefh, 1 document, 0.857, at numSug=1, because the n-gram search reaches it first). The @return text is the 2004 description of the only order that existed, and the returned list is already ordered frequency first, against it. The weaker reading "score picks a heuristic subset, the comparator only sorts it" is not refuted by one call, but no score-first precondition is documented and the hit-order dependence fits no rule; made consistent and documented, it is option C. DirectSpellChecker is no precedent for today's code: it sorts its pool with the comparator and then trims it to numSug (DirectSpellChecker.java:386-391), so there the comparator selects too, and its score cutoff applies only inside its score-ordered pool queue (:435). The performance remark is about the scoring, not the cutoff. The small harm bears on which consistent rule to choose (see Suggested fix), not on whether today's behaviour is coherent. Verdict: not intended.

Drop the full-queue raise (accuracy = sugQueue.top().score): insertWithOverflow already keeps the comparator's best numSug words. A: drop the full-queue raise (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

Remove the if (sugQueue.size() == numSug) { accuracy = sugQueue.top().score; } block after insertWithOverflow (SpellChecker.java:396-399) and leave a comment. On every call that reaches the candidate loop the result becomes the comparator's top numSug among the qualifying 10 * numSug hits, for every comparator (a word already in the field still returns itself under SUGGEST_WHEN_NOT_IN_INDEX, as today). +3/-4, comment only besides the removed block.

+        // accuracy to sugQueue.top().score once the queue is full: that is a lower bound only for
+        // a score-first comparator, and a frequency-first one can still prefer a lower score.

Trade-offs

Simplest; it makes the comparator select, for any comparator. With the default comparator the results are unchanged (0 differences in 8,000 measured lookups), but the shortcut is gone: after the queue fills, each examined hit may also do one docFreq lookup and one queue offer, on top of the stored-field read and distance computation the loop already does per hit; still at most 10 * numSug hits. Not benchmarked. On the measured corpus, frequency-first selection includes the typo's intended word less often than today (74.0-94.9% vs 95.9-98.0%).

C: score selects, the comparator only sorts (document it)

#

Select the top numSug by score with the existing shortcut, then sort the chosen words with the configured comparator, and say so in the constructor and @return javadoc. This matches the literal "for sorting the results" and the 2004 @return text. DirectSpellChecker is not a precedent for it: its comparator sorts a pool of up to numSug * maxInspections terms and trims it to numSug, so it selects.

Trade-offs

Its set equals today's default-comparator set, which included the intended word in 96.4-98.1% of the measured lookups. But SuggestWordFrequencyComparator would no longer select by frequency in SpellChecker, results change even where today's output already follows the comparator, and the repro (which asserts A's semantics) would need a different test. Not run.

Document the current behaviour

#

Describe the cutoff in the javadoc.

Trade-offs

Not recommended: the procedure can be described, but that would codify a mixed rule whose result depends on n-gram hit order.

How it was found

#

Translation validation: a harness compared the stock suggestSimilar with a reference written from its documentation, read as "the comparator selects" (exact n-gram retrieval, the filters, then the configured comparator's top numSug). Over 544,485 checks on random dictionaries it found 5,721 mismatches, all with the frequency comparator in restricted mode, none with the default comparator and none with the fix.

The method in full169 words

Method: bounded translation validation (the hunt's "semi-formal" tier: a tool witnessed the mismatch on the real jar against an exact reference; no model checker, solver or proof).

  • The finder wrote a reference for SpellChecker.suggestSimilar from its documented behaviour: every spell-index word that shares a gram with the query is retrieved, the self, accuracy and suggest-mode filters apply, then the configured comparator picks the top numSug. Cases with more retrievable words than 10 * numSug were skipped, so the javadoc's retrieval caveat cannot apply.

Bug record

LFV-566, snapshot of 2026-10-28

Registry title
SpellChecker.suggestSimilar with SuggestWordFrequencyComparator drops more frequent suggestions: once the queue is full it raises the accuracy cutoff to the score of the least frequent queued word, which is a valid bound only for a score-first order (since 3.1, LUCENE-2479)
Area
suggest / spell: SpellChecker.suggestSimilar (index-based n-gram spell checker; comparator vs. full-queue accuracy cutoff)
Priority
Low, rank 372: SpellChecker with the frequency comparator returns a set that is not the documented frequency-first selection, losing the correction in only 0.2-0.4% of lookups, so it follows LFV-518.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that use the index-based n-gram SpellChecker of lucene-suggest with SuggestWordFrequencyComparator (through the constructor or setComparator; Solr's index- and file-based spell checkers map comparatorClass=freq to it) and restrict suggestions to a field (a reader and field with SUGGEST_WHEN_NOT_IN_INDEX or SUGGEST_MORE_POPULAR) get, with no error and at any index size, a suggestion set that is not the frequency-first selection the comparator documents whenever numSug qualifying words fill the queue before a more frequent, slightly lower-scored word is reached; on a small English vocabulary that was most lookups (65-76% returned a different set, 19-37% a less frequent first suggestion), but the misspelled word's intended correction was lost in only 0.2-0.4% of lookups, and the documented frequency-first selection would lose it more often (5-26% against 2-4% today), so the user-visible harm is mild; the default SuggestWordScoreComparator, a null reader or field, DirectSpellChecker and search results are not affected, and every release from 3.1.0 to 10.5.2 is affected.
Summary
You build an index-based SpellChecker with the shipped SuggestWordFrequencyComparator ('Frequency first, then score') and ask for suggestions restricted to a field, expecting the most frequent qualifying words among the retrieved candidates. Once the queue holds numSug words, suggestSimilar raises its accuracy cutoff to the score of the queue's head, which under this comparator is the least frequent queued word, so a later candidate that is more frequent but scores slightly lower is rejected before its frequency is even read. With 'receive' in 50 documents and one-edit neighbours such as 'relieve', 'reprieve' and 'retrieve' in one document each, suggestSimilar('recieve', 1, reader, field, SUGGEST_WHEN_NOT_IN_INDEX) returns [relieve] instead of receive (numSug 1-3 all leave it out; numSug 4 ranks it first).
Introduced
