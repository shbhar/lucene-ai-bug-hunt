The finding in 30 seconds

#

A setSpellIndex call brought the lookups back in the runs. No javadoc sentence promises a usable spell checker after a call that threw; what has no intended reading is the reader left open. Run: the lookups on the releases 4.7.1 to 10.6.0 (119), the reader left open on 2.9.2 and 3.0.1 to 10.6.0 (145).

Background: how SpellChecker holds its index

#
  1. SpellChecker (lucene-suggest, package org.apache.lucene.search.spell) keeps its dictionary as a small Lucene index in a Directory you give it: one document per word.
  2. It holds one internal searcher over a reader of that index. exist and suggestSimilar borrow that reader for the length of the call.
  3. indexDictionary, clearIndex and setSpellIndex end by replacing the searcher with a new one (the private method swapSearcher), so that later lookups see the change. close() closes the searcher's reader.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Create a SpellChecker over a directory (in-memory in the runs) and add the word apple with indexDictionary. exist("apple") is true. The spell checker holds one reader, over one index file, _0.cfs.
  2. Add banana the same way. The word is committed and the spell checker opens a new reader over _0.cfs and _1.cfs. It then closes the old reader, and that close throws an IOException once (injected). indexDictionary throws it.
  3. You expect the spell checker, which nobody closed, to answer from the reader it had just opened: exist("banana") true. No javadoc sentence promises this after a call that threw.
  4. Instead exist("banana") and suggestSimilar("banan", 5) throw AlreadyClosedException: this IndexReader is closed, and so does exist("apple"). The new searcher is assigned only after the close, so the field still holds the old reader, which is closed. setSpellIndex(dir) brought the lookups back in the runs.
  5. Close the spell checker: close() returns. The reader opened in step 2 was not installed, so close() does not reach it: it keeps reference count 1, and _0.cfs and _1.cfs stay open in the test directory (inputs that this directory counts, not operating-system handles). The index is intact: a new reader on it has both words.

Who is affected

#

An application that uses the classic n-gram SpellChecker and keeps using it after indexDictionary, clearIndex or setSpellIndex threw an IOException from closing the old reader. In the runs it was injected, over an in-memory directory; by reading, the default MMapDirectory does not raise one there. Loud and without a wrong answer on 4.7.1 and later; nothing lost from the index.

Mechanism, terms and numbers1,279 words

Small, loud on 4.7.1 and later, and reached in the runs only with an injected fault. SpellChecker (lucene-suggest, package org.apache.lucene.search.spell) keeps its dictionary in a Lucene index and holds one searcher over it. After indexDictionary, clearIndex or setSpellIndex it replaces that searcher in the private method swapSearcher (SpellChecker.java:650-669, lines at main 1ae70c6e45b8): it opens the new searcher (:656), closes the old reader (:662-664) and only then assigns the new searcher and the directory to its fields (:666-667).

If the close of the old reader throws an IOException, the method ends between the two steps. The field still holds the old searcher, and its reader is closed: IndexReader.decRef marks a reader closed before it runs doClose (IndexReader.java:222-226), so the reader is closed although its close threw. Nothing holds the new reader, so nothing closes it.

Two things follow, of different weight.

  • The lookups throw. exist and suggestSimilar begin with obtainSearcher (:472, :316), which takes a reference on the installed reader (:615) and gets AlreadyClosedException: this IndexReader is closed, on a spell checker that was not closed (its package-private isClosed() answers false in the runs). No javadoc sentence promises a usable spell checker after a call that threw, so this page does not call that half a broken contract. It says what happens.
  • The reader that was just opened stays open. After SpellChecker.close() it has reference count 1, and in the run with two one-word builds the index files _0.cfs and _1.cfs are open in the test directory: inputs that this directory counts, not operating-system handles. By reading, no public call reaches that reader. This is the half without an intended reading: the class is a Closeable whose close() is documented as "Close the IndexSearcher used by this SpellChecker" (:632-636; the sentence names the searcher in use, and this reader was opened and never installed), and the method itself closes the new reader in its other failure branch (:658-661).

What the runs are. In every run the IOException was injected: one failed close of one index input of the old reader, over an in-memory directory, through the test framework's MockDirectoryWrapper (the test) or a hand-written wrapping directory (the evidence program); with both the file is really closed although its close throws. None of these runs used a real file system. By reading the input closes of the three stock directories: that of MMapDirectory, which FSDirectory.open picks on a 64-bit JVM, and that of ByteBuffersDirectory have no statement that throws an IOException; NIOFSDirectory's is FileChannel.close(). So it can take NIOFSDirectory with a failing channel close, or a custom, filter or remote Directory. Only an IOException from an input close was run; by the code, the assignment at :666 is skipped for whatever the close at :663 throws.

What is not lost. The new word is committed before the swap: a new reader on the directory has both documents, and a second SpellChecker on it finds both words (run). On 4.7.1 and later no wrong answer is returned, because the lookups throw (run for this page on 10.6.0, branch_11x b98f116b3179 and main 1ae70c6e45b8); on 3.1.0 to 4.7.0 exist answered false, and on 4.7.0 suggestSimilar an empty list, for a word that is in the index. The failing call throws the IOException to its caller.

Recovery in the runs. setSpellIndex(the same directory) or clearIndex(), called straight after the failed call, brought the lookups back (clearIndex empties the index, as documented). If the application first calls indexDictionary again, that call throws AlreadyClosedException and leaves its IndexWriter open (a separate finding, LFV-549); then clearIndex throws LockObtainFailedException and the lookups keep throwing, while setSpellIndex still brought the lookups back. Neither call closes the reader that was left open.

Since when. The statement order came with swapSearcher in LUCENE-2108 (2009), first released in 2.9.2 and 3.0.1: from there on the reader opened for the swap is left open. The AlreadyClosedException is from 4.7.1 on: LUCENE-5553 (2014) made a reader whose close threw closed for good. In the releases before 4.7.1 that were run the lookups answered false or threw NullPointerException, by release; on 4.7.1 to 5.5.5 the failing call reports the IOException wrapped in a RuntimeException.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • close() is documented as "Close the IndexSearcher used by this SpellChecker", yet a reader that the spell checker opened stays open; the method's other failure branch closes the new reader.
  • ReferenceManager.swapReference installs first, then releases. With that order the test passes.
  • For the lookups no contract is claimed; maintainers may read that half as hardening.
The full argument608 words
  • The class is a Closeable, and close() says what it closes. SpellChecker implements java.io.Closeable (SpellChecker.java:65, lines at main 1ae70c6e45b8) and documents close() as "Close the IndexSearcher used by this SpellChecker" (:632-636). After the failed swap and close(), a reader that the spell checker opened has reference count 1 (run) and, by reading, no public call reaches it. The sentence names the searcher "used by" the spell checker; this reader was opened by it and never installed, so no sentence names it word for word. What carries this half is that the class is a Closeable, the next point, and the subject of the issue that added the method.
  • The method closes the new reader in its other failure branch. When the spell checker was closed while the new searcher was being opened, swapSearcher closes the new reader before it throws (:658-661). The branch in which the close of the old reader throws leaves it open.
  • Lucene's own test states the invariant. TestSpellChecker records each searcher the spell checker creates and asserts, on paths without a fault, that after close() each has reference count 0 (assertSearchersClosed, TestSpellChecker.java:502-506) and that before it the last one is open and the others are closed (assertLastSearcherOpen, :486-500). After the failed swap the last created reader is open and unused, the one in use is closed, and after close() one created reader is still open (run: [0, 0, 1]).
  • The issue that added the method is about this resource. LUCENE-2108 (#3184, 2009) is titled "SpellChecker file descriptor leak - no way to close the IndexSearcher used by SpellChecker internally". In its review Michael McCandless proposed the name swapSearcher "(Because it releases the old one and installs the new one)" and asked "to open the new searcher without the lock, then only acquire the lock to do the swap". No comment of the issue speaks of a close that throws.
  • The order predates a change in core. From 3.1.0 to 4.7.0 IndexReader.decRef put the reference back when the close threw (read at each release tag; at the tags of 2.9.2 and 3.0.1 it left the count unchanged), so the old reader stayed nominally open. LUCENE-5553 (#6615, 2014, first in 4.7.1) changed that on purpose; Simon Willnauer wrote there: "a close call should close and shouldn't leave a broken reader behind". The change touched no file of the spell checker, and swapSearcher still treats a throwing close as if the old reader were unchanged. The javadoc of decRef still reads "If an exception is hit, the refCount is unchanged" (IndexReader.java:208-209), which the code at :222-226 no longer does (a separate finding, LFV-497); under that sentence, keeping the old reader would be coherent.
  • The sibling in core installs first. ReferenceManager.swapReference assigns the new reference and then releases the old one (ReferenceManager.java:53-58). In a control run on main 1ae70c6e45b8, the stock SearcherManager under one failed final close of its old reader during maybeRefresh threw the injected exception, served the new reader and left no file open. That run shows this one shape; a release that throws inside the swap of ReferenceManager is a separate finding (LFV-1221).
  • For the lookups no contract is claimed. No javadoc sentence promises that a spell checker stays usable after a call that threw IOException. The seven @throws AlreadyClosedException if the Spellchecker is already closed sentences name one reason for that exception and do not say that it is the only one. This page says what the lookups do and does not call it a broken contract.
The devil's-advocate check334 words

The strongest case for "intended": the failing call already threw an IOException, and no sentence promises anything about a SpellChecker after that. A caller that gets an IOException from an object that manages files can be expected to treat the object as suspect and to discard it. setSpellIndex, documented as "re-open the existing index", brought the lookups back in the runs, and the exception that the lookups throw is loud. On this reading the state of the spell checker after the failed call is unspecified, and the report asks for hardening.

Where it holds and where it fails. It holds for the lookups, and the page is worded accordingly: no contract is claimed for them. It fails for the reader that was opened for the swap. A caller that does what the argument asks (catches the IOException, closes the spell checker, closes the directory) is left with that reader open: its reference count is 1, and in the runs the test framework's directory then refuses to close because two inputs are open. That refusal is the test directory's (MockDirectoryWrapper.java:873-887): by reading, the close() of a stock directory does not check for open inputs (FSDirectory.java:277-280, ByteBuffersDirectory.java:216-219), so a caller with one sees nothing at that point. The open files are inputs that the test directory counts, not operating-system handles, and no run behind this page used a real file system or let the garbage collector run, so "stays open" is shown for the moment after close(). close() says that it closes the searcher the spell checker uses (this reader was opened by it and never installed), the same method closes the new reader in its other failure branch, and the issue that added the method was filed because the spell checker kept its internal searcher's files open. Nothing that was found presents the order as chosen for a failing close: the review of LUCENE-2108 discussed the method's name and the lock, not a close that throws.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Install the new searcher, then close the old reader (validated)

Assign the new searcher and the directory first, then close the old reader: the order that ReferenceManager.swapReference has. The failing call still throws the IOException; the lookups answer and no reader stays open. Validated: the test passes, and the suggest module's 237 tests (7 skipped by Lucene's own annotations) and its lint show no failure.

final IndexSearcher oldSearcher = searcher;
// set the spellindex in the sync block - ensure consistency.
searcher = indexSearcher;
this.spellIndex = dir;
if (oldSearcher != null) {
  oldSearcher.getIndexReader().close();
}

Trade-offs

  • The failing call still throws. The IOException reaches the caller as today; what changes is the state behind it: the spell checker uses the new reader and holds no other.
  • A failed setSpellIndex(otherDirectory) then switches. When the close of the old reader throws, the other directory is active although the call threw (run: the other directory's word is found, the first directory's is not, and nothing is open after close()). The javadoc of setSpellIndex gives no rollback promise; today the same call leaves the spell checker on a closed reader.

Impact note
Hits an application that uses the classic n-gram SpellChecker of lucene-suggest (default settings suffice) and keeps using it after indexDictionary, clearIndex or setSpellIndex threw an IOException that came from the one close inside swapSearcher, the close of the spell checker's old reader. In every run that IOException was injected: one failed close of one index input of the old reader, through a hand-written wrapping Directory or the test framework's MockDirectoryWrapper, over an in-memory directory; none of these runs used a real file system. By reading, the input close of the default MMapDirectory and of ByteBuffersDirectory has no statement that throws an IOException and NIOFSDirectory's is FileChannel.close(), so it can take NIOFSDirectory with a failing channel close or a custom, filter or remote Directory. Run on branch_11x b98f116b3179, main ec3b3cca8f10 and the 10.6.0 release with -ea and -da [exec]: after the failed call exist and suggestSimilar throw AlreadyClosedException ('this IndexReader is closed') although the spell checker is open, for a fault in the swap of each of the three callers; the same steps without the fault work and leave nothing open. Recovery, run on the same builds: setSpellIndex(the same directory) or clearIndex(), called straight after the failed call, brings the lookups back (clearIndex empties the index, as documented); if the application first retries indexDictionary, that call throws AlreadyClosedException after opening an IndexWriter that it does not close (the separate finding LFV-549), and then clearIndex throws LockObtainFailedException and the lookups keep throwing, while setSpellIndex still brings the lookups back and indexDictionary stays locked out. Neither recovery call closes the lost reader: after SpellChecker.close() the reader opened for the swap has reference count 1 and its files are open, [_0.cfs, _1.cfs] in the indexDictionary runs (one word, then one word; default IndexWriterConfig), [_0.cfs] for setSpellIndex(the same directory) and none for clearIndex, whose lost reader is over an empty index; these are inputs counted by the test directory, not operating-system handles. Nothing is lost from the index (the new word is committed before the swap: after setSpellIndex both words are found), and no scale or timing is needed. On these builds and on each release from 4.7.1 on that was run no wrong answer is returned and the failure is loud, because the lookups throw; on the releases 3.1.0 to 4.7.0 exist answered false, and on 4.7.0 suggestSimilar an empty result, for a word that is in the index. The 10.6.0 release behaves as the build of branch_11x b98f116b3179 does.
Summary
You add a word to a spell index with SpellChecker.indexDictionary; the word is committed, and then, while the spell checker replaces its internal reader, the close of the old reader fails once with an IOException (injected in the runs through a wrapping Directory), so the call throws it. You expect a spell checker that was not closed to answer lookups from the reader it had just opened, and to leave no reader of its own open once you close it (close() is documented to close the spell checker's searcher; no javadoc sentence promises a usable spell checker after a call that threw). Instead swapSearcher closes the old reader before it stores the new one, so the spell checker keeps the closed reader and exist and suggestSimilar throw AlreadyClosedException ('this IndexReader is closed'), and the new reader is lost: after SpellChecker.close() it still has reference count 1 and, in the run, two index files open.
