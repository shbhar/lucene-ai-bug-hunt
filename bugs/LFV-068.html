HNSW merge repair (addConnections) walks down from the farthest upper-level candidate, not the closest one its javadoc describes

When a merge repairs a reused HNSW graph, addConnections walks down from the farthest upper-level candidate, not the closest one its javadoc names. After deletes concentrated in one region this costs 16-41% more merge search work, yet kept better recall than the documented walk: maintainers must choose.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

When a merge reuses a segment's HNSW graph with deletions, addConnections re-links nodes left with no neighbours and nodes promoted to a higher level. On each upper level it collects up to 100 candidates in a min-heap and continues from popNode(), the top of that heap: the farthest candidate, not the closest one its javadoc names.

HNSW merge repair in three lines

#
  1. An HNSW graph has levels: level 0 holds every vector, each level above holds about 1 in 16 of the level below (default M = 16).
  2. To place a vector, Lucene walks down: on each upper level it moves to the node closest to the new vector, then searches level 0 for its neighbours.
  3. Since 10.4.0 a merge can reuse a graph that has deletions: it drops the deleted nodes, re-links nodes left with no neighbours, and promotes nodes to keep the upper levels full. Re-linking and promotion both do that walk, in addConnections.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. You delete documents and merge. Since 10.4.0 the merge can reuse the HNSW graph of the segment with the most live vectors (if at most 40% of it is deleted): it drops the deleted nodes and re-links each node left with no neighbours by walking down the upper levels towards it, then searching level 0.
  2. Tiny example, 1-D vectors: the entry E at 0 and P at 100 are on level 1; X at 1 lost its only neighbour. The level-1 search finds E (distance 1) and P (distance 99). You expect the walk to go on from E, as the javadoc says: "find the closest node".
  3. It goes on from P, the farthest: it takes the top of a 100-slot min-heap, which is the worst candidate. X is re-linked to P (distance 99), not E (distance 1). Normal insertion does the same walk with a 1-slot collector and gets E.
  4. In real IndexWriter merges with the default codec, every walk step started off target in the random-delete runs (49 of 49 and 169 of 169), and 40-100% of them in the other runs. With random deletes the repairs are under 0.5% of the merge's graph-search work and recall moved by at most 0.0002.
  5. After deletes concentrated in one region of vector space, repairs are 20-44% of that work. The documented walk would cut the merge's total by 14-29%, but measured 2.6-8.2 points lower recall@10 near the deleted regions (synthetic data): a mismatch with a trade-off, not a recall bug.

Who is affected

#

Anyone whose merges reuse an HNSW graph with deletes: the default codec since 10.5.1 (10.4.0 and 10.5.0 only with a non-per-field vectors format), with the single-worker or the concurrent merger. With random deletes the effect is negligible; after deletes concentrated in vector space, such as one tenant's documents, repairs become 20-44% of merge search work. No errors.

Mechanism, terms and numbers597 words

Since 10.4.0 (#15003) a merge can reuse a segment's HNSW graph that has deletions: it copies the graph without the deleted nodes, re-links every node left with no neighbours on a level, and promotes nodes to keep the upper levels populated. Both steps call HnswGraphBuilder.addConnections, whose javadoc says it will "Navigate down from the top level to find the closest node at the target level". The loop instead collects up to beamWidth (default 100) candidates per upper level in a min-heap GraphBuilderKnnCollector and continues from popNode(), the top of that heap, which is the farthest of the candidates it kept. Normal insertion does the same walk with a size-1 collector.

The minimal test shows the choice directly: X (at 1) is re-linked to P (at 100) instead of E (at 0). On the real merge path, with the default codec, every walk step started from a non-best node in the random-delete runs, and 40-100% of them in the others. With random deletes the effect is negligible. After deletes concentrated in vector space, the documented walk would cut the merge's graph-search work by 14-29%, but recall@10 near the deleted regions was 2.6-8.2 points lower with it in synthetic probes. So this is a mismatch between code and documented intent with a measured trade-off; no recall loss from the current code is claimed, and the remedy (change the walk after a benchmark, or document it) is for the maintainers to choose.

The code (main 645d4fe14a20)

#
// HnswGraphBuilder.java:774 (javadoc): Navigate down from the top level to find the closest node at the target level
int beamWidth = beamCandidates.k();                                              // :787
GraphBuilderKnnCollector candidates = new GraphBuilderKnnCollector(beamWidth);   // :788
int[] eps = {hnsw.entryNode()};                                                  // :789
// Navigate down from top to target level, greedily moving toward the new node   // :791
for (int level = hnsw.numLevels() - 1; level > targetLevel; level--) {
  graphSearcher.searchLevel(candidates, scorer, level, eps, hnsw, null);         // :793
  eps[0] = candidates.popNode();                                                 // :794
  candidates.clear();
}
  1. GraphBuilderKnnCollector wraps new NeighborQueue(k, false) (HnswGraphBuilder.java:822), which is Order.MIN_HEAP (NeighborQueue.java:59-62); pop() "Removes the top element" (:127-130). The top of a min-heap is the lowest score.
  2. HnswGraphSearcher.searchLevel documents the order: "the most distant neighbor of the topK found, i.e. the one with the lowest score/comparison value, will be at the top of the heap, while the closest neighbor will be the last to be popped" (HnswGraphSearcher.java:272-277). popToScratch says the same for this collector: "sorted from worst to best" (HnswGraphBuilder.java:546-547).
  3. So at each upper level the walk continues from the farthest of up to beamWidth (default 100) candidates; on a small top level, from the farthest reachable node of the level.
  4. Normal insertion (addGraphNodeInternal) does the same walk with entryCandidates = new GraphBuilderKnnCollector(1) (HnswGraphBuilder.java:193, :334-340, "for levels > nodeLevel search with topk = 1"), so its pop is the best node.

Who calls it

#
  • fixDisconnectedNodes, for a node with no surviving neighbours on a level: "Node has no neighbors, add connections from scratch" (HnswGraphBuilder.java:757-759).
  • rebalanceGraph, for every promoted node that is not the first on its level (InitializedHnswGraphBuilder.java:472-478).
  • Both run whenever the reused graph has deletes (InitializedHnswGraphBuilder.java:303-316). Among the input graphs with at most 40% deleted vectors (an integer percentage), a merge reuses the one with the most live vectors (IncrementalHnswGraphMerger.java:65, :133-139), via MergingHnswGraphBuilder.fromGraphs (MergingHnswGraphBuilder.java:107-109), the default single-worker merger (Lucene99HnswVectorsWriter.java:688); the concurrent merger calls the same two methods (HnswConcurrentMergeBuilder.java:199, :427). Flush-time building and query-time search do not call addConnections.

Minimal repro

#

One JUnit test, one method, deterministic, single thread. It hand-builds a 4-node graph with 1-D vectors (same-package access, only for a package-private constructor) and calls the public InitializedHnswGraphBuilder.initGraph, as a merge does. It asserts that X, whose only neighbour was deleted, is re-linked through E, the level-1 node closest to it.

Expected
X (at 1) is re-linked to E (at 0, distance 1), the level-1 node closest to it.
Observed
AssertionError: X's level-0 neighbor (E=0, P=1) expected:<0> but was:<1>: X is linked to P (at 100, distance 99), with assertions on and off.

More files, environment, full output349 words

The source graph: E at 0 (the entry, levels 0 and 1), P at 100 (levels 0 and 1), X at 1 and D (level 0); level-1 link E -> P, level-0 link X -> D. D is deleted (ord map {0, 1, 2, -1}, 3 surviving vectors, beamWidth 100).

  • X kept 0 of its 1 level-0 neighbours, so the repair calls addConnections(X, 0). The level-1 search from E finds E (score 0.5) and P (score 0.000102); popNode() returns P.
  • E and P have no level-0 links, so the level-0 search finds only the node it starts from: the start shows up directly as X's neighbour. On a connected level 0 the beam search usually recovers, and the cost is extra work instead.
  • With 3 nodes and M = 16, rebalanceGraph promotes nothing. Nothing is randomized; the seed only fixes the test framework's own choices.
  • Access: the package-private OnHeapHnswGraph(int, int) constructor, from the same package as Lucene's own HNSW tests; everything else is public (some of it @lucene.internal or @lucene.experimental). The test compiles unchanged against 10.4.0-10.6.0 and main.
  • As a regression test: the same test, but only if the maintainers choose the greedy walk.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The javadoc says the walk finds "the closest node at the target level"; the loop comment says "greedily moving toward the new node".
  • Its heap documents the top as "the most distant neighbor"; normal insertion does the same walk with a 1-slot collector.
  • No PR discussion, test, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry gives a reason to start from the farthest candidate.
The full argument206 words
  • Own documentation: "Navigate down from the top level to find the closest node at the target level" (HnswGraphBuilder.java:774) and "greedily moving toward the new node" (:791).
  • The primitive documents its order: "the most distant neighbor ... will be at the top of the heap" (HnswGraphSearcher.java:272-277); "sorted from worst to best" (HnswGraphBuilder.java:546-547).
  • The sibling walk in normal insertion uses a size-1 collector for this exact step (HnswGraphBuilder.java:193, :334-340).
  • Other code and PR text describe it as a descent from the entry node to the level: the concurrent merger orders repairs "because addConnections descends through the already-repaired upper levels" (HnswConcurrentMergeBuilder.java:435-436); #16627's PR body says the zero-neighbour path "walks from the entry node to reach the current level". Neither says which candidate the descent continues from.
  • History: #15003 wrote the method with this loop, javadoc and comment; #16627 moved it unchanged. Neither PR's discussion (#15003: 48 comments, 19 review comments; #16627: 8 comments, 36 review comments) mentions which candidate the walk starts from, and CHANGES.txt and MIGRATE.md document nothing about it.
  • Tests check that repaired nodes have neighbours and that the graph stays rooted and searchable; none pins where the walk starts.
The devil's-advocate check214 words

The reviewer from another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6) built the strongest case for "intended", which it rated moderate: the repair is a heuristic, and a non-closest seed can add exploration or diversity; the public builders are @lucene.experimental and promise a usable approximate graph, not particular links; tests check only rootedness and searchability; the failing graph is hand-built; and the documented walk lowered recall in the region-delete measurements.

Why it fails: the method's own javadoc and comment are the contract the code contradicts, and @lucene.experimental does not make a contradiction intended. The same choice, with changed links, shows on the public IndexWriter.forceMerge path. Tests that check only rootedness are missing coverage, not a statement of intent. Nobody designed or documented a farthest-candidate start, and its recall benefit is measured here, not explained anywhere upstream. The recall result is why the remedy is open (document it, or change it after benchmarks), not a sign that the mismatch is intended. Verdict: a real mismatch, low priority; which side to fix is the maintainers' call. Limits: the javadoc and comment are internal documentation, not a public contract, and the silence of the PR threads is absence of evidence; so this shows a mismatch between the code and its local documentation, not what the repair was meant to do.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Settle which walk is intended with a recall benchmark

Settle which walk is intended with a recall benchmark: neither side wins yet. The documented walk is a one-hunk change, a 1-slot collector as in normal insertion; with it the repro and 339 HNSW and kNN tests pass. It cut merge search work 14-29% after region deletes but lowered recall there 2.6-8.2 points. Else, document the current walk.

All options and trade-offs4 options · 659 words

Four options. The greedy walk was validated with gradle; none was benchmarked for recall or merge time with luceneutil.

Settle which walk is intended with a recall benchmark (not run) (recommended)

#

Which behaviour is intended is the maintainers' choice. Benchmark the greedy walk against main with luceneutil on real embeddings, with random deletes and with deletes concentrated in vector space, measuring merge time and recall. If the greedy walk holds recall, take it; if not, document the current walk.

Trade-offs

Slower than a patch, but the only measurements so far (synthetic data) point in opposite directions for cost and recall, so a patch either way would be a guess.

Trade-offs

  • Matches the javadoc and the sibling walk; each upper-level search keeps 1 result instead of up to 100.
  • Measured: with random deletes -0.1% to -0.3% merge graph-search work and recall within 0.0002; after region-concentrated deletes it cuts the merge's graph-search work by 14-29% but lowers recall@10 near the deleted regions by 2.6-8.2 points (synthetic data). The study's initGraph-only runs agree: -11.7% to -30.2% score() calls, recall -0.9 to -7.4 points in 8 of 8 region-delete configurations.
  • The change alters two things at once: which candidate the walk continues from, and how far each upper-level search explores (1 result kept instead of up to 100). So the cost and recall differences cannot be credited to the nearest-versus-farthest choice alone; the beam-search variant below separates the two in part.
  • Changes repaired links only in merges that reuse a graph with deletes.

What the checks validated

  • Gradle in a scratch worktree of 645d4fe14a20 (removed afterwards), seed DEADBEEF: without the change the repro fails with assertions on and off; with it, it passes with assertions on and off.
  • The 16 closest suites pass: 339 tests, 0 failures, 9 skipped (340 tests in 17 classes with the repro itself; all of org.apache.lucene.util.hnsw.*, including the repair and merge-reuse tests of TestHnswFloatVectorGraph; TestLucene99HnswVectorsFormat, TestLucene99HnswVectorsFormatV0, TestKnnGraph, TestHnswMergeAbort, TestKnnFloatVectorQuery, TestKnnByteVectorQuery).
  • ./gradlew :lucene:core:check -x test passes with the repro test in place.
  • These suites check correctness, not recall or merge cost.

Keep the behaviour and document it

#

Change the javadoc (HnswGraphBuilder.java:774) and the loop comment (:791) to say the walk continues from the farthest of the candidates it kept, and why.

Trade-offs

No behaviour change, so no runtime risk; the remaining risk is documenting behaviour that may be accidental as intended. But it keeps the 16-41% extra merge graph-search work after region-concentrated deletes, and nobody has shown why the far start helps recall (the study's bridge explanation is unverified). If the benefit is real, a cheaper way to get it could be explored.

Keep the beam search per level, but continue from its best candidate (validated by the checks)

#

Pop all but the last candidate before taking eps[0] (the study's second patch).

Trade-offs

Same rule as the greedy walk (continue from the best candidate found), but over a full beamWidth search per level, so it can pick a different entry than the greedy walk, and it saves less (-9.6% to -27.4% initGraph score() calls) and lost more recall (-2.4 to -10.3 points) in the study's 8 region-delete configurations. The greedy walk is the simpler form.

What the checks validated

Only by the study's own tests (classpath shadowing, 2026-10-22).

Downstream note

#

Downstream engines that merge vector indexes after deletes, for example after removing one tenant's documents, run this repair on every such merge; it affects merge cost and which links repaired nodes get, not errors or which documents can match.

How it was found

#

TLA+ model checking (TLC) of Lucene's HNSW merge repair. A property written before any look for this bug, DescentGreedy ("addConnections descends to the best candidate"), failed in a 7-step trace (16 states, 1 s). An independent verifier and later checks reproduced it on the stock jars and measured it in real merges.

The method in full167 words

Method: TLA+ model checking (TLC) of the HNSW build, merge and repair code (the formal verification study, 2026-10-22, main 7cecf79523).

  • An AI modeller wrote a TLA+ model of the concurrent HNSW build, merge and repair, with a mapping table from model actions to Java lines (the action that takes eps[0] = candidates.popNode() maps to line 794).
  • Among its properties, DescentGreedy ("addConnections descends to the best candidate") was written before looking for this bug. On configuration R3 (2 levels, one node with no neighbours) TLC reported its violation in a 7-step trace: the level-1 search finds nodes 0 and 1 and the walk continues from 0, the worse one (16 distinct states, 1 s).
  • An independent AI verifier wrote its own tests on the stock jars (main and 10.5.2), measured reach in real IndexWriter merges and the effect of two candidate fixes. The hunt later cut the repro to one 4-node test and re-checked it on main 645d4fe14a20 and releases; a tie-breaker added the paired merge probe.

LFV-068, snapshot of 2026-11-08

Registry title
HNSW addConnections descends from the worst upper-level candidate instead of the best
Area
search / HNSW
Priority
Low, rank 1035: HNSW addConnections descends from the worst upper-level candidate on every merge with deletes, but the extra cost is negligible and naive fixes lowered recall, so it needs benchmarks before any change.
Impact class
minor
Impact note
Default configuration since 10.5.1 (in 10.4.0 and 10.5.0 only with a vectors format that is not the per-field wrapper, GITHUB#16400) during merge-time HNSW repair and rebalance; performance only (49 of 49 descent steps were not the best in real merges); released since 10.4.0.
Summary
During merge-time HNSW graph repair and rebalancing, addConnections descends through the upper levels to find entry points. You expect it to descend from the best candidate, as its javadoc says. Instead it pops from a min-heap and descends from the worst; the naive fixes cut distance computations by 10-30% but lowered recall in 8 of 8 region-delete configurations.
