SmartChineseAnalyzer indexes THIN SPACE, EM SPACE, FORM FEED and 11 other whitespace characters as one-character terms

SmartChineseAnalyzer skips only five space characters, so THIN SPACE, EM SPACE, FORM FEED and 11 other Unicode whitespace characters each become a one-character term: hello<U+2009>world is indexed as [hello, U+2009, world] and the exact phrase "hello world" misses it. In every release since 2.9.0 (2009).

The finding in 30 seconds

#

Before it finds words, SmartChineseAnalyzer gives every character a class. SPACE_LIKE characters are skipped; OTHER characters become one-character terms. Fourteen whitespace characters land in OTHER.

Background in three steps

#
  1. SmartChineseAnalyzer (module analysis-smartcn) splits Chinese text into words with a dictionary, and keeps runs of Latin letters or digits together as one term.
  2. First Utility.getCharType gives each character a class: Chinese character, letter, digit, punctuation (DELIMITER), SPACE_LIKE ("Characters that act as a space"), full-width letter or digit, or OTHER.
  3. The segmenter skips SPACE_LIKE characters and joins Chinese characters across them; punctuation becomes a token that the default stop list removes; each OTHER character becomes a term of its own.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Analyze hello world and hello<U+2009>world (a THIN SPACE between the words) with the stock new SmartChineseAnalyzer().
  2. You expect [hello, world] for both: the segmenter has a class for "Characters that act as a space" and skips them, as it does a space, a tab or the ideographic space U+3000.
  3. The second gives [hello, U+2009, world]. Only five characters are in that class; THIN SPACE falls into "Other" and becomes a term of its own. The same holds for VERTICAL TAB, FORM FEED, OGHAM SPACE MARK and all of U+2000-U+200A: 14 characters.
  4. Search changes with it: the exact phrase "hello world" matches 3 of 7 documents that differ only in the separator, a TermQuery for U+2003 alone finds its document, and 中<U+2009>国 gives [中, U+2009, 国] where 中 国 gives [中国].
  5. No error. HTML does the same when an application puts Lucene's HTMLStripCharFilter in front of the analyzer: &emsp;&emsp; adds two U+2003 terms (&nbsp; decodes to a plain space and is fine).

Who is affected

#

Users of the optional smartcn module (SmartChineseAnalyzer with any constructor, or HMMChineseTokenizer) whose text contains one of the 14 characters, literally or via &emsp;, &ensp; or &thinsp; in HTML. Usually it only adds a term; results go wrong when a phrase or Chinese word spans the character, or a query targets it. Rare and silent; a fix needs reindexing.

Mechanism, terms and numbers655 words

SmartChineseAnalyzer's segmenter has a character class for "Characters that act as a space" (CharType.SPACE_LIKE), which it skips, but Utility.getCharType puts only space, tab, CR, LF and U+3000 in it. VERTICAL TAB, FORM FEED, OGHAM SPACE MARK and the eleven spaces U+2000-U+200A (EN QUAD to HAIR SPACE, among them EN, EM and THIN SPACE) fall through to OTHER, and every OTHER character is emitted as a one-character term that the default stop list does not remove. So hello<U+2009>world is indexed as [hello, U+2009, world], an exact phrase typed with a normal space misses it, a TermQuery for the space character alone finds it, and 中<U+2009>国 is no longer segmented as the word 中国. Nothing throws. It has been there since the original contribution (2.9.0, 2009) and is still on main.

The mechanism (main 5adff78bb168)

#
  1. The class table. Utility.getCharType (Utility.java:140-159) returns SPACE_LIKE only for ' ', '\t', '\r', '\n' and U+3000 (:146-147). The "Punctuation Marks" ranges come next: 0x0021-0x00BB, 0x2010-0x2642, 0x3001-0x301E (:148-151), then full-width letters and digits and 0xFE30-0xFF63 (:153-157). U+000B and U+000C lie below 0x0021, U+1680 is in no range, and U+2000-U+200A lie just below 0x2010, so all 14 reach return CharType.OTHER (:158).
  2. What the classes mean. CharType.SPACE_LIKE: "Characters that act as a space" (CharType.java:38-39); CharType.OTHER: "Other (not fitting any of the other categories)" (:47-48).
  3. The segmenter. HHMMSegmenter.createSegGraph skips SPACE_LIKE characters (HHMMSegmenter.java:56-58) and steps over them while it extends a Chinese word (:92), which is why 中 国 becomes 中国. Any other class ends the word. The default branch, which OTHER reaches, makes a one-character WordType.STRING token ("Treat the unrecognized char symbol as unknown string. For example, any symbol not in GB2312 is treated as one of these.", :156-165).
  4. The term text. WordSegmenter.convertSegToken copies the original character back into a STRING token (WordSegmenter.java:69-75), so the term is the whitespace character itself.
  5. No stop entry. SmartChineseAnalyzer.createComponents chains HMMChineseTokenizer, PorterStemFilter and a StopFilter with the default stop set (SmartChineseAnalyzer.java:124-135). That set "is simply a list of punctuation" (:103-104); it lists U+3000 with the comment "Used as a space in Chinese" (stopwords.txt:54-55) and none of the 14 characters, so the term is indexed.
  6. Why exactly 14. Lucene's pinned Unicode 17 White_Space set (UnicodeProps.WHITESPACE, UnicodeProps.java:33-40) has 25 code points: 5 are SPACE_LIKE; U+0085, U+00A0, U+2028, U+2029, U+202F and U+205F fall into the DELIMITER ranges and are removed like a comma; the other 14 fall through to OTHER.

The 14 characters

#

U+000B VERTICAL TAB, U+000C FORM FEED, U+1680 OGHAM SPACE MARK, U+2000 EN QUAD, U+2001 EM QUAD, U+2002 EN SPACE, U+2003 EM SPACE, U+2004 THREE-PER-EM SPACE, U+2005 FOUR-PER-EM SPACE, U+2006 SIX-PER-EM SPACE, U+2007 FIGURE SPACE, U+2008 PUNCTUATION SPACE, U+2009 THIN SPACE, U+200A HAIR SPACE.

Exposure and likelihood403 words
  • Who is hit. Users of the optional analysis-smartcn module: SmartChineseAnalyzer with any constructor, or HMMChineseTokenizer / the hmmChinese factory with the SmartChinese stop list. Default settings, any scale. Only text that contains one of the 14 characters, in documents or queries.
  • What goes wrong. (1) A whitespace-only term is indexed, and a TermQuery for that character finds the document. (2) An exact phrase typed with an ordinary space misses words on both sides of the character; slop 1 still matches, because the extra term takes one position. (3) Chinese characters are no longer joined into a word across it: 中<U+2009>国 is indexed as 中, U+2009, 国, so the term 中国 is not indexed for that text. A FORM FEED inside a word splits it the same way (道<U+000C>具 gives 道, U+000C, 具, where a newline keeps 道具). (4) Each extra term also counts toward the field length that the norms store (IndexingChain.java:2055), so such a document's scores shift slightly (read from the code, not run).
  • How the characters arrive. As literal characters (typographic spacing, text extracted with form feeds), or through HTML when an application puts Lucene's HTMLStripCharFilter in front of the analyzer (it is not part of the stock SmartChineseAnalyzer): the char filter decodes &emsp;, &ensp; and &thinsp; to U+2003, U+2002 and U+2009, so an &emsp;&emsp; paragraph indent adds two U+2003 terms. &nbsp; decodes to U+0020 and is not a trigger.
  • Silent. Nothing throws, the index stays valid, and assertions on or off behave the same.
  • Likelihood: rare. Most occurrences, such as a paragraph indent, only add an extra term; a wrong match needs a query that spans the character (an exact phrase across it, or a Chinese word split by it) or a query for the character itself. No report in the 17 years since 2.9.0.
  • Not affected. Space, tab, CR, LF and U+3000 are skipped as intended. StandardAnalyzer, CJKAnalyzer and WhitespaceAnalyzer emit no whitespace-only term for the same inputs.
  • After a fix. Indexed terms change for text with these characters, so affected fields need reindexing.
  • Workaround today (from the code, not run): map these characters to U+0020 with a char filter (for example a MappingCharFilter) in front of HMMChineseTokenizer in a custom analyzer. A custom stop set that lists the 14 characters removes the terms too, but, like fix D, it leaves a position gap and does not join Chinese characters across them.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • CharType.SPACE_LIKE is documented as "Characters that act as a space"; THIN SPACE and FORM FEED act as spaces, yet land in OTHER.
  • The stop-list design points the same way: the stop list even lists the already-skipped U+3000, "Used as a space in Chinese".
  • Lucene fixed an earlier getCharType misclassification as a bug (LUCENE-8325, 7.4.0).
The full argument314 words

This is the case for calling it a bug. It is medium confidence: no public contract promises how Unicode whitespace is handled, and maintainers may reasonably treat the change as an enhancement (see the devil's advocate below).

  • Contract. CharType.SPACE_LIKE is "Characters that act as a space" and OTHER is "Other (not fitting any of the other categories)" (CharType.java:38-39, :47-48). THIN SPACE, EM SPACE and FORM FEED act as spaces, so OTHER contradicts the documented meaning of both classes.
  • The stop-list design points the same way. The default stop list lists U+3000 "Used as a space in Chinese" (stopwords.txt:54-55) although U+3000 is already skipped (perhaps only redundancy, but it fits an intent to keep spaces out of the index); HMMChineseTokenizerFactory says the tokenizer "will currently emit tokens for punctuation" and tells users to remove them, for example with the SmartChinese stop list (:27-30); and in #3089 (LUCENE-2014, 2009) rmuir described smartcn as creating "individual tokens for each piece of punctuation (including things like whitespace)" and putting "these in the stopword list". None of the 14 characters is in that list.
  • The list was meant to be open. Utility.SPACES reads "Space-like characters that need to be skipped: such as space, tab, newline, carriage return." (Utility.java:41-44); the original Chinese javadoc in e01aad89fe2 ends in "等等" ("etc."), and the original CharType comment says "space, \t\r\n and other space or line-break characters".
  • Not a GB2312 rule. VT and FF are ASCII and encodable in GB2312, yet they are not SPACE_LIKE and become terms.
  • Other Lucene analyzers tried do not do this. StandardAnalyzer, CJKAnalyzer and WhitespaceAnalyzer emit no whitespace-only term for the same inputs.
  • Precedent. #9372 (LUCENE-8325, fixed in 7.4.0) fixed an earlier getCharType misclassification (surrogates turned into bad one-character tokens) as a bug.
  • Nothing pins it. No upstream test, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry asserts or mentions a whitespace term.
The devil's-advocate check194 words

The strongest case for "intended" came from an independent reviewer from another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6, refute lens), which reproduced the behaviour, agreed on the cause and the fix, and still called the change an enhancement: Utility.SPACES lists exactly five characters; HHMMSegmenter says "any symbol not in GB2312" becomes an unknown-string token; testNonChinese says other text is split per character "(regardless of Unicode category)" (TestSmartChineseAnalyzer.java:182-185); the classes are @lucene.experimental; and where Lucene promises Unicode whitespace splitting it says so (UnicodeWhitespaceTokenizer).

Why it fails: Utility.SPACES has no reader on main (its last one, SentenceTokenizer, was removed in 6.0.0 by LUCENE-5969) and introduces its examples with "such as"; the GB2312 comment explains the fallback for unrecognized characters, not why VT and FF are unrecognized; testNonChinese is about letters of other writing systems (it asserts Arabic letters), not separators; the claim is "do not index whitespace as a term", not "split like UnicodeWhitespaceTokenizer"; and @lucene.experimental did not stop LUCENE-8325 from being fixed as a bug. What is left is that the contract is descriptive, not a sentence saying "whitespace is never indexed"; that is why the confirmation is medium confidence.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Treat the remaining Unicode whitespace as SPACE_LIKE at the OTHER fall-through (validated)

Just before return CharType.OTHER in Utility.getCharType, return SPACE_LIKE for UnicodeProps.WHITESPACE, Lucene's pinned White_Space set: +3 lines, and only the 14 characters change. The repro passes with assertions on and off, and so do the smartcn module's 22 tests and its static checks. Indexed terms change, so affected fields need reindexing.

import org.apache.lucene.analysis.util.UnicodeProps;
...
    if (ch >= 0xFE30 && ch <= 0xFF63) return CharType.DELIMITER;
    // Other Unicode whitespace (VT, FF, U+1680, U+2000-U+200A) also acts as a space
    if (UnicodeProps.WHITESPACE.get(ch)) return CharType.SPACE_LIKE;
    return CharType.OTHER;

A. Treat the remaining Unicode whitespace as SPACE_LIKE at the OTHER fall-through (validated) (recommended)

#

UnicodeProps.WHITESPACE is Lucene's pinned Unicode 17 White_Space set. At the fall-through it can only catch characters no earlier branch classified, which are exactly the 14. The six White_Space characters in the DELIMITER ranges (U+0085, U+00A0, U+2028, U+2029, U+202F, U+205F) keep today's behaviour; U+2007 FIGURE SPACE, a no-break space, is one of the 14 and changes (see the trade-offs). smartcn already requires analysis-common (module-info.java:21, build.gradle:24).

import org.apache.lucene.analysis.util.UnicodeProps;
...
    if (ch >= 0xFE30 && ch <= 0xFF63) return CharType.DELIMITER;
    // Other Unicode whitespace (VT, FF, U+1680, U+2000-U+200A) also acts as a space
    if (UnicodeProps.WHITESPACE.get(ch)) return CharType.SPACE_LIKE;
    return CharType.OTHER;

Trade-offs

  • Changes indexed terms for text with these characters: reindex, and a CHANGES entry.
  • The set follows Lucene's pinned Unicode version, not the JDK's.
  • U+2007 FIGURE SPACE, a no-break space, becomes skipped like any space, so Chinese characters are joined across it (中<U+2007>国: [中/1, U+2007/1, 国/1] today, [中国/1] with the fix); the other no-break spaces, U+00A0 and U+202F, stay DELIMITER and still split, with a position gap (中<U+00A0>国 -> [中/1, 国/2] today and with the fix). In Latin text all three keep separating the words. Treating U+2007 like an ordinary space is a policy choice to flag for the maintainers.

Downstream note

#

Products that bundle Lucene's smartcn module, such as the Elasticsearch Smart Chinese analysis plugin, inherit this behaviour, and a fix changes their indexed terms as well.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal: an exhaustive sweep ran every BMP character through the stock Utility.getCharType and compared its class with its Unicode category. Of the 17 space separators, 2 are SPACE_LIKE, 3 DELIMITER and 12 OTHER; each was then run through SmartChineseAnalyzer. A later check restated the scope over Unicode White_Space: 14 characters become terms.

The method in full124 words

Method: semi-formal, exhaustive over the BMP character table.

  • A sweep ran all 65,536 BMP characters through the stock Utility.getCharType on the main jars and cross-tabulated the class against the JDK 25 Unicode general category. For the space separators (Zs, 17 characters): SPACE_LIKE 2, DELIMITER 3, OTHER 12.
  • Each space separator was then driven through the stock SmartChineseAnalyzer and its terms printed; the OTHER ones came out as one-character terms.
  • The finding was reduced to a public-API JUnit test on the real jars.
  • The confirming check restated the scope over the Unicode White_Space property (Lucene's pinned UnicodeProps.WHITESPACE, checked over the whole BMP): that added VERTICAL TAB and FORM FEED and left out U+00A0, U+202F, U+205F, U+0085, U+2028 and U+2029, which behave like punctuation.

History

LFV-728, snapshot of 2026-11-06

Registry title
SmartChineseAnalyzer indexes whitespace characters as terms: VERTICAL TAB, FORM FEED, OGHAM SPACE MARK and the eleven spaces U+2000-U+200A (EN QUAD to HAIR SPACE, among them EN, EM and THIN SPACE) each become a one-character term, because Utility.getCharType classifies them OTHER instead of SPACE_LIKE
Area
analysis-smartcn / HHMM segmenter character classes (Utility.getCharType SPACE_LIKE test -> HHMMSegmenter OTHER branch -> one-character STRING term; SmartChineseAnalyzer and HMMChineseTokenizer)
Priority
Medium, rank 146: The stock SmartChineseAnalyzer indexes 14 rare Unicode spaces as terms, which breaks phrases that cross one of them, a rare-input relevance error.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Only users of the optional smartcn module (SmartChineseAnalyzer with any constructor, or the HMMChineseTokenizer it wraps; default settings, no scale) whose documents or queries contain one of the 14 characters are hit: a whitespace-only term is indexed and a TermQuery for that character finds the document, an exact phrase typed with an ordinary space misses the words on both sides of it (slop 1 still matches), and Hanzi are no longer joined into a word across it ('中 国' -> [中国], '中<U+2009>国' -> [中, U+2009, 国]); HTML run through Lucene's own HTMLStripCharFilter triggers it with &emsp; &ensp; and &thinsp; while &nbsp; does not (it decodes to U+0020); nothing throws, the index stays valid, -ea and -da behave the same, and because a fix changes indexed terms, affected fields need reindexing after it.
Summary
You index text with the stock SmartChineseAnalyzer, whose segmenter has a character class for 'Characters that act as a space' (CharType.SPACE_LIKE) and skips those characters, so you expect any space to separate words without a trace, as an ordinary space, a tab or the ideographic space U+3000 does. Instead Utility.getCharType knows only those five, and VERTICAL TAB, FORM FEED, OGHAM SPACE MARK and U+2000-U+200A fall through to OTHER and are each indexed as a one-character term: 'hello<THIN SPACE>world' gives [hello, U+2009, world], a TermQuery for U+2009 alone finds the document, and '中<U+2009>国' gives [中, U+2009, 国] where '中 国' gives [中国]. So the exact phrase "hello world" matches only 3 of 7 documents that differ only in the separator (space, tab and U+3000 match; FORM FEED, U+1680, EM SPACE and THIN SPACE miss), and HTML indented with &emsp;&emsp; gets two whitespace terms, with no error.
