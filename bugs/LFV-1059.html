DirectoryReader.openIfChanged leaks segment reader references when the leaf sorter throws while the new reader is built

If a leaf sorter throws while openIfChanged builds the new reader, the failed call keeps a reference on every segment shared with the old reader and never closes the new segment's reader. The shared segment ends at refCount 2, not 1, so closing the old reader leaves its files open.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

Background: leaf sorters and shared segment readers

#
  1. A DirectoryReader is a list of segment readers, one per segment. DirectoryReader.open(dir, leafSorter) takes a Comparator<LeafReader> that orders them, for example newest segment first for time-based indices, so sorted queries can stop early.
  2. Segment readers are reference counted. openIfChanged builds a new reader that shares the unchanged segments with the old one: it adds one reference to each shared segment reader and opens readers only for new segments.
  3. Closing a reader removes its references, and a segment reader closes (with its files) when its count reaches 0. After a normal refresh a shared segment has refCount 2, one per reader, and closes once both readers are closed.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Commit one document (segment _0) and open DirectoryReader.open(dir, sorter), where sorter is a Comparator<LeafReader> that orders the segments. One segment needs no comparison, so the open succeeds: _0's reader has refCount 1.
  2. Commit a second document (segment _1) and call DirectoryReader.openIfChanged(reader). The new reader has two segments, so the sorter runs, and here it throws, as a sorter bug on a segment without the sort field or an I/O error it rethrows would.
  3. You expect only the exception. No new reader was returned, so _0 should be back at refCount 1 and closing reader should close it. The first open cleans up after exactly this failure.
  4. Instead _0's refCount is 2 after the failed call and still 1 after reader.close(), and _1's new reader is never closed: both segment files, _0.cfs and _1.cfs, stay open. Every retried refresh leaks again.

Who is affected

#

Applications that refresh a reader opened with a leaf sorter (DirectoryReader.open(Directory, Comparator) or a commit overload, since 8.9.0) through openIfChanged, SearcherManager or ReaderManager, or refresh an NRT reader of a leaf-sorting IndexWriter to an explicit IndexCommit. Opt-in; it takes a sorter that throws during a refresh. Each failure leaks segment readers; results stay correct.

Mechanism, terms and numbers571 words

DirectoryReader.open(Directory, Comparator<LeafReader> leafSorter) (LUCENE-9507, since 8.9.0) orders the segments with an application comparator, and the reader keeps that sorter for its refreshes. A non-NRT openIfChanged (and also an NRT reader's refresh to an explicit IndexCommit) goes through the reopen factory StandardDirectoryReader.open(Directory, SegmentInfos, List, Comparator, ExecutorService). It first takes a reference on every segment reader it shares with the old reader and opens readers for new segments, then constructs the new reader with no try/catch. That constructor runs Arrays.sort with the sorter. If the sorter throws, openIfChanged throws too, no new reader is returned, and nothing releases those references: with two one-document segments the shared segment reader's refCount is 2 instead of 1 after the failed call, 1 instead of 0 after the old reader is closed, and the new segment's reader is never closed. The initial open and the NRT open catch any Throwable and release their readers; the reopen factory is the only open path with no cleanup at all. Results are unaffected and the sorter failure is loud; the leak is silent and repeats on every retry. A try/catch of 7 code lines, mirroring the initial open, fixes it. Every release from 8.9.0 to 10.6.0 and main is affected.

Terms

#
  • Leaf sorter: the Comparator<LeafReader> given to DirectoryReader.open(Directory, Comparator) or a commit overload (LUCENE-9507). It is null by default. The reader keeps it as subReadersSorter and uses it for every refresh.
  • Reopen factory: StandardDirectoryReader.open(Directory, SegmentInfos, List, Comparator, ExecutorService) (StandardDirectoryReader.java:174-184 at 645d4fe14a20), @lucene.internal, behind every openIfChanged of a reader that was not opened from an IndexWriter (also the ExecutorService and IndexCommit overloads), and behind openIfChanged(reader, IndexCommit) of an NRT reader.

The bug

#
  1. openIfChanged(reader) reaches doOpenNoWriter (StandardDirectoryReader.java:477-494), doOpenFromCommit (:496-505) and doOpenIfChanged(SegmentInfos, ExecutorService) (:507-511), which calls the reopen factory with the reader's own sorter.
  2. The factory calls createSegmentReaders (:181). For an unchanged segment, "this reader will be shared between the old and the new one, so we must incRef it": oldReader.incRef() (:317-323), refCount 1 to 2. A new segment gets new SegmentReader(...) (:292-296), refCount 1. createSegmentReaders releases these itself only when one of its own steps fails (:218-228, :241-244).
  3. Then return new StandardDirectoryReader(directory, newReaders, null, infos, leafSorter, false, false); (:182-183) runs with no try/catch. Through DirectoryReader's constructor (DirectoryReader.java:539) it reaches BaseCompositeReader, which runs Arrays.sort(subReaders, subReadersSorter) (BaseCompositeReader.java:75-77): the application's comparator.
  4. The comparator throws, openIfChanged throws, and the array newReaders is gone: the caller has no handle on the extra reference or on the new reader.
  5. The initial open wraps the same constructor call: "it must be inside try clause so we close readers in that case", catch (Throwable t) { IOUtils.closeWhileSuppressingExceptions(t, readers); throw t; } (:96-105). The NRT open also catches Throwable and decRefs its readers (:128-165).

Exposure and likelihood532 words
  • Who is hit. Readers opened with DirectoryReader.open(Directory, Comparator) (DirectoryReader.java:88-91), its ExecutorService overload (:106-110) or the commit overloads (:189-194, :218-226), refreshed with openIfChanged, SearcherManager(DirectoryReader, SearcherFactory) (SearcherManager.java:158; refreshIfNeeded calls openIfChanged, :194-205) or ReaderManager(DirectoryReader) (ReaderManager.java:82); a fact-check probe ran all three. Also NRT readers from an IndexWriter with IndexWriterConfig#setLeafSorter when they are refreshed to an explicit commit: openIfChanged(reader, IndexCommit) (DirectoryReader.java:282-301) goes to doOpenFromWriter, which for a non-null commit calls doOpenFromCommit and so the same reopen factory with the writer's leaf sorter (StandardDirectoryReader.java:155, :406-431, :456-460); a SearcherManager(IndexWriter, ..., RefreshCommitSupplier) (SearcherManager.java:114-131) takes this route whenever its supplier returns a commit (:199-200). In the fact-check probe the segment files stay open after the failed refresh, the NRT reader and the writer are closed ("cannot close: there are still 2 open files: {_1.cfs=1, _0.cfs=1}"); a sorter that does not throw leaves nothing open, and the fix covers this route too. There the old segment readers are NRT readers, so the factory opens new readers that share their cores (:298-315): the symptom is the open files, not the old reader's refCount.
  • Default vs opt-in. The leaf sorter is an expert option, null by default. Plain NRT refreshes (openIfChanged(reader) or openIfChanged(reader, writer) on an NRT reader, or a SearcherManager whose refresh commit supplier returns null, the default) go through the NRT open, which releases its readers, so this leg does not hit them.
  • What triggers it. A sorter that throws during a refresh: a comparator bug on a segment that lacks the sort field (for example a timestamp sorter that dereferences getPointValues("ts"), which is null for such a segment, LeafReader.java:510-514), or an I/O error inside a correct sorter: Comparator.compare cannot throw checked exceptions, while per-segment readers such as getNumericDocValues declare IOException (LeafReader.java:175), so such a sorter that propagates the error must wrap it in an unchecked exception.
  • How much. Per failed refresh: one extra reference on every segment reader shared with the old reader (every unchanged segment) and one open reader per new segment. Lucene never releases them: after the old reader is closed the shared reader stays at refCount 1 and the segment files stay open. It grows linearly with failed attempts and happens at any index size; one failed call leaks once (the commit lookup retries only after an IOException, SegmentInfos.java:884). Whether garbage collection later reclaims the file handles was not measured.
  • What does not happen. No wrong results, no index change, no hang. The refresh fails loudly with the sorter's exception: SearcherManager.maybeRefresh() rethrows it to its caller and keeps serving the previous searcher (executed), and the old reader keeps working.
  • Related legs, not part of this report (found by the first finder on 645d4fe14a20): the NRT open with IndexWriterConfig#setLeafSorter keeps the IndexFileDeleter references it took (StandardDirectoryReader.java:148) when the constructor throws, so files of merged-away segments stay until the writer closes; and the initial open's cleanup iterates the array that Arrays.sort was rearranging, so a late sorter failure in a TimSort merge (32+ segments) leaks some readers ("still 4 open files").

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The initial open wraps the same constructor call in catch (Throwable): "it must be inside try clause so we close readers in that case".
  • openIfChanged makes the caller own only the reader passed in and a returned one; a failed call returns none.
  • testOverDecRefDuringReopen expects a failed openIfChanged to leave no file open.
The full argument225 words
  • Ownership contract. openIfChanged (DirectoryReader.java:236-243): "The provided reader is not closed (you are responsible for doing so); if a new reader is returned you also must eventually close it."
  • Reference counting. IndexReader.incRef (IndexReader.java:164-174): "Be sure to always call a corresponding {@link #decRef}, in a finally clause; otherwise the reader may never be closed."
  • The same class. The initial open (StandardDirectoryReader.java:96-105), the NRT open (:128-165) and createSegmentReaders (:218-228, :241-244) all release their readers on failure (the two related legs under "Who is affected" are remaining gaps of the first two). An independent verifier's control: the initial open with the same throwing sorter over two segments leaves no file open.
  • Tests. TestDirectoryReaderReopen.testOverDecRefDuringReopen (:673-735) makes openIfChanged fail with a FakeIOException, checks that the old reader still works and closes its MockDirectoryWrapper, which fails on files left open. No test makes a leaf sorter throw.
  • History. LUCENE-5262 (#6326, 2013, "StandardDirectoryReader should decRef readers on exception, not close them") made the NRT open exception-safe; LUCENE-6299 (#7360, 2015; 4e53fae38e9 on its branch, 33fad60de91 on trunk) guarded the initial open only; LUCENE-9507 (48715fe898bf, 2021, #32) put the user callback into the constructor and passed it to the reopen factory's unguarded call. None of the 69 comments and reviews on LUCENE-9507, #32, apache/lucene-solr#2256 and #2473 mentions exceptions.
The devil's-advocate check123 words

The strongest case for "intended" (the devil's-advocate verifier and the intended-lens reviewer): the exception comes from the application's own comparator, so "fix your comparator"; nothing documents exception safety for callbacks; the factory is @lucene.internal (StandardDirectoryReader.java:168-173).

Why it fails: the path is public and untagged (DirectoryReader.open(Directory, Comparator), then openIfChanged); Comparator.compare may throw, and no javadoc of the leaf-sorter overloads or of IndexWriterConfig.setLeafSorter (IndexWriterConfig.java:517-527) asks for a sorter that never throws; a correct sorter that reads doc values and propagates an I/O error must wrap it in an unchecked exception; and Lucene already cleans up after this very failure on the initial open. The argument explains the Low priority, not the leak. Reviewers rated it weak to moderate.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Release the reopen factory's readers when the constructor throws (with a copy of the array)

In the reopen factory, wrap the constructor call in try/catch (Throwable), pass newReaders.clone() (the sort rearranges the array in place) and on failure decRef newReaders and rethrow, as the initial open does. Validated through gradle: the repro passes with assertions on and off; 104 tests of the closest suites pass.

     SegmentReader[] newReaders = createSegmentReaders(infos, oldReaders, executor);
-    return new StandardDirectoryReader(
-        directory, newReaders, null, infos, leafSorter, false, false);
+    try {
+      // The constructor sorts the array it is given in place (leaf sorter), so pass a copy and
+      // release from the original if it throws:
+      return new StandardDirectoryReader(
+          directory, newReaders.clone(), null, infos, leafSorter, false, false);
+    } catch (Throwable t) {
+      decRefWhileSuppressingException(t, newReaders);
+      throw t;
+    }

Release the reopen factory's readers when the constructor throws (with a copy of the array) (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In StandardDirectoryReader.open(Directory, SegmentInfos, List, Comparator, ExecutorService) (StandardDirectoryReader.java:181-183), wrap the constructor call in try { ... } catch (Throwable t), pass newReaders.clone() to the constructor, and on failure call decRefWhileSuppressingException(t, newReaders) (:364-374, already used by createSegmentReaders) and rethrow. A shared reader goes back from 2 to 1 (the old reader keeps its own reference); a new reader goes from 1 to 0 and closes.

     SegmentReader[] newReaders = createSegmentReaders(infos, oldReaders, executor);
-    return new StandardDirectoryReader(
-        directory, newReaders, null, infos, leafSorter, false, false);
+    try {
+      // The constructor sorts the array it is given in place (leaf sorter), so pass a copy and
+      // release from the original if it throws:
+      return new StandardDirectoryReader(
+          directory, newReaders.clone(), null, infos, leafSorter, false, false);
+    } catch (Throwable t) {
+      decRefWhileSuppressingException(t, newReaders);
+      throw t;
+    }

Trade-offs

  • Why the copy. The cleanup must see every reader exactly once, and Arrays.sort rearranges the array it is given. A probe on JDK 25: when the comparator throws mid-sort, arrays of up to 31 elements stay a permutation (insertion sort), but from 32 elements on (TimSort merges) they can hold duplicates and miss elements, up to 7 of each at 32 elements and 15 at 64. Without the copy, the cleanup could release a shared reader twice and miss others.
  • Cost. One array copy per refresh; no API or behaviour change when the sorter succeeds.
  • Also covers. Each original entry is decRef'd exactly once, so the readers are also released when the same constructor throws "Too many documents", and the NRT-reader-to-commit route passes the fact-check probe with the fix.
  • Scope. Fixes the reopen leg only; the NRT deleter leg and the initial open's late-TimSort leg need the same treatment separately.

What the checks validated

Through gradle in a scratch worktree of 645d4fe14a20 (OpenJDK 25.0.3, seed DEADBEEF): the minimal test fails on stock and passes with the fix, assertions on and off. The closest suites (TestDirectoryReader, TestDirectoryReaderReopen, TestIndexWriterReader, TestIndexReaderClose, TestReaderClosed, TestSearcherManager: 6 classes, 104 tests) pass on stock and with the fix (1 skipped both times); none of them makes a sorter throw. google-java-format 1.37.0 leaves the fix's lines and the test unchanged. Earlier, the hunt's shadow check, an independent verifier's controls and the refute reviewer's run (57 of 57 tests of TestDirectoryReader and TestIndexWriterReader) passed with the same diff.

One exception-safe pattern for all three open paths

#

Also pass a copy of the readers array in the initial open (its cleanup iterates the array being sorted) and, in the NRT open, release the deleter references taken by writer.incRefDeleter(segmentInfos) (StandardDirectoryReader.java:148) when the constructor throws. Not run.

Trade-offs

Fixes the two related legs as well, but it is three changes needing three tests, two of them (32+ segments; deleter state) harder to keep minimal.

The same try/catch without the copy

#

Catch and decRef newReaders directly. Not recommended.

Trade-offs

Correct below 32 segments, but a late sorter failure on a larger reader leaves the array with duplicates and gaps, so the cleanup could release a shared reader twice, closing it under the old reader, and miss others.

Downstream note

#

Services that retry a failing refresh on a schedule, for example SearcherManager.maybeRefresh() from a refresh thread, leak once per attempt for as long as their sorter keeps failing on the same segment.

How it was found

#

Formal: TLA+ model checking (TLC) of Lucene's reader reference counting and its open and reopen paths. With a "constructor throws" fault, the invariant that no reference leaks fails (27 and 137 distinct states) and the modelled fix passes. A static exception-path scan flagged the same call the same day; a public-API JUnit test confirmed it.

The method in full150 words

Method: formal, TLA+ model checking with TLC (a hunt unit, 2026-10-26), on a model of IndexReader reference counting and the StandardDirectoryReader open and reopen factories built from the code with a file:line map.

  • With the ctorThrow fault (the StandardDirectoryReader constructor throws, for example from the leaf sorter), the NoLeak invariant fails in the sequential and executor configurations (27 and 137 distinct states); the same configurations with the modelled fix (decRef the new readers when the constructor throws) pass.
  • The fault edge was added while modelling the code, so a strict reading could call the discovery semi-formal.
  • Found independently the same day by another hunt unit's static exception-edge resource scan: the SegmentReader[] from createSegmentReaders is live on the exception edge of new StandardDirectoryReader(...) with no handler.
  • A later hunt unit re-ran the model on 645d4fe14a20 (violation without the fix, pass with it) and reduced the counterexample to the one-test repro.

History

#

Introduced by LUCENE-9507 (48715fe898bf, 2021-03-26; branch_8x backport 1cfd0df4e7bb), which put the user's leaf sorter inside a constructor call the reopen factory never guarded. First released in 8.9.0; every release through 10.6.0 and main is affected (17 releases executed, the other 9.x/10.x tags by a static check). Not fixed upstream.

Versions tested6 rows

Introduced by 48715fe898bf, LUCENE-9507 "Custom order for leaves in IndexReader and IndexWriter (#32)" (#10546; author date 2021-03-26), backported to branch_8x as 1cfd0df4e7bb (apache/lucene-solr#2473, 2021-03-30). It added Arrays.sort(subReaders, subReadersSorter) to the BaseCompositeReader constructor and passed leafSorter into the reopen factory's final constructor call, which had been outside any try since at least LUCENE-4986 (2013); until then that call could only throw "Too many documents", which needs out-of-limit input.

LUCENE-6299 (4e53fae38e9, 2015) had guarded the initial open only. The 2025-11 refactor (#15428, 7d4e22454b53; #15445 backport, first released in 10.4.0) moved reader creation into createSegmentReaders and kept the unguarded call, so 10.4.0 is not a regression. A static check of the 33 9.x/10.x release tags up to 10.5.2 finds the call unguarded in each; 10.6.0, the 34th, has the same unguarded call and fails the minimal test. 9.2.0-9.12.2, 10.1.0-10.3.1, 10.5.0 and 10.5.1 are covered by the static check only. Upstream main 2f424fd428d (2026-10-30) has no commit to the fix-site files since 2026-10-28; the last change to StandardDirectoryReader.java is from 2026-02-11 (#15681).

LFV-1059, snapshot of 2026-11-05

Registry title
DirectoryReader.openIfChanged on a reader opened with a leaf sorter leaks segment reader references when the sorter throws while the new reader is built, so closing the old reader no longer closes the shared segments and the new segment's reader is never closed
Area
core / index reader reopen (StandardDirectoryReader reopen factory behind every non-NRT openIfChanged, with a leaf sorter, LUCENE-9507)
Priority
Low, rank 1091: A bounded openIfChanged leak of segment reader references that happens only when an opt-in leaf sorter throws during a refresh, which is already a loud failure.
Impact class
minor
Impact note
Applications that open readers with the opt-in leaf sorter (DirectoryReader.open(Directory, Comparator) or a commit overload that takes one, since 8.9.0) and refresh them with openIfChanged, SearcherManager or ReaderManager: when the sorter throws during a refresh (a comparator bug on a segment that lacks the sort field, or an I/O error that a sorter reading per-segment data rethrows unchecked), the failed refresh keeps an extra reference on every segment reader it shares with the old reader and never closes the new segment's reader, so Lucene never releases their files and cores (executed: after closing the old reader, MockDirectoryWrapper still reports both segment files open); it repeats on every retry while the sorter keeps failing; the sorter failure itself is loud, search results are unaffected, any index size.
Summary
You open a DirectoryReader with a leaf sorter and later call DirectoryReader.openIfChanged; if the sorter throws while the new reader sorts its leaves, you expect only that exception, because the javadoc makes you responsible just for the old reader and the initial open releases its readers in exactly this case. Instead the reopen factory has already taken a reference on each reused segment reader and opened the new ones, and it builds the new reader with no try/catch, so after the failed call the shared segment reader's refCount is 2 instead of 1 (1 instead of 0 after you close the old reader) and the new segment's reader is never closed.
