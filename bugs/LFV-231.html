The bug in 30 seconds

#

In Thai, ๆ (Maiyamok) means "say the previous word again". A token's offsets are the [start, end) range of the original text it came from; highlighters put their markup there.

  1. Analyze บ่อยๆ + U+200B (a zero-width space) + ครับ with the stock ThaiAnalyzer of 10.6. ThaiRepeatFilter turns บ่อยๆ ("often") into two tokens บ่อย: the word, and a repeat that stands for the ๆ at offset 4.
  2. You expect what the same text without the zero-width space gives: the word at [0,4) and the repeat at [4,5), exactly the ๆ. The filter's CHANGES entry promises "exact OffsetAttribute and highlighter compatibility".
  3. ThaiCharFilter first deletes the zero-width space, and Lucene's offset correction then ends the token after the deleted character: บ่อยๆ reaches the filter as [0,6), 6 source characters for a 5-character term.
  4. ThaiRepeatFilter assumes the span's last character is the ๆ and counts back from the end: the word gets [0,5), which includes the ๆ, and the repeat gets [5,6), only the deleted zero-width space.
  5. Terms, positions and matches are right; highlights are not. A phrase highlight gives บ่อยๆ<b>‹ZWSP›ครับ</b> (‹ZWSP› marks the invisible U+200B), with the ๆ left out; behind HTMLStripCharFilter the repeat gets the > of the next tag and the highlighter cuts it: <i><b>บ่อยๆ</i</b><b>></b>.

Who is affected

#

Users of the stock ThaiAnalyzer from 10.6.0 on, when a zero-width character follows a word with ๆ that the JDK keeps in one token (on JDK 25: จริงๆ, บ่อยๆ, ดีๆ, อื่นๆ), and custom chains with HTMLStripCharFilter before ThaiRepeatFilter. Only offsets and highlights are wrong; deterministic, any index size.

Mechanism, terms and numbers557 words

ThaiRepeatFilter (new in 10.6, #16720) expands the Thai repetition mark Maiyamok ๆ (U+0E46): บ่อยๆ becomes the token บ่อย plus a second บ่อย that stands for the ๆ. It computes their offsets by counting back from the end of the incoming span: the word gets [start, end - 1) and the repeat the last character (ThaiRepeatFilter.java:114-123 on main 645d4fe14a20). That is right only when the span maps 1:1 onto the term. The stock ThaiAnalyzer puts ThaiCharFilter in front (ThaiAnalyzer.java:119-123), which deletes zero-width characters (ThaiCharFilter.java:89-94), and CharFilter offset correction ends a token after the characters deleted right behind it (BaseCharFilter.java:40-53). So for บ่อยๆ + U+200B + ครับ the span is [0,6), the word gets [0,5) (which contains the ๆ) and the repeat [5,6), only the deleted zero-width space. Terms, positions, matching and scoring are correct; offsets in the index and highlights are wrong. Lucene's other offset-splitting filters keep the whole span when its length differs from the term's; this one has no such check.

Terms

#
  • Maiyamok ๆ (U+0E46): the Thai repetition mark. บ่อยๆ ("often") is read บ่อย บ่อย. ThaiRepeatFilter (10.6, #16720) emits the word, then one copy per ๆ, each copy at its own position with the offsets of its ๆ.
  • Offsets: OffsetAttribute start and end, the range of the original text a token came from. They are stored with postings or term vectors and used by highlighters.
  • CharFilter: edits the text before the tokenizer sees it and records where, so the tokenizer can map offsets back (correctOffset). ThaiCharFilter, which the stock ThaiAnalyzer always uses, deletes U+200B, U+200C, U+200D and U+FEFF; HTMLStripCharFilter deletes markup.

The bug, step by step (main 645d4fe14a20)

#

Input offsets: บ0 ่1 อ2 ย3 ๆ4 U+200B 5 ค6 ร7 ั8 บ9.

  1. ThaiAnalyzer builds ThaiTokenizer -> LowerCaseFilter -> DecimalDigitFilter -> ThaiNormalizationFilter -> ThaiRepeatFilter -> StopFilter and wraps every reader in ThaiCharFilter (ThaiAnalyzer.java:101-109, :119-123).
  2. ThaiCharFilter drops U+200B at output offset 5 and records addOffCorrectMap(5, 1) (ThaiCharFilter.java:89-94). The tokenizer sees บ่อยๆครับ.
  3. BaseCharFilter.correct(off) adds the diff recorded at the largest offset <= off (BaseCharFilter.java:40-53), so the token บ่อยๆ, which ends at output offset 5, ends at source offset correct(5) = 6: it arrives as [0,6). That widening is the long-known CharFilter end-offset behaviour (#6796, #7653) and harmless by itself: the span still covers the whole word.
  4. ThaiRepeatFilter (case 2, ThaiRepeatFilter.java:104-129) counts 1 trailing ๆ, cuts the term to บ่อย, sets baseEnd = Math.max(origStart, origEnd - maiyamokCount) = 5 (:114), gives the word [0,5) (:115) and the repeat [baseEnd, origEnd) = [5,6) (:118-123). Nothing compares origEnd - origStart with the term length (:75-77).
  5. For a token of two or more ๆ (case 1, :79-102) the copies get one character each, counted from the start, the last ending at origEnd (:87-96): the same one-character-per-ๆ assumption. A single standalone ๆ is not split: the if (count > 1) block (:87-100) is skipped and the token keeps its incoming span.

With HTMLStripCharFilter + ThaiTokenizer + ThaiRepeatFilter, บ่อยๆ</b>ครับ arrives as [0,9), so the word gets [0,8) = บ่อยๆ</b and the repeat [8,9) = >.

Exposure and likelihood300 words
  • Default chain, no opt-in. ThaiAnalyzer always uses ThaiCharFilter and ThaiRepeatFilter. Any of U+200B, U+200C, U+200D or U+FEFF right after an attached ๆ triggers it. ThaiCharFilter's javadoc names zero-width characters among the typographic anomalies it exists to remove (ThaiCharFilter.java:27-30).
  • JDK-dependent reach. The JDK word BreakIterator must keep the ๆ in the word's token. On JDK 25.0.3 it does for จริงๆ, บ่อยๆ, ดีๆ, อื่นๆ and not for เร็วๆ, มากๆ, whose ๆ becomes its own token.
  • Custom chains: chains with ThaiRepeatFilter (SPI name thaiRepeat) where a CharFilter deletes characters right after a word ending in ๆ and the tokenizer keeps that ๆ in the word's token, so the deleted characters land in the tail of the span. Executed with HTMLStripCharFilter (a tag right after the word is enough) and ThaiCharFilter, also with a whitespace tokenizer and with two or more ๆ.
  • What breaks: offsets in postings, term-vector offsets and highlights. Terms, positions, matching, scoring and filtering are unchanged; nothing throws; every span stays inside the token's original span.
  • How it shows: a phrase or other position-sensitive highlight of the repeated word leaves the ๆ out; a term query highlights both tokens, so it still covers the ๆ. In raw-HTML highlighting the highlighter's tags land inside the following tag (</i + >). HTMLStripCharFilter already mis-nests highlight markup before a closing tag for plain tokens (<i><b>abc</i></b>); ThaiRepeatFilter adds the cut in the tag text. With escape=true only the highlight position is wrong.
  • Timing: unreleased, but in the 10.6.0 release candidate (RC1, whose vote was passing on 2026-10-28), so 10.6.0 will most likely ship with it.
  • Downstream: Elasticsearch merged a thai_repeat port of the filter into its Lucene-snapshot branch (elastic/elasticsearch#160853); opensearch-project/OpenSearch#23151 (open) plans one.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The CHANGES entry promises "exact OffsetAttribute and highlighter compatibility"; the introducing commit says the repeats' offsets "match the corresponding 'ๆ' character(s)".
  • The trigger is Lucene's default chain: ThaiAnalyzer itself puts the deleting ThaiCharFilter in front of ThaiRepeatFilter.
  • Lucene's other splitting filters keep the whole span when it differs from the term length (WordDelimiterGraphFilter, CJKBigramFilter); this one does not.
The full argument250 words
  • The record promises exact offsets. lucene/CHANGES.txt:494-495: "Add ThaiRepeatFilter and ThaiRepeatFilterFactory to expand Thai repetition mark (Maiyamok) with exact OffsetAttribute and highlighter compatibility." The squash commit b7c2c202ba69 carries the in-PR message: "Ensure both the primary term and repeated Maiyamok tokens receive precise, non-overlapping start/end offsets. For attached Maiyamok (e.g. 'เร็วๆ'), set primary token endOffset to boundary before 'ๆ' and set repeated token offsets to match the corresponding 'ๆ' character(s)." TestThaiRepeatFilter#testAttachedMaiyamok asserts [0,4) and [4,5) for เร็วๆ (TestThaiRepeatFilter.java:38-44). The class javadoc does not restrict the input (ThaiRepeatFilter.java:27-34).
  • The repro asks for less than "exact": only that the repeat's span contains its ๆ. The current output breaks even that.
  • The default analyzer composes it. ThaiCharFilter (#16717) and ThaiRepeatFilter (#16720) came from the same author and were merged 45 minutes apart; ThaiAnalyzer uses both (ThaiAnalyzer.java:101-123).
  • Lucene guards this case elsewhere. WordDelimiterGraphFilter: "if length by start + end offsets doesn't match the term's text then set offsets for all our word parts/concats to the incoming offsets" (:257-263); WordDelimiterFilter.hasIllegalOffsets (:432-434); CJKBigramFilter: "crazy offsets (modified by synonym or charfilter): just preserve" (:284-285).
  • Why the tests missed it. The explicit tests use MockTokenizer or ThaiTokenizer input with no CharFilter (TestThaiRepeatFilter.java:30-91); the randomized test checks consistency and offset bounds, not which characters a token covers. #16720 had one approving comment and no review threads.
  • Not to report: the repeats' position increment of 1 was a deliberate choice in #16720.
The devil's-advocate check211 words

The strongest case for "works as designed" (the refute review and the cross-model review rated it moderate; the intended-behaviour check built it and rejected it; nobody accepted it): the analysis package says "Token filters should not modify offsets. If you feel that your filter would need to modify offsets, then it should probably be implemented as a tokenizer" (package-info.java:416-417); #9496 (LUCENE-8450, open) says filters "cannot perform simple arithmetic to calculate the original ('correct') offset of a character in the interior of the token" once CharFilters change token lengths, and #8817 (LUCENE-7766, open) reports the same for WordDelimiter(Graph)Filter; OffsetAttribute allows a span that differs from the term length (OffsetAttribute.java:27-29); and CharFilter end-offset attribution is an old open limitation (#6796, #7653).

Why it fails: the filter's record promises exact offsets and highlighter compatibility; a span that is too wide would be the accepted outcome, but here the repeat's span misses its own ๆ and the word's span takes it, and the defensive handling Lucene uses elsewhere (keep the whole span, a convention that #8817 disputes) is missing; the trigger is the shipped default analyzer; and in LUCENE-4984 (#6048) the earlier Thai splitting filter was replaced by ThaiTokenizer because, as a token filter, it created positions and updated offsets.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Whole-span guard in ThaiRepeatFilter (validated)

Split per character only when the span length equals the term length (endOffset - startOffset == length); otherwise give the word and every repeat the whole incoming span, as WordDelimiterGraphFilter does: +10 -6 in ThaiRepeatFilter. The test and all 1910 analysis-common tests pass. Safe, not exact: after a CharFilter change both tokens share the span.

+      // or markup after the Maiyamok); otherwise every emitted token keeps the original span.

A. Whole-span guard in ThaiRepeatFilter (validated) (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

The finder's guard, re-made as a git diff on 645d4fe14a20 (1 file, +10 -6). Compute exactOffsets once per token; when it is false, the word and every repeat keep [origStart, origEnd), in both branches (case 1 gets the same change for firstEnd, s and e). The idiom of WordDelimiterGraphFilter and CJKBigramFilter: a conservative fallback, not an exact mapping. The length test is a heuristic: equal lengths do not prove a 1:1 mapping (a replacement that keeps the character count, or a deletion and an insertion that cancel out, were not tested), and a length difference does not prove that a CharFilter acted.

+      // Offsets can only be split per character if they map 1:1 onto the term (a CharFilter may
+      // have removed or contracted characters inside the token's span, e.g. a zero-width space
+      // or markup after the Maiyamok); otherwise every emitted token keeps the original span.
+      boolean exactOffsets = origEnd - origStart == len;
...
-        int baseEnd = Math.max(origStart, origEnd - maiyamokCount);
+        int baseEnd = exactOffsets ? Math.max(origStart, origEnd - maiyamokCount) : origEnd;
...
-          int s = Math.min(origEnd, baseEnd + i);
-          int e = (i == maiyamokCount - 1) ? origEnd : Math.min(origEnd, s + 1);
+          int s = exactOffsets ? Math.min(origEnd, baseEnd + i) : origStart;
+          int e = (i == maiyamokCount - 1 || !exactOffsets) ? origEnd : Math.min(origEnd, s + 1);

Trade-offs

  • Safe, not exact: after a CharFilter change the word's span also covers the ๆ. Tests that require the word not to cover it still fail: the independent reproduction's test fails 5 of 9 with the guard (its 4 ownership tests and one control; its 2 highlight tests pass), the refute reviewer's exact-offset tests 2 of 4.
  • It widens a case main gets right by coincidence: a deletion before the ๆ (มาก + U+200B + ๆ).
  • The same coarseness in WordDelimiterFilter is the subject of open #8817 (LUCENE-7766).
  • Today's output is kept only when the incoming span length equals the term length (the exactOffsets branch is today's code).
  • Any other mismatch also gets the whole span, whatever caused it, including a token filter that changed the term's length. ThaiNormalizationFilter, which runs right before ThaiRepeatFilter in ThaiAnalyzer, shortens terms (ThaiNormalizationFilter.java:44): with WhitespaceTokenizer + ThaiNormalizationFilter + ThaiRepeatFilter and no CharFilter, ดีีๆ นะ gives the exact ดี[0,3), ดี[3,4) on main and ดี[0,4) twice with the guard.

B. Do the split where CharFilter offsets can be mapped (a tokenizer)

#

A tokenizer can map each character back through the CharFilter chain with correctOffset(int); a token filter cannot. Expanding ๆ in ThaiTokenizer (or handing it the split points) would let the word end at the ๆ's own corrected offset. This follows the analysis package's guidance ("it should probably be implemented as a tokenizer", package-info.java:416-417), rmuir's position on #9496 ("The thing dividing text up into tokens, tokenizer is the lucene class for that.") and LUCENE-4984 (#6048), where the earlier Thai splitting filter was replaced by ThaiTokenizer.

Trade-offs

A design change to a new public filter (thaiRepeat works after any tokenizer today); the split would have to account for ThaiNormalizationFilter, which runs before ThaiRepeatFilter. Not built; the offsets it would give are reasoning from BaseCharFilter.java:40-53, not executed.

C. Make ThaiCharFilter attribute a removed zero-width character to the following token

#

Change where ThaiCharFilter records the correction for a removed zero-width character.

Trade-offs

Fixes only the default-analyzer trigger; HTMLStripCharFilter and other CharFilters still produce it. It moves the next token's start back over the removed character, and since correct() serves start and end offsets alike, a deletion at a token boundary must belong to one side. Not built.

D. Narrow the promise

#

Reword the CHANGES entry and the class javadoc so that exact offsets hold only when no CharFilter changed the token's characters. Could accompany A.

Trade-offs

No behaviour change; on its own it leaves the misattribution in place.

How it was found

#

A bounded-exhaustive check (semi-formal): the real ThaiAnalyzer chain ran on every Thai text of up to 3 characters from a 16-character slice in 12 word contexts (52,428 texts, 460,810 checks), against an oracle written from the CHANGES entry. 306 texts broke it; the first, at 2 characters, was ดีๆ + U+200B.

The method in full174 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation of the ThaiAnalyzer chain on the stock main jar, by a hunt unit (analysis, new filters). The harness runs the real classes, so there is no transcription gap; no model checker, SMT solver or proof produced the signal.

  • Scope: every text of up to 3 characters over a 16-character Thai slice (consonants, vowels, tone marks, the Maiyamok, a Thai digit, space, U+200B and U+200D), inside 12 prefix/suffix word contexts: 52,428 texts, 460,810 checks at length 3, in 1.7 s.
  • Oracle O4, written from the CHANGES entry: each repeated token covers exactly its own Maiyamok and the base token covers none. 306 texts violate it at length 3; the first violation came at length 2 (ดีๆ + U+200B: expected [0,2), [2,4), got [0,3), [3,4)).
  • Vacuity check: the oracle goes quiet with the guard fix.
  • The HTMLStripCharFilter and highlighter symptoms were then found by targeted runs, and the finder wrote a 5-method JUnit; this doc's repro is its first method as a self-contained class.

Bug record

LFV-231, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
ThaiRepeatFilter gives the repeated word the offsets of a deleted character or of the following markup instead of the Maiyamok's, when a CharFilter removed characters right after the Maiyamok (a zero-width space in the default ThaiAnalyzer, a closing tag with HTMLStripCharFilter)
Area
analysis / Thai (ThaiRepeatFilter offset split; ThaiAnalyzer with ThaiCharFilter, or a custom chain with HTMLStripCharFilter)
Priority
Low, rank 818: In the unreleased 10.6.0 code ThaiRepeatFilter gives the repeated word the offsets of a character a CharFilter removed (a zero-width space in the stock ThaiAnalyzer, or markup behind HTMLStripCharFilter), so highlights drop the Maiyamok or cut into a tag, ordinary Thai text but offsets only, with terms, positions and matching correct and a fix due before the release.
Impact class
minor
Impact note
Only offsets are wrong (postings and term-vector offsets, and highlights built from them; terms, positions, matching and scoring are unchanged): with the stock ThaiAnalyzer of the coming 10.6.0, a zero-width character (U+200B, U+200C, U+200D or U+FEFF) directly after a Maiyamok that the JDK word BreakIterator keeps attached to its word (on JDK 25 for example จริงๆ, บ่อยๆ, ดีๆ, อื่นๆ, but not เร็วๆ or มากๆ) makes a phrase or other position-sensitive highlight leave the Maiyamok out ('บ่อยๆ<b>U+200Bครับ</b>'), and in a custom HTMLStripCharFilter + tokenizer + ThaiRepeatFilter chain a tag right after the reduplicated word makes the UnifiedHighlighter cut that tag ('<i><b>บ่อยๆ</i</b><b>></b>'); deterministic at any index size, with -ea and -da, in unreleased code only (main, branch_10x and branch_10_6).
Summary
You analyze 'บ่อยๆ' + U+200B + 'ครับ' (a word written with the repetition mark ๆ, then a zero-width space) with the stock ThaiAnalyzer and expect what happens without the zero-width space, and what the filter's CHANGES entry promises ('exact OffsetAttribute and highlighter compatibility'): the base token บ่อย covers the word [0,4) and the repeated token บ่อย covers the ๆ [4,5). Instead the base token gets [0,5), which includes the ๆ, and the repeated token gets [5,6), which is only the removed zero-width space: ThaiRepeatFilter assumes that the last characters of the token's offset span are its trailing ๆ, but the CharFilter's offset correction put the removed character at the end of that span. With HTMLStripCharFilter the repeated token points at the last character of the tag after the word instead, so a highlighter writes its markup inside the tag; terms and positions are correct and nothing fails.
