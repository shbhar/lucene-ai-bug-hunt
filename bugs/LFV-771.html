A valid LatLon distance query that stops just short of a pole can fail with "invalid longitude NaN"

For a few valid circles that stop a few centimetres short of a pole, LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery throws IllegalArgumentException: invalid longitude NaN: the bounding-box code takes asin of 1.0000000000000002, a rounding of a value just below 1. The inputs are contrived; the fix is a one-line clamp.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

The circle's bounding box takes asin of a ratio of two fast cosines; at one of SloppyMath's table switches the ratio rounds just above 1, and asin of more than 1 is NaN.

Background: the bounding box of a distance query

#
  1. A LatLon distance query first draws a latitude/longitude box around the circle (Rectangle.fromPointDistance) and uses it to skip index cells and points far away; candidates inside the box are then checked by their real distance.
  2. If the circle reaches a pole, the box spans every longitude. Otherwise its half-width in longitude is asin(sin(r) / cos(lat)), where r is the radius as an angle and lat the centre latitude. In exact arithmetic that ratio is at most 1 whenever the circle reaches neither pole.
  3. Lucene computes sin, cos and asin with SloppyMath, fast approximations (the cosine is documented as accurate to about 1e-15), not exact. asin of anything above 1 is NaN, as in Math.asin.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Index one point at (0.43945312499999994, 0) and count LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery("p", 0.43945312499999994, 0, 9958692.080819247). Centre and radius are valid: the pole is 9,958,692.15 m away, so the circle stops 7 cm short of it. You expect 1 hit, the point itself.
  2. Before searching, Lucene draws a lat/lon box around the circle. It pads the radius by 7 cm, so the box's top edge is 47 nanometres below the pole: no pole is inside, and the box's half-width in longitude is asin(sin(r) / cos(lat)). In exact arithmetic that ratio is 0.99999999999999994, just below 1.
  3. Lucene's fast cosine (a table with a step of 2pi/2048 plus a short polynomial) is not monotone at some of the points where it switches table points, here at 0.439453125 degrees. sin(r), computed as cos(r - pi/2), comes out one rounding step above cos(lat): 0.9999705864309741 vs 0.999970586430974.
  4. The quotient is 1.0000000000000002, asin returns NaN, the box's longitudes are NaN, and the search throws IllegalArgumentException: invalid longitude NaN; must be between -180.0 and 180.0. The same centre with the radius 1 m smaller or larger works.

Who is affected

#

Any application running a LatLon circle query on user-supplied coordinates: LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery (default configuration, not experimental), newGeometryQuery with a Circle, LatLonDocValuesField.newSlowDistanceQuery. Only for a centre within about 1e-13 degree below one of five latitudes (0.44, 0.79, 2.02, 2.55, 12.22 degrees, or their southern mirrors) and a radius within about 1e-7 m of "distance to the pole minus 7 cm". Contrived.

Mechanism, terms and numbers547 words

Before a LatLon distance query runs, Rectangle.fromPointDistance draws a latitude/longitude box around the circle. When the circle, padded by 7 cm, reaches neither pole, it computes the box's half-width in longitude as asin(sin(radDistance) / cos(radLat)) (Rectangle.java:157) with no clamp. The quotient is at most 1 in exact arithmetic, but sin, cos and asin are SloppyMath's fast versions, and SloppyMath.cos is not monotone where it switches table entries. For the circle centred at (0.43945312499999994, 0) with radius 9,958,692.080819247 m, which stops 7 cm short of the north pole, the quotient comes out 1.0000000000000002 (StrictMath: 0.9999999999999999), asin returns NaN, and the Rectangle constructor throws IllegalArgumentException: invalid longitude NaN; must be between -180.0 and 180.0. LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery therefore fails at search time, and LatLonDocValuesField.newSlowDistanceQuery when built, although their javadoc accepts every valid centre and finite, non-negative radius. No wrong hits and nothing corrupted; the inputs are contrived. It dates from LUCENE-6908 (5.5.0, 2015). Fix: asin(Math.min(1, ...)), the clamp SloppyMath.haversinMeters already uses.

The bug, step by step (file:line at 645d4fe14a20)

#
  1. LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery accepts the query: the constructor checks only a non-null field, a finite non-negative radius and the centre (LatLonPointDistanceQuery.java:57-71). At search, createWeight calls Rectangle.fromPointDistance(latitude, longitude, radiusMeters) (:83).
  2. fromPointDistance pads the radius by 7 cm, radDistance = (radiusMeters + 7E-2) / EARTH_MEAN_RADIUS_METERS (Rectangle.java:150), and takes the non-pole branch when minLat > MIN_LAT_RADIANS && maxLat < MAX_LAT_RADIANS (:156). For the repro the padded top edge is 7.3e-15 rad (47 nm) below the pole, so this branch runs.
  3. double deltaLon = asin(sin(radDistance) / cos(radLat)); (:157), with asin, sin and cos imported from SloppyMath (:31-33). SloppyMath.sin(a) is cos(a - PIO2) (SloppyMath.java:203-205), so the ratio is cos(0.007669903939435452) / cos(0.007669903939428205). The numerator's angle is 7.2e-15 rad larger, so exactly the ratio is below 1.
  4. SloppyMath.cos (SloppyMath.java:92-114) rounds the angle to the nearest table point (a step of 2pi/2048; 2049 entries), index = (int) (a * SIN_COS_INDEXER + 0.5) (:100), and adds a 4th-order Taylor correction. The two angles straddle the switch between points 2 and 3, at 2.5 x 2pi/2048 = 0.007669903939428206 rad (0.439453125 degrees). The correction from point 3 lands one ulp higher: 0.9999705864309741 against 0.999970586430974. The documented error, "around 1E-15" (:80), still holds; monotonicity is not promised.
  5. The ratio is 1.0000000000000002; SloppyMath.asin returns NaN above 1, as documented (:124-125, code :171-176). minLon and maxLon become NaN, and new Rectangle(...) (Rectangle.java:174-178) rejects them in GeoUtils.checkLongitude (Rectangle.java:88, GeoUtils.java:98-100).

Applications that run a LatLon circle query on a user-supplied centre and radius: LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery, LatLonPoint.newGeometryQuery(INTERSECTS, Circle), LatLonDocValuesField.newSlowDistanceQuery and LatLonShape.newGeometryQuery with a Circle. Default codec, writer and searcher.
  • What it takes. A centre latitude within about 1e-14 to 1e-13 degree below one of five latitudes, k x 360/4096 degrees for k = 5, 9, 23, 29, 139 (0.44, 0.79, 2.02, 2.55, 12.22 degrees), together with a radius within about 1e-7 m of "distance to the pole minus 7 cm". The repro's centre is one double (5.6e-17 degree) below 0.439453125; in a scan of the 4,097 doubles at and below each latitude the throwing centres reach at most 5.6e-14, 1.45e-13, 9.7e-14, 9.3e-15 and 1.07e-14 degree below. The southern mirrors fail too (the repro's centre negated throws the same). 0 of 2,000,000 random legal circles throw, drawn as TestGeoUtils does (latitude in [-90, 90], longitude in [-180, 180], radius in [0, 50,000 km)); an independent run with radius in [0, 20,000 km) also found 0 of 2,000,000. The five latitudes lie on LatLonPoint's 32-bit encoding grid (step 4.2e-8 degree) and the exact knot passes, so a centre decoded from an indexed LatLonPoint cannot trigger it (the repro's indexed point decodes to 0.43945308309048414, which gives a box); the repro itself passes the raw double as the query centre.
  • What happens. That one query fails with an IllegalArgumentException that blames a longitude the caller never passed, before any index data is read.
  • What does not happen. No wrong hits, no corruption, no hang, no effect on other searches or on the index (a larger query that contains such a clause, for example a BooleanQuery, fails as a whole).
  • Why fix it anyway. It is a one-line guard, and the unclamped ratio is fragile in general: with Math.sin/Math.cos instead of SloppyMath the same formula goes above 1 in 450 of 1,280,000 near-pole test cases (OpenJDK 25, Linux aarch64, JIT intrinsics).

Why it looks unintended

#
  • newDistanceQuery documents IllegalArgumentException only for a null field, invalid coordinates or an invalid radius (LatLonPoint.java:293-294); none holds here.
  • The non-pole branch appears intended to keep the asin argument at most 1; SloppyMath.haversinMeters clamps the same hazard with asin(Math.min(1, ...)) (SloppyMath.java:58).
  • TestGeoUtils builds boxes for random legal circles and expects a box every time.
The full argument228 words
  • Contract. LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery: "@param radiusMeters maximum distance from the center in meters: must be non-negative and finite", "@throws IllegalArgumentException if {@code field} is null, location has invalid coordinates, or radius is invalid." (LatLonPoint.java:290-294); the same for LatLonDocValuesField.newSlowDistanceQuery (LatLonDocValuesField.java:204-208). fromPointDistance checks only the centre (Rectangle.java:145-146): "Compute Bounding Box for a circle using WGS-84 parameters" (:142).
  • The guard shows the intent. The non-pole branch (:156) appears intended to keep asin's argument at most 1 (no comment says so, but in exact arithmetic that is what the guard ensures); rounding breaks that at the boundary.
  • Lucene clamps the same hazard elsewhere. SloppyMath.haversinMeters: asin(Math.min(1, Math.sqrt(sortKey * 0.5))) (SloppyMath.java:58). spatial3d Tools.safeAcos: "Java acos yields a NAN if you take an arc-cos of an angle that's just a tiny bit greater than 1.0, so here's a more resilient version" (Tools.java:27-38). XYRectangle.fromPointDistance rounds its box up "to avoid numerical errors" (LUCENE-9243, XYRectangle.java:79-81). The 7 cm pad in this method (Rectangle.java:149-150, comment "LUCENE-7143") is the same kind of guard.
  • Tests. TestGeoUtils.testRandomCircleToBBox calls fromPointDistance on random legal circles with radii up to 50,000 km and expects a box every time (TestGeoUtils.java:34-51); it even carries "TODO: randomly quantize radius too, to provoke exact math errors?" (:49). No test expects an exception for a valid circle.
The devil's-advocate check179 words

The strongest case for "intended" (the study's intended-behaviour verifier and cross-model reviewers): SloppyMath trades accuracy for speed on purpose ("This is performance sensitive, check SloppySinBenchmark and RectangleBenchmark", SloppyMath.java:183); its asin is specified to return NaN above 1 and TestSloppyMath asserts asin(2) is NaN (TestSloppyMath.java:95); Circle is @lucene.experimental and says "For very large circles or circles near poles, consider using polygons instead" (Circle.java:33); the Rectangle class notes that rectangles near poles are distorted (Rectangle.java:63-64); and the witness needs nanometre-scale tuning.

Why it fails: SloppyMath keeps its own contract (one ulp is within "around 1E-15"), which is exactly why the caller must keep asin's argument in range, as haversinMeters does. The pole notes come from a docs-only change (#14534) and are accuracy advice, not a precondition that permits an exception. LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery is not experimental and documents no such case. A Math.min leaves the speed trade-off untouched. The argument supports low priority, not intended behaviour; every verifier rated it weak. Verdict: not intended, a defect. Not security: a clean exception on one query.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Clamp the asin argument to 1 (one line)

Clamp the ratio: asin(Math.min(1, sin(radDistance) / cos(radLat))) at Rectangle.java:157, the idiom SloppyMath.haversinMeters already uses. It is conservative (asin(1) = pi/2 is at least the exact half-width) and the box only pre-filters, so hits do not change. With it the repro passes (assertions on and off), as do 433 geo and LatLon tests and the core module's static checks.

Trade-offs

  • Conservative: SloppyMath.asin(1) is exactly pi/2 (SloppyMath.java:172-173), at least the exact half-width (89.99999914622636 degrees here, 90.0 with the clamp), so the box grows by a hair and still contains the circle.
  • The box is only a pre-filter; LatLonPointDistanceQuery then checks each candidate's distance (LatLonPointDistanceQuery.java:115, :187), so hits do not change.
  • Cost: one Math.min per box (not measured). Low-risk rather than proven risk-free: no regression in the validation suites listed under "What the checks validated", which are not the full test suite.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal: while checking SloppyMath's documented error bounds, an AI tool found the table switches of SloppyMath.cos where a larger angle gets a larger cosine, evaluated the box's asin argument for every input pair in a bounded window around each switch, and replayed the first hit at each switch through Rectangle.fromPointDistance. SMT solvers timed out on the same question.

The method in full194 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation of the stock main jar (study unit on SloppyMath and geo distance error bounds, main a9779ef3c193, 2026-10-27).

  • Step 1: a scan of every SloppyMath.cos table switch in [0, pi] for the two doubles on either side, looking for a cosine that goes up as the angle grows: 12 such switches.
  • Step 2: around each of the 512 switches in [0, pi/2], an exhaustive window scan of the ratio sin(radDistance) / cos(radLat) at Rectangle.java:157 for circles that stay in the non-pole branch, evaluated with the same public SloppyMath.sin/SloppyMath.cos calls; any ratio above 1 is a witness, and the first one at each switch was replayed through Rectangle.fromPointDistance. The south side was not scanned (it is the mirror image).
  • SMT encodings of the same branch (QF_FP, with Z3 4.12.2 and cvc5 1.4.2) returned unknown or timed out, so the witness comes from the executed scans, not from a solver model.
  • The finder then wrote a public-API JUnit with controls and the clamp check; an independent verifier found its own witness with its own scan (487 failing legal pairs in 49M). The one-test minimal repro was reduced from them.

History

#

Introduced by LUCENE-6908 (92b659ab9e1a / 3a189ff68dec, 2015-12-07, first released in 5.5.0), which switched this formula from StrictMath to SloppyMath trig. There never was a clamp here; the failure came in with that change (bounded scans of 5.4.1 found no failure); 18 releases from 5.5.0 to 10.6.0 and main were executed and all fail.

Versions tested6 rows

Introduced by LUCENE-6908 (migrated issue #7966, Nick Knize, JIRA patch era): 92b659ab9e1a on trunk, 3a189ff68dec on branch_5x, both 2015-12-07. Its diff replaces StrictMath.asin(StrictMath.sin(radDistance) / StrictMath.cos(radLat)) with the SloppyMath versions and adds sin(a) = cos(a - PIO2). The unclamped ratio itself is older (d369057766ae, LUCENE-6780, 2015-10, sandbox, StrictMath), but that form threw for no input in the scans.

Later commits moved the code and kept the formula: LUCENE-6997 (to lucene-spatial), LUCENE-7123 (GeoUtils.sloppySin), LUCENE-7147 (the 7 cm pad, 6.1.0; it only moves the witness radius), LUCENE-7163 (core Rectangle.fromPointDistance, 6.1.0), LUCENE-7314 (LatLonPoint in core, 7.5.0), #14516 (renamed to SloppyMath.sin, 10.3.0). SloppyMath.cos and asin have the same bodies from 5.5.0 to main.

LFV-771, snapshot of 2026-11-02

Registry title
Rectangle.fromPointDistance throws 'invalid longitude NaN' for some legal circles whose edge stops just short of a pole, so LatLon distance queries on them fail with IllegalArgumentException
Area
core / geo (Rectangle.fromPointDistance circle bounding box, used by LatLon distance and Circle queries)
Priority
Low, rank 938: It is a loud IllegalArgumentException for contrived, ulp-precise legal circles in core LatLon distance queries (0 of 2M random circles hit it), the same valid-input-rejected boundary profile as LFV-219, in core code rather than spatial3d like LFV-742.
Impact class
crash
Impact note
Hits applications that run a LatLon circle query with the default configuration (LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery and LatLonPoint.newGeometryQuery(INTERSECTS, Circle) fail at search time in LatLonPointDistanceQuery.createWeight; LatLonDocValuesField.newSlowDistanceQuery and LatLonShape.newGeometryQuery with a Circle fail when the query is built), but only for contrived legal circles: a centre latitude about 1e-14 to 1e-13 degree below one of five dyadic latitudes (0.44, 0.79, 2.02, 2.55 or 12.22 degrees, or their southern mirrors) together with a radius within about 1e-7 m of 'distance to the pole minus 7 cm'; the valid query is then rejected with IllegalArgumentException 'invalid longitude NaN' (no wrong hits, nothing corrupted; 0 of 2,000,000 random legal circles hit it, and a centre decoded from an indexed point cannot), in every release from 5.5.0 to 10.6.0 and on main [exec, -ea and -da].
Summary
You run LatLonPoint.newDistanceQuery("p", 0.43945312499999994, 0, 9958692.080819247), a valid centre and a finite, non-negative radius whose circle stops about 7 cm short of the north pole, and expect it to match the two indexed points inside the circle. Instead IndexSearcher.count throws IllegalArgumentException 'invalid longitude NaN; must be between -180.0 and 180.0' from Rectangle.fromPointDistance, and LatLonDocValuesField.newSlowDistanceQuery throws the same when it is built. The bounding box computes asin(sin(r) / cos(lat)) without a clamp, and SloppyMath.cos, which is not monotone across its lookup-table switches, makes the quotient 1.0000000000000002 although its exact value is below 1, so asin returns NaN.
Introduced
92b659ab9e1a (trunk) / 3a189ff68dec (branch_5x, shipped in 5.5.0): LUCENE-6908 (migrated as #7966) by nknize, which replaced StrictMath.asin/sin/cos with SloppyMath in the non-pole branch deltaLon = asin(sin(radDistance) / cos(radLat)) and added sin(a) = cos(a - pi/2) [read: git log -S and the blame chain of Rectangle.java:157 in the history clone; exec: 5.4.1 passes, 5.5.0 fails]. The unclamped quotient itself is older (d369057766ae, LUCENE-6780, 2015-10-21, sandbox, StrictMath trig), but that form threw for no input in the bounded scans. Later commits moved or renamed the code and kept the formula: 665041c52f08 (LUCENE-6997, to lucene-spatial), ce3114233bdc (LUCENE-7123, GeoUtils.sloppySin), 6c219e99e4b / 951563aa427 (LUCENE-7163, core Rectangle.fromPointDistance, 6.1.0) and 3fcc87752a42 (#14516, renamed to SloppyMath.sin, 10.3.0); 045659533cd (LUCENE-7147, the 7 cm pad, 6.1.0) only moved the witness radius. Never clamped: not a regression (2015-12-07)
