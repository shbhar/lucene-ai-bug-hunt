CheckIndex's cross-level impacts check never re-tests an impact that missed, so one extra higher-level entry hides it

CheckIndex requires every impact (freq, norm) on a lower level to be covered by one on the level above, but after a miss it steps the higher level forward and never re-tests the miss: level 1 [(1,1),(2,2)] passes over level 0 [(1,1),(5,5)], while [(1,1)] alone is rejected.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

CheckIndex's check that each higher impact level covers the level below gives up on an uncovered entry whenever the higher level still has another entry.

Background: impact levels and the check

#
  1. For each term, a postings format exposes impacts: for a range of documents, the (freq, norm) pairs that could score best. Top-k queries use them to skip blocks that cannot beat the current k-th hit.
  2. Impacts come in levels: level 0 covers a small range, higher levels cover larger ranges that include it. CheckIndex requires each pair on level N-1 to be covered by a pair on level N with freq at least as high and norm at most as high.
  3. CheckIndex.checkImpacts checks this rule ("Make sure that impacts at level N trigger better scores than an impactsLevel N-1"); CheckIndex and Lucene's codec test framework call it.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Hand CheckIndex.checkImpacts two impact levels, both valid for every document: level 0 [(1,1),(5,5)] and level 1 [(1,1),(2,2)]. No level-1 pair has freq 5 or more, so nothing on level 1 covers (5,5).
  2. You expect the error CheckIndex already has for this case: "Found impact (5,5) on level 0 but no impact on level 1 triggers a better score". With level 1 [(1,1)] it does throw exactly that.
  3. You get no error. (1,1) is covered by (1,1). (5,5) is not covered by (1,1), but level 1 still has an entry, so the scan moves its level-1 cursor to (2,2) and goes on to the next level-0 pair.
  4. There is none, so (5,5) is never compared with (2,2). A miss is reported only when it finds the higher level used up. Same with assertions on and off on main, and with assertions off on every release tested from 8.0.0 to 10.6.0 (8.0.0, 8.11.4, 9.12.3, 10.3.2, 10.4.0, 10.5.2, 10.6.0).

Who is affected

#

Authors of custom or future postings formats who rely on CheckIndex at level 2 or 3, TestUtil.checkIndex, or the codec test framework to catch a higher impact level that under-states the one below, the shape that could let top-k pruning skip a competitive hit. Lucene's own writers never produce such levels, so no index or search result changes.

Mechanism, terms and numbers422 words

For each term, a postings format exposes impacts on one or more levels: the (freq, norm) pairs that bound the best score in each level's document range. Higher levels cover larger ranges, so CheckIndex.checkImpacts requires every impact on level N-1 to be dominated by one on level N (freq at least as high, unsigned norm at most as high). Its scan walks level N-1 once with a single cursor into level N. When an impact is not dominated and level N still has entries, the cursor moves one step and the loop goes on to the next lower-level impact without re-testing the one that missed. So level 0 [(1,1),(5,5)] with level 1 [(1,1),(2,2)] passes, although no level-1 impact has freq 5 or more, while level 1 [(1,1)] is rejected. It has been so since 8.0.0. No Lucene writer produces such levels: the gap only weakens CheckIndex and the codec test framework as checks for a custom or future postings format.

Through CheckIndex.testPostings

#

The same levels reach the scan through the public CheckIndex.testPostings: an independent integration test indexed two documents, a and a a a a a (so term a has (freq, norm) (1,1) and (5,5)), and wrapped the reader in a FilterCodecReader whose postings reader (CodecReader.getPostingsReader, @lucene.internal) reports these two levels, both valid to NO_MORE_DOCS. It does not write and reopen a custom postings format. status.error is null on main, 10.6.0, 10.5.2 and 9.12.3. With BM25 the real (5,5) scores 0.13406, above level 1's best bound (2,2) at 0.12574, so level 1 under-states document 1. No wrong top-k result was reproduced; the numbers only show that level 1's bound is too low.

Exposure and likelihood280 words
  • Where the scan runs. CheckIndex.testPostings, from level 2 (CheckIndex.java:1114-1125): its score-block and advance walks run, at level 2, for terms with docFreq > 1024 and for every 1024th term, and at level 3 for every term (:2090-2094); level 3 adds a walk over every document (:2108). Each walk calls checkImpacts again whenever it moves past the range it last checked (:2128, :2173, :2215). testPostings(reader, infoStream), TestUtil.checkIndex and TestUtil.checkReader use level 3. The codec test framework calls it too: AssertingLeafReader on every getImpacts() and RandomPostingsTester in its walks.
  • Not default. The command-line default is level 1 (Level.DEFAULT_VALUE, :4673, :4742-4757); a CheckIndex created in code runs at level 0 until setLevel is called (:499, :505-508). Neither level runs the postings checks.
  • No Lucene-written index is affected. Lucene104PostingsWriter builds level 1 from level 0 (:482), and SimpleTextSkipWriter does the same (SimpleTextSkipWriter.java:92), so their levels always dominate; with the fix, every Lucene postings-format test class passes.
  • The framework is not blind in general. RandomPostingsTester also checks each document's (freq, norm) against the level it selects (RandomPostingsTester.java:1355-1368); only this structural check is lax. For an under-stated norm it is CheckIndex's only check: level 1 [(1,1),(5,6)] over level 0 [(5,5)] is accepted too.
  • The API surface. A custom postings format implements PostingsFormat, FieldsProducer and ImpactsEnum, which are public but marked @lucene.experimental (PostingsFormat.java:39, FieldsProducer.java:27, ImpactsEnum.java:22). The testPostings test injects its levels through CodecReader.getPostingsReader, which is @lucene.internal (CodecReader.java:66-71). No wrong top-k result was reproduced.
  • No scale needed: two levels of two entries each.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • CheckIndex states the rule itself: "Make sure that impacts at level N trigger better scores than an impactsLevel N-1" (CheckIndex.java:2633).
  • It rejects (5,5) against level 1 [(1,1)] but accepts it against [(1,1),(2,2)]: one more entry that also misses cannot satisfy a dominance rule.
  • Nothing documents the scan as partial, and the fixed scan is still one linear pass.
The full argument173 words
  • The rule is CheckIndex's own: the comment at :2633 and the message "Found impact ... on level N-1 but no impact on level N triggers a better score" (:2656-2671).
  • It is enforced for one higher-level shape and not the other: a miss is reported only if level N has no entry left to step to; one more level-N entry that also misses hides it.
  • The repro's level 1 is unsound by the Impacts javadoc too, in the testPostings form: both levels are valid to NO_MORE_DOCS, level 0 is exactly the postings, and getImpacts must "trigger scores that are greater than or equal to the impacts that actually appear in postings" (Impacts.java:42-43). The javadoc does not in general require a higher level to dominate a looser lower one; the claim rests on CheckIndex's rule plus this.
  • No sign of intent: in #9190 (LUCENE-8142) nobody discussed the cross-level check; #15151, which rewrote the loop, has no review comment on CheckIndex; no upstream test calls checkImpacts directly.
The devil's-advocate check130 words

The strongest case, made by an executed cross-model review (OpenAI GPT-5.6): the inputs break the Impacts contract, they come in through test hooks (checkImpacts is "For use in tests only", @lucene.internal, CheckIndex.java:2562-2567; the testPostings form overrides the @lucene.internal CodecReader.getPostingsReader), Lucene's writers cannot produce them, and CheckIndex need not diagnose every contract violation of a custom codec.

Why it fails: detecting a producer whose impacts break that contract is exactly what this scan is for. Its own error message names the case, and it does reject it when level 1 has one entry. The hooks are only test scaffolding: checkImpacts runs from the public CheckIndex.testPostings, and a custom PostingsFormat can return the same Impacts through the codec API, which is public but @lucene.experimental (read, not executed).

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Advance in a loop until the lower-level impact is dominated (validated)

Replace the single step with a loop: while the current level-(N-1) impact is not dominated, throw if level N is used up, else advance (CheckIndex.java:2652-2674; 21 insertions, 22 deletions). Still linear, because both lists are sorted. The repro passes with assertions on and off; TestCheckIndex and every Lucene postings-format test class pass (0 failures).

          // advance on level N until an impact dominates this impact of level N-1
          while (prevFreq > freq || Long.compareUnsigned(prevNorm, norm) < 0) {
            if (index >= size) {
              throw new CheckIndexException(/* same message as today */);
            }
            freq = freqs[index];
            norm = norms[index];
            index++;
          }

A. Advance in a loop until the lower-level impact is dominated (validated) (recommended)

#

At CheckIndex.java:2652-2674, the if (dominated) continue; plus the one-step advance becomes a while (not dominated) loop that throws the same message when level N is used up. 1 file, 21 insertions, 22 deletions.

          // advance on level N until an impact dominates this impact of level N-1
          while (prevFreq > freq || Long.compareUnsigned(prevNorm, norm) < 0) {
            if (index >= size) {
              throw new CheckIndexException(/* same message as today */);
            }
            freq = freqs[index];
            norm = norms[index];
            index++;
          }

Trade-offs

  • Exact and linear. Both lists are sorted by increasing freq and norm (Impacts.java:39-40). An entry skipped because its freq is too low for one lower-level impact is too low for every later one; an entry that fails on norm is followed only by higher norms, so the loop runs out and throws. A skipped entry is never the only dominator of a later impact.
  • Relies on sortedness, which the per-level check just above is meant to enforce and which has its own gap (LFV-1272). Fixing both restores the full check.

  • The suites the name filter missed, with the same fix (standalone runs, assertions on, seed 5EED1273): TestOrdsBlockTree, TestAssertingPostingsFormat, backward-codecs TestLucene101PostingsFormat, TestLucene101PostingsFormatV0, TestLucene90PostingsFormat (163 tests, 153 passed, 10 skipped by assumptions), and TestSimpleTextPostingsFormat with -Dtests.nightly=true (30 tests, 29 passed, 1 skipped): 0 failures.

B. Check every pair (not run)

#

For each level-(N-1) impact, search all level-N impacts for a dominator.

Trade-offs

Does not rely on sortedness, but costs O(n·m) per call, and checkImpacts is called many times per postings list in CheckIndex's walks and on every getImpacts() under AssertingLeafReader.

C. Document the scan as best-effort (not recommended)

#

Say in the comment that the cross-level check is partial.

Trade-offs

Keeps a check that reports nothing for exactly the case its own message names.

Downstream note

#

Products or plugins that ship their own postings format and vet it with CheckIndex or Lucene's codec test framework get a clean report for a higher impact level that under-states the one below until the fix lands.

How it was found

#

A proof-obligation audit of CheckIndex.checkImpacts: its cross-level scan must reject any lower-level impact that no higher-level impact dominates. Walking the loop on a higher level with two entries showed that a missed impact is never re-tested, and a one-test JUnit repro on the stock jars witnessed it.

The method in full119 words

The obligation came from the scan's own comment and error message (CheckIndex.java:2633, :2656-2671): every level-(N-1) impact must be dominated by a level-N impact. Following the loop's two cursors through a miss showed that the code advances the higher-level cursor and then moves to the next lower-level impact, so the obligation for the missed impact is discharged only when the higher level is used up. A one-test JUnit repro then fed CheckIndex.testPostings, through a reader that reports these impacts, level 0 [(5,5)] and level 1 [(1,1),(2,2)] for a one-document index. The independent checks later moved (5,5) to second place, added the level 1 [(1,1)] control and reduced the test to the direct checkImpacts call used here.
