ThaiTokenizer drops a number written right after a period, '#' or a currency sign other than ¢

ThaiAnalyzer silently drops a number glued to a leading period, # or currency sign (any except ¢): ราคา ฿1,500 ("price ฿1,500") is indexed as just ราคา, and a document dated 1 ม.ค.2567 is not found by its year, 2567. Every release since 4.8.0 (2014), at default settings.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

Java's word splitter cuts Thai text into pieces; ThaiTokenizer then decides which pieces are words. That decision looks at one character only.

  1. Index the Thai sentence ประชุมวันที่ 1 ม.ค.2567 ("meeting on 1 Jan. 2567") with the stock ThaiAnalyzer and count TermQuery(f:2567). You expect 1 hit, as when the date is written with a space (ม.ค. 2567). You get 0.
  2. Prices too: ราคา ฿1,500 is analyzed as just [ราคา], and ราคา $100 as [ราคา]. Written ราคา 1,500 บาท, without the sign, the amount 1,500 is a term.
  3. Why: Java's word splitter keeps a period, # or currency sign (any except ¢) together with the number after it, so .2567 and ฿1,500 are one piece each. That split is correct.
  4. ThaiTokenizer then skips every piece whose first character is not a letter or digit (ThaiTokenizer.java:173-180). It looks only at that first character, so ., ฿ or # takes the whole number with it.
  5. No error, and the index is well formed. Skipping a piece only when it has no letter or digit, and starting the token at its first one, keeps 1,500 and 2567; with that change all of analysis-common (1,918 tests including 9 new ones, 18 skipped) runs with 0 failures.

Who is affected

#

Anyone indexing or searching Thai text with the stock ThaiAnalyzer, ThaiTokenizer or the thai tokenizer, at default settings. Everyday-looking text triggers it: prices with a currency sign, item numbers with #, dates like พ.ศ.2567, Latin page.42. Every occurrence is lost, whatever the index size, queries lose the number too, and recovering it needs a reindex.

Mechanism, terms and numbers535 words

ThaiAnalyzer, ThaiTokenizer and the thai tokenizer SPI silently drop a number when the JDK word BreakIterator returns it in one segment with a leading period, # or currency sign (any currency sign except the cent sign). ราคา ฿1,500 ("price ฿1,500") is analyzed as [ราคา], the Buddhist Era date พ.ศ.2567 as [พ, ศ], and page.42 as [page]; a document containing ประชุมวันที่ 1 ม.ค.2567 gets 0 hits for TermQuery(f:2567) instead of 1. The JDK segments correctly; ThaiTokenizer.incrementWord skips every segment whose first code point is not a letter or digit, so the whole number is thrown away. Nothing throws and the index is well formed. Every release from 4.8.0 (2014, LUCENE-4984) to 10.5.2 and main is affected (8 releases and main executed; all 107 release tags in that range contain the loop); a small change to the skip loop fixes every tested case.

Terms

#
  • Segment: a piece of text between two boundaries of the JDK word BreakIterator for Thai. Some segments are words or numbers, others are spaces or punctuation.
  • Number prefix: the JDK word rules' <pre-num> class, "the period, the number sign, and all currency symbols except the cents sign". Such a character goes into the same segment as the number after it, by the rule <pre-num>(<number><word>)*{<number>{<post-num>}}.

The bug

#
  1. ThaiAnalyzer starts its chain with new ThaiTokenizer() (ThaiAnalyzer.java:101-102); the filters after it only see what the tokenizer emits.
  2. The JDK splits พ.ศ.2567 into [พ][.][ศ][.2567], ราคา ฿1,500 into [ราคา][ ][฿1,500] and page.42 into [page][.42]. The cent sign is not a prefix: ราคา ¢50 gives [ราคา][ ][¢][50].
  3. ThaiTokenizer.incrementWord skips "non-tokens" with while (end != BreakIterator.DONE && !Character.isLetterOrDigit(Character.codePointAt(buffer, sentenceStart + start, sentenceEnd))) (ThaiTokenizer.java:173-180): it tests the first code point of the segment only.
  4. ., #, ฿ and $ are not letters or digits, so the whole segment, digits included, is skipped. The term is never created; the user-dictionary lookup (:186-207) also starts after the skip.

End to end, one document ประชุมวันที่ 1 ม.ค.2567: TermQuery(f:2567) counts 0 (expected 1); written ม.ค. 2567 it counts 1. At query time the same analyzer drops the number: the classic QueryParser turns +ราคา +฿1,500 into +body:ราคา, which matches any price.

Secondary symptoms of the same loop: a Latin word glued to a prefixed number is lost with it (#1word gives no token, because the JDK rule also absorbs (<number><word>)*); a prefix right after a digit starts its own segment, so 1#2, 1$2 and 1฿2 are analyzed as [1]; a segment that starts with a combining mark (่า, a tone mark with no base consonant, malformed Thai) gives no token.

Exposure and likelihood302 words
  • Who is hit. Users of ThaiAnalyzer, ThaiTokenizer, or the thai tokenizer through the SPI (CustomAnalyzer, factory configurations), with default settings. It is the Thai analyzer of analysis-common; outside its own package (where ThaiAnalyzer and ThaiTokenizerFactory create the tokenizer), no main Lucene code uses these classes. Downstream engines that expose Lucene's Thai analysis were not tested.
  • Everyday-looking input. A price with its sign (ราคา ฿1,500, even after a space), an item number (ข้อ #3), a decimal with a leading period (.5), a date abbreviation followed by the year without a space (พ.ศ.2567, ม.ค.67), Latin text such as page.42 or No.5. Numbers that start with a digit (1,500, 1.5) and spaced dates are not affected. How often each form occurs in real Thai text was not measured.
  • Silent and deterministic. No exception or warning; CheckIndex is clean and the stored value is intact. The loss happens in the tokenizer, one segment at a time, so it is the same for every occurrence, with -ea and -da, whatever the index size, on JREs that segment like OpenJDK 11, 17 and 25 (all three were run).
  • Both sides. Indexed documents lack the number, so term, phrase and boolean searches for it miss them. In a query the number is dropped: +ราคา +฿1,500 matches any price, and a query of only ฿1,500 matches nothing.
  • Not more than that. No crash and no corruption; indexing one document does not change another. One warm timing run, not a benchmark, showed no slowdown. At query time the dropped number can add false positives, as above.
  • Reindex. Documents indexed before a fix lack the terms; a fix changes analysis output, so it needs a CHANGES/MIGRATE note.
  • How common. Not measured on a corpus.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The loop's comment says it skips "non-tokens", and it tests letter-or-digit: digits are meant to be tokens (TestThaiAnalyzer.testDigits).
  • Java's word rules call these signs "punctuation that can occur at the beginning of a number"; only Lucene's first-character shortcut drops the number.
  • Before 4.8 ThaiAnalyzer kept these numbers; ICUTokenizer and StandardTokenizer keep them today.
The full argument300 words
  • Comment and test. // find the next set of boundaries, skipping over non-tokens (ThaiTokenizer.java:173), tested with Character.isLetterOrDigit (:176). .2567 contains four digits.
  • Upstream tests. TestThaiAnalyzer.testDigits (TestThaiAnalyzer.java:131-135) checks that ๑๒๓๔ is indexed as 1234. No explicit upstream test puts a prefix character before a number (random-text tests may generate one but do not check which characters become tokens), and no test, javadoc, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry documents the drop. With it removed (fix A), the 56 upstream Thai tests pass, and all of analysis-common, 1,918 tests including the 9 new ones (18 skipped), runs with 0 failures.
  • The JDK means these as numbers. BreakIteratorRules.java:100-102 and :136-139 (JDK 25): the prefix class is "punctuation that can occur at the beginning of a number", and the rule keeps a sequence "that starts with a number-prefix character and a number" together.
  • Where the loop comes from. It copies ICUTokenizer's // find the next set of boundaries, skipping over non-tokens (rule status 0) (ICUTokenizer.java:209-214), which skips a span with no letter or number. java.text.BreakIterator has no rule status; the first-code-point test stands in for it (our reading; the commit and issue do not explain the choice).
  • Before and beside. 4.7.2's ThaiAnalyzer (StandardTokenizer + ThaiWordFilter) gives [พ, ศ, 2567] and [ราคา, 1,500]; so does the 4.8.0 jar with matchVersion LUCENE_47. ICUTokenizer, named in the ThaiTokenizer javadoc as the portable alternative (ThaiTokenizer.java:35-37), gives [ค.ศ, 2024] and [page, 42]; StandardAnalyzer gives [page, 42]. LUCENE-4984 changed the chain to fix offsets with CharFilters; its discussion says nothing about numbers.
  • Precedent. LUCENE-2404 (2010): a committer called the old ThaiWordFilter's first-character test "cheating with the UnicodeBlock check on charAt(0)". LUCENE-5076 (open): a first-code-point-only word test in ICU-module code, called a bug by a committer.
The devil's-advocate check298 words

The case for "intended" (raised by the cross-model reviews, which rated it moderate, and examined by a third verifier, which found that it does not survive): ThaiTokenizer promises only that it "uses BreakIterator to tokenize Thai text" and warns that it may not be supported by all JREs; the JDK's own BreakIterator javadoc (the nextWordStartAfter example) calls a span a word only "If the characters between this boundary and the next boundary include at least one letter", and .2567 has none; and พ.ศ.2567 without a space is informal.

Why it fails:

  • That idiom scans every character of the span; Lucene's loop scans one. And Lucene's test is letter or digit, not letter. Together, .2567 is a token.
  • The JRE splits these inputs correctly: OpenJDK 11, 17 and 25 split every input of the table the same way. The loss is Lucene's.
  • The currency case, ราคา ฿1,500 after a space, is a plausible everyday way to write a price (not checked against a corpus), so "informal input" does not cover it.
  • "Twelve years and nobody complained": nobody discussed it or closed it as won't-fix either, and the loss is silent.

What a maintainer could still raise:

  • Prefix-free terms are not a written contract, so which terms a prefixed number gives is a design choice, and changing it changes analysis output (reindex; CHANGES and MIGRATE notes).
  • The input kinds differ: the price after a space is the hardest to call intended; a date without a space (พ.ศ.2567) and Latin or decimal forms (page.42, .5) leave more room for a style or token-policy argument.
  • Against a shared design: in the same SegmentingTokenizerBase family, HMMChineseTokenizer has no first-character test, and the test tokenizer in TestSegmentingTokenizerBase skips forward to the first letter or digit, as the fix does.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A: skip only segments with no letter or digit; start the token at the first one

In ThaiTokenizer.incrementWord, skip a word piece only if none of its characters is a letter or digit; otherwise start the token at the first one (฿1,500 -> 1,500, offsets [6,11)). +16/-4 lines; all of analysis-common (1,918 tests including the 9 new ones, 18 skipped) runs with 0 failures. Existing indexes need a reindex to gain the numbers.

All options and trade-offs3 options · 996 words

Option A was validated with gradle in a scratch worktree at ea57360912ed (JDK 25.0.3) and through the repro runner; option C on the 10.5.2, 9.12.3 and main jars; option B was not run. A: skip only segments with no letter or digit; start the token at the first one (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

Replace the first-code-point test with a scan of the segment: if no code point is a letter or digit, skip the segment as before; otherwise move start to the first letter or digit and emit from there. Results: ราคา ฿1,500 -> ราคา, 1,500 [6,11); พ.ศ.2567 -> พ, ศ, 2567 [4,8); ข้อ #3 -> 3 [5,6); page.42 -> 42 [5,7); #1word -> 1word [1,6). Punctuation-only and space segments are still skipped (ราคา - บาท -> [ราคา, บาท]), and every control is unchanged. A prefix after a digit is fixed too (1#2 -> [1, 2]). This is the finder's patch; an independent verifier wrote an equivalent one.

// ThaiTokenizer.incrementWord: replaces the loop at lines 174-180
int end = wordBreaker.next();
while (end != BreakIterator.DONE) {
  int s = start;
  while (s < end) {
    int cp = Character.codePointAt(buffer, sentenceStart + s, sentenceEnd);
    if (Character.isLetterOrDigit(cp)) {
      break;
    }
    s += Character.charCount(cp);
  }
  if (s < end) {
    start = s; // drop a leading '.', '#' or currency sign, keep the number
    break;
  }
  start = end;
  end = wordBreaker.next();
}

Trade-offs

  • New terms, so reindex. Old indexes lack the numbers. Mixing analyzers changes results: after the fix, ราคา ฿1,500 in a query analyzes to ราคา and 1,500, which an old index cannot match. Positions after a recovered number shift by one. Needs a CHANGES entry and a MIGRATE note.
  • The token excludes the prefix (1,500, not ฿1,500), as ICUTokenizer and StandardTokenizer produce for these inputs.
  • User dictionary. The lookup starts at the trimmed start: with {1,500บาท}, ฿1,500บาท gives 1,500บาท [1,9) (stock: [บาท]). An entry that itself starts with a prefix character (฿1,500) still never matches, as on stock.
  • Every leading non-letter/digit is trimmed, not only ., # and currency signs. A segment that starts with a combining mark now yields the rest: ้ำ gives ำ and ่า gives า (stock: nothing); ICUTokenizer gives the same ำ and า for these inputs. Whether a stray tone mark should leave its vowel as a token is its own question. With {แม่น}, แม่น้ำ gives [แม่น, ำ] instead of [แม่น]; that is LFV-229, which needs its own fix.
  • Cost. A segment that starts with a letter or digit costs one check, as now; a punctuation or space segment is scanned to its end (usually one character). Not benchmarked.

B: keep the whole segment as the token (not run) (validated by the checks)

Trade-offs

A smaller change in offsets, but TermQuery(f:2567) still misses พ.ศ.2567, and the terms differ from ICUTokenizer and StandardTokenizer. Not recommended.

C: workaround on released versions: split the prefix off with a char filter (validated by the checks)

#

Without patching Lucene, put a PatternReplaceCharFilter in front of the ThaiTokenizer chain that inserts a space between a prefix character and a digit, unless the prefix follows a digit (so 1.5 is untouched). Switching to ICUTokenizer (analysis-icu) also keeps the numbers, but segments Thai differently, so it is a reindex with different terms.

Pattern p = Pattern.compile("(?<!\\p{N})([.#\\p{Sc}])(?=\\p{N})");
// in Analyzer.initReader(field, reader):
return new PatternReplaceCharFilter(p, "$1 ", reader);

Trade-offs

Restores the number of every prefixed input in the table, with the same offsets as option A, and needs no Lucene change. But it is an application-side chain that must be used at index and at query time, documents already indexed need a reindex, it leaves a prefix after a digit alone (1#2, 1$2, 1฿2 still lose the 2), and it also changes the segmentation of other ./#/currency-digit pairs that follow a non-digit.

What the checks validated

On 10.5.2, 9.12.3 and main, with ThaiTokenizer + LowerCaseFilter + DecimalDigitFilter + StopFilter(ThaiAnalyzer.getDefaultStopSet()): all 11 prefixed inputs of the table get their number back with the same offsets as option A; 1.5, 50%, ราคา ¢50, ราคา 1,500 บาท and พ.ศ. 2567 are unchanged. On main it also works in front of a copy of the full stock ThaiAnalyzer chain, with ThaiCharFilter and the 10.6 ThaiNormalizationFilter and ThaiRepeatFilter; that copy without the extra char filter gives exactly ThaiAnalyzer's tokens and offsets.

How it was found

#

An AI tool exploring ThaiTokenizer with user-dictionary words that contain periods saw a year go missing, confirmed it with a public-API JUnit and then checked all 111,110 strings of up to 5 symbols. Independent verifiers and a reviewer from another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6) reproduced it with their own tests.

The method in full213 words

Method: targeted exploration, then a bounded-exhaustive check (the hunt's non-formal tier; no formal tool produced the first signal).

  • The finder explored ThaiTokenizer with user-dictionary words that contain periods (พ.ศ.), noticed that พ.ศ.2567 lost its year, and wrote a public-API JUnit: 3 of 4 tests fail on main with -ea and -da, and on 10.5.2 and 9.12.3.
  • It then enumerated all 111,110 strings of 1 to 5 symbols over {ก, า, 1, x, '.', ',', '-', space, U+0E48, U+0E4C} with the oracle "every letter or digit lies inside some token": 13,107 strings lose one on main (3,927 after a period, the rest after a tone or cancellation mark), 0 with the fix. The alphabet has no # or currency sign, and the oracle checks coverage, not that the tokens are right. That oracle is stronger than anything Lucene documents; the verdict rests on the number cases.
  • An independent reviewer from another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6) ran its own checks (release.2026 gives [release], ปี.2026 gives [ปี]). Claude verifiers wrote their own tests: the independent reproduction (ค.ศ.2024, ม.ค.67, page.42, a search) and the intended-behaviour check (฿1,500, $100, #3), which also found the JDK's number-prefix rule. An adjudication re-ran both with fresh seeds.

Bug record

#

LFV-225, snapshot of 2026-10-28

Registry title
ThaiTokenizer and ThaiAnalyzer silently drop a number written directly after a period, '#' or currency sign (2567 in 'พ.ศ.2567', 1,500 in 'ราคา ฿1,500', 42 in 'page.42'), because a word segment is skipped whole when its first character is not a letter or digit
Area
analysis / Thai (ThaiTokenizer#incrementWord skip loop; ThaiAnalyzer and the 'thai' tokenizer SPI)
Priority
Medium, rank 30: Since 4.8.0 the stock ThaiAnalyzer and ThaiTokenizer silently drop numbers written after a period, '#' or currency sign (baht amounts, Thai dates written without a space), ordinary Thai text that needs a reindex once fixed, limited to Thai-language indexes.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Anyone who indexes or searches Thai text with the stock ThaiAnalyzer, ThaiTokenizer or the 'thai' tokenizer SPI at default settings silently loses every number that the JDK word BreakIterator returns in one segment with a leading period, '#' or currency sign (every currency sign except the cent sign): Thai dates written without a space ('พ.ศ.2567', 'ค.ศ.2024', 'ม.ค.67'), amounts even after a space ('ราคา ฿1,500', 'ราคา $100'), item numbers ('ข้อ #3'), leading-period decimals ('.5') and Latin text such as 'page.42' or 'No.5'; a TermQuery for the number then misses the document, deterministically at any index size, with -ea and -da, in every release from 4.8.0 (2014) to 10.5.2, while numbers that start with a digit ('1.5', '1,500') and spaced dates ('พ.ศ. 2567') are unaffected, nothing throws and the index stays well formed.
Summary
You index Thai text such as 'ประชุมวันที่ 1 ม.ค.2567' or 'ราคา ฿1,500' with the stock ThaiAnalyzer and expect the year 2567 or the amount 1,500 to be searchable, as they are when written 'พ.ศ. 2567' or 'ราคา 1,500 บาท'. Instead the number never reaches the index: the JDK word BreakIterator returns it together with its leading period or currency sign as one segment ('.2567', '฿1,500'), and ThaiTokenizer skips any segment whose first character is not a letter or digit, so the whole number is thrown away. A TermQuery for 2567 then finds 0 documents instead of 1, with no error.
