ExitableDirectoryReader's vector values forward scorer() but not rescorer(), so full-precision rescoring of a quantized field returns the quantized scores

Through ExitableDirectoryReader, even with a timeout that never fires, rescorer() and the full-precision rescoring APIs return the quantized scores: a document whose exact score is 1/7 = 0.1429 gets 0.2234. In a 300-document probe with 1-bit codes only 5 of the exact top 10 survived rescoring; affects 10.4.0 to 10.6.0 and main.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

A timeout wrapper should not change scores, but this one does: read through ExitableDirectoryReader, exact rescoring of a quantized vector field silently uses the quantized codes instead of the raw vectors.

Background in three steps

#
  1. A quantized vector field (for example Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsFormat) stores every vector twice: small codes, here 1 bit per dimension, and the raw floats.
  2. FloatVectorValues#scorer(query) scores against the codes, for the fast first pass. FloatVectorValues#rescorer(query) scores against the most exact data the format has, the raw floats. FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource and RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery use rescorer().
  3. ExitableDirectoryReader wraps a reader to add timeout checks. It hands out its own vector-values wrappers, which pass each call through to the real ones.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. You index vectors with a quantized format, here Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsFormat with 1-bit codes: two documents, 4 dimensions, EUCLIDEAN.
  2. You open the index with ExitableDirectoryReader.wrap(reader, () -> false), a timeout that never fires, and rescore with FloatVectorValues#rescorer(query) or RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery.
  3. You expect the exact score from the raw floats, as the plain reader gives: doc 0 = {4, -1, -4, 4}, query {3, -2, -2, 4}, squared distance 1 + 1 + 4 + 0 = 6, EUCLIDEAN score 1/(1+6) = 0.14285715.
  4. Instead you get 0.22344624, the 1-bit codes' estimate. The wrapper forwards scorer() (codes) but not rescorer() (raw floats), and the inherited default rescorer() just calls scorer().
  5. No error. In a 300-document probe, 1-bit codes kept only 5 of the exact top 10 (2-bit: 7 of 10); with the 8-bit default the scores were off by at most 0.0016 and the top 10 was unchanged.

Who is affected

#

Applications that combine a quantized vector format (opt-in), full-precision rescoring (rescorer(), createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery or the 2-argument FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource) and ExitableDirectoryReader. Every full-precision rescoring through the wrapper, any index size, since 10.4.0. In a 300-document probe the exact top 10 changed with 1- and 2-bit codes and not with the 8-bit default. IndexSearcher#setTimeout is not affected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers587 words

A scalar-quantized vector field keeps two copies of each vector: small codes, which FloatVectorValues#scorer scores against, and the raw floats, which FloatVectorValues#rescorer (added in 10.4.0 by #15176) scores against. ExitableDirectoryReader, one of Lucene's two timeout mechanisms, wraps every vector-values object it hands out in ExitableFloatVectorValues, ExitableFloat16VectorValues or ExitableByteVectorValues. These forward scorer(), prefetch and copy() but not rescorer(), so they inherit the base-class default rescorer(t) = scorer(t) and return the quantized scorer. FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource (2-argument constructor) and RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery, both documented to use the full-precision vectors, call rescorer() and so return quantized scores too, with no error, even when the timeout never fires.

In the minimal test (2 documents, 4 dimensions, 1-bit codes, EUCLIDEAN) doc 0 scores 0.22344624 instead of the exact 1/7 = 0.14285715. Over 300 random 16-dimension documents, rescoring through the wrapper kept 5 of the exact top 10 with 1-bit codes and 7 of 10 with 2-bit codes; with the 8-bit default the values were off by at most 0.0016 and the top 10 was unchanged. Releases 10.4.0 to 10.6.0 and main are affected; in 10.3.x the full-precision values source rescored exactly through the wrapper (rescorer() itself is new in 10.4.0). The fix is three one-line overrides that forward rescorer() like scorer(); the test passes with it and the nearby core suites stay green.

The mechanism (main 5adff78bb168)

#
  1. The format splits the two scorers on purpose. Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsReader.ScalarQuantizedVectorValues answers scorer(query) with quantizedVectorValues.scorer(query) and rescorer(target) with rawVectorValues.rescorer(target) (Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsReader.java:799-807; FLOAT16 twin :866-874). The backward-codecs readers of Lucene99 scalar-quantized and Lucene102 binary-quantized segments override rescorer() the same way (by reading).
  2. The base class falls back. FloatVectorValues#rescorer(float[]) is return scorer(target); (FloatVectorValues.java:92-94), documented as valid "assuming that the scorer is already the highest fidelity implementation available" (:84-86). Float16VectorValues.java:92-94 and ByteVectorValues.java:91-92 are the same.
  3. The wrapper forwards scorer() and inherits rescorer(). getFloatVectorValues wraps whatever the inner reader returns in ExitableFloatVectorValues (ExitableDirectoryReader.java:334-350). That class (:499-547) forwards dimension, vectorValue, prefetch, ordToDoc, size, scorer (:536-539) and copy(), makes only iterator() check the timeout (:531-534), and has no rescorer. So wrapper.rescorer(t) = wrapper.scorer(t) = inner.scorer(t): the quantized scorer. ExitableFloat16VectorValues (:549-597, scorer(short[]) at :586-589) and ExitableByteVectorValues (:599-647, scorer(byte[]) at :636-639) are the same.
  4. The full-precision APIs call rescorer(). FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource reads ctx.reader().getFloatVectorValues(fieldName) (:64-67, the wrapper) and, when no similarity function was given, scores with fullPrecisionRescorer = rescorer(queryVector) (AbstractFullPrecisionVectorSimilarityValuesSource.java:68-78, FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource.java:79-82). RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery builds that 2-argument source (RescoreTopNQuery.java:285-290; FLOAT16 :302-307).
  5. Not affected. The 3-argument constructor with an explicit similarity function scores with compare(queryVector, vectorValue(ord)) (FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource.java:84-88), and the wrapper forwards vectorValue() (ExitableDirectoryReader.java:511-514), so it stays exact. IndexSearcher#setTimeout does not wrap the reader. For the in-tree unquantized formats rescorer() is the inherited default, scorer(), anyway.
  6. What does not change. RescoreTopNQuery#rewrite rescores only the documents its inner query matched (RescoreTopNQuery.java:67-91): candidates, filters and deletions are untouched; only the rescored values, and so the top-n order and cut, differ.

The numbers of the example

#

Query {3, -2, -2, 4}. Doc 0 = {4, -1, -4, 4}: squared distance 1 + 1 + 4 + 0 = 6, EUCLIDEAN similarity 1/(1+6) = 0.14285715; the 1-bit codes estimate 0.22344624. Doc 1 = {-4, 3, 1, -2}: squared distance 119, exact 1/120 = 0.008333334; the codes estimate 0.008161623. Through the wrapper rescorer() returns the estimates, which are exactly what scorer() returns on the plain reader.

Exposure and likelihood409 words
  • Who is hit. Applications that (a) index vectors with a quantized format (Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsFormat or Lucene104HnswScalarQuantizedVectorsFormat; by reading also older Lucene99 scalar-quantized and Lucene102 binary-quantized segments), (b) rescore with full precision (FloatVectorValues#rescorer, the 2-argument FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource, or RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery), and (c) read through ExitableDirectoryReader. Upstream's own test runs this combination and calls it "the common case" (TestFilterReadersForwardVectorPrefetch#testFullPrecisionRescoringWorksThroughExitableReader, on a Lucene104HnswScalarQuantizedVectorsFormat field), but asserts only the hit count.
  • Default vs opt-in. The default codec's vector format (Lucene99HnswVectorsFormat) is not quantized, so default users are not hit. Each piece of the trigger is a documented public API.
  • Scale. Any index size (two documents are enough); deterministic: with a timeout that never fires, every full-precision rescoring through the wrapper returns the quantized scores (a timeout that fires was not tested; it may stop the query instead).

Why it looks unintended

#
  • ExitableDirectoryReader is documented only to add timeout checks, yet with a timeout that never fires the scores change.
  • The quantized format answers rescorer() from the raw vectors on purpose (Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsReader.java:804-807), and FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource promises "the raw full-precision vectors".
  • The wrappers forward scorer(), prefetch and copy() (the last two fixed by #16705); a maintainer described them as forwarding "everything except prefetch".
The full argument336 words
  • The wrapper's contract is only timeouts. "The ExitableDirectoryReader wraps a real index DirectoryReader and allows for a QueryTimeout implementation object to be checked periodically" (ExitableDirectoryReader.java:33-38). With () -> false nothing should change.
  • The format chose the raw vectors; the wrapper undoes that by accident. The quantized reader overrides rescorer() to read the raw vectors (Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsReader.java:804-807). The default's own precondition, "assuming that the scorer is already the highest fidelity implementation available" (FloatVectorValues.java:84-86), is false for a pass-through wrapper over a quantized field.
  • The higher-level APIs promise full precision. FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource "computes vector similarity between a query vector and the raw full-precision vectors" (:28-31; not @lucene.experimental), and createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery "uses full-precision vectors for rescoring" (RescoreTopNQuery.java:276-277).
  • The introducing change wanted the opposite. #15176's commit message: "the current API always returns a scorer against the fastest fidelity vectors, not the most accurate. This commit adds a new method ... that is specifically a rescorer" (8beb3c65c4f). It moved the 2-argument values source from vectorValue() + compare, which was exact through the wrapper in 10.3.x, onto rescorer(), and did not touch ExitableDirectoryReader.
  • Maintainers expect complete forwarding. TestFilterReadersForwardVectorPrefetch says filter readers "have to forward prefetch(int, int) along with everything else" (:43-49). In the #16705 discussion jimczi described these wrappers as forwarding "everything except prefetch" and wrote "Maybe worth a test that checks each wrapper forwards, this keeps coming back" (2026-09-24).
  • No timeout reason to skip it. The forwarded scorer() returns the inner scorer with no timeout checks, so forwarding rescorer() the same way loses nothing.
  • Nothing documents the degradation: no javadoc, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry.
  • No other reader wrapper with this gap was found. A bytecode scan of the 98 vector-values subclasses in the 28 main module jars found that, among the scanned built-in classes, the three Exitable wrappers are the only non-sandbox reader wrappers that forward scorer() to wrapped values and inherit the default rescorer() (the scan sees delegation in bytecode, not runtime reachability).
The devil's-advocate check181 words

The strongest case for "works as designed": FloatVectorValues and RescoreTopNQuery are @lucene.experimental; rescorer() says it will only often use the highest fidelity, and its documented default is to call scorer(); the ExitableDirectoryReader javadoc does not promise that every vector-values method is forwarded; and Lucene's filter classes follow a convention of not overriding methods whose default is implemented through other methods (TestFilterLeafReader.java:196-200).

Why it fails: "often" describes the choice a format makes, and here the format does choose the raw vectors; the same call on the unwrapped reader is exact. The filter convention assumes the default gives the right answer through the forwarded methods; here it does not, because the forwarded scorer() is lower fidelity than the inner rescorer(). @lucene.experimental allows API changes between releases, not silently different values from an API documented as full precision, and the values source that promises "raw full-precision vectors" is not experimental. Cross-model reviews (an independent reviewer from another model family, OpenAI GPT-5.6, refute and intended lenses) rated the argument moderate and not holding; a devil's-advocate verifier and the adjudication agreed.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Forward rescorer() in the three wrappers (validated)

Forward rescorer() in ExitableFloatVectorValues, ExitableFloat16VectorValues and ExitableByteVectorValues, exactly as they forward scorer() (3 overrides, +15 lines in ExitableDirectoryReader.java). The repro passes with assertions on and off, and 9 nearby core test classes plus the repro pass (29 tests, 0 failures).

// ExitableFloatVectorValues; the same with short[] and byte[] in the FLOAT16 and byte wrappers
@Override
public VectorScorer rescorer(float[] target) throws IOException {
  return vectorValues.rescorer(target);
}
All options and trade-offs4 options · 507 words

Four options. A was built and validated on main 5adff78bb168; B to D were not built. Workaround until a fix ships: pass the similarity function explicitly to FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource, or, if its bulk-scoring-only timeout fits, use IndexSearcher#setTimeout.

A. Forward rescorer() in the three wrappers (validated) (recommended)

#

Add one override per wrapper, next to the existing scorer() forward. One file, +15 lines. Written independently in two of the hunt's checks with identical results.

// ExitableFloatVectorValues; the same with short[] and byte[] in the FLOAT16 and byte wrappers
@Override
public VectorScorer rescorer(float[] target) throws IOException {
  return vectorValues.rescorer(target);
}

Trade-offs

  • Changes behaviour only where it is wanted: for the in-tree unquantized formats rescorer() is the inherited default and already equals scorer(); for quantized fields rescoring through the wrapper becomes exact, as on the plain reader.
  • Rescoring then reads the raw vectors, as it does without the wrapper.
  • The forwarded rescorer, like the forwarded scorer, has no timeout checks of its own (unchanged; see B).
  • The byte forward fixes no in-tree symptom today (no in-tree byte format overrides rescorer()) but keeps the three wrappers consistent for other formats.
  • CHANGES: a Bug Fixes entry under the next release that gets it (10.6.0 is released).

What the checks validated

  • Gradle in a scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168, OpenJDK 25.0.3, seed DEADBEEF: stock, the repro fails with assertions on and off; with the fix it passes, on and off.
  • With the fix and the repro in place: TestExitableDirectoryReader, TestFilterReadersForwardVectorPrefetch, TestFilterLeafReader, TestFilterDirectoryReader, TestRescoreTopNQuery, TestRescoreTopNQueryPrefetch, TestRescoreTopNQueryFloat16Prefetch, TestFullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource, TestFullPrecisionFloat16VectorSimilarityValuesSource and the repro: 10 classes, 29 tests, 0 failures, 5 skipped (all @Nightly; none of them uses vector values through ExitableDirectoryReader: the one in TestExitableDirectoryReader, testExitableTermsEnumSampleTimeoutCheck, checks the terms-enum timeout sampling, and the other four do not use the wrapper).
  • checkGoogleJavaFormat, ecjLint, forbiddenApisMain and forbiddenApisTest of lucene/core pass.
  • Earlier, in the hunt's checks, the same diff made the one-test repro, an independent reproducer's 8-bit test and the adjudication's re-run pass, and four nearby suites passed 80 of 80 over 5 iterations.

B. Forward rescorer() and make the returned scorers check the timeout

#

Wrap the inner VectorScorer so its iterator checks the QueryTimeout, for both scorer() and rescorer().

Trade-offs

Hardening, not needed for this bug: it also changes the timeout behaviour of scorer() and touches bulk scoring paths.

C. Add the forwarding test a maintainer suggested in #16705

#

For each Exitable vector wrapper, compare scorer(), rescorer(), prefetch and copy() with the unwrapped values on a quantized field ("a test that checks each wrapper forwards").

Trade-offs

Complementary to A: it would have caught the prefetch and copy() gaps of #16705 and this one. The reflective TestFilterLeafReader#testOverrideMethods checks the opposite rule (do not override defaulted methods), so it is not a template here.

D. Change the base-class default

#

Make rescorer() abstract or throwing, so a wrapper cannot silently fall back to scorer().

Trade-offs

Breaks every implementation outside Lucene that relies on the default; much broader than A.

Downstream note

#

Solr wraps every searcher's reader in ExitableDirectoryReader, so Solr plugin code that rescores a quantized field with these APIs gets the quantized scores; a code search found no use of them in Solr's own code.

History

#

Introduced in 10.4.0 by #15176 (8beb3c65c4f, 2025-09-12), which added rescorer() and moved the full-precision values source onto it without touching ExitableDirectoryReader. 10.3.x rescored exactly through the wrapper; 10.4.0 to 10.6.0 and main fail. Not fixed upstream.

Versions tested8 rows

Introduced by apache/lucene#15176, "Adds new simplified rescorer API to [Float|Byte]VectorValues" (benwtrent): 8beb3c65c4f on main and its cherry-pick 6473972b9e5 on branch_10x, both with author date 2025-09-12; first released in 10.4.0. It added rescorer() with the scorer() default, overrode it with raw-vector rescorers in the quantized readers, and moved the 2-argument FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource from vectorValue(ord) + compare onto rescorer(). It did not touch ExitableDirectoryReader, whose wrappers forward scorer() since #13181 (b60e86c4b91, 2024-05-09). ec4cd9f66b1 (#15223, in 10.4.0) gave the new Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsReader the same override.

FLOAT16 twin: bfe5c847d3c (#16383, 2026-07-27) added Float16VectorValues with the same default and an ExitableFloat16VectorValues that forwards only scorer(short[]); it became observable with 50c1e6acb7f (#16473, FLOAT16 scalar quantization). main and branch_11x only.

LFV-1381, snapshot of 2026-11-05

Registry title
ExitableDirectoryReader's vector-values wrappers forward scorer() but not rescorer(), so full-precision rescoring of a quantized vector field read through ExitableDirectoryReader (even with a timeout that never fires) silently returns the quantized scores (since 10.4.0)
Area
index / ExitableDirectoryReader vector-values wrappers (ExitableFloatVectorValues, ExitableFloat16VectorValues, ExitableByteVectorValues) x search / full-precision vector rescoring (FloatVectorValues#rescorer, FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource, RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery) on scalar-quantized fields
Priority
Medium, rank 54: Full-precision rescoring of a quantized vector field read through ExitableDirectoryReader, even with a timeout that never fires, silently returns the quantized scores on every query in every release since 10.4.0, and in a small 1-bit probe half of the exact top 10 was lost. It is Medium, not High, because it needs a quantized format, full-precision rescoring and that timeout wrapper together, no major search server reaches it today, and with the 8-bit default encoding the score error is tiny.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that index vectors with a quantized format (opt-in: the Lucene104 scalar-quantized flat or HNSW formats; by reading also older Lucene99 scalar-quantized and Lucene102 binary-quantized segments), rescore with full precision (FloatVectorValues#rescorer, the 2-argument FullPrecisionFloatVectorSimilarityValuesSource or RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery) and read through ExitableDirectoryReader (one of Lucene's two timeout mechanisms; IndexSearcher#setTimeout is unaffected) get the quantized scores on every query, with no error and at any scale: in a 300-document, 16-dimension probe 1-bit codes keep only 5 of the exact top 10 (2-bit: 7 of 10), while the 8-bit default encoding is off by at most 0.0016 with the top 10 unchanged; releases 10.4.0 through 10.6.0 and main.
Summary
You index a vector field with a scalar-quantized format such as Lucene104ScalarQuantizedVectorsFormat, wrap the reader with ExitableDirectoryReader.wrap(reader, () -> false), a timeout that never fires, and ask for full-precision scores with FloatVectorValues#rescorer(query) or RescoreTopNQuery.createFullPrecisionRescorerQuery. You expect the same exact scores as the unwrapped reader gives, because the wrapper only adds timeout checks and the format's rescorer() reads the raw vectors. Instead the wrapper's vector values forward scorer() but not rescorer(), so the inherited default rescorer() calls scorer() and returns the quantized scorer: with 2 documents, 4 dimensions and 1-bit codes, doc 0 scores 0.22344624 instead of the exact 1/7 = 0.14285715, with no error.
