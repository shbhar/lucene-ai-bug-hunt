DateRangePrefixTree rejects day 32 but silently rolls a day its month does not have into the next month, so [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31] also matches December 1st

The spatial-extras DateRangePrefixTree rejects 2014-11-32 with a ParseException but reads 2014-11-31 as December 1st, so the range [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31] also matches a document dated 2014-12-01. In every release since 5.0.0; a 9-line month-length check fixes it.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

The parser reads a date string field by field and checks each number against a fixed range: month 1..12, day 1..31, hour 0..24. The day is never compared with the length of its month. A lenient java.util.Calendar then quietly turns November 31 into December 1, and that later date is what gets indexed or searched.

  1. You index three documents dated 2014-11-15, 2014-12-01 and 2014-12-02 with NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy over DateRangePrefixTree, and search with the range [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31], a natural way to write "all of November".
  2. You expect a ParseException: November has 30 days, and the same parser rejects 2014-11-32 and 2014-13-01 with one.
  3. You get no error: the range becomes [2014-11 TO 2014-12-01] and the query returns 2014-11-15 and 2014-12-01.
  4. Why: the day is checked only against 1..31 and then set on a lenient java.util.Calendar. When the fields are read, November 31 turns into December 1. 2014-02-30 becomes 2014-03-02 and 2013-02-29 becomes 2013-03-01, at most 3 days past the month's end.

Who is affected

#

Applications using the opt-in, experimental DateRangePrefixTree that parse date strings typed by people or built by code, for example [2014-02-01 TO 2014-02-31] for "all of February": the range silently covers up to 3 extra days, and such a document value is indexed as the later date. Valid dates are unaffected. Rare: no report found since 5.0.0.

Mechanism, terms and numbers638 words

DateRangePrefixTree (spatial-extras, @lucene.experimental) parses ISO-like date strings for NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy. Since LUCENE-8640 (7.7.0) its parseCalendar range-checks the month, day, hour, minute and second fields (not the year or the fraction digits) and throws ParseException for day 32, month 13, hour 25 or minute 60. But the day is checked only against 1..31, never against the length of its month, and is then set on a lenient java.util.Calendar. When the fields are read, the impossible day silently becomes a real day of the next month: 2014-11-31 becomes 2014-12-01, 2014-02-30 becomes 2014-03-02, 2013-02-29 becomes 2013-03-01. The range [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31] parses to [2014-11 TO 2014-12-01], and an Intersects query on a three-document index returns 2014-11-15 and 2014-12-01. Both shipped template calendars give these results on these modern dates (they differ only for Julian leap years and the 1582 cutover), with assertions on and off. Every release since 5.0.0 (the first with the class) does it; the inconsistency with the day-32 check exists since 7.7.0. A 9-line month-length check makes these strings throw ParseException, keeps the deliberate hour 24 working, and passes the module's tests.

Terms

#
  • DateRangePrefixTree: a spatial-extras prefix tree for dates and date ranges. NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy indexes and queries its shapes; parseCalendar reads one date (2014-11-30, 2014-11-30T10, ...) and parseShape also reads a range [a TO b].
  • Template calendar: the tree clones a java.util.Calendar for every value. The two shipped templates, DEFAULT_CAL and JAVA_UTIL_TIME_COMPAT_CAL, are lenient (isLenient() == true): a lenient calendar accepts November 31 and normalizes it to December 1 when it computes its fields.

Step 1: the day is checked against 1..31 only

#

parseCalendar (DateRangePrefixTree.java:469-539 at a9779ef3c193) starts from a clone of the template (:472). The month goes through parseAndCheck(str, offset, 1, 12) (:491-492), the day through parseAndCheck(str, offset, 1, 31) and then cal.set(Calendar.DAY_OF_MONTH, parsedVal) (:498-499). parseAndCheck (:548-555) only compares with the bounds it is given. Any exception inside its try block becomes a ParseException (:534-538), so day 32 is rejected; November 31 passes.

Step 2: reading the fields rolls the day over

#

toShape(Calendar) (:308-334) reads the fields with cal.get(...) (:315-326), and toString(Calendar) (:391 on) does the same (:396-429). The first get makes the lenient calendar compute the time and rewrite its fields: November 31 becomes December 1. That is the cell that is indexed or searched.

Step 3: ranges

#

NumberRangePrefixTree.parseShape (NumberRangePrefixTree.java:205-221) parses each bound of [a TO b] the same way (:215, through parseUnitShape, DateRangePrefixTree.java:457-459), and toRangeShape (NumberRangePrefixTree.java:117-128) normalizes [2014-11-01 TO 2014-12-01] to [2014-11 TO 2014-12-01]: all of November plus December 1st.

Exposure and likelihood262 words
  • Who. Applications that use DateRangePrefixTree with NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy and parse date strings that people type or code builds. A range written as [2014-02-01 TO 2014-02-31] covers 2014-03-01..03; code that builds month ends by appending -31 covers the next month's first day for every 30-day month. A document value 2014-11-31 is indexed as 2014-12-01.
  • Downstream (code reading only, not executed): Solr's DateRangeField builds this tree on JAVA_UTIL_TIME_COMPAT_CAL and sends range strings to parseShape, and single values without Z that do not start with NOW to parseCalendar, for documents and queries. Values with Z go through Solr's own date parser, which rejects these dates. Other downstream search engines checked do not use this class.
  • Opt-in, experimental. The spatial-extras module is opt-in; DateRangePrefixTree and NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy are @lucene.experimental (DateRangePrefixTree.java:39, NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy.java:42). Within it nothing special is needed: either shipped template calendar, any IndexWriterConfig and codec, one value.
  • Silent and small. No exception and no index damage; the value means a different real date, at most 3 days past the named month's end. Deterministic; the same with assertions off.
  • How likely. Rare: standard date pickers and formatters normally do not produce November 31 (stored or hand-built strings can). Registry likelihood "rare", priority low.
  • Workaround today. Validate the date part first, for example with java.time.LocalDate.parse for modern dates (it rejects these strings). A non-lenient template calendar is no clean way out: 2014-11-31 then fails with IllegalArgumentException: MONTH: 10 -> 11 from toString/parseShape, later than and unlike the fixed-field checks, which throw ParseException at parse time; and the deliberate T24 is rejected too.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • Since LUCENE-8640 (7.7.0) the parser rejects every other out-of-range field value: day 32, month 13, hour 25, minute 60.
  • parseCalendar reverses toString, documented as ISO_INSTANT-consistent; Instant.parse rejects 2014-11-31 but accepts T24:00, the hour-24 rollover the parser allows on purpose.
  • No upstream test or LUCENE-8640 discussion mentions month length.
The full argument315 words
  • The method rejects every other out-of-range field value. LUCENE-8640 ("Date Range format validation", CHANGES.txt:6973, 7.7.0 section) added parseAndCheck for the month, day, hour, minute and second (DateRangePrefixTree.java:491, 498, 505, 512, 519; the year and the fraction digits are not range-checked). Day 32, month 13, hour 25 and minute 60 throw ParseException (executed controls). The upstream TestDateRangePrefixTree.testInvalidDateException (TestDateRangePrefixTree.java:282-353) asserts it for month 13 and hour 25. November 31 shows the same symptom as November 32, a silent move into December, yet only one is rejected.
  • The formatter it reverses cannot emit it, and the analogous ISO parser rejects it. No javadoc says how impossible input is handled, but parseCalendar is "The reverse of toString(java.util.Calendar)" (DateRangePrefixTree.java:462); toString(Calendar) is "consistent with DateTimeFormatter#ISO_INSTANT" (:386). Instant.parse rejects 2014-11-31T00:00:00Z and 2014-02-29T00:00:00Z and accepts 2014-11-30T24:00:00Z as the next midnight, the rollover the parser allows on purpose (hours 0..24). The parser's looser T24:30 (read as 00:30 of the next day) is rejected by Instant.parse and is not part of this claim.
  • The range parser promises an exception: parseShape "@throws ParseException If there is a problem" (NumberRangePrefixTree.java:203).
  • Nothing asks for the rollover. No upstream test parses a day missing from its month. LUCENE-8640's issue and PR review discuss delimiters, cost and hour 24 ("We might have hours from 0 to 24 inclusively"), never month length or leap years.
  • Filing advice (judge and verifiers): present it as a gap in LUCENE-8640's validation ("day 32 is rejected but November 31 rolls into December 1st"), not as a violation of a documented contract, and do not claim the parser is ISO-strict in general (it also accepts T24:30, a leading +, an optional Z and any number of fraction digits). The LUCENE-8640 comment "reject it absolutely since we parting a range not a date" was about malformed delimiters, not month length.
The devil's-advocate check195 words

The strongest case for "works as designed": no javadoc sentence says impossible dates are rejected; parseCalendar is specified only as the reverse of toString, whose output never contains 2014-11-31, so this is out-of-contract input and a fix is hardening. The class is Calendar-based and @lucene.experimental, a Calendar is lenient by default, the template is configurable (LUCENE-7278), LUCENE-8640 was an enhancement about syntax, and other Lucene date parsers are lenient too (DateTools.stringToDate reads 20140230 as 2014-03-02; the classic and flexible query parsers call setLenient(true)).

Why it fails: the parser itself already rejects every other out-of-range field value (day 32, month 13, hour 25, minute 60), whatever the lenient calendar would do with it; the rollover it keeps on purpose, hour 24, is one ISO also accepts for T24:00; and the "use a strict calendar" way out is undocumented, surfaces the error later as an IllegalArgumentException instead of the ParseException the fixed-field checks give at parse time, and also rejects hour 24. @lucene.experimental lowers the severity, not the inconsistency. The adjudication confirmed with medium confidence for this reason: the mechanism is certain, but maintainers may reasonably call the fix validation hardening.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Check the day against the month's real length (+9 lines in parseCalendar)

After parsing the day, compare it with the month's real length (getActualMaximum(DAY_OF_MONTH) on a clone of the calendar, so its precision state is kept) and throw; the existing catch turns that into a ParseException. +9 lines, no reindexing. The repro and the whole spatial-extras suite (120 tests) pass; the swept real dates and hour 24 parse as before.

Check the day against the month's real length (+9 lines in parseCalendar) (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

After parsing the day, take a clone of the calendar, set the day to 1 and compare the parsed day with getActualMaximum(DAY_OF_MONTH); throw IllegalArgumentException, which the existing catch turns into ParseException like the other field checks. The clone keeps the isSet() precision state that toShape relies on (the class javadoc warns that any get sets every field, DateRangePrefixTree.java:35-37). The finder wrote this fix; the independent reproducer and the adjudication ran it too.

--- a/lucene/spatial-extras/src/java/org/apache/lucene/spatial/prefix/tree/DateRangePrefixTree.java
+++ b/lucene/spatial-extras/src/java/org/apache/lucene/spatial/prefix/tree/DateRangePrefixTree.java
@@ -496,6 +496,15 @@ public class DateRangePrefixTree extends NumberRangePrefixTree {
       checkDelimiter(str, offset - 1, '-');
 
       parsedVal = parseAndCheck(str, offset, 1, 31);
+      // reject a day that does not exist in this month (e.g. Feb 30); a lenient Calendar would
+      // otherwise roll it over into the next month. Checked on a clone to keep cal's isSet() state.
+      Calendar monthCal = (Calendar) cal.clone();
+      monthCal.set(Calendar.DAY_OF_MONTH, 1);
+      int daysInMonth = monthCal.getActualMaximum(Calendar.DAY_OF_MONTH);
+      if (parsedVal > daysInMonth) {
+        throw new IllegalArgumentException(
+            "Invalid value: " + parsedVal + ", expecting from 1 to " + daysInMonth + "]");
+      }
       cal.set(Calendar.DAY_OF_MONTH, parsedVal);
       offset += 3;
       if (lastOffset < offset) return cal;

Trade-offs

  • Invalid input only: strings that name a non-existent day now throw ParseException, for queries and for documents. An application that relied on the rollover (for example appending -31 for "end of month") gets an error instead of a slightly wrong range; worth a CHANGES entry. Every real date in the sweep parses as before: 5,107 (DEFAULT_CAL) and 5,115 (JAVA_UTIL_TIME_COMPAT_CAL) of 5,376 day-precision strings per template round-trip before and after. Other precisions and years were not swept (the module's 120 tests pass with the fix).
  • Calendar-aware: getActualMaximum follows the template's calendar system, so 1500-02-29 stays valid under DEFAULT_CAL (Julian leap year) and is rejected under the proleptic JAVA_UTIL_TIME_COMPAT_CAL. A java.time-based check would get the first case wrong.
  • Shipped templates only: validated with DEFAULT_CAL and JAVA_UTIL_TIME_COMPAT_CAL; the constructor accepts any caller-supplied Calendar template, and custom Calendar subclasses were not tested.
  • Leaves the 1582 cutover gap: under DEFAULT_CAL, 1582-10-05..1582-10-14 (days the hybrid calendar skips) still move forward 10 days. Out of scope here; the round-trip option covers it.
  • Keeps hour 24 (2014-11-30T24 -> 2014-12-01T00) and the looser T24:30 form unchanged.
  • Cost: one Calendar clone and one getActualMaximum per parsed value with a day; parse path only. Not benchmarked.
  • No reindexing: indexes built from valid strings are unchanged; a value indexed earlier from an impossible string keeps the date it rolled to.
  • The message mirrors parseAndCheck's, including its stray ]; a PR may tidy both.

Document the leniency

Trade-offs

Workaround for users today (not a fix): validate the date first

Downstream note

#

Solr's DateRangeField is built on this tree and passes range strings to parseShape (read from code, not run), so a Solr range query or rule written with a non-existent day, such as [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31], silently covers the next month's first days instead of failing.

How it was found

#

Found by reading the parser while writing the java.time reference for an exhaustive check of DateRangePrefixTree: the reference rejects dates such as 2014-02-30 that the parser accepts. A direct public-API probe and a JUnit on the stock jars confirmed it.

The method in full128 words

Method: code reading (non-formal tier), in a hunt unit on spatial-extras range trees and counters.

  • The unit was building a bounded-exhaustive harness for DateRangePrefixTree with a java.time reference model, which rejects dates such as 2014-02-30. Reading parseCalendar while writing it showed that the day is checked only against 1..31.
  • A direct public-API probe showed the parser accepts it and rolls it into March; a JUnit on the stock main jars, with the day-32 rejection as its control, then failed with assertions on and off, and on 10.5.2 and 9.12.3.
  • It was then checked by an independent reproducer, an intended-behaviour devil's advocate, read-only reviews by a model from another family (OpenAI GPT-5.6), an adjudication, a history, prior-report and security follow-up, and an audit. Found 2026-10-26 on main a9779ef3c193.

Bug record

#

LFV-619, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
DateRangePrefixTree.parseCalendar (and parseShape) rejects day 32 but accepts a day that its month does not have (2014-11-31, 2014-02-30, 2013-02-29) and silently rolls it into the next month, so the range [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31] also matches December 1st
Area
spatial-extras / prefix trees (DateRangePrefixTree date parsing: parseCalendar, also reached through NumberRangePrefixTree.parseShape for single dates and range bounds)
Priority
Low, rank 354: An opt-in, experimental spatial-extras parser silently rolls impossible dates like 2014-11-31 into the next month so a query matches up to 3 extra days, but it needs an invalid date string and has gone unreported for about 11 years, so it joins the spatial-extras edge cases after LFV-301.
Impact class
wrong error
Impact note
Hits applications that use the opt-in, @lucene.experimental DateRangePrefixTree of the spatial-extras module (in practice also Solr's DateRangeField, which is built on it; read from code, not executed) and parse a date or range string that names a day its month does not have (day 29-31 in a short month, February 29 of a common year), with either shipped template calendar and no scale: instead of the ParseException that day 32 or month 13 gets, the value silently becomes a real date up to 3 days later, so a query range such as [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31] also matches documents dated 2014-12-01 and an indexed value is indexed as the later date; valid strings are unaffected and nothing crashes or corrupts; deterministic, -ea and -da, every release from 5.0.0 (the day-32 check that makes the gap inconsistent exists since 7.7.0).
Summary
You parse a date such as 2014-11-31, or a range such as [2014-11-01 TO 2014-11-31], with DateRangePrefixTree.parseCalendar or parseShape and expect a ParseException, which the parser has given since LUCENE-8640 for day 32, month 13, hour 25 and minute 60. Instead the day is checked only against 1..31 and set on a lenient java.util.Calendar, which moves it into the next month when the fields are read: 2014-11-31 becomes 2014-12-01, 2014-02-30 becomes 2014-03-02, and the range parses to [2014-11 TO 2014-12-01], so an Intersects query on a NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy index also returns a document dated 2014-12-01.
Introduced

The specific inconsistency, day 32 rejected but a day past the month's end rolled over, dates from 8e69d12dd7e2b6d2341322e4ac7e5c33b789507a ('LUCENE-8640: date range syntax validation', committed by GitHub mkhludnev, 2019-01-26; backports d482312b854 branch_8x and 62ee5df5e17 branch_7x; issue #9686; the contributed PR apache/lucene-solr#538 was closed and the change committed directly), which added parseAndCheck with fixed per-field bounds (day 1..31) and no month-length check [read: git blame of the current day check, git log on the file; exec: the version-neutral probe shows day 32 accepted on 7.6.0 and rejected on 7.7.0].
