ShingleFilter with minShingleSize >= 3 ends the stream early after a hole near the end, so the last word(s) are never indexed

With minShingleSize 3, ShingleFilter indexes "a cup of tea" (stop words removed) without "tea": the hole left by "of" makes incrementToken() return false while "tea" is still in its window, so a search for tea finds nothing. Affected since 3.1.0; the default minimum of 2 is not.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

ShingleFilter glues neighbouring words into shingles and, by default, also outputs every word on its own (a unigram). A removed stop word leaves a hole, filled with _.

  1. Analyze "a cup of tea" with WhitespaceTokenizer, StopFilter {a, of, the} and new ShingleFilter(ts, 3, 3); unigrams are on by default. The words left are cup (position 1) and tea (position 3); the holes at 0 and 2 become the filler _.
  2. You expect every word as a unigram plus the 3-word shingles: _ cup _, cup, cup _ tea, tea. The javadoc says unigrams are "always output" and that this is "independent of the min shingle size".
  3. You get _ cup _, cup, cup _ tea, then end of stream. After indexing, TermQuery(title:tea) finds 0 documents while title:cup finds 1. Nothing throws, with assertions on or off.
  4. Why: the 4th call sees the window [_, tea]. It skips the filler-only unigram and jumps to a 3-word shingle, but only 2 entries are left, so it builds nothing and returns false, which means end of stream.
  5. With minShingleSize 4 the last two words can go ("one of two three" keeps only "one"); with min 3, "the cat" yields no token at all. Min 2 is fine, and so is a value whose last hole is followed by at least minShingleSize-1 words, such as a hole in the middle of a value.

Who is affected

#

Applications that run ShingleFilter with minShingleSize 3 or more (directly, through the shingle factory or ShingleAnalyzerWrapper) after a stop filter or anything else that leaves holes, with unigrams on (the default). Values in which a removed word is followed by only 1 to minShingleSize-2 words at the very end lose those words. The default minShingleSize 2 is not affected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers534 words

ShingleFilter builds shingles (word n-grams) and, by default, also outputs every input word as a unigram. A removed stop word leaves a hole, which the filter fills with the token _. With minShingleSize 3 or more, a hole followed by only 1 to minShingleSize-2 words at the end of a value ends the stream early: incrementToken() skips the filler-only unigram, finds too few entries for a shingle and returns false while real words are still in its window. "a cup of tea" is indexed without "tea", "the cat" without any token, and term queries for those words miss the document. Nothing throws. It has been in every release since 3.1.0 (LUCENE-2400, 2010); minShingleSize 2, the default, is not affected.

Terms

#
  • Shingle: a word n-gram, for example cup _ tea. minShingleSize and maxShingleSize bound its length (both default 2). Unigram: a single input word, output too when outputUnigrams is true (the default).
  • Hole and filler: a position with no token, for example where StopFilter removed "of". ShingleFilter puts a filler token _ there so shingles keep their spacing; LUCENE-2400 (2010) made it skip shingles and unigrams that are only fillers.
  • Window: the filter keeps up to maxShingleSize entries (words and fillers) and builds one unigram or shingle from the head of the window per call.

The bug

#
  1. incrementToken() makes one attempt per call and returns whether that attempt produced a token (ShingleFilter.java:262-315, return tokenAvailable at :314).
  2. When the window starts with a filler, the filler-only unigram is skipped by jumping to the next gram size, minShingleSize (:285-288).
  3. Near the end of the value the window can hold fewer than minShingleSize entries (words and fillers both count). Then nothing is built either (:293), and the call returns false, which TokenStream documents as "end of stream" (TokenStream.java:138). The consumer stops, although the words after the filler are still in the window.
  4. Before the input is exhausted the window is full, so a filler-led window still yields a shingle and the next call outputs the word; with minShingleSize 2 the jump lands on a 2-entry shingle such as _ tea, which fits. That is why only minShingleSize >= 3 and only the end of a value are affected.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The javadoc says unigrams are "always output, regardless of whether any shingles are available" and "independent of the min shingle size".
  • The same word is output with min 2, mid-value, or (with min 3) when another stop word follows it; testTriGramFilterMinTriGram asserts tail unigrams for min 3.
  • LUCENE-2400 meant to drop filler-only tokens; "tea" is a real word.
The full argument259 words
  • Javadoc. setOutputUnigramsIfNoShingles (ShingleFilter.java:200-201, and ShingleAnalyzerWrapper.java:70-71): "Note that if outputUnigrams==true, then unigrams are always output, regardless of whether any shingles are available." setMinShingleSize (:228): "The unigram output option is independent of the min shingle size." setOutputUnigrams (:184-185): the output contains "the input tokens (unigrams) as well as shingles". The class javadoc (:40-41) says the filter "handles position increments > 1 by inserting filler tokens".
  • outputUnigramsIfNoShingles. A narrower promise: unigrams "for those times when no shingles are available (because there are fewer than minShingleSize tokens in the input stream)" (ShingleFilter.java:196-197). "the cat" is such a value: shiftInputWindow() switches to unigrams for it (:439-444), and the call then ends the stream before outputting one.
  • End of stream. TokenStream.incrementToken() returns "false for end of stream" (TokenStream.java:138); ShingleFilter returns false with unread input tokens in its window.
  • Tests. testTriGramFilterMinTriGram (min 3, max 3) asserts the trailing unigrams into and shingles where no trigram fits; every upstream test with holes uses minShingleSize 2, and the min 3 and 4 tests have no holes, so no upstream test asserts the combination.
  • History. LUCENE-2400 (#3475, 2010) changed the filter to "don't output all-filler shingles and unigrams". Precedents in the same package treat missing output at the end of a stream as a bug: LUCENE-5180 (#6244, trailing holes) and LUCENE-8719 (#9765, FixedShingleFilter missed output at the end of a stream, the same "too short, so stop" pattern).
  • Legal trigger. Validated settings (minShingleSize 3, maxShingleSize 3), an ordinary StopFilter, public constructors.
The devil's-advocate check147 words

Strongest case for "intended": LUCENE-2400 deliberately skips filler-led unigrams, and near the end a filler-led window shorter than minShingleSize has nothing left to output, so the tail could be called "no more n-grams", an old best-effort limit of fillers combined with minShingleSize >= 3.

Why it fails: the skipped token is the filler, not the word after it; the javadoc promises unigrams "always" and "independent of the min shingle size"; the same word is output with min 2, mid-value, and (in the min 3 example) when a trailing stop word follows it; "the cat" loses every token; no test, javadoc, issue or CHANGES entry allows dropping real words. The devil's advocate itself, the independent reproducer and the adjudication concluded real; the cross-model reviewer's intended-behaviour lens said real and rated the "intended" case weak, and its refute lens said "not real" only because it could not run code.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Move on to the next position instead of returning false

When a position yields no token and the window is not empty, move on to the next position instead of returning false, and add the skipped positions to the next token's position increment. ShingleFilter.java only, +20 lines. Validated: it passes the new test and the whole analysis-common module, assertions on and off.

int skippedPositions = 0;
while (true) {
  if (nextGram()) { // the old incrementToken() body
    if (skippedPositions > 0) {
      posIncrAtt.setPositionIncrement(posIncrAtt.getPositionIncrement() + skippedPositions);
    }
    return true;
  }
  if (inputWindow.isEmpty()) {
    return false;
  }
  gramSize.reset(); // no token at this position: move on to the next one
  skippedPositions++;
}

Move on to the next position instead of returning false (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

shingle-skip-empty-position.diff (the finder's sketch with reworded comments): the old body of incrementToken() becomes a private nextGram(), and incrementToken() loops while a position yields no token and the window still has entries, resetting gramSize so the next attempt shifts the window. The first token after skipped positions gets them added to its position increment, so tea lands at position 3, as with minShingleSize 2.

int skippedPositions = 0;
while (true) {
  if (nextGram()) { // the old incrementToken() body
    if (skippedPositions > 0) {
      posIncrAtt.setPositionIncrement(posIncrAtt.getPositionIncrement() + skippedPositions);
    }
    return true;
  }
  if (inputWindow.isEmpty()) {
    return false;
  }
  gramSize.reset(); // no token at this position: move on to the next one
  skippedPositions++;
}

Trade-offs

  • Output changed only where words were lost in the bounded exhaustive check and the test suites (observed, not proved); end() still reports the input's final position increment. No token starts at the skipped filler position, which maintainers may want to discuss; the test pins the positions.
  • Terminates: each iteration resets gramSize, so the next attempt shifts the window, and the window empties once the input is exhausted. Cost: at most a window's worth of extra attempts at the end of a value; a 1.75-million-character timing probe ran at 0.85x the time of the min 2 control (one loaded host). No benchmark beyond that.
  • Indexes built before the fix keep the missing terms until reindexed. The open refactor PR #13390 touches the same file and may conflict textually.

What the checks validated

  • The new test: 5 of 5 pass (4 of 5 fail on stock), assertions on and off.

  • The whole analysis-common module: 341 classes, 1,918 tests (1,909 upstream + 9 added), 0 failures, 18 skipped, assertions on and off; :lucene:analysis:common:check -x test passes.
  • TestRandomChains (a separate module, :lucene:analysis.tests), run by the fact check with the fix: 2 tests pass, seed DEADBEEF, assertions on. One seed, and a random chain need not include ShingleFilter: a single data point, not coverage.
  • The independent reproducer's 8-test JUnit passes 8 of 8 with the fix (5 of 8 fail on stock); in the hunt, the exhaustive check gave 0 mismatches with this change (14,884 without), against the finder's own reference.

Retry only when the window starts with a filler and is too short (untested)

#

Inside the existing method, when the head is a filler and the window has fewer than minShingleSize entries, shift the window and try again, keeping the position increment.

Trade-offs

Narrower, but it duplicates the shift logic; the recommended loop already covers this case and the outputUnigramsIfNoShingles case. Not run.

Document the limitation (rejected)

#

Say in the javadoc that trailing words after a hole may be dropped when minShingleSize >= 3.

Trade-offs

Contradicts the existing promise that unigrams are always output, independent of the min shingle size, and leaves values like "the cat" unsearchable.

Workaround in affected releases

#

Use minShingleSize 2 (every word is output; executed as the control), or index the unigrams in a separate field whose analyzer has no ShingleFilter and query both fields.

Trade-offs

Each application has to know about it. The separate-field workaround was not run.

Downstream note

#

A rule that excludes documents by one of the lost words (for example a MUST_NOT term clause) does not exclude a document whose value ends that way, while the stored text still shows the word.

How it was found

#

A checker compared the real ShingleFilter with a reference built on its javadoc over every token stream of up to 5 tokens and every small setting: 14,884 of 157,248 checks lost trailing unigrams, all with minShingleSize >= 3 and a hole. A public-API test, independent verifiers and an adjudication then confirmed it.

The method in full202 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation (a hunt unit, analysis pattern, shingle and compound filters, 2026-10-26, main ea57360912ed). No model checker, SMT solver or prover was involved.

  • The finder (an AI tool) wrote a reference built on ShingleFilter's documented unigram and filler behaviour (javadoc and option docs), with the other attribute details (filler cap, position lengths, offsets, types, end() state) taken from the implementation.
  • Its harness (ShingleTV.java) ran the real filter against it on every token stream of 0-5 tokens with position increments 1-3, final position increments 0-2, minShingleSize 2-4, maxShingleSize min..5, both unigram options, 2 separators and 2 fillers: 157,248 checks, 14,884 mismatches, every one with minShingleSize >= 3, a hole and a missing trailing unigram; 0 with the fix. The check is bounded to those sizes: strong testing, not a proof. A registry re-run reproduced the log byte for byte; it uses the same reference, so it is not independent. The same log lists 3,237 differences between FixedShingleFilter and ShingleFilter, a separate question and not part of this report.
  • Then a public-API JUnit, a cross-model review, independent verifiers, an adjudication, a 69-release sweep with a commit bracket, a prior-report search and a security screen (see Independent checks).

Impact note
Hits applications that run ShingleFilter (directly, through ShingleFilterFactory 'shingle' or ShingleAnalyzerWrapper(analyzer, 3, 3), or a downstream shingle filter that passes min_shingle_size through) with minShingleSize >= 3 and unigrams on (the default) after a filter that leaves holes such as StopFilter: every field value that ends with a stop word followed by 1 to minShingleSize-2 words silently loses those words from the index, so term, match and phrase-of-unigram queries for them miss the document; ordinary text, no scale, deterministic, with -ea and -da; minShingleSize 2 (the default) and values without such a hole are not affected.
Summary
You index 'a cup of tea' with WhitespaceTokenizer, StopFilter{a, of, the} and ShingleFilter(min 3, max 3) with unigrams on (the default), and expect every real word to be indexed as a unigram, because the javadoc says unigrams 'are always output, regardless of whether any shingles are available' and that the unigram option 'is independent of the min shingle size'. Instead, when the window starts with the filler left by the removed stop word, incrementToken() skips the all-filler unigram, finds fewer than minShingleSize positions left for a shingle and returns false, which ends the stream although 'tea' is still in its window: 'tea' is never indexed and TermQuery(tea) finds 0 documents. With min 4 up to two trailing words are lost, a value such as 'the cat' yields no token at all, and nothing throws, with assertions on or off.
