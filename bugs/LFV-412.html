The finding in 30 seconds

#

Range grouping asks DoubleRangeFactory for each document's bucket and returns that bucket, a [min, max) range, as the group's value. The factory computes the bucket number and the bucket's two bounds with separately rounded formulas, and near a boundary they can disagree.

Background: grouping by double ranges

#
  1. GroupingSearch groups hits by one value per document. DoubleRangeGroupSelector computes that value with DoubleRangeFactory.getRange(value).
  2. new DoubleRangeFactory(min, width, max) makes one bucket below min, buckets of width from min up to max, and one bucket for everything at or above max. Each bucket is a DoubleRange from an inclusive minimum to an exclusive maximum.
  3. Doubles are binary: 0.1 is really 0.1000000000000000055..., and every +, * or / rounds to the nearest double. The double next to 0.6 is 0.6000000000000001, one ulp (unit in the last place) away.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Group documents by a double field with DoubleRangeGroupSelector and new DoubleRangeFactory(0, 0.1, 10): buckets 0.1 wide from 0 to 10. Three documents have the values 0.55, 0.6 and 0.65.
  2. You expect each group's label, a DoubleRange documented as [min, max), to contain the values grouped under it, and each label to end where the next one begins.
  3. You get the group DoubleRange(0.5, 0.6) holding 0.55 and 0.6: its label leaves out its own value 0.6, and the next label starts at 0.6000000000000001, so no label contains 0.6. A range query over the label's bounds finds 1 of the group's 2 documents.
  4. Why: getRange picks the bucket floor((0.6 - 0) / 0.1) = floor(5.999999999999999) = 5 and ends it at 0.5 + 0.1, which rounds to 0.6, while the next bucket starts at 6 * 0.1 = 0.6000000000000001. For 1.7 the division rounds up to exactly 17, so its label DoubleRange(1.7000000000000002, 1.8000000000000003) starts above the value.
  5. Scale: 27 of the 100 values 0.0, 0.1, ..., 9.9 lie outside their own label, and 27 of 99 neighbouring labels leave a one-ulp gap or overlap. Every release since 8.6.0. Lucene's own tests use a positive integer min and width, where no wrong label was found; a decimal min or width, or a negative min, can trigger it.

Who is affected

#

Applications that group by double ranges with DoubleRangeGroupSelector (opt-in, rarely used) and a min or width that binary cannot represent, such as 0.1, or a negative min. Every search, any index size. Groups, counts and order stay right; the bounds of boundary values' labels, and range queries built from them, are off by about one ulp.

Mechanism, terms and numbers673 words

DoubleRangeFactory(min, width, max) turns a document's double value into the range that DoubleRangeGroupSelector groups it by, and that range comes back as the group's value. DoubleRange is documented as an inclusive minimum and an exclusive maximum, and the factory's ranges as "inclusive at the lower end, and exclusive at the upper end". getRange picks the bucket number with Math.floor((value - min) / width) and computes the bounds as min + bucket * width and then + width: three separately rounded results that can disagree near bucket boundaries, most visibly when width or min is not exactly representable in binary. With new DoubleRangeFactory(0, 0.1, 10), getRange(0.6) is DoubleRange(0.5, 0.6) and getRange(1.7) is DoubleRange(1.7000000000000002, 1.8000000000000003); 27 of the 100 values 0.0 to 9.9 lie outside their own range, and neighbouring ranges leave one-ulp gaps or overlap. Group membership, counts and order stay consistent; only the returned ranges are wrong, and anything built from them, such as a drill-down range query, which then misses or adds boundary documents. Present since the class was added in 8.6.0, in every release through 10.6.0 and on main; a small fix takes both bounds from one grid and moves the bucket by one when needed.

The mechanism (main 645d4fe14a20)

#
  1. DoubleRangeFactory.getRange (DoubleRangeFactory.java:62-64):
   double bucket = Math.floor((value - min) / width);
   reuse.min = min + (bucket * width);
   reuse.max = reuse.min + width;
  1. 0.6: the range ends too early. (0.6 - 0) / 0.1 = 5.999999999999999, so bucket 5, which is right for the binary values (the double 0.6 lies below 6 times the double 0.1). reuse.min = 0.5, and reuse.max = 0.5 + 0.1, which rounds to 0.6. The next bucket starts at 6 * 0.1 = 0.6000000000000001. So 0.6 lies in no range.
  2. 1.7: the bucket is one too high. 1.7 / 0.1 rounds up to exactly 17.0, although the double 1.7 lies below 17 * 0.1 = 1.7000000000000002; the range starts above the value.
  3. The ranges do not tile. reuse.max is the rounded reuse.min plus width, rounded again, while the next range starts at min + (bucket + 1) * width, rounded once.
  4. Membership stays consistent. DoubleRangeGroupSelector calls getRange once per document on its own value (DoubleRangeGroupSelector.java:71) and keeps a document in the second pass if its range is one of the top groups (:75); DoubleRange.equals compares both bounds exactly (DoubleRange.java:44-50). Only the reported bounds are off.

The numbers

#
  • DoubleRangeFactory(0, 0.1, 10): getRange(0.6) = DoubleRange(0.5, 0.6), getRange(1.7) = DoubleRange(1.7000000000000002, 1.8000000000000003), getRange(4.1) = DoubleRange(4.0, 4.1).
  • Of the 100 values 0.0, 0.1, ..., 9.9, 27 lie outside their own range (0.6, 1.7, 3.4, 3.9, 4.1, ...). Of the 99 pairs of neighbouring bucket ranges, 27 do not meet: 21 gaps and 6 overlaps such as [1.2000000000000002, 1.3000000000000003) and [1.3, 1.4000000000000001), each one ulp.
  • End to end: documents with the values 0.55, 0.6 and 0.65 in a field price form the groups DoubleRange(0.5, 0.6) (0.55 and 0.6) and DoubleRange(0.6000000000000001, 0.7000000000000001) (0.65); DoublePoint.newRangeQuery("price", 0.5, Math.nextDown(0.6)) over the first label counts 1 document.
  • A boundary effect: over six factories every gap or overlap is at most one ulp of the factory's largest bound, and no bucket midpoint is outside its range. Within 200 doubles of every boundary, a value outside its range is one double outside in five of the factories and up to 31 doubles outside in (-5, 0.7, 5) near -0.1, where doubles are denser.
  • Which factories: an inexact min is enough. With widths 1, 10, 100, 0.5 and 0.25, no checked value (bucket boundaries and two-decimal values) is outside its range when min is an integer, but 8,519 of 499,985 are when min has two decimals, e.g. DoubleRangeFactory(248.8, 1.0, 928.8).getRange(520.8) = DoubleRange(519.8, 520.8). A negative min is enough too, even an integer one, because value - min then rounds: new DoubleRangeFactory(-10, 1, 10).getRange(0.9999999999999999, null) = DoubleRange(1.0, 2.0). With a non-negative integer min and an integer width, like Lucene's tests, 0 of 600,000 values next to a boundary were outside.
Exposure and likelihood206 words
  • Who. Range grouping through GroupingSearch(Supplier) (GroupingSearch.java:85) or the grouping collectors, with a decimal width (the natural choice for decimal data) or a decimal (or negative) min. A read-only code search on 2026-10-26 found no use of DoubleRangeFactory in Elasticsearch, OpenSearch or Solr (default branches). GroupingSearch and GroupDocs are marked @lucene.experimental (GroupingSearch.java:44, GroupDocs.java:33).
  • What goes wrong. On every search, with assertions on or off: some values on a bucket boundary get a range that excludes them (27 of 100 for width 0.1), and neighbouring ranges leave one-ulp gaps or overlaps. A drill-down built from a group's range misses that group's boundary documents (a gap) or also matches the neighbour's (an overlap).
  • What stays right. Which documents are in which group, the counts, the group sort and the documents within each group. Factories with a non-negative integer min and an integer width, the only ones in Lucene's tests, showed no value outside its range.
  • How likely. Plausible within a rarely used API: decimal data puts many values exactly on boundaries (27 of the 100 one-decimal values for width 0.1), but it shows only once an application reads the bounds or filters by them, and nobody has reported it since 8.6.0 (2020).

Why it looks unintended

#
  • DoubleRange is documented as [min, max) and getRange as finding "the DoubleRange that a value should be grouped into"; 0.6's range excludes 0.6.
  • Lucene's own selector tests filter each group by its bounds; their factories use a positive integer min and width.
  • Even for 0.1's binary value the ranges exclude values, leave gaps and overlap; one consistent formula fixes that.
The full argument250 words
  • The contract. DoubleRange.java:23: "Represents a contiguous range of double values, with an inclusive minimum and exclusive maximum"; :27 and :30: "The inclusive minimum value of this range", "The exclusive maximum value of this range". DoubleRangeFactory.java:31-33: "Ranges are inclusive at the lower end, and exclusive at the upper end." :43: "Finds the DoubleRange that a value should be grouped into". Nothing documents a rounding tolerance or a restriction on width (javadoc, package-info.java, the 8.6.0 CHANGES entry).
  • Lucene's tests read the range as a range. BaseGroupSelectorTestCase declares filterQuery(groupValue) (:50) and checks each group's documents against it (:71, :108, :160, :193, :215, :261), which TestDoubleRangeGroupSelector.java:57 builds as DoublePoint.newRangeQuery("double", groupValue.min, Math.nextDown(groupValue.max)). Its factory is (100, 100, 900) (:46), and TestDoubleRangeFactory uses (10, 10, 50) (:26): a positive integer min and width, where no wrong range was found.
  • The binary reading fails too. Reading 0.1 as its exact binary value, the stock ranges still exclude their own values, leave gaps and overlap; taking both bounds from one grid removes all three.
  • History. The formula, the javadoc and the selector test's label filter come from the introducing commit 7c350d22c707 (LUCENE-7889); the generic oracle in BaseGroupSelectorTestCase that runs the filter came a week earlier with LUCENE-9348 (0c58687a978e, 2020-05-04, #10388). The PR of 7c350d22c707 (apache/lucene-solr#1484) has no review comments, and issue #8940 discusses javadocs and tests, not rounding. No CHANGES, MIGRATE, test or comment endorses gaps.
The devil's-advocate check157 words

The strongest case (the cross-model reviews and the intended-behaviour checker rated it moderate; the reproducer and the adjudication make the same case): the API works on binary doubles and 0.1 is not exact, so ranges can only approximate decimal boundaries; another engine's histogram uses the same floor((value - offset) / interval) formula, documents it and treats this symptom as normal; inside Lucene both grouping passes compute the same key, so membership is right, and no Lucene code outside its tests turns a label into a query; and no binary fix gives decimal buckets (with the fix, 1.7 moves to [1.6, 1.7000000000000002)).

Why it fails: Lucene, unlike that histogram, returns an explicit range object and documents what it contains; the stock ranges are not a consistent binary reading either; the introducing commit's own test uses the range as an exact half-open filter; and the trigger is a public constructor with an ordinary width. Verdict: a defect, minor.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended One grid for both bounds, step the bucket by one (validated)

Take both bounds from one grid, min + bucket * width and min + (bucket + 1) * width, and step the bucket by one when the value falls outside. In every tested factory of ordinary scale the ranges then tile and contain their value; the grouping module passes. Some boundary values change group (1.7 down, 4.3 up).

     double bucket = Math.floor((value - min) / width);
+    if (bucket > 0 && value < min + bucket * width) {
+      bucket--;
+    } else if (value >= min + (bucket + 1) * width) {
+      bucket++;
+    }
     reuse.min = min + (bucket * width);
-    reuse.max = reuse.min + width;
+    reuse.max = min + ((bucket + 1) * width);
All options and trade-offs3 options · 787 words

One fix was validated in a scratch worktree of 645d4fe14a20 with gradle; it is the hunt's fix, unchanged. A membership-preserving alternative (label each bucket with exactly the doubles it receives, found one double at a time) was tried and rejected: it hangs when a bucket boundary is at 0, e.g. new DoubleRangeFactory(-1000, 1, 1000).getRange(0.5, null) did not return within 30 s. Combined with LFV-407's clamp on the same line, the recommended fix becomes reuse.max = Math.min(min + ((bucket + 1) * width), max); (validated on both bugs' tests, assertions on). Fix A alone leaves LFV-407's problem (the last range is not cut at max) as it is.

One grid for both bounds, step the bucket by one (validated) (recommended)

#

After the floor, move the bucket down if the value is below min + bucket * width, or up if it is at or above min + (bucket + 1) * width, then use exactly those two grid points as the range. 1 file, +9/-1.

     double bucket = Math.floor((value - min) / width);
+    if (bucket > 0 && value < min + bucket * width) {
+      bucket--;
+    } else if (value >= min + (bucket + 1) * width) {
+      bucket++;
+    }
     reuse.min = min + (bucket * width);
-    reuse.max = reuse.min + width;
+    reuse.max = min + ((bucket + 1) * width);

Trade-offs

  • Behaviour change. For (0, 0.1, 10), 27 of the 100 bucket ranges change their max by one ulp ((1.7000000000000002, 1.8000000000000003) becomes (1.7000000000000002, 1.8)), and group membership changes: 10 of the 100 values 0.0 to 9.9 move to a neighbouring bucket, each into the range [min + k * width, min + (k + 1) * width) that contains it. 6 move down (1.7, 3.4, 3.9, 6.8, 7.3, 7.8: the division rounded up to exactly k), and 4 move up (4.3, 8.1, 8.6, 9.1: k * 0.1 rounds down onto the value, so DoubleRange(4.2, 4.3) becomes [4.3, 4.4)). Over 889,912 boundary values the grouping partition differs from stock.
  • Not decimal buckets. 0.6 gets [0.5, 0.6000000000000001) and 1.7 gets [1.6, 1.7000000000000002): correct ranges, but not what a decimal reading expects.
  • Limits. "One step is enough" held in every check of factories whose width is well above the spacing of doubles near the values (all finite, with a positive width); that is a test result, not a proof. It fails when width is below the spacing of doubles near the values: for DoubleRangeFactory(1e6, 1e-12, 1e6 + 1) only about one grid range in 116 is non-empty, getRange(1000000.0000000001) computes bucket 116 while the grid range that contains the value is bucket 174, and stock and the fix both return the empty DoubleRange(1000000.0000000001, 1000000.0000000001).
  • Cost. Up to four min + k * width evaluations per call (two distinct values, each computed twice) instead of one plus an addition, and two or three compares; no benchmark was run.

Shared bounds only, no step (not recommended) (validated by the checks)

#

reuse.max = min + ((bucket + 1) * width) and keep the bucket as computed.

Trade-offs

The ranges tile, but 10 of the 100 grid values are still outside their range, because the bucket number still comes from the division alone: for 1.7, 3.4, 3.9, 6.8, 7.3 and 7.8 the division rounds up to exactly k; for 4.3, 8.1, 8.6 and 9.1 it rounds down and the shared upper bound (k + 1) * 0.1 rounds down onto the value.

What the checks validated

Side probe on main 645d4fe14a20 with the change shadowed: 10 of 100 outside, 0 gaps or overlaps.

Document the limitation (fallback)

#

Say in the javadoc that min and width should be exactly representable and min non-negative (for example integers), or that decimal data should be grouped with LongRangeGroupSelector over scaled values (e.g. cents).

Trade-offs

Keeps ranges that contradict DoubleRange's own [min, max) javadoc. Only a non-negative integer min with an integer width was measured clean, and no setting helps once width is below the spacing of doubles near the values. Within those limits it is also the workaround until a fix ships.

Downstream note

#

Applications that turn range-group labels into filters miss or add only documents whose value lies within about one ulp of a bucket boundary; no Lucene code outside its tests builds a query from a label.

How it was found

#

A solver model of the IEEE-754 double arithmetic in getRange found an input whose range does not contain the value. A bounded-exhaustive check of the real factory over decimal grids and a scan of the ordinary factory (0, 0.1, 10) then gave the natural example 0.6, reduced to a public-API JUnit.

The method in full126 words
  • Solver model. An SMT model of getRange in IEEE-754 double arithmetic (Z3; round-to-nearest subtraction, division, multiplication and addition, round-down floor) answered the query "min <= v < max, finite inputs, width > 0, yet the returned range does not contain v" with a satisfying input, DoubleRangeFactory(-31.051544275134813, 0.2985725411070654, 7.706943822235074E-301).getRange(-1.42110715556365E-14), which a replay on the real jar confirmed.
  • Bounded-exhaustive check of the real factory over decimal grids: 1,594 containment failures among 1,029,006 checks, 0 with the fix.
  • Grid scan of DoubleRangeFactory(0, 0.1, 10) over 0.0 to 9.9: 27 of 100 values outside their range, the first being 0.6.
  • Then a public-API JUnit, independent verifiers, cross-model reviews (reading only) and an adjudication. Every query of the model was a targeted counterexample search, so the finding counts as semi-formal.

minor
Impact note
Applications that group by a double field with DoubleRangeGroupSelector (through GroupingSearch or the grouping collectors) and a DoubleRangeFactory whose width is not exactly representable in binary (0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.01, ...) get, in every release from 8.6.0 to 10.6.0 and on main (-ea and -da, any index size), group labels that exclude a value lying on a bucket boundary by one or a few representable doubles, and adjacent labels that leave gaps or overlap by about one ulp, so a drill-down range query built from a label (the way Lucene's own selector tests rebuild a group) misses the boundary documents or picks up a neighbour's; which group a document is in, the counts and the group order stay consistent, Lucene itself never turns a label into a query, and exactly representable widths (integers, 0.5, 0.25) are not affected.
Summary
You group documents by a double field with new DoubleRangeFactory(0, 0.1, 10), buckets 0.1 wide, and expect each group's DoubleRange label to contain the values grouped under it, because DoubleRange is documented as [min, max). Instead the value 0.6 is grouped under DoubleRange(0.5, 0.6), which excludes 0.6, and 1.7 under DoubleRange(1.7000000000000002, 1.8000000000000003); 27 of the 100 values 0.0, 0.1, ..., 9.9 lie outside their own label, adjacent labels leave gaps (no label contains 0.6) or overlap, and a range query built from a label misses or picks up documents (values 0.55, 0.6, 0.65 form a group DoubleRange(0.5, 0.6) of 2 documents whose label matches 1). The cause is that getRange picks the bucket with Math.floor((value - min) / width) but computes its bounds separately as min + bucket * width and then that + width, and the three roundings disagree near boundaries; group membership itself stays consistent.
