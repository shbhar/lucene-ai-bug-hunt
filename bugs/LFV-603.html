nori gives the parts of a decompounded word offsets counted from the end of the word, so parts that do not cover the whole word point at the wrong characters

When nori splits a compound into parts that cover less than the whole word, each part's offsets land right of its own text by the uncovered length: the default KoreanAnalyzer puts 픽토, from 픽토그램, at [1,3), over '토그'. Highlights mark the wrong characters; matching is unchanged.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

The part's text and its offsets are computed from opposite ends of the word. They meet only when the parts cover the whole word.

Background: how nori splits a compound

#
  1. Nori's dictionary marks some nouns as COMPOUND and stores their parts as lengths. 가락지나물 is stored as 3 + 2 characters, 가락지 and 나물. A user dictionary line such as 세종시 세종 시 does the same for 세종시.
  2. In DISCARD mode (the default) the tokenizer emits the parts instead of the word, each with a start and end offset: the positions of its characters in the source text. Highlighters and snippets use these offsets to mark matches.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Nori splits compound nouns into parts and gives each part its own offsets, the characters it came from: 가락지나물 becomes 가락지 [0,3) and 나물 [3,5).
  2. You add the user-dictionary entry 세종시 세종 (the word 세종시 with one part, 세종) and tokenize "세종시" in the default DISCARD mode. You expect 세종 at [0,2).
  3. Instead you get 세종 at [1,3), which covers '종시'. Nori cuts each part's text from the start of the word but counts its offsets back from the end, and here the part covers only 2 of the 3 characters.
  4. With no configuration at all, 7 compounds of the bundled dictionary do the same. 픽토그램 ('pictogram') is stored as 픽토 + 그, so the default KoreanAnalyzer gives 픽토 [1,3) = '토그' and 그 [3,4) = '램', and a highlighter marks 픽토그램 for the term 픽토 (픽토그램 for the phrase 픽토그램), also inside ordinary sentences.
  5. Only offsets are wrong: terms and positions stay the same, and in the tested setups matches and scores were unchanged and nothing failed. Every release tested, from 7.4.0 through 10.6.0, and main does it.

Who is affected

#

Applications that analyze Korean with nori in DISCARD (the default, KoreanAnalyzer) or MIXED mode and use offsets for highlighting, snippets, term vectors or postings. Default settings hit 7 bundled compounds (of 160,029) wherever the tokenizer picks their stored split; so does any user entry whose parts are shorter than the word. Matching and scoring are unaffected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers678 words

KoreanTokenizer (nori) splits a COMPOUND dictionary entry into parts in DISCARD mode (the default, used by KoreanAnalyzer) and MIXED mode. Each part's text is cut from the start of the word using the stored part lengths (UserMorphData.java:85, TokenInfoMorphData.java:124), but its offsets are counted back from the end of the word (Viterbi.java:264, :267-292). The two agree only when the parts add up to the whole word. When they add up to less, every part's offsets move right by the uncovered length, onto other characters of the same word: the user-dictionary entry 세종시 세종 gives 세종 [1,3) = '종시', and the bundled 픽토그램 (parts 픽토 + 그) gives 픽토 [1,3) = '토그' and 그 [3,4) = '램'. Lucene accepts both inputs: UserDictionary rejects only a segmentation longer than the word (UserDictionary.java:114), and 7 of the 160,029 bundled compounds have parts that do not add up to the word. Highlights, snippets and stored offsets mark the wrong characters; terms and positions are unchanged, and so were matches and scores in the tested setups. Present since nori's first commit: it fails on every release tested from 7.4.0 through 10.6.0 and on main. Counting the offsets from the start of the word fixes it; rejecting such user entries, as the Japanese tokenizer does, and repairing the 7 bundled rows are complementary.

With part lengths summing to C and the word length L, the parts' offsets are shifted right by L - C against their text. For 세종시 세종, L = 3 and C = 2: 세종 gets [3 - 2, 3) = [1,3). In DISCARD mode '시' is in no token; MIXED mode emits 세종시 [0,3) and 세종 [1,3). KoreanTokenizer emits the offsets as given (KoreanTokenizer.java:236).

Nothing rejects such input: UserDictionary throws only if (offset > token.length()) (dict/UserDictionary.java:114), and the dictionary builder asserts only compoundOffset <= entry[0].length() (dict/TokenInfoDictionaryEntryWriter.java:161). The Japanese tokenizer's UserDictionary rejects any segmentation that does not concatenate to the word (ja/dict/UserDictionary.java:114, LUCENE-8933).

Exposure and likelihood284 words
  • Default settings. The 7 bundled words need no configuration. Each was hit in every sentence tested, also inside ordinary sentences with particles (all 7 on main and 10.5.2). Whether a word is hit depends on the tokenizer choosing its stored compound in that context: since 10.0.0, 일로역 on its own is split as 일 [0,1) + 로역 [1,3) and is not hit, while '일로역에 도착했다.' still hits.
  • User dictionaries. Any entry whose segmentation is shorter than the word, for example to keep only the first part of a name, is accepted and misplaces the kept part.
  • What goes wrong. Highlights and snippets mark characters 1-2 positions to the right (bundled words) or shifted by the uncovered length (user entries), always inside the same word. Term vectors and postings store the same shifted offsets.
  • What does not change. Terms, positions and position lengths: only the offset arithmetic is wrong. In a check with 148 tokens, 88 of them misplaced (assertions on and off), all misplaced offsets stayed inside their own word, IndexWriter accepted them (they never went backwards), CheckIndex was clean and 46 phrase queries matched their documents. Each per-term span of the unified highlighter stayed inside its own word (term 픽토 gives 픽토그램); a phrase span covers the matched words, as usual, but starts one character late (phrase 픽토그램 gives 픽토그램, phrase '오늘 픽토그램 소식' gives 오늘 픽토그램 소식). Only the unified highlighter was run.
  • Not affected: DecompoundMode NONE, which does not split compounds.
  • How likely. Narrow: 7 of 160,029 bundled compounds, or a user entry of this shape. No remark on the misplaced offset was found since 2018, although it is visible in output posted on #9570.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • Upstream tests assert source-aligned part offsets (가락지나물 gives 가락지 [0,3) and 나물 [3,5), TestKoreanTokenizer.java:246-252) and define parts as left-to-right cuts of the word (TestTokenInfoDictionary.java:168-183).
  • OffsetAttribute says the offsets are the source characters "corresponding to this token"; 세종 [1,3) covers '종시'.
  • Lucene accepts both triggers silently, and the bundled one needs no configuration.
The full argument304 words
  • Contract. OffsetAttribute.startOffset(): "the position of the first character corresponding to this token in the source text"; endOffset(): "one greater than the position of the last character corresponding to this token" (OffsetAttribute.java:23-50). For COMPOUND parts nori computes a separate sub-span per part, and the characters corresponding to a part are the ones its text was cut from.
  • Tests. testPartOfSpeechWithCompound expects 가락지 [0,3) and 나물 [3,5) (TestKoreanTokenizer.java:246-252). TestTokenInfoDictionary.java:168-183 checks that each COMPOUND part equals the next left-to-right substring of the word.
  • Javadoc. UserDictionary presents an entry as a split of the word: "custom nouns (세종) or compounds (세종시 세종 시)" (UserDictionary.java:33-34).
  • History. Right-anchored offsets, left-cut text and the longer-only check all came with nori's first commit (LUCENE-8231). The saved design thread (#9278) gives no rationale for counting part offsets from the word's end. There Jim Ferenczi wrote "For compound nouns I serialize the segmentations with the term but we could just use offset from the surface form", which treats the parts as pieces of the word; a later patch there "fixes an issue with offsets of the compound nouns", without detail. The introducing commit's test userdict.txt has the entry 세종시 세종 시, which covers the word.
  • Precedent. For the Japanese tokenizer, Tomoko Uchida wrote on #9976 (LUCENE-8933) that "the sum of length of segments are expected to be exactly same to the length of corresponding surface form", and that such entries should be rejected "even if this does not cause any exceptions". That thread checked nori only with an entry without segmentation.
  • The right contract to state. "A COMPOUND part's offsets must cover the characters its text was cut from", not "a token's text equals its offset slice": INFLECT parts carry the whole word's offsets by design (TestKoreanTokenizer.java:311-317: 감싸이 [0,3) and 어 [0,3) for 감싸여).
The devil's-advocate check204 words

The strongest case for "intended" or "misuse": nori tolerates parts that do not cover the word on purpose (the <= in TestTokenInfoDictionary.java:183 and in the dictionary builder since 2018); the 7 bundled rows are errors in the source data (Jim Ferenczi called #9570's 그레이맨 case one of the "bugs due to invalid rules in the mecab-ko-dic"); a shorter user segmentation is arguably a misconfiguration; KoreanTokenizer is @lucene.experimental; and it is excluded from TestRandomChains with @IgnoreRandomChains(reason = "LUCENE-10359: fails with incorrect offsets") (KoreanTokenizer.java:54), which the cross-model intended lens called "the strongest documented warning supporting a works-as-designed defense".

Why it fails: the tolerance confirms that parts are left-anchored text, yet nothing documents or tests offsets counted from the word's end; the annotation points to #11395, a different defect (its failing chain had no Korean text, and Robert Muir and Tomoko Uchida traced it to KoreanNumberFilter on that thread); Lucene accepts both inputs without a warning; the bundled case needs only default settings. Even if dropping a suffix were deliberate, the kept part's offsets must still point at its own text. Verdict: a defect. The adjudication kept medium confidence because maintainers may prefer repairing the data and rejecting such entries over changing Viterbi.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A: count part offsets from the start of the word (Viterbi)

Count the parts' offsets from the start of the word: end them at backWordPos + min(length, sum of part lengths) in Viterbi. Validated with gradle on main: the repro passes with assertions on and off, and the whole nori suite (88 tests) and :lucene:analysis:nori:check -x test pass.

Downstream note

#

Search products that highlight or snippet Korean documents with nori show the mark shifted inside the same word (1-2 characters for the bundled words, the uncovered length for a user entry); matching, filtering and scoring are unaffected.

How it was found

#

A bounded-exhaustive sweep of Lucene's tokenizers checked, for every input of up to 6 symbols, that each token's offsets point at its own text; it failed for a nori user entry with a shorter segmentation. An exhaustive scan of all 816,283 bundled dictionary entries then found the 7 compounds with the same shape.

The method in full121 words

Method: semi-formal (bounded-exhaustive translation validation of the stock analysis jars with a twin-run provenance oracle), on main a9779ef3c193, 2026-10-26.

  • The sweep ran every input of up to 6 symbols per component over 136 component and char-filter configurations, 74,769,564 checks. Its check that a tokenizer's token slices its own text failed only for the Korean user-dictionary configuration with a shorter segmentation.
  • An exhaustive scan of all 816,283 bundled nori entries found the 7 COMPOUND entries, of 160,029, whose parts do not cover the word, and a script traced them to their source rows.
  • No solver or model checker produced the signal. An independent reproducer, reviews by another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6), a devil's-advocate verifier and an adjudication then checked the claim.

LFV-603, snapshot of 2026-11-08

Registry title
KoreanTokenizer gives the parts of a decompounded word offsets counted back from the end of the word while their text is cut from the start of the word, so when the parts do not cover the whole word (a user-dictionary entry with a shorter segmentation such as '세종시 세종', or 7 compounds of the bundled dictionary such as 픽토그램) the offsets point at other characters (픽토 at [1,3) covers '토그')
Area
analysis / nori KoreanTokenizer (Viterbi decompound backtrace; UserDictionary and bundled-dictionary compounds)
Priority
Low, rank 1059: Nori puts decompounded-part offsets on the wrong characters for 7 bundled compounds and for short user-dictionary segmentations, which affects offsets (highlighting) only (output posted on #9570 in 2018 showed one such offset without describing it).
Impact class
minor
Impact note
Applications that analyze Korean text with the default KoreanAnalyzer or KoreanTokenizer (decompound mode DISCARD, the default, or MIXED) and use offsets, mainly highlighting and snippets, term vectors or offsets in postings, in every release from 7.4.0 to 10.6.0 and main: every occurrence of 7 bundled compounds (그레이맨, 픽토그램, 녹색시민연합, 포럼신사고, 안지랑역, 일로역, 테크노파크역; all 7 also inside ordinary sentences on main [exec]) and of any user-dictionary compound whose segmentation is shorter than its word gets part offsets shifted right by the uncovered length (1-2 characters for the bundled words), always inside the same word, so a highlight marks the wrong characters; terms, positions, matches and scores are unchanged, there is no exception or index damage, and no scale is needed.
Summary
You analyze text containing 픽토그램 ('pictogram') with the default KoreanAnalyzer, or 세종시 with the user dictionary entry '세종시 세종', and expect each decompounded part's offsets to cover the characters its text came from, as they do when the parts cover the whole word (가락지나물 gives 가락지[0,3) and 나물[3,5)). Instead nori cuts each part's text from the start of the word but counts its offsets back from the end of the word, so 픽토그램 gives 픽토[1,3), covering '토그', and 그[3,4), covering '램', and '세종시 세종' gives 세종[1,3), covering '종시'; highlighters mark the wrong characters. Lucene accepts both inputs without a check: UserDictionary rejects only a segmentation longer than the word, and 7 compounds in the bundled dictionary have parts that do not add up to the word.
