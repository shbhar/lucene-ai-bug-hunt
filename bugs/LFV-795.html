Tessellator rejects a valid polygon whose holes touch nothing, depending on where the outer ring's vertex list starts

Since 8.2.0, XYShape/LatLonShape.createIndexableFields can reject a valid polygon whose two holes touch nothing, depending on which vertex the outer ring is listed from. A bridge that continues an outer edge straight on leaves a 180-degree corner that isLocallyInside never accepts, so the next hole misses its only correct bridge vertex.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

How Lucene cuts a polygon with holes into triangles

#
  1. LatLonShape and XYShape index a polygon as triangles, computed by Tessellator.tessellate (ear clipping, inspired by the earcut library).
  2. Holes are removed first, left-most hole first: each hole's left-most vertex is joined to a vertex of the outer ring by a two-way cut, a bridge. Both ends of a bridge then appear twice in the ring, once on each side of the cut.
  3. To pick the outer vertex, a ray goes left from the hole vertex to the nearest outer edge. Another vertex may replace that choice only if the hole vertex lies inside its corner (isLocallyInside).

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Build an XYPolygon with the clockwise outer ring (5,1) (0,0) (1,1) (4,5) and two small counter-clockwise triangle holes, A (3,2) (3,1) (4,1) and B (2,2) (4,3) (3,3), strictly inside and touching nothing.
  2. You expect XYShape.createIndexableFields to return 12 triangles with a total area of 9, as it does when the same ring is listed from (4,5) or (1,1).
  3. Instead it throws IllegalArgumentException: Unable to Tessellate shape. Possible malformed shape detected. Listed from (0,0) it fails too, and so do LatLonShape and the doc-values fields: the document cannot be indexed.
  4. Why: hole B is joined first, by a bridge from (2,2) to (1,1) that continues the outer edge (0,0)-(1,1) in a straight line. The ring now passes through (1,1) twice, and only the copy with the straight 180-degree corner faces hole A.
  5. Hole A's leftward ray from (3,1) lands on (1,1) and keeps whichever copy comes first from the ring's start. isLocallyInside returns false for every straight corner (area == 0), so a wrong pick is never corrected: the bridge leaves (1,1) outside that copy's corner, the ring overlaps itself at (1,1) and triangulation fails.

Who is affected

#

Anyone indexing polygons with two or more holes through LatLonShape or XYShape (indexed fields or doc values) since 8.2.0, with default settings and either checkSelfIntersections value. Loud: the document is refused and nothing wrong is indexed. It needs exact collinearity, which grid-snapped or rounded coordinates can produce; generic floating-point data rarely does.

Mechanism, terms and numbers615 words

A valid polygon with two holes that lie strictly inside it and touch nothing is indexed or rejected by XYShape/LatLonShape.createIndexableFields depending only on which vertex its outer ring is listed from: of the four listings of the ring (4,5) (5,1) (0,0) (1,1), two give 12 triangles of exact area 9 and two throw "Unable to Tessellate shape". The first hole's bridge continues an outer edge in a straight line, so the outer vertex (1,1) appears twice in the ring, and only its copy with a straight corner faces the second hole. Tessellator.isLocallyInside returns false for every straight corner, so fetchHoleBridge cannot move the second hole's bridge to that copy when its ray found the other one first. A regression in 8.2.0 (LUCENE-8775); 8.1.1 accepts every listing.

The mechanism (main 5adff78bb168, Tessellator.java; confirmed by a print-only trace)

#
  1. Holes are joined one at a time, sorted by their left-most vertex (:310-323, :325). For each, fetchHoleBridge (:536) picks an outer vertex and splitPolygon (:399, :1175-1199) cuts a two-way bridge, duplicating both ends (:1176-1177).
  2. Hole B goes first (left-most vertex (2,2)) and is bridged to (1,1). (0,0), (1,1) and (2,2) are collinear, so the bridge continues the outer edge (0,0)-(1,1). The ring now holds two copies of (1,1): one between (4,5) and (2,2), whose corner does not contain hole A, and one between (2,2) and (0,0), whose corner is a straight angle; only that copy faces hole A.
  3. Hole A's bridge vertex is (3,1) (its left-most, lowest vertex; fetchLeftmost tie-break :600-601). Step 1 of fetchHoleBridge (:542-560) casts a ray to the left and keeps the nearest hit; on a tie, the first segment in ring order from the start node (x > qx is strict, :549). The ray hits exactly (1,1), so the ring's start decides which copy it keeps (:555). Trace: listings from (4,5) and (1,1) keep the straight copy; listings from (5,1) and (0,0) keep the other one.
  4. Step 2 (:568-591) replaces the step-1 vertex only with one that passes isLocallyInside(p, holeNode) (:584). isLocallyInside (:1251-1268) starts with if (area == 0) { // parallel return false; } (:1255-1257). Trace: the wrong copy is rejected (corner area -1.0, correctly), and the straight copy is rejected too (area 0.0), so the unchecked step-1 vertex stays.
  5. The bridge from the wrong copy runs outside that copy's corner, ear clipping finds no ear and no valid split, and earcutLinkedList throws (:681-688).
Exposure and likelihood184 words
  • Who is hit: applications that index polygons with two or more holes through LatLonShape or XYShape (createIndexableFields and createDocValueField; LatLonShape.java:102,114, XYShape.java:100,112), with the default codec and IndexWriterConfig, either checkSelfIntersections value. One 10-vertex polygon is enough; no scale.
  • Effect: loud. The document's polygon is refused with an IllegalArgumentException; no wrong triangles and no wrong query result were seen. The simplest caller-side workaround is to list the ring from another vertex.
  • Trigger: one hole's bridge must continue an outer edge exactly (its left-most vertex on that edge's line through the bridge end), and a later hole's leftward ray must pass exactly through that bridge end; then the ring's listing decides (2 of 4 listings in the example).
  • Others meet this geometry: five small integer test polygons with this kind of geometry (outer rings of 4 to 11 vertices, coordinates between -9 and 9), published independently by others against earcut (#219, #221), are rejected by Lucene main in 18 of 74 listing/hole-order configurations, by 8.2.0 in 10 and by 8.1.1 in none.
  • Real-world frequency was not measured.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The polygon meets every javadoc requirement: no self-intersections, holes inside, holes touching at most at one vertex, a non-zero area, and no tiny values such as E-200.
  • Re-listed from (4,5) or (1,1) the same ring is accepted; 8.1.1 and earcut accept every listing.
  • The area == 0 branch came with LUCENE-8775, a shared-vertex fix; nothing documents straight corners as unsupported.
The full argument222 words
  • Contract: the class javadoc's notes (Tessellator.java:40-50) list "Requires valid polygons:" with the items "No self intersections", "Holes must be inside the polygon", "Holes may only touch at one vertex", "Polygon must have an area (e.g., no "line" boxes)" and "sensitive to overflow (e.g, subatomic values such as E-200 can cause unexpected behavior)"; small integer coordinates are far from the last one. Polygon.java:31-32: "Coordinates must be in clockwise order, except for holes. Holes must be in counter-clockwise order." The polygon meets all of them without using the "touch at one vertex" allowance, and no contract lets the result depend on where a ring's list starts.
  • History: before LUCENE-8775 a straight corner fell into a strict half-plane test, as in earcut's locallyInside. LUCENE-8775 ("Improve tessellator to handle better cases where a hole share a vertex with the polygon", 8.2.0) added area == 0 -> false; its PR and issue discuss diagonal choice and shared vertices only.
  • Precedent: comparable "Unable to Tessellate" reports on valid polygons were fixed as bugs (#15205 / #15211, #15554 / #15657, #15695 / #15700, #15731 / #15739, #13841 / #13980, ...).
  • Reference behaviour: earcut, whose locallyInside has no such branch (src/earcut.js:738-742 at 934e54d8be), gives area 9 for all four listings and both hole orders; Lucene 8.1.1 accepts every listing.
The devil's-advocate check110 words

The strongest case for "intended" (a devil's-advocate verifier and an independent reviewer from another model family, OpenAI GPT-5.6): the branch is deliberate code, Tessellator is @lucene.internal (:72) and only "inspired by" earcut (:34), the trigger needs exact collinearity, and the failure is loud.

Why it fails: the public XYShape and LatLonShape APIs reach the code with a valid polygon; no javadoc, commit, CHANGES entry or issue documents the limitation; the branch was added for holes that share a vertex, not to reject straight corners; the same ring is accepted when re-listed; and the release before the change handled it. All verifiers rated the "intended" argument weak.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended After step 1 of fetchHoleBridge, move to a copy of the vertex whose corner contains the hole vertex (validated sketch)

Recommended (validated sketch): in fetchHoleBridge, after the ray picks a vertex, if that copy's corner does not contain the hole vertex, move to a coincident copy whose corner does, reading a straight corner as a half-plane as earcut does (+35 lines in Tessellator.java). The test passes, and so do 390 geo and shape tests and :lucene:core:check.

    // an earlier hole's bridge can leave several copies of this vertex: if the angle of the copy
    // found by the ray does not contain the hole vertex, take a copy whose angle does (step 2
    // below never picks a copy with a straight angle)
    if (isSectorContaining(connection, holeNode) == false) {
      Node q = connection.next;
      while (q != connection) {
        if (isVertexEquals(q, connection) && isSectorContaining(q, holeNode)) {
          connection = q;
          break;
        }
        q = q.next;
      }
    }

After step 1 of fetchHoleBridge, move to a copy of the vertex whose corner contains the hole vertex (validated sketch) (recommended)

#

If the vertex found by the ray fails the corner test, walk the ring for a coincident copy that passes it. The test is a private helper, isSectorContaining(a, b): earcut's locallyInside (no area == 0 branch; strict < 0 where isLocallyInside's ccw branch uses <= 0 since #15739), with neighbouring copies of a skipped. isLocallyInside itself, used for diagonals elsewhere, is unchanged.

    // an earlier hole's bridge can leave several copies of this vertex: if the angle of the copy
    // found by the ray does not contain the hole vertex, take a copy whose angle does (step 2
    // below never picks a copy with a straight angle)
    if (isSectorContaining(connection, holeNode) == false) {
      Node q = connection.next;
      while (q != connection) {
        if (isVertexEquals(q, connection) && isSectorContaining(q, holeNode)) {
          connection = q;
          break;
        }
        q = q.next;
      }
    }

Trade-offs

  • A sketch checked at one seed. The extra ring walk runs only when the ray's vertex fails the test (at most one O(n) pass per hole).
  • It touches the same code as LFV-757 (the same area == 0 line, reached there through coincident copies after a shared-vertex merge); this diff leaves LFV-757's polygon failing, and maintainers may prefer one rewrite of copy selection that covers both.

Downstream note

#

Products that index user-supplied polygons through these APIs see a per-document indexing error for such a polygon; other documents and queries are unaffected.

How it was found

#

Bounded translation validation of Tessellator.tessellate on small integer grids: random valid polygons with 2-4 holes that touch nothing were tessellated in every ring listing, hole order and grid symmetry and checked for the exact area. A polygon accepted in one form and rejected in another was reduced to this public-API test.

The method in full120 words

Method: bounded translation validation (the hunt's semi-formal tier): the oracle is the polygon's exact area and acceptance, which must not change under re-listing a ring from another vertex, reordering the holes, or rotating or mirroring the grid. The polygons were drawn on 8x8 and 13x13 integer grids, so every coordinate and area is exact in double.

The failing polygons were then reduced to a public-API JUnit test, separated from the other Tessellator defects by applying their fixes (this one still fails), and checked by an independent reproducer, cross-model reviews (OpenAI GPT-5.6), a devil's-advocate verifier and an adjudication. The finder's population counts (for example 9 of 1,455 polygons on 8x8 rejected in some form) were not re-checked by a verifier.

Possible malformed shape detected.' for some outer-ring start vertices (2 of 4 in the repro, either hole order) and the document cannot be indexed; the failure is loud, the other start vertices give the exact triangulation, no wrong query result was verified, and the trigger needs exact collinearity, which grid-snapped coordinates can produce and generic floating-point coordinates rarely do.
Summary
You index a valid polygon with two holes that touch nothing, for example the clockwise outer ring (4,5) (5,1) (0,0) (1,1) with the counter-clockwise holes (2,2) (4,3) (3,3) and (3,2) (3,1) (4,1), and you expect XYShape or LatLonShape.createIndexableFields to return its triangles whichever vertex the outer ring starts at. The first hole is joined to the ring by a bridge from (2,2) to (1,1) that continues the outer edge (0,0)-(1,1) in a straight line; the second hole's bridge search then lands on (1,1), and the only copy of (1,1) whose angle contains that hole has a straight angle, which Tessellator.isLocallyInside always rejects ('area == 0 -> return false'). When the outer ring starts at (5,1) or (0,0) the search keeps the other copy, the bridge crosses the ring and indexing throws 'Unable to Tessellate shape. Possible malformed shape detected.', while the starts (4,5) and (1,1) give the exact area 9; 8.1.1 accepts every start, 8.2.0 to main reject two of them.
