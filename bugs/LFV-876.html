ThaiCharFilter leaves a typo in place when a zero-width character or a repeated tone mark sits inside it, so ThaiAnalyzer merges words

When two typos ThaiCharFilter documents fixing fall in one Thai character cluster (the tone mark of ที่ typed twice and before its vowel), it repairs only one, so the default ThaiAnalyzer glues the word to its neighbour and a search for บ้าน ("home") misses ฉันทำงานท่่ีบ้าน. New in the unreleased 10.6.0.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

Thai words with two of the documented typos in one character cluster keep one typo, and the word splitter glues them to their neighbours.

  1. Index ฉันทำงานที่บ้าน ("I work at home") with the default new ThaiAnalyzer(). It gives the terms [ฉัน, ทำ, งาน, บ้าน] (ที่, "at", is a stop word). ThaiCharFilter cleans the text before the word splitter sees it.
  2. Type ที่ with one typo: the tone mark twice (ที่่) or before its vowel (ท่ี). The char filter fixes either one, and you still get [ฉัน, ทำ, งาน, บ้าน].
  3. Now make both typos at once: ท + ่ + ่ + ี. You expect the same four terms, and a search for บ้าน to find the document.
  4. Instead the filter outputs ท่ี: it tests "tone before vowel" while the second ่ still sits between them, drops that duplicate one step later and never re-tests the pair. The word splitter then merges: [ฉัน, ทำ, งาน, ที่บ้าน], and a TermQuery for บ้าน finds 0 documents instead of 1.
  5. An invisible zero-width joiner between the two Sara E of แ does the same: ฉันรักเ+ZWJ+เมวมาก ("I love cats a lot") gives [ฉัน, รักแมวมาก] instead of [ฉัน, รัก, แมว]. No error; each typo on its own is fixed.

Who is affected

#

Thai fields on the default ThaiAnalyzer or the thai char filter, in the unreleased 10.6.0 (main, branch_10_6 and its RC1). It takes two documented typos in one character cluster, so it is expected to be rare; then a word and its correctly typed neighbour become one term, silently, at index and query time.

Mechanism, terms and numbers652 words

ThaiCharFilter, new in 10.6.0 and applied by the default ThaiAnalyzer before tokenization, exists because Thai typos such as a double Sara E or a zero-width character stop the JDK dictionary BreakIterator from finding word boundaries. It fixes each documented typo on its own. But it makes one pass and tests its multi-character rules (double Sara E, decomposed Sara Am, tone mark before an above/below vowel) on the raw next characters, while zero-width removal and deduplication act only on the character at the front of its buffer. When a zero-width character or a second copy of a tone mark sits inside such a pattern, the pattern is never rewritten, and the word splitter merges the word with its neighbour: a correctly typed neighbour such as บ้าน becomes unfindable. Silent, deterministic, unreleased (in the 10.6.0 RC1).

Terms

#
  • Tone marks Mai Ek ่ (U+0E48) and Mai Tho ้ (U+0E49) sit above a consonant, after any above/below vowel such as Sara Ii ี (U+0E35): ที่ is ท + ี + ่. Typing the tone before the vowel (ท่ี) or twice (ที่่) is among the "common keyboard typing errors" the issue that proposed the class lists.
  • Sara Ae แ (U+0E41) can be typed as two Sara E เเ (U+0E40 U+0E40), which look the same. Sara Am ำ (U+0E33) is often typed decomposed, as Nikhahit ํ (U+0E4D) + Sara Aa า (U+0E32).
  • Zero-width characters (ZWSP U+200B, ZWJ U+200D, ...) are invisible, so a stray one is easy to miss.
  • The JDK's Thai dictionary BreakIterator finds word boundaries; any of these slips can make it miss one and glue words together. ThaiCharFilter repairs the text first, character by character, so that it does not.

A TermQuery for บ้าน or แมว finds 0 documents instead of 1. A second pass of the char filter cleans every char-filter row.

Why

#

ThaiCharFilter.read() (ThaiCharFilter.java:71-162) looks at the first character of a buffer of up to 4 characters (the current one and up to 3 ahead) and tries, in order: drop it if it is zero-width (:89-95); drop it if it is a diacritic equal to the last character emitted (:97-103); join a double Sara E (:105-113); recompose Sara Am (:115-137); swap a tone with the above/below vowel after it (:139-147); else emit it. The joining, recomposing and swapping rules read the raw next one or two characters. The cleaning rules only run when a character reaches the front, after the character before it has been decided and emitted, and nothing revisits a character already emitted.

Trace for ท ่ ่ ี: ท is emitted. The first ่ is at the front; the next character is the second ่, not a vowel, so no swap; ่ is emitted. The second ่ reaches the front, equals the last emitted character, and is dropped. ี is emitted after the first ่. Output ท ่ ี. For เ ZWJ เ, the join rule sees ZWJ next and emits เ; ZWJ is dropped; the second เ is emitted. For ํ ZWSP า, the Sara Am rule sees ZWSP and emits ํ.

Inside the merged token the spelling looks clean (ที่บ้าน) because ThaiNormalizationFilter runs after the tokenizer; the word boundary is already lost.

Exposure and likelihood329 words
  • Who is exposed. Every Thai field that uses new ThaiAnalyzer() (it wraps the reader in ThaiCharFilter for indexing and for Analyzer.normalize()), and custom chains that put one thai char filter (SPI name of ThaiCharFilterFactory) before a dictionary-based word tokenizer such as the thai tokenizer, as the factory's javadoc example does. No option is involved.
  • What it takes. Two of the documented typos in one character cluster: a tone mark typed twice and before its above/below vowel, or a zero-width character between two Sara E, between a tone and its vowel, or inside a decomposed Sara Am. Each typo alone is fixed. Likelihood: expected to be rare, because two typos must meet in one cluster (a judgement; no frequency was measured).
  • What breaks. The affected word and a neighbour become one term, so queries for either word miss the document. Queries go through the same analyzer, so a query matches the merged term only if its text produces the same merge. No error and valid offsets; IndexWriter accepted all 30,000 random strings of a security probe (no CheckIndex was run); deterministic at any index size.
  • How often in a bounded space. Over 824,691 inputs over 21 symbols (every string of up to 5 symbols that starts with ก, น, ฤ or เ, plus every string of up to 3 symbols), 3,633 inputs made of a clean word plus documented typos keep a pattern the filter's own rules rewrite (approximate: the reference uses a simplified Thai cluster grammar). Over all 813,615 strings of length 1-5 over 15 symbols, removing the zero-width characters changes today's output on 13,282, and typing one diacritic twice changes it on 4,813 of 85,236 checks.
  • Not a regression. 10.5 had no char filter, and the raw JDK segmentation merges the lead sentence too. It is an incomplete new feature, found before its first release.
  • JDK dependence. The word merge depends on the JDK's Thai dictionary BreakIterator (all runs OpenJDK 25.0.3); the char-filter outputs do not.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The javadoc says the filter exists because such typos make the BreakIterator merge adjacent words (ThaiCharFilter.java:27-30); this is that merge.
  • Zero-width removal and deduplication come first in the javadoc and the code, so no documented rule order explains the output.
  • A second pass of the filter gives the clean word, and so does ThaiNormalizer from the same PR for ท่่ี.
The full argument309 words
  • Purpose (ThaiCharFilter.java:27-30): "Applying normalization before tokenization is critical for Thai, because typographic anomalies (such as two consecutive Sara E characters instead of Sara Ae, or zero-width characters) prevent the dictionary-based BreakIterator from locating word boundaries, causing multiple adjacent words to be mistakenly merged into one token."
  • Rules (:32-43): removes zero-width characters, replaces double Sara E with Sara Ae, recomposes decomposed Sara Am, deduplicates consecutive repeated diacritics and tone marks, reorders a misplaced tone mark before an above/below vowel. Each lead output still holds a pattern that one of the multi-character rules (double Sara E, Sara Am, reordering) rewrites.
  • No documented order explains it. Zero-width removal is first in the javadoc and the code; deduplication is before the reordering in both. In ท่่ี the literal input already contains "tone + above vowel" (่ ี) and the output still does. What does explain it is the code's per-position priority: at each position the first matching rule wins and a rejected pair is not re-tested. That is how the code works; no javadoc, test or PR comment says a rule may be skipped because a character between its two halves is about to be deleted.
  • MIGRATE.md (10.5 to 10.6) lists decomposed Sara Am, doubled Sara E, repeated or misordered tone marks and zero-width characters together as input 10.6 now analyzes differently.
  • Tests. The upstream TestThaiCharFilter checks each typo alone, including ฉันรักเเมวมาก -> [ฉัน, รัก, แมว, มาก], the sentence whose ZWJ variant merges; testRandom checks offsets, not normalization. No test asserts today's two-typo output; #16716 / #16717 never discuss rule order.
  • Concession. Two other sub-cases depend on order: Lakkhangyao after Nikhahit (ทํๅ) and a doubled tone inside a decomposed Sara Am (นํ้้า). Read as passes in the javadoc's list order they stay decomposed, and ThaiNormalizer agrees. They are not the lead, and they overlap the sibling ThaiNormalizer bug (LFV-873).
The devil's-advocate check163 words

The strongest case for "works as designed" (the cross-model reviewer and the intended-behaviour checker): the class is a bounded, single-pass typo corrector; its javadoc lists separate repairs and promises neither idempotence nor repair of two typos in one cluster; the upstream tests check one typo at a time; the code's per-position first-match priority explains every output; and the raw JDK segmentation (the pre-10.6 path, no char filter) merges these inputs too.

Why it fails for the lead cases: no documented order explains the output, and the per-position priority is how the code works, not a documented limit; the filter itself deletes the zero-width character or the duplicate and then leaves a pattern its own rule rewrites; the result is the merge the class says it exists to prevent, on the upstream test sentence plus one invisible character; ThaiNormalizer from the same PR normalizes the same inputs (except the ZWJ one: it documents removing only ZWSP and ZWNJ); and nothing documents a single-typo limit.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Run the one-character rules in a first stage (recommended, validated)

Run zero-width removal, deduplication and the Lakkhangyao rule in a small first-stage char filter, so the multi-character rules see cleaned look-ahead (+61/-1). The new test, all 63 Thai tests and the analysis-common suite pass. In an exhaustive check of short strings, an added zero-width character or a repeated mark no longer changes the output (today: 13,282 and 4,813 cases).

public ThaiCharFilter(Reader in) {
  // the one-character rules run first, so read() matches its
  // multi-character patterns against cleaned look-ahead
  super(new SingleCharRules(in));
}

private static final class SingleCharRules extends BaseCharFilter {
  // read(): drop U+200B/U+200C/U+200D/U+FEFF and a diacritic equal to the
  // previous char (addOffCorrectMap), map U+0E45 to U+0E32 unless after
  // U+0E24/U+0E26; read(char[], int, int) loops over read()
}

Trade-offs

  • Also recomposes the two order-dependent sub-cases (ทํๅ -> ทำ, นํ้้า -> น้ำ), where ThaiNormalizer keeps the decomposed form today (LFV-873); fixing that bug the same way keeps the two classes in agreement.
  • Residual: interleaved repeats (ก่ั่ั) and two different tone marks before one vowel still change on a second pass; no documented rule covers two different tone marks.
  • Terms change only for inputs with such a typo pair; no released version has the class, but indexes built with snapshots or the RC already hold the merged terms; if 10.6.0 ships unchanged, indexes built with it need reindexing for those words after the fix (a MIGRATE note).
  • One more Reader layer with a per-character read(); no benchmark was run.

Trade-offs

Offset corrections are keyed by output offset, which is not known when a character is buffered (a later contraction can shift it), so the deletions must be recorded later; more intricate than a second stage.

Workaround: apply the char filter twice

How it was found

#

A bounded-exhaustive check compared the real filter with a reading of its javadoc rules on 824,691 short strings over 21 symbols: 3,633 typo inputs kept a pattern the filter's own rules rewrite, and iterating the filter removed all of them. A sentence-level check then showed the word merge.

The method in full221 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation (the hunt's semi-formal tier), a hunt unit on Thai normalizers, planned from two probes (an idempotence check of ThaiNormalizer and a char-filter versus normalizer differential).

  • The documented rules were encoded as a string-rewriting system; the reference computes every normal form of each input by exhaustive rewriting. The stock filter was compared with it on 824,691 inputs over 21 symbols (including a surrogate pair): every string of up to 5 symbols that starts with ก, น, ฤ or เ, plus every string of up to 3 symbols; with identical counts with assertions on and off.
  • 3,633 inputs made of a clean word plus documented typos, whose documented result is unique, got an output that one of the filter's own rules still rewrites. Iterating the filter to a fixed point brought these 3,633 to 0, which pointed at the single-pass look-ahead.
  • A second leg applied every single and paired typo to 16 real sentences and ran them through the stock ThaiAnalyzer: every leftover of the char filter's own rules (104 variants) came from a pair of typos; no single typo the char filter documents left one. (Single typos of rules that only ThaiNormalizer documents can merge words too; out of scope here.)
  • The finder then wrote a public-API JUnit on the stock main jar (10 of 10 fail).

LFV-876, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
ThaiCharFilter tests its multi-character rules (double Sara E, decomposed Sara Am, tone mark before an above/below vowel) on the raw look-ahead characters, before its own zero-width removal and deduplication have run on them, so a zero-width character or a repeated tone mark inside such a pattern leaves the anomaly in the output and the default ThaiAnalyzer merges the word with its neighbours
Area
analysis-common / Thai (ThaiCharFilter.read single-pass look-ahead; the 'thai' char filter factory and the default ThaiAnalyzer as consumers; new in 10.6.0)
Priority
Low, rank 397: In the unreleased 10.6 ThaiCharFilter used by the default ThaiAnalyzer, words silently merge with their neighbours only when two documented typos fall in one character cluster, so it joins the rare-input analysis rows right after LFV-337.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Thai-language indexes on the unreleased 10.6.0 code (main, branch_10_6 at the 10.6.0 RC1 revision 4ae946e5a9b, branch_10x) that use the default ThaiAnalyzer or the 'thai' char filter are hit when one character cluster carries two of the typos the filter documents fixing, for example a tone mark typed twice and before its vowel (ท่่ี for ที่) or a zero-width character between the two Sara E of แ or inside a decomposed Sara Am: the char filter output keeps the anomaly, the JDK dictionary BreakIterator then merges the word with its neighbours, and a query for a merged word (บ้าน, แมว) misses the document, deterministically at any index size, with no error and a well-formed index; each typo on its own is handled.
Summary
You index Thai text with the default ThaiAnalyzer, which runs ThaiCharFilter before the tokenizer, and expect a word typed with two of the typos the filter documents fixing (ที่ with its tone mark typed twice and before the vowel, ท่่ี, or แมว written as two Sara E with an invisible zero-width joiner between them) to be indexed like the clean word, because the filter's javadoc says it removes zero-width characters, deduplicates repeated tone marks, reorders a tone mark typed before an above/below vowel, joins double Sara E and recomposes decomposed Sara Am precisely so that the BreakIterator does not merge adjacent words. ThaiCharFilter makes one pass and tests its multi-character patterns against the raw next characters, while zero-width removal and deduplication act only on the character at the front of its buffer, after the earlier character has already been emitted, so the output still holds the tone-before-vowel, double Sara E or decomposed Sara Am sequence (ท่ี, เเมว, ทํา), which a second pass of the same filter would clean. The BreakIterator then merges words: ฉันทำงานท่่ีบ้าน is indexed as [ฉัน, ทำ, งาน, ที่บ้าน] instead of [ฉัน, ทำ, งาน, บ้าน], ฉันรักเ + ZWJ + เมวมาก as [ฉัน, รักแมวมาก], and a TermQuery for บ้าน or แมว finds 0 documents.
