QueryBuilder counts a removed leading stop word as a position, so quoted "the dogs" with a same-position synonym becomes a MultiPhraseQuery instead of a SynonymQuery and scores differently

When the query analyzer removes a stop word and stacks a synonym or stem on the next word, QueryBuilder turns quoted "the dogs" into a one-position MultiPhraseQuery instead of the SynonymQuery that "dogs" gives. Same matches, different ranking (doc dogs: 0.2774 vs 0.7135 in a five-document probe), since 6.0.0.

The finding in 30 seconds

#
  1. Your query analyzer removes the stop word the and stacks dog on dogs at the same position (here a synonym filter; KeywordRepeatFilter plus a stemmer is another trigger, with its own numbers below). The index holds five documents, analyzed with StandardTokenizer alone: dogs, dog, dog cat, dog bird, cat.
  2. Search the quoted phrase "dogs" with createPhraseQuery or the classic QueryParser: you get SynonymQuery(dogs, dog), 4 hits, and docs dogs and dog tie at 0.2774 in this setup, as synonyms should.
  3. You expect "the dogs" to give the same: once the is removed only one position is left, and QueryBuilder makes a phrase only "when terms occur at more than one position".
  4. You get MultiPhraseQuery field:"(dogs dog)". It matches the same 4 documents, but doc dogs now scores 0.7135 while doc dog stays at 0.2774. "dogs the" still gives the SynonymQuery.
  5. Why: QueryBuilder adds up position increments instead of counting positions. The removed the leaves a hole, so dogs arrives with increment 2 and one position counts as two. A one-position MultiPhraseQuery rewrites to a plain OR of term queries, each scored with its own IDF.

Who is affected

#

Applications whose query analyzer leaves a stop-word hole in front of stacked tokens: SynonymGraphFilter before StopFilter, or StopFilter followed by KeywordRepeatFilter plus a stemmer, or by ASCIIFoldingFilter with preserveOriginal. Reached through createPhraseQuery and quoted text in the classic and simple query parsers. StopFilter then SynonymGraphFilter is not affected. Ranking changes; matches do not.

Mechanism, terms and numbers617 words

QueryBuilder.createFieldQuery, which createPhraseQuery, the classic and simple query parsers use, decides between a phrase and a synonym query by the number of positions in the analyzed text: its quoted parameter means "phrases should be generated when terms occur at more than one position". It gets that number by adding up position increments (QueryBuilder.java:321), so the hole a StopFilter leaves for a removed leading word counts as a position. Quoted "the dogs", where dog is stacked on dogs, then becomes the one-position MultiPhraseQuery field:"(dogs dog)" instead of SynonymQuery(dogs, dog), the query "dogs" and "dogs the" get. The MultiPhraseQuery rewrites to a plain disjunction of term queries, so the same documents match but the stacked terms are scored separately: with default BM25, doc dogs scores 0.2774 under "dogs" and 0.7135 under "the dogs", while doc dog stays at 0.2774. It needs a query analyzer that leaves a stop-word hole in front of stacked tokens (for example SynonymGraphFilter before StopFilter, or StopFilter then KeywordRepeatFilter and a stemmer); the common StopFilter then SynonymGraphFilter order is not affected. Affected: 6.0.0 to 10.6.0 and main; a one-line fix (count positions, not increments) was validated.

Terms

#
  • Position increment: each token carries the distance to the previous token's position. A normal next word has 1; a synonym or variant stacked on the same position has 0; a word after a removed stop word has 2 (the StopFilter leaves a hole).
  • SynonymQuery: scores several terms "as if you had indexed them as one term" (SynonymQuery.java:47-49): one pseudo-term with the larger document frequency of the terms and their summed total term frequency (SynonymQuery.java:210-226). So in the example below docs dogs and dog score alike.
  • One-position MultiPhraseQuery: a multi-phrase with a single term array. Its rewrite ("optimize one-term case") is a BooleanQuery of SHOULD TermQuerys and drops the position (MultiPhraseQuery.java:182-191): each term keeps its own IDF.

The bug

#
  1. Phase 1 of createFieldQuery is "counting the number of tokens/positions" (QueryBuilder.java:308-309): for every token with a non-zero increment, positionCount += positionIncrement (:320-321); an increment of 0 sets hasSynonyms (:322-323).
  2. For "the dogs" the tokens are dogs (increment 2) and dog (increment 0): numTokens 2, positionCount 2, hasSynonyms true. For "dogs the" the hole is only in end(), so positionCount is 1.
  3. Phase 2: quoted && positionCount > 1 with synonyms goes to analyzeMultiPhrase (:347-351); positionCount == 1 would have gone to analyzeBoolean (:358-360), which returns newSynonymQuery (:395). A single token (numTokens == 1) goes to analyzeTerm first (:337-339), so plain stop-word phrases are not affected.
  4. The resulting MultiPhraseQuery holds one term array at position 1 and rewrites to dogs OR dog. The hole adds no constraint; it only changes the scoring model. The rare term dogs (document frequency 1 in the example) now gets its own, higher IDF.

The numbers

#

Five documents dogs, dog, dog cat, dog bird, cat, default similarity; the index is analyzed with StandardTokenizer alone, the query with StandardTokenizer, StopFilter for the and MockSynonymFilter (the repro's two filters): quoted "dogs" scores dogs 0.27742466, dog 0.27742466, dog cat and dog bird 0.20845169; quoted "the dogs" matches the same 4 documents and scores dogs 0.7135338, the others unchanged. With production filters only, the same analyzer at index and query time (StandardTokenizer, LowerCaseFilter, English StopFilter, KeywordRepeatFilter, PorterStemFilter, RemoveDuplicatesTokenFilter, the classic QueryParser): doc dogs 0.20100057 and doc dog 0.15446018 under "dogs" and "dogs the", doc dogs 0.8987793 under "the dogs". How far and in which direction a score moves depends on the corpus statistics and the similarity.

Exposure and likelihood211 words

Why it looks unintended

#
  • Contract: phrases are generated "when terms occur at more than one position" (QueryBuilder.java:241); here the terms occupy one.
  • TestQueryBuilder pins quoted "dogs" to SynonymQuery, "dogs the" gets it too, and PhraseQuery documents leading holes as meaningless.
  • The one-position MultiPhraseQuery drops its position on rewrite: the hole adds no constraint, only another scoring model.
The full argument241 words
  • Contract. @param quoted "true if phrases should be generated when terms occur at more than one position" (QueryBuilder.java:241, :286); phase 1 says it counts "tokens/positions" (:309).
  • Test. TestQueryBuilder.testSynonyms asserts createPhraseQuery("field", "dogs") equals SynonymQuery(dogs, dog) (TestQueryBuilder.java:144-156). No test anywhere asserts the leading-hole outcome.
  • Leading holes carry no meaning. PhraseQuery: "Leading holes don't have any particular meaning for this query and will be ignored" (PhraseQuery.java:52), from LUCENE-6255 (#7317, 5.1.0). There Robert Muir objected to an exception for leading holes: "I don't think its their fault if they type \"the query\", and the search engine has a stopword filter in the chain. It will confuse them, they dont get an error with \"query the\"." Here too, "dogs the" and "the dogs" behave differently.
  • History. The sum comes from the classic QueryParser (2ce046a7a6d, 2005: "Make QueryParser work with analyzers that return tokens with a position increment > 1"); the goal was to stop treating an increment > 1 as a synonym, and its test covers only multi-position text. LUCENE-5261 (2013, 4.6.0) moved it into QueryBuilder. Up to 5.5.5 both branches built the same BooleanQuery, so the wrong branch was invisible. a944ab17a71 (LUCENE-6789, 2015) switched the single-position boolean branch (and stacked positions in boolean queries) to SynonymQuery but left MultiPhraseQuery's one-position rewrite a BooleanQuery; the issue never mentions phrases, stop words or holes, and the commit's own tests expect a SynonymQuery for single-position quoted text.
The devil's-advocate check153 words

The strongest case for "intended": createPhraseQuery's @return lists MultiPhraseQuery (QueryBuilder.java:118-119); setEnablePositionIncrements says "result phrase and multi-phrase queries will be aware of position increments. Useful when e.g. a StopFilter increases the position increment of the token that follows an omitted token" (:201-203); the user asked for a phrase; the sum is 20 years old; and Lucene does not promise equal scores for different query texts. A cross-model reviewer rated it moderate.

Why it fails: a one-position MultiPhraseQuery drops its position on rewrite, so the hole gives no positional meaning at all; PhraseQuery documents leading holes as meaningless; the trailing-hole text gets the SynonymQuery; and even with setEnablePositionIncrements(false), whose purpose is to ignore increments, the hole still selects the MultiPhraseQuery (executed). Verdict: not intended. A maintainer may still prefer another fix site (MultiPhraseQuery's rewrite) or call a score-only defect low priority; that is about where and when, not whether.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Count positions, not increments (recommended, validated)

Count positions instead of adding increments: positionCount++ instead of positionCount += positionIncrement (QueryBuilder.java:321). Only text with a single occupied position after a leading hole changes; it then gets the SynonymQuery, as without the stop word. Validated: the repro passes, and so do TestQueryBuilder, the queryparser and highlighter test tasks and :lucene:core:check -x test.

All options and trade-offs3 options · 485 words

From reading the code of QueryBuilder as shipped: positionCount is read only in the two branch tests (QueryBuilder.java:347, :358). The count and the sum differ only when an increment is greater than 1, and the branch changes only when exactly one position holds terms after a leading hole. Then quoted text gets the SynonymQuery, and unquoted text the bare SynonymQuery instead of a one-clause BooleanQuery around it (same scores). Token boosts on that path are kept as a side effect, because analyzeBoolean carries them and analyzeMultiPhrase does not (a separate finding, LFV-584). Subclasses that override newSynonymQuery, newMultiPhraseQueryBuilder or the protected analyze* methods may see other query types change. LFV-829 (the classic Highlighter counts a MultiPhraseQuery's leading positions as gaps) is related but independent: it needs two or more positions (quoted "the dogs bark"), which this fix leaves a MultiPhraseQuery with the same highlight, and for the one-position query of this bug the Highlighter already marks the same words as for the SynonymQuery (executed). Neither fix affects the other.

Trade-offs

Scores of affected phrase queries change on purpose, back to what the same text without the stop word gives: a CHANGES entry with a note on the score change. Unquoted text with a leading hole gets a bare SynonymQuery instead of a one-clause BooleanQuery, a compatibility point for code that inspects query classes. The flexible parser's identical sum (AnalyzerQueryNodeProcessor.java:137) shows no score difference today and can be left or changed for consistency. Downstream copies of this logic are not reached.

How it was found

#

A proof-obligation audit of QueryBuilder.createFieldQuery's contract: the obligation that quoted text becomes a phrase only when its terms occupy more than one position was checked against phase 1, which adds increments instead of counting positions. A one-test JUnit on the stock main jars witnessed the gap, and a BM25 probe measured its effect.

The method in full86 words

Method (semi-formal): state the documented obligation of the quoted parameter ("phrases should be generated when terms occur at more than one position"), find the code that decides it (positionCount in phase 1, the tests at :347 and :358), and look for an input that satisfies the precondition's wording but not the code's: one occupied position whose increment sum is 2. The witness is the leading stop word in front of a stacked token, run on the stock main jars with assertions on and off.

Summary

You expect the SynonymQuery(dogs, dog) that "dogs" and "dogs the" give, because the remaining terms sit at one position and QueryBuilder generates phrases only 'when terms occur at more than one position'; you get the one-position MultiPhraseQuery field:"(dogs dog)", which rewrites to a plain disjunction of the two terms.
