The finding in 30 seconds

#

Each of the three classes shows a short block headed "Example usage" in its class javadoc. The blocks are text inside a comment, which the compiler does not read; Lucene's javadoc build renders them as they are. #16962 made the constructor private and changed 18 files, none of the three.

Two things to know first

#
  1. MatchAllDocsQuery matches every document. Since 10.4.0 it has a shared instance, MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE, and its no-argument constructor is deprecated in favour of it. On branch_11x and main the constructor is private.
  2. A collector manager is handed to IndexSearcher.search(query, manager), which returns the manager's result. Each of the three examples shows that one call, with a match-all query as the query.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. You read the class javadoc of AllGroupHeadsCollectorManager (grouping), DocValuesStatsCollectorManager (misc) or LargeNumHitsTopDocsCollectorManager (sandbox) and take its "Example usage" block into your code. Its first line is an elision, IndexSearcher searcher = ...; // your IndexSearcher, so you supply your own searcher.
  2. You expect the block to compile. Against the 10.6.0 release jars it does: the one test of this page, which hands the three blocks to javac in one source, passes 1 of 1, with assertions on and off. There the constructor is public and already deprecated: javac compiles the call and notes a deprecated API.
  3. Against builds of branch_11x (262e019500ec) and main (b5a4546a5964), both commits of 2026-11-22 and both unreleased, the same test fails 1 of 1, with assertions on and off: three javac errors, one per block, each MatchAllDocsQuery() has private access in org.apache.lucene.search.MatchAllDocsQuery.
  4. The private constructor is deliberate and is not the defect. #16962 made it private on both lines on 2026-11-03, and the migration guide says the call "has to be replaced by" MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE. The three javadoc lines were left as they were; javadoc text is not compiled.
  5. With MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE in its three calls the test passes on both builds and on 10.6.0. No release has the defect: 11.0.0 would be the first if branch_11x is released with the three lines as they were on 2026-11-21.

Who is affected

#

A developer who adopts one of these three opt-in collector managers from its class javadoc on a build of branch_11x or main. They get a compile error that names the constructor; nothing wrong runs. One class is marked experimental and one is in the sandbox module. No release is affected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers748 words

Three classes show a short block headed "Example usage" in their class javadoc: AllGroupHeadsCollectorManager (grouping module), DocValuesStatsCollectorManager (misc module) and LargeNumHitsTopDocsCollectorManager (sandbox module). Each block searches with new MatchAllDocsQuery(). On branch_11x and main that constructor is private since #16962 (2026-11-03), on purpose: the change is listed in CHANGES.txt, and the migration guide of both lines says that the call has to be replaced by MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE. The three javadoc lines were left as they were. A reader who takes one of the blocks into code built against either line, and supplies the searcher that the block's first line elides, gets one javac error at the block's search call. With MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE in that call the block compiles on both lines and on 10.6.0.

This is a documentation finding and nothing wider. The private constructor is deliberate and is not the defect. Nothing wrong runs: no search result, no index content and no run-time exception comes from the three lines. The code is on branch_11x (11.0.0-SNAPSHOT) and main (12.0.0-SNAPSHOT) and in no release; on 10.5.0, 10.5.1, 10.5.2 and 10.6.0, the four releases that have the three examples, the same blocks compile and run with the elided searcher supplied. On each of the four the call is already deprecated: javac, with its default options, compiles it and notes a deprecated API.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The private constructor is intended. The claim is only the three lines, which show the call the migration guide says "has to be replaced".
  • The blocks are offered as usage: reviewing #15653, which added one, javanna asked for "a usage example for users".
  • With MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE in the three calls the test passes on both lines and on 10.6.0.
The full argument308 words

The code is intended: #16962 made the constructor private on purpose and announced it. The claim is only that three javadoc lines still show the call.

  • The same line says the opposite. The migration guide of branch_11x and of main says that new MatchAllDocsQuery() "has to be replaced by" MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE; the class javadoc of the same line shows new MatchAllDocsQuery() under "Example usage".
  • The blocks are offered as usage, and the review of one of them looked at whether it compiles. In the review of #15653, the merged pull request that added the misc example, javanna wrote on 2026-04-14: "Could we expand javadocs to include a usage example for users?" and, at the example's search line, on 2026-05-11: "I believe this usage example won't compile." The author answered: "Changed it to include the T type." By that answer and by the line the remark stands on, it was about another error, a missing type argument, nearly six months before the constructor became private. Both remarks are javanna's. They are one person's review remarks on one of the three examples, not a stated rule.
  • Nothing in the threads that were read says the lines were kept on purpose. #16962 changed 18 files, none of the three. Its description, its two review comments (on a benchmark file) and its three reviews, read on 2026-11-22, do not mention a javadoc example.
  • Upstream repairs examples of this kind. #16508, "Fix errors in grouping package-info javadocs" (merged 2026-08-13), and #16904, "docs: update collation package examples to current APIs." (merged 2026-11-02), are merged repairs of other stale examples.
  • With the token changed, javac rejects nothing else. The control test, which is the test of this page with MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE in its three calls (three lines differ), passes on main b5a4546a5964, on branch_11x 262e019500ec and on the 10.6.0 release jars, assertions on and off.
The devil's-advocate check224 words

The strongest case for "not a defect", in four points. (1) A <pre> block whose first line is an elision is an illustration, not a program: pasted whole, a block is rejected by javac on 10.6.0 as well, and Lucene does not compile its javadoc examples (#9534, opened in 2018 and open when read on 2026-11-22, asks for that). (2) The change is deliberate and announced, and the announcement names the replacement. (3) The error is loud and names the constructor. (4) One class is experimental and one is in the sandbox module.

Why it does not settle the matter: all four are true, and they defeat a claim that the constructor change is wrong, which this page does not make. They do not make the three lines usable as shown: the blocks are headed "Example usage", one of them was asked for as "a usage example for users" and drew the remark, in its own review, that it would not compile, and DocValuesStatsCollectorManager carries no experimental marker. What the records carry is this much: the three lines show the call that the migration guide of the same line says has to be replaced, and nothing that was read says they were kept on purpose. It is a small finding, and a maintainer may well fold it into a wider pass over the examples.

Suggested fix

#

No patch is proposed. The repair, as a shape: the same token in each of the three blocks, MatchAllDocsQuery.INSTANCE in place of new MatchAllDocsQuery(), as the migration guide says. As run: with it the blocks compile on both lines and on 10.6.0, and the javadoc build and the lint of the three modules pass. The maintainers choose the form.
