On WGS84, geo3d path end caps are shifted up to about 21 km toward the equator

On WGS84, geo3d path end caps sit up to about 21 km too close to the equator. A 20 km path from (44N,20E) to (45N,20E) misses documents 12 and 16 km past its north end and matches documents 30 and 40 km past its south end.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

A geo3d path is a corridor plus a round cap at each end. On WGS84 the cap at an end whose segment runs north-south is centred about 21 km (at 45N) toward the equator, so the poleward cap falls short and the equatorward cap overshoots.

  1. You index points with Geo3DPoint on WGS84 and search the path (44N,20E) to (45N,20E) with Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery(..., 20000, WGS84), sorting with Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort.
  2. You expect a 20 km corridor with a round 20 km cap at each end: documents 12 and 16 km north of the last point match, documents 30 and 40 km south of the first point do not.
  3. You get the reverse at both ends: 12 and 16 km north are missed (sort value +Infinity), and 30 and 40 km south are matched (sort values 30023 and 40034 m). No error.
  4. Why: each cap is a plane slice through the end segment's two corners, facing the end point's direction from the earth's centre. On the flattened earth that direction is 'straight up' only at a point 21 km nearer the equator, so the cap is centred there: it reaches 8 km past the north end and 50 km past the south end.
  5. The shift does not depend on the width: a 1 km path matches points 42.8 km past its start. In the tested controls, east-west ends, the corridor sides and spheres are right. All 16 releases run, from 5.3.0 (2015) to 10.5.2, and main are affected.

Who is affected

#

Applications that use lucene-spatial3d path queries or path sorts (Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery, Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort, newOutsidePathSort) on WGS84 or another ellipsoid, whenever an end segment runs partly north-south away from the equator. One document is enough; only documents near the path ends are affected. Spheres and east-west ends are fine.

Mechanism, terms and numbers769 words

Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery and Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort build a GeoStandardPath: a corridor of the given width along the path, closed at each end by a cap that is round on a sphere. Each end cap is the part of the planet cut off by one plane through the two corners of the end segment, whose normal is constrained to the plane through the corners and the earth's centre. For an end segment that runs north-south that normal is the end point's direction from the centre. On a sphere that is the surface normal, so the cap is centred on the end point; on the WGS84 ellipsoid it is the surface normal of a point about 0.19 degrees (21.4 km at 44-45N) closer to the equator, so the cap circle is centred there while still passing through the corners. With pathWidthMeters 20000 on the path (44N,20E) to (45N,20E), documents 12 and 16 km north of the last point are missed (newPathSort +Infinity) and documents 30 and 40 km south of the first point are matched (30023 and 40034 m). The caps reach 7.8 km past the poleward end and 50.5 km past the equatorward end; a 1 km path reaches 42.8 km past its start. Nothing throws. All 16 releases run, from 5.3.0 (the first with WGS84) to 10.5.2, and main are affected; the releases in between have the same construction.

Terms

#
  • geo3d path (lucene-spatial3d): a GeoStandardPath, built by Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery, Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort / newOutsidePathSort and GeoPathFactory.makeGeoPath. It is meant to be a corridor of half-width w along the segments between the points, closed past the first and last points by a cap (on a sphere, a round cap of radius w), with joints at turns. pathWidthMeters is used as w, the distance from the path (Geo3DUtil.java:139).
  • End cap: geo3d represents it as the part of the planet on one side of one plane, a plane slice, kept only beyond the end segment's cutoff plane. The plane goes through the segment's two corners, so corridor and cap meet.
  • WGS84: the ellipsoid model (a = 6378137 m, b = 6356752.314245 m). On it, 'straight up' (the surface normal) and the direction from the earth's centre differ by up to 0.19 degrees, at 45 degrees latitude.

The bug

#
  1. GeoStandardPath.done() makes the first and last points CutoffSingleCircleSegmentEndpoints, with the end segment's cutoff plane and two corners (GeoStandardPath.java:192-241).
  2. The cap plane is SidedPlane.constructSidedPlaneFromTwoPoints(point, upper, lower) (GeoStandardPath.java:997-1007), that is Plane.constructPerpendicularCenterPlaneTwoPoints (Plane.java:250-260): a plane through the two corners, perpendicular to the plane through the corners and the origin.
  3. For a north-south end segment the corners are mirror images east and west of the end point, so the cap plane's normal is the end point's direction from the centre.
  4. A plane cuts an ellipsoid in an ellipse whose centre lies inside the earth, on the line from the earth's centre to the surface point whose surface normal is parallel to the plane's normal; that point is the cap's apex, and the cap is centred on it. For (45N, 20E) it is at 44.81N: 0.19 degrees, 21.4 km toward the equator (18.6 km at 60N, zero at the equator and the poles). That holds for oblate models such as WGS84 and Clarke 1866; on a prolate model (b > a, which PlanetModel also accepts) the same formula moves the apex poleward (not run).
  5. The circle still passes through the corners, w east and west of the end point, so its radius is about sqrt(21.4^2 + w^2) km. Past a poleward end it reaches sqrt(21.4^2 + w^2) - 21.4 km; past an equatorward end 21.4 + sqrt(21.4^2 + w^2) km.

The numbers

#
  • Path (44N,20E) to (45N,20E), w = 20 km: the north cap reaches 7.8 km, the south cap 50.5 km (the flat sketch above gives 7.9 and 50.7).
  • w = 5 km: 0.55 km and 43.3 km; w = 1 km: 0.02 km and 42.75 km; w = 200 km: 0.89 w and 1.11 w.
  • The reversed path and the southern mirror (44S to 45S) behave the same at their poleward and equatorward ends; a north-east end segment gives 0.50 w and 2.02 w; at 60N 0.44 w and 2.29 w.
  • Controls within about 1% of w, in the tested cases: a sphere (1.00 w), east-west end segments (1.01 w), the corridor sides (0.995 w), one-point paths and interior joints at 90-degree turns (0.99 w). A path from the equator to 1N gives 0.99 w at its equator end and 0.95 w at its 1N end, where the shift is already about 0.7 km.
Exposure and likelihood345 words
  • Who is hit. Users of the lucene-spatial3d path query, path sort and outside-path sort, or of GeoPathFactory.makeGeoPath, with PlanetModel.WGS84, CLARKE_1866 or another oblate model (a prolate model is mis-centred the other way). Also, by reading, spatial-extras Geo3dShapeFactory buffered line strings when the context factory is configured with planetModel wgs84 or clarke1866 (other spellings, clarke_1866 included, throw "Unknown planet model"), or with its public planetModel field set to PlanetModel.WGS84 or CLARKE_1866; its default is the sphere, which is not affected.
  • Default vs opt-in. Opt-in by module and API (Geo3DPoint and GeoPath are @lucene.experimental). Within it the trigger is ordinary: WGS84 is what Geo3DPoint's 3-argument constructor uses, and newPathQuery / newPathSort take the PlanetModel explicitly, so a caller passes WGS84 to match points indexed that way; any route with a north-south end at mid latitudes then hits it. Index size does not matter.
  • Effect. Wrong matches and wrong sort values near the path ends: documents within the width past a poleward end are dropped (sort value +Infinity, which marks "points outside of the shape"), documents far past an equatorward end match with finite sort values. For a 20 km path the poleward cap keeps 39% of its straight-out reach and the equatorward cap reaches 2.5 times the width; for narrow paths the equatorward cap reaches about 43 km whatever the width. Nothing throws, nothing is written.
  • Where it is small. Near the equator and the poles (on a path from the equator to 1N the reach is 0.99 w at the equator end and already 0.95 w at the 1N end), and, in the tested cases, for east-west end segments, along the corridor sides, at 90-degree interior joints and for one-point paths. The shift is a fixed distance (about 21 km at mid latitudes), so wide paths suffer less: 0.89 w and 1.11 w at 200 km.
  • How likely. The module is opt-in and only the path ends are affected; the recorded search found no report since 5.3.0 (2015), and no upstream test checks points past a path end on WGS84.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The class promises "a specified half-width"; newPathQuery states no error, while newDistanceQuery allows 0.5% on WGS84.
  • Corridor sides, east-west caps and one-point paths stay within 1% when tested; only end caps of north-south segments move.
  • LUCENE-6544: "Circles are now always centered on their center point, no matter how significantly ellipsoid the planet is" (about circles and one-point paths).
The full argument276 words
  • Contract. GeoStandardPath: "GeoShape representing a path across the surface of the globe, with a specified half-width. Path is described by a series of points. Distances are measured from the starting point along the path, and then at right angles to the path." (GeoStandardPath.java:29-32). The end components are "Endpoint that's a simple circle" (:990) and "Endpoint that's a single circle with cutoff(s)" (:1084). newPathQuery: "@param pathWidthMeters width of the path in meters." with no error statement (Geo3DPoint.java:192-213); newDistanceQuery: "if PlanetModel#WGS84 is used, the query is approximate and may have up to 0.5% error" (:112-113).
  • Size. For a 20 km path 61% of the poleward cap's straight-out reach is lost and the equatorward cap reaches 2.5 times the width; a 1 km path matches documents 42.8 km away.
  • History. Before 5.3.0 the cap was new SidedPlane(point, point, -originDistance), exact on the unit sphere. LUCENE-6487 replaced it with the two-corner plane so the corridor would "line up with the circles at the waypoints"; the cap's position was not discussed. LUCENE-6544 (#7602): "Circles are now always centered on their center point, no matter how significantly ellipsoid the planet is." That re-anchored circles and the one-point path endpoint, not multi-point path ends, and left this end cap as it was. LUCENE-8696's commit: "Rework how endpoint circles are represented to allow for consistency on WGS84".
  • Tests. No upstream test asserts end-cap extent on WGS84 (TestGeoPath's WGS84 tests cover bounds, a degenerate-path failure, LUCENE-8696 and identical points); the random query tests compare the query with the shape's own isWithin, so a wrong shape agrees with itself.
The devil's-advocate check314 words

Strongest case for "intended" (rated strong by the reviews from another model family, OpenAI GPT-5.6, and argued in full by a devil's advocate): geo3d builds shapes from plane slices and is approximate on ellipsoids; GeoStandardCircle says a one-plane circle "is a circle for spherical planets and an ellipse otherwise"; the private SegmentEndpoint javadoc says an endpoint "is not necessarily a circle" and case (2) "must be constructed to go through two supplied points and be perpendicular to a connecting plane"; constructPerpendicularCenterPlaneTwoPoints is "useful for building path endpoints on worlds that can be ellipsoids"; GeoStandardCircle notes "In an ellipsoidal world, cutoff distances make no sense, unfortunately. Only membership can be used to make in/out determination."; the classes are experimental or internal; and the geo3d author wrote in LUCENE-8696 that retrofitting paths to WGS84 "involved compromises".

Why it still fails: the private javadoc does pin this plane (a plane through both corners and perpendicular to the plane through them and the origin is unique), so the code does what it says, and fix A would change that javadoc line. But the javadoc fixes a construction, chosen in LUCENE-6487 so the corridor lines up with the end circles; where the cap then sits on an ellipsoid was not discussed. The class promises a half-width, and the corridor sides and one-point paths keep it. An ellipse is fine; GeoStandardCircle is one on WGS84, but anchored on its centre point. Its "only membership can be used" comment is about distance values; here membership itself is wrong. The "compromises" remark was about a random-test path with a 1.34 rad (77-degree) half-width and about joints. A 1 km path matching documents 42.8 km away is hard to read as an accepted approximation. Verdict: not intended, a defect, with a strong case the other way, so it is best filed as a mismatch between the half-width contract and the end-cap construction.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A: three-point cap plane through the corners and the cap tip

Build each end cap plane through the two corners and the cap tip, one half-width past the end point, with the existing three-point plane helper (+46/-1 in GeoStandardPath.java). In every sampled case each cap then reaches 0.99-1.00 of the width; the new test, all 183 spatial3d tests, the spatial-extras geo3d tests and the module checks pass.

super(planetModel, previous, point,
    endCapPlane(planetModel, point, cutoffPlane, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint));
...
// t = unit normal of the cutoff plane, away from the segment; u = point / |point|
final double alpha =
    0.5 * (point.arcDistance(topEdgePoint) + point.arcDistance(bottomEdgePoint));
final GeoPoint tip = planetModel.createSurfacePoint(
    ux * Math.cos(alpha) + tx * Math.sin(alpha), /* y, z likewise */ ...);
final SidedPlane plane = SidedPlane.constructNormalizedThreePointSidedPlane(
    point, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint, tip);
return plane != null
    ? plane
    : SidedPlane.constructSidedPlaneFromTwoPoints(point, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint);

A: three-point cap plane through the corners and the cap tip (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In CutoffSingleCircleSegmentEndpoint, compute the tip (the surface point at the mean corner angle past the end point, along the outward normal of the cutoff plane) and build the cap with SidedPlane.constructNormalizedThreePointSidedPlane, the helper interior joints already use at turns; fall back to today's plane if that helper returns null (nearly collinear points, or the inside point on the plane). The finder's sketch, reworded and tidied.

super(planetModel, previous, point,
    endCapPlane(planetModel, point, cutoffPlane, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint));
...
// t = unit normal of the cutoff plane, away from the segment; u = point / |point|
final double alpha =
    0.5 * (point.arcDistance(topEdgePoint) + point.arcDistance(bottomEdgePoint));
final GeoPoint tip = planetModel.createSurfacePoint(
    ux * Math.cos(alpha) + tx * Math.sin(alpha), /* y, z likewise */ ...);
final SidedPlane plane = SidedPlane.constructNormalizedThreePointSidedPlane(
    point, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint, tip);
return plane != null
    ? plane
    : SidedPlane.constructSidedPlaneFromTwoPoints(point, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint);

Trade-offs

  • A few trig calls per path end at construction; not benchmarked.
  • On a sphere the tip lies on the same circle, so the plane is today's up to rounding (the sphere control and probe give 1.00 w).
  • TestGeoPath.testPathBounds passes; on its extreme test planet the bounds still contain every sampled path point (0 of 120,359 grid points outside).
  • The tip is the end point's radial direction rotated by the mean corner angle and projected to the surface, not a point at exactly the half-width along a geodesic: 1.00 w in every sampled case, but no proof for all geometries. The fallback is today's plane. The private SegmentEndpoint javadoc's case (2) would need rewording.

B: take the cap plane's normal from the surface normal at the end point (validated by the checks)

#

Keep the plane through the two corners, but use the surface normal at the end point, (x / a^2, y / a^2, z / b^2), minus its component along the corner chord. +31/-1, no trigonometry; on a sphere it is exactly today's plane.

final double nx = point.x * planetModel.inverseXYScalingSquared; // ny likewise
final double nz = point.z * planetModel.inverseZScalingSquared;
final double dx = topEdgePoint.x - bottomEdgePoint.x;            // dy, dz likewise
final double t = (nx * dx + ny * dy + nz * dz) / (dx * dx + dy * dy + dz * dz);
final Vector normal = new Vector(nx - t * dx, ny - t * dy, nz - t * dz).normalize();
if (normal == null) {
  return SidedPlane.constructSidedPlaneFromTwoPoints(point, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint);
}
return new SidedPlane(point, normal, -normal.dotProduct(topEdgePoint));

Trade-offs

  • Breaks one upstream assertion: TestGeoPath.testPathBounds (TestGeoPath.java:256, assertTrue(area.isWithin(point))). On its extreme planet (a = 0.75, b = 1.25, width 0.75 rad) the test point lies outside the path on stock and with both fixes; with B the bounds no longer include it, but they still contain every sampled path point (0 of 129,751). So the assertion looks like it pins old bounds rather than correctness, but a finite grid does not prove the new bounds complete; adopting B means changing it after a bounds review.
  • Reach 0.99-1.00 w, the reach of the corridor sides.

What the checks validated

Downstream note

#

An application that uses a geo3d path query as a route geofence, for example for eligibility along a route, admits documents tens of km past an equatorward end and drops some within the width of a poleward end.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal: a bounded-exhaustive harness recomputed geo3d path membership with an independent 50-digit reference and flagged 114 points robustly inside a WGS84 end cap that the path called outside, all at the poleward end. A sweep over latitude, heading and width showed the pattern; reading the cap construction gave the cause.

The method in full117 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation (the semi-formal tier), in a hunt unit on geo3d distance functions against a reference.

  • A harness recomputed the documented path decomposition (corridor, end caps, joints) with an independent 50-digit BigDecimal reference and compared GeoStandardPath.computeDistance with it. Check "inside membership" flagged 114 points per distance style that the reference puts robustly inside an end cap of a WGS84 path but for which the path returned +Infinity, all at the poleward end vertex.
  • A targeted sweep over latitude, heading and width showed the pattern; reading the cap construction gave the cause, and a fix sketch removed it.
  • Verifiers then corrected the first write-up: the equatorward cap over-includes, so it is a shift, not a truncation.

Bug record

LFV-736, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
On WGS84, geo3d path end caps are shifted toward the equator, so Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery and Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort miss documents just beyond a poleward path end (sort value +Infinity) and match documents tens of km beyond an equatorward end
Area
spatial3d / geom path membership (GeoStandardPath end caps; Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery, Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort)
Priority
Medium, rank 160: Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery and newPathSort on the default WGS84 model silently drop or add documents tens of km from ordinary north-south path ends, the same kind of matching error in the little-used spatial3d module as LFV-209.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that run geo3d path queries or path sorts (Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery, Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort or newOutsidePathSort, GeoPathFactory.makeGeoPath, and spatial-extras Geo3dShapeFactory buffered line strings) on WGS84, Geo3DPoint's default planet model, or any other non-spherical model are hit with ordinary data at any index size whenever the path's first or last segment has a north-south component away from the equator and the poles: documents within the width just beyond the poleward end are silently dropped (newPathSort gives them +Infinity) and documents up to about 43-50 km beyond the equatorward end are matched with finite sort values (at 44-45 degrees), while SPHERE, east-west ends, interior joints, corridor sides and one-point paths stay within about 1% of the width.
Summary
You run Geo3DPoint.newPathQuery (or Geo3DDocValuesField.newPathSort) on WGS84 with a north-south path, for example from (44N,20E) to (45N,20E), and expect it to match the points within pathWidthMeters of the path, with round end caps of that radius around the first and last points, as it does on PlanetModel.SPHERE. Instead both end caps are shifted about 21 km toward the equator: with pathWidthMeters 20000 the query misses documents 12 and 16 km beyond the north end (sort value +Infinity), and with pathWidthMeters 1000 it matches documents 10 to 40 km south of the first point (sort values 10006 to 40034 m). The end-cap plane (GeoStandardPath.CutoffSingleCircleSegmentEndpoint, built by CircleSegmentEndpoint through the two segment corners with its normal inside the origin-containing cutoff plane) points along the end point's geocentric direction, which on an ellipsoid is not the surface normal there, so the cap circle is centred equatorward of the end point.
Introduced
560f3a1a010407f9fd8ed1d84636464891248172 (2015-05-27, lucene6487 branch, 'LUCENE-6487: Geo3D with WGS84 in-progress with David's mods'; JIRA LUCENE-6487, now apache/lucene#7546; patch by DaddyWri (Karl Wright), committed by dsmiley (David Smiley); svn era, no pull request), merged to trunk as 77de0ed1cf8 and to branch_5x as 8c3e6b0ed32 (2015-06-02). In GeoPath.SegmentEndpoint case (2) (path start and end) it replaced the sphere-only cap new SidedPlane(point, point, -originDistance) (normal = the end point's direction, exact on the unit sphere) with SidedPlane.constructNormalizedPerpendicularSidedPlane(point, cutoffPlane, topEdgePoint, bottomEdgePoint), added in the same commit (plane through the two corners, normal inside the cutoff plane). Later changes kept the geometry: LUCENE-6544/6632 (2015) and LUCENE-8696 (2019, interior joints only) did not touch end caps, and the 2022 hierarchical refactor (fc7ce768514 on main, 83a81262b85 on 9.x, first in 9.5.0; #11965) re-expressed it as SidedPlane.constructSidedPlaneFromTwoPoints, the same plane.
