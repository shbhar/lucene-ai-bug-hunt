SpanContainingQuery and SpanWithinQuery miss matches when the little query's spans end out of order

SpanContainingQuery and SpanWithinQuery silently drop matches when the little query is an OR of a longer and a shorter clause: within one sentence, cake OR (apple NEAR/5 pie) finds bos cake eos bos pie eos but not bos apple cake eos bos pie eos. Every release since 5.2.0 is affected.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

SpanContainingQuery(big, little) keeps the big spans that contain a little span; SpanWithinQuery(big, little) keeps the little spans that lie inside a big span. Both walk the document's big and little spans once, forward only.

  1. You search inside one sentence (bos ... eos) for cake, or apple up to 5 words before pie: SpanContainingQuery(big = spanNear([bos, eos], 100), little = spanOr(spanNear([apple, pie], 5), cake)). SpanWithinQuery with the same pair asks the same question.
  2. On bos cake eos bos pie eos both queries find the document. Add one word to the first sentence: bos apple cake eos bos pie eos. That sentence still contains cake, so you expect 1 hit.
  3. You get 0 hits from both queries, and IndexSearcher.count says 0. No error. The Intervals equivalents (Intervals.containing, Intervals.containedBy) return 1.
  4. Why: spans are sorted by start, then by end. The little spans are apple ... pie [1,6), which runs into the second sentence, then cake [2,3): the end goes down. Containing tests sentence [0,4) only against [1,6) and moves on; within skips sentence [0,4) while placing [1,6) and never goes back for cake.
  5. It only loses matches. An exhaustive check over 5,460 short documents and 648 queries found 10,762 missed document hits, 0 extra, and no miss when the little ends rise.

Who is affected

#

Applications that run SpanContainingQuery or SpanWithinQuery with a little query whose spans can end out of order, typically an OR of a longer phrase or near clause and a shorter clause that can occur inside it. Default codec and settings. Fixed-width little queries (terms, exact phrases, ORs of single terms) are safe; no Lucene query parser builds these queries.

Mechanism, terms and numbers762 words

SpanContainingQuery(big, little) should return every big span that contains at least one little span, and SpanWithinQuery(big, little) every little span inside a big span. Spans are enumerated by start and then by end, so when the little query is a SpanOrQuery of a longer clause and a shorter one that starts inside it, the little spans' ends go down: [1,6), then [2,3). Both queries merge big and little in one forward pass that assumes the ends go up: containing tests each big span against only the first little span that starts at or after it, and within advances the big iterator past big spans that a later, shorter little span would fit in, and never goes back. Whole documents and individual spans are missed, IndexSearcher.count is wrong too, SpanWithinQuery(A, B) and SpanContainingQuery(A, B) can disagree, and adding a word to a document or an OR alternative to the little query can remove a hit; the queries never return an extra match. It happens with the default codec and settings; the merge is per document, so index size does not matter; and every release since the classes were added (5.2.0) is affected. A local fix buffers each document's spans instead of skipping them and keeps collect() working.

Terms

#
  • Span: a match of a span query in one document, written [start, end) in token positions. In bos apple cake eos bos pie eos the tokens are at positions 0 to 6, and cake is the span [2,3).
  • Order of spans: a Spans iterator returns a document's spans "by increasing start position and finally by increasing end position" (Spans.java:25-28). The ends may go down across starts.
  • Big and little: SpanContainingQuery "matching spans from big that contain at least one spans from little" (SpanContainingQuery.java:33-35); SpanWithinQuery "matching spans from little that are inside of big" (SpanWithinQuery.java:32-34).
  • Out-of-order ends: a SpanOrQuery ("the union of its clauses") of a longer clause and a shorter one that starts inside it. spanOr(spanNear([apple, pie], 5), cake) on the document above gives [1,6), then [2,3).

The bug

#
  1. Containing (SpanContainingQuery.java:94-132): for each big span, advance little while little.start < big.start, then test only that one little span (big.end >= little.end). Sentence [0,4) is tested against [1,6) only (6 > 4); for sentence [4,7) the little iterator runs past [2,3) and is exhausted. cake is never tested against the sentence that contains it.
  2. Within (SpanWithinQuery.java:93-131): for each little span, advance big while big.end < little.end, then test big.start <= little.start. Little [1,6) pushes the big iterator past sentence [0,4) to [4,7); little [2,3) is then tested only against [4,7) (4 > 2). A Spans cannot be rewound, so [0,4) is gone.
  3. Only out-of-order ends trigger it. If the little ends rise, skipping is safe: a big span that ends before the current little span ends before every later one. A TLA+ model of both loops (TLC, every pair of span lists of up to 3 spans over 4 and 5 positions) shows them sound and complete whenever the little ends rise, whatever the big spans are.
  4. Trigger detail. For containing, the long little span that hides the match must be the first little span that starts at or after big.start. Within fails whenever an earlier, longer little span pushes the big iterator past the big span that contains a later, shorter one.

The numbers

#

Exhaustive check of the stock jar (every document of 1 to 6 tokens over 4 letters, 5,460 documents; 18 span queries as big and as little; both query types: 648 queries; expected results built from the spans Lucene itself returns for big and little): 68 queries miss documents, 10,762 missed document hits and 20,016 missed spans, 0 extra hits or spans, IndexSearcher.count wrong on the same 68 queries, 28 big/little pairs where within and containing disagree, and no failure when the little ends rise.

Exposure and likelihood266 words
  • Who is exposed. Code that builds SpanContainingQuery or SpanWithinQuery (public API in lucene-queries) with such a little clause: a sentence- or paragraph-scoped "X OR (Y NEAR Z)" search, or a nested containment query as the little clause. The registry also lists downstream query languages with span_containing / span_within queries as a route (not checked here).
  • What happens. Documents are missed, so counts, top-k and facets built on the query are too low. Documents that still match can lose spans and so score lower (sloppy frequency): 76 queries in the exhaustive check return a wrong span set against 68 with a wrong hit set; for example SpanWithin(b, spanOr(spanNear([a, b], 3, unordered), b)) on b b a returns [0,1) but not [1,2).
  • Inside an exclusion (MUST_NOT), a missed match lets a document through: +*:* -SpanContaining(sentence, little) returns bos apple cake eos bos pie eos (1 hit, expected 0); with the fix, 0.
  • Within and containing disagree. SpanWithinQuery(A, B) and SpanContainingQuery(A, B) match different documents for 28 big/little pairs, which an upstream test asserts cannot happen.
  • Scale. Deterministic and per document (seen from one-document indexes to 100,000 documents); the default codec, IndexWriterConfig and IndexSearcher in the plain main() probes, randomized codecs in the JUnits; assertions on and off. On 100,000 random 40-word documents, SpanContainingQuery(near(w1, w2, 10), spanOr(near(w3, w4, 5), w5)) returns 1,122 hits on main and 1,124 with the fix: rare on random text; real phrasing patterns decide.
  • How likely. Rare: span queries are a legacy API (intervals are the replacement), the shape is specific, and nobody has reported it since 2015. Not a 10.6.0 RC regression.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The javadoc promises exact containment: "matching spans from big that contain at least one spans from little".
  • Falling ends are legal; an OR "matches the union of its clauses", yet adding a clause removes a hit.
  • An upstream test asserts SpanWithinQuery(A, B) and SpanContainingQuery(A, B) match the same documents; on new york city with big york: 0 and 1.
The full argument185 words
  • Exact containment is documented (SpanContainingQuery.java:33-35, :76-79; SpanWithinQuery.java:32-34, :75-78; package-info.java:35-38). No javadoc, package doc, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry says the result is approximate or lazy. The only "minimum slop matches" caveat in the package is on NearSpansOrdered (NearSpansOrdered.java:26), which is not involved: the expected results are built from the spans Lucene itself returns for big and little.
  • The input is legal. Spans.java:25-28 allows falling ends; the module's test wrapper AssertingSpans checks that starts never go backwards and that start <= end, not the order of ends (every query of the union-law test was wrapped in AssertingSpanQuery; no assertion fired).
  • Upstream asserts the broken property. TestSpanSearchEquivalence.testSpanWithinVsContaining (:452-465): "SpanWithinQuery(A, B) = SpanContainingQuery(A, B)", with a single-term little clause.
  • Tests never covered it. Every upstream little clause in TestSpanContainQuery, TestSpanSearchEquivalence, TestSpanWithinQuery and the nightly payload test is a term, an OR of single terms or a two-term near: all have rising ends.
  • History. The loops came with the classes (LUCENE-6083, 2015) and are unchanged; the discussion covered containment semantics, two-phase iteration and naming, not completeness.
The devil's-advocate check168 words

The strongest case for "works as designed": span queries are forward-only and lazy, and upstream accepts the consequences. In #8451 the author of ContainSpans defined lazy as "all subspans are only moved forward" and wrote "To allow collecting/payloads easily, I'd rather accept the limitations/bugs of the current lazy implementation"; #10247 stopped QueryBuilder from building span graph queries because of #8451; #16820 says that span queries "have some basically unfixable correctness bugs".

Why it fails here: #8451's statements are about SpanNearQuery and #10247 about the span graph queries QueryBuilder used to build, where a complete answer needs backtracking over child spans; #16820's remark names no class and no specific bug. Here the child spans are correct, the miss is in the two containment loops, the fix needs no rewinding of any child Spans, and the javadocs of these two classes promise exact containment. Every verifier judged it real, and the two that built the works-as-designed case rejected it; expect upstream to raise it anyway and to suggest intervals.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Buffer the document's spans, replay the partner's leaves with their real freq

Buffer per document instead of skipping: within keeps the largest end of the big spans starting at or before the little span; containing keeps the smallest end of the little spans starting at or after the big span. Record the buffered spans' leaves, with their real freq(), for collect(). Validated: every repro, the exhaustive check, the queries and highlighter modules.

private boolean littleInsideBig() throws IOException {
  final int littleStart = littleSpans.startPosition();
  while (bigSpans.startPosition() <= littleStart) { // NO_MORE_POSITIONS ends the loop
    if (bigSpans.startPosition() != -1) {
      bigEnds = ArrayUtil.grow(bigEnds, numBig + 1);
      bigEnds[numBig++] = bigSpans.endPosition();
      bigLeaves.record(bigSpans);
      maxBigEnd = Math.max(maxBigEnd, bigSpans.endPosition());
    }
    bigSpans.nextStartPosition();
  }
  return maxBigEnd >= littleSpans.endPosition();
}

A. Buffer the document's spans, replay the partner's leaves with their real freq (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#
  • SpanWithinQuery: keep the big spans of the current document that start at or before the current little span, with the maximum of their ends; the little span matches iff that maximum is >= little.end. Nothing is rewound.
  • SpanContainingQuery: buffer the document's little spans with the suffix minima of their ends; a big span matches iff the minimum end over the little spans that start at or after big.start is <= big.end.
  • collect() still reports a partner (ContainSpans.java:55-58 collects big and little, and payload scoring relies on it). A new package-private SpanLeafRecorder records the buffered spans' leaves (term, position, doc, freq, offsets, payload copy) and replays the partner's: for containing the first buffered little span inside the big span, for within the first buffered big span that contains the little span.
  • What this doc changed in the hunt's sketch: the replayed PostingsEnum returns the recorded freq() and docID() of the original postings. The sketch returned freq() == 1, and the UnifiedHighlighter (which sizes its offset arrays by postings.freq(), PhraseHelper.java:355, :377-378) and a freq-sized SpanCollector threw ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException, also on queries that work today.

Trade-offs

  • Cost. Each candidate document's little spans (containing) or big spans (within) are drained and their leaves recorded, with a payload copy when payloads exist; memory grows with the spans and the recorded leaves (term, position, doc, freq, offsets, payload copy) per document, and high-frequency leaves, deep span trees and large payloads were not measured. A rough probe on a shared, loaded machine (100,000 random documents, count(), 7 alternating runs each; not a benchmark), median main vs fix: containing with an OR little clause 9.1 vs 10.7 ms, within with the OR 7.2 vs 7.9 ms, with a term little clause 5.5 vs 5.7 ms and 4.3 vs 4.7 ms. The run-to-run spread is about as large: probably a modest slowdown; benchmark properly before quoting numbers.
  • Partner reported to collect(). Where main already matches, containing reports the same little span as today. Within can report another big span when ends are out of order: on a b c d e, big spanOr(phrase a b c, phrase a b c d e), little spanOr(phrase a b c d, b), the little span [1,2) is collected with big [0,5) on main and with big [0,3) with the fix (both contain it). Payload scores and highlights of such spans can change; keep today's partner if the PR must not change them.
  • Review nits. Containing's collect() ends with throw new AssertionError(...); upstream is replacing such errors with proper exceptions (#16919), so use IllegalStateException. The recorder allocates one small PostingsEnum per replayed leaf.
  • Scope. Local to the two classes plus one package-private helper; no API change.

B. Lazy buffering (not run)

#

Keep today's fast path (test the current span first) and buffer only the spans the loop advances past while it looks further, with a sliding minimum (containing) or maximum (within).

Trade-offs

Avoids draining whole documents when the first candidate matches, at the price of more complex state; needs its own model check or exhaustive run.

C. Rewrite a top-level OR little clause (not run)

#

SpanContaining(big, spanOr(a, b)) becomes spanOr(SpanContaining(big, a), SpanContaining(big, b)), and the same for within, as #9523 did for intervals.

Trade-offs

Partial: fixes only a top-level OR whose clauses each have rising ends, changes scoring and span duplication, and leaves nested shapes broken.

D. Document the limitation

#

State in both javadocs that the little query's spans must end in start order.

Trade-offs

Cheapest; fixes nothing.

Downstream note

#

Used inside an exclusion (a MUST_NOT clause), a missed match lets a document through, so an application that filters with these queries can show documents it meant to hide.

How it was found

#

A bounded-exhaustive check of the stock jars against the javadoc, with expected results built from the spans Lucene itself returns for big and little, flagged the misses. A TLA+ model of the two loops then gave the 6-state minimal counterexample and showed, within its bounds, both loops complete whenever the little ends rise.

Impact note
Applications that run SpanContainingQuery or SpanWithinQuery (directly or through a downstream span_containing / span_within query) with a little query whose spans can end out of order, typically a SpanOrQuery that mixes a longer near or phrase clause with a shorter clause that can occur inside it, silently lose whole documents and individual spans (IndexSearcher.count is wrong too, documents that still match can get fewer spans and so lower sloppy-frequency scores, and SpanWithin(A, B) and SpanContaining(A, B) can match different documents), with the default codec, IndexWriterConfig and searcher, -ea and -da, at any index size, in every release from 5.2.0 to 10.5.2, the 10.6.0 release candidate and main; nothing extra is ever returned, and little queries made of terms, phrases or ORs of single terms are not affected.
Summary
You index 'a b a' and search with SpanContainingQuery(big = SpanNear([a, b], slop 0), little = SpanOr(SpanNear([a, a], slop 5), b)): the big span 'a b' at [0,2) contains the little span 'b' at [1,2), so you expect a hit, and SpanWithinQuery with the same pair should match too. Instead both return nothing, because the little spans arrive sorted by start as [0,3) and then [1,2) (their ends go down, which the Spans contract allows), and each query's single forward pass gives up after testing only the longer little span [0,3): containing moves on to the next big span, within moves the big iterator past [0,2) and never comes back. The same happens in a sentence-scoped search for '(apple NEAR/5 pie) OR cake' on 'bos apple cake eos bos pie eos' (the 'cake' sentence is missed only because 'apple ... pie' crosses into the next sentence), so adding a word to a document or an OR alternative to the query can remove a hit; nothing extra is ever returned.
