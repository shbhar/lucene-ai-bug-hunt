BengaliNormalizer's visarga rule copies the next letter before folding it, so নিঃশব্দ and নিঃসব্দ get different terms

Lucene's Bengali normalizer folds শ and ষ to স and turns a visarga ঃ inside a word into a copy of the next letter, but it copies that letter before folding it: নিঃশব্দ ("silent") is indexed as নিশসব্দ, নিঃসব্দ as নিসসব্দ, and the two do not match. Present since 7.1.0.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

Bengali has three s-letters, শ, ষ and স, which Lucene's Bengali normalizer folds to স so that spelling variants match. A visarga ঃ inside a word doubles the next consonant, so the normalizer replaces it by a copy of that letter. It copies the letter before folding it.

  1. Index Bengali text with the stock new BengaliAnalyzer() (or BengaliNormalizationFilter). Its normalizer folds the s-letters শ and ষ to স, so শব্দ and সব্দ both index as সব্দ.
  2. It also turns a visarga ঃ in the middle of a word into a copy of the next letter. You expect নিঃশব্দ ("silent"), নিঃষব্দ and নিঃসব্দ all to become নিসসব্দ, just as the class's other doubling rule turns বিশ্ব into বিসস in an upstream test.
  3. You get নিশসব্দ, নিষসব্দ and নিসসব্দ: three terms. The normalizer is one left-to-right pass, and s[i] = s[i + 1] (BengaliNormalizer.java:121) copies শ before the loop reaches it and folds it to স; the copy is never revisited.
  4. In an index with one document per spelling, each spelling's query finds 1 of 3 documents; with the fix, 3 of 3. No error, and identical spellings still match. The other folded characters (ণ, ঙ, ড় U+09DC, ঢ় U+09DD, ৎ, ী, ূ) behave the same way after a visarga.

Who is affected

#

Applications that index Bengali with the stock BengaliAnalyzer (default constructor), BengaliNormalizationFilter or the bengaliNormalization factory, on every release since 7.1.0. A word with a medial visarga before শ, ষ, ণ or another folded character does not match its variant spelling. Identical spellings still match; a fix needs a reindex.

Mechanism, terms and numbers634 words

BengaliNormalizer (package-private; used by BengaliNormalizationFilter, by BengaliAnalyzer at index and query time and by the bengaliNormalization factory) is one left-to-right pass over the term. It folds SHA শ and SSA ষ to SA স everywhere (and NNA, RRA, RHA, khanda-ta, II, UU and NGA one to one), and it turns a medial visarga ঃ into a copy of the next letter: s[i] = s[i + 1] at BengaliNormalizer.java:121. That copy is made before the loop has folded the next letter, and the loop never returns to it, so নিঃশব্দ ("silent") becomes নিশসব্দ (SHA SA), নিঃষব্দ becomes নিষসব্দ, and only নিঃসব্দ gives নিসসব্দ. The class's other gemination, Ba-phalaa, copies an already folded letter (বিশ্ব -> বিসস, pinned by an upstream test), and the cited paper gives SHA, SSA and SA one code. Through BengaliAnalyzer the three spellings get three terms and do not match each other. Every release from 7.1.0 to 10.5.2 and main has it. The fix, s[i] = foldSimple(s[i + 1]), changes indexed terms of the affected words, so those fields need a reindex.

Terms

#
  • Visarga ঃ (U+0983): inside a word it doubles the sound of the next consonant, so নিঃশব্দ is said like nishshabdo. The normalizer models this as gemination: the visarga becomes a copy of the next letter. A final visarga becomes হ in a short word and is deleted in a longer one.
  • The folds: one-to-one rewrites the class applies at every position: SHA শ U+09B6 and SSA ষ U+09B7 to SA স U+09B8 (// All sh), NNA ণ to NA ন, RRA ড় U+09DC and RHA ঢ় U+09DD to RA র U+09B0, khanda-ta ৎ to TA ত, NGA ঙ to anusvara ং, and the vowel signs II ী to I ি and UU ূ to U ু.
  • BengaliNormalizer: a left-to-right rewrite of each token, one switch (s[i]) case per character. It is package-private and reached through BengaliNormalizationFilter, which BengaliAnalyzer uses in its main chain (indexing and analyzed queries) and in normalize() (multi-term queries), and through the bengaliNormalization factory. Tokens marked as keywords skip it.

The bug

#
  1. The visarga case (BengaliNormalizer.java:112-123) runs s[i] = s[i + 1]; (:121) for a medial visarga.
  2. At that moment position i + 1 has not been visited, so the copy is the raw letter. The loop then folds the original at i + 1 (// All sh, :125-129) and never returns to i. For নিঃশব্দ: ন ি ঃ শ ব ্ দ -> ন ি শ শ ব ্ দ -> ন ি শ স ব ্ দ.
  3. Ba-phalaa, the class's other gemination, copies backwards, s[i - 1] = s[i - 2] (:106), from a position already folded: বিশ্ব -> বিসস. The visarga version of the same doubling, বিঃশ, gives বিশস.
  4. The result is not even a fixed point of the class's own rules: normalizing নিশসব্দ again gives নিসসব্দ.

The hunt's bounded check found 23,947 violations of "no character that an unconditional rule rewrites or deletes may survive" (chandrabindu and the visarga included) over all 551,881 strings of up to 4 characters on a 27-character alphabet; every one contains a visarga. Exposure and likelihood382 words
  • Who is exposed. BengaliAnalyzer (the default constructor included), BengaliNormalizationFilter, and the bengaliNormalization factory, for example in a CustomAnalyzer. No setting turns the rule off. Only tokens marked as keywords skip it, together with the whole normalizer (BengaliNormalizationFilter.java:48); BengaliAnalyzer marks the words of its stem-exclusion set, if one is given (BengaliAnalyzer.java:123-125).
  • Ordinary input. Tatsama words such as নিঃশব্দ, নিঃশেষ, নিঃশ্বাস and দুঃশাসন have a medial visarga before শ. Every such word gets the unfolded term, at index and query time alike.
  • What the damage looks like. Silent: no error, and identical spellings still match. What fails is the match the folds exist for: a document and a query that spell the letter after the visarga differently (শ, ষ or স; ণ or ন) do not meet, although the analyzer conflates them anywhere else in a word. With one document per spelling of নিঃশব্দ, each spelling's query finds 1 of 3 documents; with the fix, 3 of 3. An independent end-to-end test: the স-spelled query finds 0 of 1 শ-spelled documents, while the no-visarga control finds 1 of 1.
  • Only lost matches. Over 2,000,718 generated short strings (a synthetic set, not a corpus), 9,779,308 pairs of strings get the same output with the fix but different outputs on stock, and no pair the other way; the fix changes the output of 167,664 strings, each containing a visarga, and never changes an output's length.
  • How likely. Rare: a missed match needs the two sides to spell that one letter differently, and no report of it was found in 9 years.
  • Scale. One word reproduces it; assertions on and off behave the same.
  • Reindex. A fix changes the term of every affected word, so fields analyzed before the fix keep the old terms until they are reindexed.
  • What is not shown. No corpus count or native-speaker check measures how often the variant spellings (নিঃষব্দ, নিঃসব্দ for নিঃশব্দ) occur; the cross-spelling impact is inferred from the class's own folds and the cited paper's codes.
  • Downstream remark. An application that filters on analyzed text (a denylist query, a MUST_NOT clause) misses a variant spelling the same way any query does. Authorization is the application's job under Lucene's SECURITY.md, so this is not a Lucene security issue.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • Ba-phalaa, the class's other doubling rule, copies an already folded letter; an upstream test pins বিশ্ব -> বিসস (স স, not শ স).
  • The cited paper says a medial visarga "gets the code of next character" and gives শ, ষ and স one code.
  • The only upstream visarga test uses খ, which no rule folds; nothing documents the order.
The full argument398 words
  • The class's own folds. // All sh, // check na and the others are unconditional. After a visarga the output still holds a letter the class rewrites everywhere else, and a second pass changes it (নিশসব্দ -> নিসসব্দ). That alone does not prove a bug: another rule interaction also changes on a second pass (কঁ্ি -> ক্ি -> খ, where deleting the chandrabindu exposes the khio pattern). It supports the diagnosis together with the next two points.
  • The other gemination. Ba-phalaa (BengaliNormalizer.java:106, s[i - 1] = s[i - 2]) copies a letter the loop has already folded, and TestBengaliNormalizer.testBaPhalaa (same commit) asserts check("বিশ্ব", "বিসস"). The visarga copy gives বিঃশ -> বিশস for the same doubling. The difference comes only from loop order: one rule looks back at a processed position, the other ahead at an unprocessed one.
  • The cited paper (UzZaman and Khan, named in the class javadoc). Visarga "Case 1: In the medial position ... acts as a diacritic, geminating the sound of the consonant it follows. So, it gets the code of next character"; its example of a medial visarga before স encodes to <dussmy>. SHA, SSA and SA share the code <s>, and the Ba-phalaa example বিশ্ব encodes to <biss>.
  • Tests. testVisarga checks দুঃখ -> দুখখ, উঃ -> উহ, পুনঃ -> পুন. খ is not folded, so a raw copy and a folded copy give the same output: no test pins the current behaviour, and the fix breaks none. testRandom can generate a folded letter after a visarga but checks only the length.
  • History. The copy and the folds arrived together in the contributor's first commit 1bca06b8a938 (2017-08-24, LUCENE-7940, gh#8989, lucene-solr PR #238). Two later commits of the same PR only added the Ja-phala and Ba-phalaa bounds checks from the review (3197d5af71d, 40dddf9324e); commits after the 2017-09-05 landing only reformat or change visibility. None of them touches the visarga lines, and the review never discussed the visarga.
  • No public javadoc for the mapping. The normalizer is package-private; the public filter says only that it normalizes "the orthography" and that "the normalization may cause unrelated terms to conflate". No javadoc promises a folded copy or a stable output, so there is no public contract; the evidence of intent is the class's rules, its tests and the cited paper.
The devil's-advocate check286 words

The strongest "intended" case (the devil's-advocate verifier and the intended-lens review by another model family, rated moderate): the class is a one-pass transcription of the paper, and "gets the code of next character" can be read as "copy the next character", which s[i] = s[i + 1] does literally; the paper describes phonetic codes for a spelling checker, not search normalization; nothing promises that the output is closed under the folds or stable under a second pass, and another rule interaction is not stable either; the normalizer is package-private; no expected-output test covers a folded letter after a visarga; the output has been stable for 9 years and the original review measured relevance with this code; a maintainer defends the Indic folds as "working as documented" in gh#14659; another contributor's evaluation found the Bengali folds too aggressive for search; and nothing measures how often the variant spellings occur. So the missing conflation may be called harmless or not worth a reindex.

Why it fails: in the paper "code" is the phonetic code, which SHA, SSA and SA share; the class's own Ba-phalaa gemination copies the folded letter and an upstream test from the same commit pins it; the output is not a fixed point of the class's rules (supporting evidence, not proof on its own); and nothing documents the ordering. Long life is not evidence that anyone chose it. Verdict: a defect (adjudication: confirmed, high). What the counter-case gets right: the impact is small, a fix changes indexed terms, and documenting the raw copy is a legitimate maintainer choice; a maintainer may also ask for a Bengali-language check first. So the report should frame it as making the two geminations agree, not as a relevance argument.

Trade-offs

  • Changes the indexed term of every word with a medial visarga before a folded character: this would likely warrant a CHANGES entry and a MIGRATE note (reindex fields analyzed with BengaliAnalyzer, BengaliNormalizationFilter or the bengaliNormalization factory); MIGRATE.md has such a note for the Romanian change in 10.0 (a precedent, not a rule). Main is the natural target; a 10.x backport is debatable.
  • The filter conflates more, but only within its own folds.
  • A larger diff than the minimal patch, but the table of one-to-one folds exists once, which removes the risk of two copies drifting apart (a special case added to the switch later could still differ from the copy). Cost: one static call for each character that reaches the switch's default and for each medial-visarga copy (chandrabindu, KA, YA, BA and the visarga keep their own cases); no allocation (by reading).

How it was found

#

A bounded-exhaustive check of the stock Bengali filter against its own unconditional rules: no character such a rule rewrites or deletes may survive, and শ and স must give the same output. Over 551,881 short strings it found 23,947 violations, all with a visarga, pointing at line 121; in 20,097 a folded character survives; the recommended fix removes those.

The method in full206 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation on the stock main jars (the study's "semi-formal" tier). A hunt unit, "script normalizers against their documented rules", 2026-10-27.

  • A closure oracle (no character that an unconditional rule rewrites or deletes may survive in the output: the folded characters, chandrabindu and the visarga) and a mapping-invariance oracle (SHA/SSA/SA and the other folded pairs must give the same output) ran over all 551,881 strings of length 0 to 4 on a 27-character alphabet (consonants, vowel signs, hasanta, chandrabindu, anusvara, visarga, khanda-ta; 23,947 closure violations) and all 5,380,840 strings of length 0 to 7 on a 9-character alphabet (1,010,898).
  • A fact check later split the 23,947: a folded character survives in 20,097, and only a chandrabindu or a visarga in the other 3,850. With the recommended fix (option B), 4,372 remain, all chandrabindu/visarga-only (cases no fix option covers), and no folded character survives.
  • Every closure violation contains a visarga, and a residual run that excludes visarga and KA + HASANTA inputs has 0 closure and 0 mapping-invariance violations, which isolates the cause to the visarga copy (KA + HASANTA is the separate khio bug).
  • Then an AI finder wrote the public-API JUnit, and independent AI tools reproduced, reviewed and judged it (see the checks).

Bug record

#

LFV-676, snapshot of 2026-10-27

Registry title
BengaliNormalizer's medial-visarga rule copies the next character before that character is folded, so SHA, SSA, NNA (and the other one-to-one folded letters) survive after a visarga and those spellings stop matching the SA/NA spelling the same normalizer conflates everywhere else
Area
analysis-common / Bengali normalizer (BengaliNormalizer.normalize; BengaliNormalizationFilter, BengaliAnalyzer and the 'bengaliNormalization' factory)
Priority
Medium, rank 113: The default BengaliAnalyzer silently fails to match variant spellings after a medial visarga, the same kind of relevance miss as its khio sibling LFV-667 with a slightly narrower trigger.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Relevance only, with no error: every application that analyzes Bengali text with BengaliAnalyzer (default constructor included), BengaliNormalizationFilter or the 'bengaliNormalization' factory, on every release from 7.1.0 through 10.5.2 and on main, gets a term that keeps the unfolded letter for every word with a medial visarga before SHA (শ), SSA (ষ), NNA (ণ) or another letter the class folds one to one (vowel signs II and UU, NGA, RRA, RHA, khanda-ta), as in common words such as নিঃশব্দ, নিঃশেষ and নিঃশ্বাস, so the SHA/SSA spelling and the SA spelling of such a word no longer match each other although the analyzer conflates them in every other position; identical spellings still match, no scale is needed, -ea and -da behave the same, and because a fix changes indexed terms, affected fields need reindexing after it.
Summary
You index or query Bengali text with the stock BengaliAnalyzer or BengaliNormalizationFilter. Its normalizer folds SHA (শ) and SSA (ষ) to SA (স) everywhere and turns a medial visarga (ঃ) into a copy of the next letter (gemination), so নিঃশব্দ ('silent'), নিঃষব্দ and নিঃসব্দ should all become নিসসব্দ (SA SA), just as the class's other gemination rule turns বিশ্ব into বিসস in an upstream test. Instead the visarga branch copies the next character before the loop has folded it and never revisits the copy, so নিঃশব্দ becomes নিশসব্দ (SHA SA) and নিঃষব্দ becomes নিষসব্দ (SSA SA): the three spellings give three different terms, and a document written with one of them is not found by a query written with another, with no error.
