MultiTermsEnum.next() after seekExact can return the wrong term when a sub-reader is a MemoryIndex reader

On a merged terms view over MemoryIndex readers, next() right after a successful seekExact returns the wrong term: with terms a to e in two readers, seekExact("a") then next() gives c, skipping b. The merged enum re-seeks each reader with one reader's own term buffer, which MemoryIndex overwrites while searching.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

After seekExact finds a term, the merged enum remembers it by pointing at the last matching reader's own term buffer. next() then re-seeks every reader with that buffer. MemoryIndex's binary search writes each probe into the very same buffer, so the target becomes the middle term, matches itself, and that reader lands on the wrong term.

  1. Put two MemoryIndex readers, each holding the five terms a, b, c, d, e in field f, into a MultiReader, and take the merged enum MultiTerms.getTerms(reader, "f").iterator().
  2. Call seekExact("a"): it returns true, docFreq 2. You expect next() to return b (docFreq 2), as plain iteration does: a, b, c, d, e.
  3. You get c: b is silently skipped. Over the five terms, seekExact(t) then next() is wrong for 4: a gives c, b gives c with docFreq 1 instead of 2, and d and e come back again instead of the next term or the end.
  4. Why: the merged enum keeps the second reader's own term buffer as its current term, and next() re-seeks every reader with it. MemoryIndex's binary search writes each probe term into that same buffer, so the target turns into the middle term, c, matches itself, and that reader stops on c.
  5. No error, with assertions on or off. The on-disk terms dictionaries surveyed handle the re-seek correctly. Every release run from 4.1.0 (2012) to 10.6.0 has the bug.

Who is affected

#

Only code that puts a MemoryIndex reader into a composite reader (a MultiReader of MemoryIndex readers, or disk segments followed by one), walks the merged MultiTerms view and calls next() right after a successful seekExact. Then many seeks go wrong (in runs with 2 to 9 terms, all but the middle term). Per-segment search is not affected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers567 words

MultiTermsEnum.seekExact remembers the found term by keeping the last matching sub-reader's own term() BytesRef as current, without a copy. The following next() re-seeks every sub with seekCeil(current) relying on the code comment's assumption that "most impls short-circuit if you seekCeil to term they are already on". MemoryIndex's terms enum does not: its binary search writes each probe term into br, which is the target itself, so the first probe overwrites the target with the middle term, compares equal and stops there. With two MemoryIndex readers holding a to e in a MultiReader, seekExact("a") then next() returns c instead of b; over the five terms only the middle one gives the right successor, and d and e come back twice. Silent, with -ea and -da, in every release run from 4.1.0 to 10.6.0 and on main; a lone MemoryIndex reader, per-segment search and the on-disk terms dictionaries surveyed are not affected.

Terms

#
  • Merged terms view: MultiTerms.getTerms(reader, field).iterator() on a composite reader (for example a MultiReader) returns a MultiTermsEnum, which merges the per-reader (per-sub) terms enums into one sorted enum. With one leaf it returns the leaf's own terms instead.
  • seekExact(term) then next(): after seekExact returns true the enum is on that term, and next() must return the following term.
  • term() buffer: a terms enum returns its current term as a BytesRef it owns and re-uses.

The bug (main f794ff5a505c)

#
  1. The alias. In seekExact, every matching sub sets current = currentSubs[i].term(); (MultiTermsEnum.java:156; the same in seekCeil, :208). TermsEnumIndex.term() returns the sub's own termsEnum.term() object, not a copy (TermsEnumIndex.java:79-81, set at :108-116). So current is the last matching sub's buffer.
  2. The re-seek. next() with lastSeekExact calls seekCeil(current) (MultiTermsEnum.java:266-277, the call at :274) so that subs which lacked the term move to the next one. The comment relies on subs that "short-circuit if you seekCeil to term they are already on". seekCeil hands the same object to every sub (:180-218).
  3. MemoryIndex overwrites the target. MemoryTermsEnum.seekCeil(text) runs binarySearch(text, br, ...) (MemoryIndex.java:1939), which writes each probe into br with hash.get(ords[mid], bytesRef) (:1917) and compares it with text. term() returns br (:1972-1973), so here text == br: the first probe turns the target into the middle term, the comparison is equal, and the search returns FOUND there. A lone enum on a moves to c on seekCeil(te.term()) and stays on a with a deep copy (executed probe).
  4. The wrong answer. After the re-seek, reader 1 is on a and reader 2 on c, both reported FOUND. pushTop() advances queue.top() once per matching sub (MultiTermsEnum.java:252-265): that is reader 1 both times (a to b to c), and pullTop() returns c with both readers on it (read in the code; matches the probe's c with docFreq 2).

The numbers

#

Two MemoryIndex readers with a to e: plain next() gives a/2 b/2 c/2 d/2 e/2 (term/docFreq). seekExact(t) then next() gives a to c/2, b to c/1, c to d/2 (right), d to d/1, e to e/2 (the end was expected). A scan over 1 to 9 terms (the same terms in both readers, assertions on and off) was right only for the term the binary search probes first, the middle one; with 9 terms, h gave g, an earlier term.

Exposure and likelihood293 words
  • Who is exposed. Applications that compose MemoryIndex readers (the MemoryIndex javadoc calls the class "a replacement/substitute for RAM-resident Directory implementations", MemoryIndex.java:106), read the merged terms view, and step with next() after a successful seekExact.
  • Which compositions. Wrong when the last reader holding the sought term is a MemoryIndex reader: two MemoryIndex readers, or disk segments followed by a MemoryIndex reader. Right with the MemoryIndex reader first and disk segments last, with a lone MemoryIndex reader, and in search. The finder's survey of 11 other postings and term-vector terms dictionaries found them all correct (default codec, SimpleText, Direct, FST50, BlockTreeOrds, three BlockTerms variants, two UniformSplit variants, TermVectorLeafReader); it is not a survey of every in-tree TermsEnum.
  • Default vs opt-in. Not the default codec path. MemoryIndex and the merged view (@lucene.experimental) are opt-in. seekCeil then next() is unaffected: there is no re-seek.
  • Silent. No exception, the same with assertions on and off. The symptom (a skipped term, a repeated or earlier term, or a wrong docFreq) depends on the term count and where the first probe falls; one term per reader is right; docFreq() and postings() for the returned term can miss the MemoryIndex reader.
  • How likely. Rare: no in-tree production caller was found that runs seekExact then next() on a merged enum with a MemoryIndex sub (taxonomy, classification and suggest were checked, and later the 22 non-test callers of MultiTerms.getTerms or MultiTermsEnum outside the index classes and the test framework), and the documented searches found no report of it.
  • Not claimed. The finder's probe suggests the default doc-values terms dictionary has the same problem when per-leaf doc-values termsEnum() views are merged through the @lucene.internal MultiTerms constructor. Only the finder checked it, so it is not part of the claim.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The TermsEnum javadoc promises "Each term in the enumeration is greater than the one before it"; here d follows d.
  • LUCENE-3260 added this re-seek so that next() after seekExact is right; TestTermsEnum asserts it through MultiTerms.getTerms.
  • The caller passes a fresh BytesRef; the shared buffer is made inside MultiTermsEnum, and nothing forbids MemoryIndex readers in a MultiReader.
The full argument166 words
  • The contract. TermsEnum class javadoc: "Term enumerations are always ordered by BytesRef.compareTo ... Each term in the enumeration is greater than the one before it." (TermsEnum.java:31-32).
  • The test. TestTermsEnum.testFloorBlocks takes MultiTerms.getTerms(r, FIELD).iterator() and asserts seekExact("aa1") then next() is aa2 (TestTermsEnum.java:518, 523-524). The index is random, so this is a MultiTermsEnum only when it has several segments; with one, MultiTerms.getTerms returns the leaf's own terms (MultiTerms.java:69-72).
  • The history. LUCENE-3260 (#4333, 2011) added the re-seek in next() so that subs without the term move on after seekExact; the code comment states the assumption that subs short-circuit a seek to their current term.
  • Known hazard elsewhere. UniformSplit guards exactly this: "required to not search with the same BytesRef instance ... Indeed getCurrentTerm() is allowed to return the same BytesRef instance." (BlockReader.java:188-190).
  • No exclusion. No javadoc, package doc, MIGRATE.md or CHANGES entry says MemoryIndex readers must not be composed; createSearcher().getIndexReader() is a plain LeafReader, and MultiReader takes any IndexReader.
The devil's-advocate check101 words

Strongest case for "works as designed": TermsEnum, MultiTerms and MultiTermsEnum are @lucene.experimental; BytesRefIterator says the returned "BytesRef may be re-used across calls to next" (BytesRefIterator.java:27); and no javadoc says a seek target may alias the enum's own term(), so MemoryIndex may use br as scratch.

Why it fails: the caller passes a fresh BytesRef, and the alias is made inside MultiTermsEnum, so the user cannot avoid it; the documented ordering is broken silently; @lucene.experimental is about API stability, not correctness. The cross-model lenses and the intended-behaviour verifier rated this argument weak. Verdict: a defect.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended (a) Re-seek with a copy of current in MultiTermsEnum.next() (recommended, validated)

In MultiTermsEnum.next(), copy current into a private BytesRefBuilder before the re-seek, so no reader is ever handed its own term buffer as the target (+5/-1 lines). With it the repro passes, assertions on and off, and so do the closest core suites (22 tests, 1 skipped) and the whole memory module (52 tests, the new one included).

private final BytesRefBuilder reseekScratch = new BytesRefBuilder();
...
if (lastSeekExact) {
  // current is the term() of one of the subs that seekCeil re-seeks: pass a copy, as
  // a sub may use its term() buffer as scratch while it seeks
  reseekScratch.copyBytes(current);
  final SeekStatus status = seekCeil(reseekScratch.get());
  assert status == SeekStatus.FOUND;
  lastSeekExact = false;
}
All options and trade-offs3 options · 461 words

Two fixes were validated; each alone makes the repro pass. (a) re-seeks with a copy of the target, for any sub; (b) makes MemoryIndex tolerate a target that is its own term(). They can be combined.

(a) Re-seek with a copy of current in MultiTermsEnum.next() (recommended, validated)

#

The diff, in MultiTermsEnum.java: one new field and the copy before the existing seekCeil call.

private final BytesRefBuilder reseekScratch = new BytesRefBuilder();
...
if (lastSeekExact) {
  // current is the term() of one of the subs that seekCeil re-seeks: pass a copy, as
  // a sub may use its term() buffer as scratch while it seeks
  reseekScratch.copyBytes(current);
  final SeekStatus status = seekCeil(reseekScratch.get());
  assert status == SeekStatus.FOUND;
  lastSeekExact = false;
}

Trade-offs

  • Fixes the internal re-seek in next() for every sub implementation by passing it a copied target (per the finder's probe it also covers the doc-values case that is not claimed here). It does not remove the alias itself (MultiTermsEnum.java:156, :208): a caller who passes the merged enum's own term() back in as a seek target is not covered by it (not tested).
  • Cost: one copy of the term's bytes per next() that follows a successful seekExact; nothing on plain iteration.

What the checks validated

  • Clean scratch worktree of f794ff5a505c, gradle, OpenJDK 25.0.3, 2026-11-09: the repro fails on stock with assertions on and off and passes with the fix, on and off.
  • With the fix: TestTermsEnum, TestTermsEnum2, TestMultiTermsEnum, TestMultiFields and TestTermsEnumIndex (22 tests, 1 skipped, 0 failures) and the whole :lucene:memory:test module (4 suites, 52 tests including the new one, 0 failures) pass; checkGoogleJavaFormat passes for core and memory.

(b) MemoryTermsEnum binary-searches into a scratch BytesRef (validated)

#

The diff, in MemoryIndex.java (+12/-3): seekExact and seekCeil pass a private probe to binarySearch and set br only once positioned.

Trade-offs

  • Narrower: fixes MemoryIndex only; another sub that uses its term() buffer as seek scratch would stay exposed.
  • Cost: negligible (BytesRefHash.get only re-points the BytesRef).

What the checks validated

  • Same worktree: the repro passes with the fix, on and off; the whole :lucene:memory:test module (52 tests including the new one, 0 failures) and :lucene:memory:checkGoogleJavaFormat pass.
  • The first layout of this diff (same code) passed the tests but failed the format check on two lines over 100 columns; the final diff is re-laid out and was re-run.

(c) Re-seek only the subs that did not match (not tried)

#

The comment in next() already notes "we could save some CPU by only seekCeil the subs that didn't match the last exact seek". The matching subs are on the term, and the others would get a buffer they do not own. It needs new queue handling in next(), so it is more invasive; not implemented or tested.

Downstream note

#

Elasticsearch once composed MemoryIndex readers in a MultiReader for its percolator (replaced in 2016, elastic/elasticsearch#19118); a downstream engine that merges terms this way and calls next() right after seekExact would get wrong terms.

How it was found

#

A proof-obligation audit of MultiTermsEnum's contract: next() after seekExact must return the following term, and the seek target handed to each reader must not be a buffer that reader owns. The second obligation fails on reading the code; a public-API test witnessed it, and a survey of 11 other terms dictionaries showed MemoryIndex is the one that breaks.

The method in full95 words

Semi-formal method: the audit listed the obligations each MultiTermsEnum method relies on and checked them against the code. next() after seekExact re-seeks with current, so the obligation is that every sub's seekCeil copes with a target that is its own term(). MemoryTermsEnum.binarySearch does not.

The witness is the one-test repro. A bounded comparison of seekExact(t) then next() over two leaves of each of 11 in-tree postings and term-vector terms dictionaries (default codec, SimpleText, Direct, FST50, BlockTreeOrds, LuceneFixedGap, LuceneVarGapFixedInterval, LuceneVarGapDocFreqInterval, UniformSplit, SharedTermsUniformSplit, TermVectorLeafReader) against the expected successor found them all correct and isolated MemoryIndex.

History

Summary
You put two MemoryIndex readers, each holding the terms a, b, c, d and e in field f, into a MultiReader, take MultiTerms.getTerms(reader, "f").iterator(), call seekExact("a"), which returns true, and then next(). You expect "b", as the TermsEnum contract ('Each term in the enumeration is greater than the one before it') and upstream TestTermsEnum require, but you get "c". After seekExact the merged enum keeps the second sub's own term() BytesRef as its current term and next() re-seeks every sub with it; MemoryIndex's seekCeil binary-searches by writing each probe term into that same BytesRef, so it overwrites the target with the middle term, finds it equal and stops there.
