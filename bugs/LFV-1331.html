LatLonShape.createDocValueField rejects a valid line along longitude -180

A valid Line along longitude -180 that ends with a tiny step east cannot get a shape doc-value field: LatLonShape.createDocValueField throws invalid longitude -180.00000000000003. Its centroid, an average of the line's own points, rounds one ulp past -180 and is encoded without a clamp (every release since 9.4.0).

The finding in 30 seconds

#

For a line, the shape doc-value centroid is the length-weighted average of the segment midpoints, computed in double. It is encoded with the range-checking geo encoders without being clamped into the line's bounding box, so a one-ulp rounding error at -180 or -90 becomes an exception.

  1. Build new Line(lats {-80, -10, -9.99999}, lons {-180, -180, -179.9999999}): 70 degrees straight down longitude -180, then a segment of about 1 m that ends one encoding step (about 1 cm) east. Line accepts it, and createIndexableFields indexes it.
  2. Call LatLonShape.createDocValueField("f", line). You expect a field whose centroid, the length-weighted average of the segment midpoints, is about -179.999999999999994: inside the line's box, longitude -180 to -179.99999992.
  3. Instead the constructor throws IllegalArgumentException: invalid longitude -180.00000000000003, with assertions on and off. Divided in double, the sums -12600.0018 / 70.00001 give a value one ulp (2.8e-14 degrees) below -180.
  4. Writer.writeTree passes that value straight to GeoEncodingUtils.encodeLongitude, which range-checks it. Only -180 and -90 have no slack: they are exact grid values, while the top grid longitude is 179.99999992, so the tested lines along +180 and +90 build. Lines along latitude -90 fail the same way.

Who is affected

#

Applications that build shape doc-value fields for lines (createDocValueField(String, Line) or a line-only Field[]), an opt-in, experimental API. It needs a segment whose vertices encode to longitude -180 or latitude -90, plus a short last segment just off it, as antimeridian-clipped data with a near-duplicate vertex might have. The document fails loudly; nothing reaches the index.

Mechanism, terms and numbers369 words

A shape doc-value field stores a centroid; for a line it is the length-weighted mean of the segment midpoints, computed in double in ShapeDocValues.buildTree. Writer.writeTree then encodes it with GeoEncodingUtils.encodeLongitude / encodeLatitude, which throw outside [-180, 180] / [-90, 90], without first clamping it into the bounding box it has just written. The exact mean of a line's own points lies inside that box, but the double sums can round one ulp past it, and on the -180 / -90 side the bound is itself a grid value, so there is no slack. new Line(lats {-80, -10, -9.99999}, lons {-180, -180, -179.9999999}) is accepted by the Line constructor and by createIndexableFields, but LatLonShape.createDocValueField("f", line) throws IllegalArgumentException: invalid longitude -180.00000000000003; must be between -180.0 and 180.0, with assertions on and off, on main and on every release run from 9.4.0 to 10.6.0. Clamping the centroid into the decoded bounding box before encoding fixes it.

Exposure and likelihood265 words
  • Who is hit. Callers of LatLonShape.createDocValueField(String, Line) and of createDocValueField(String, Field[]) with a line-only tessellation (same exception). The shape doc-values API is opt-in and @lucene.experimental (LatLonShapeDocValuesField.java:52); no IndexWriter setting is involved.
  • What they see. An IllegalArgumentException at document construction that blames a longitude the caller never supplied. The document can be indexed only without its doc-value field, or not at all. No index change, no wrong results.
  • What it takes. A segment whose vertices encode to the lowest grid value: longitude -180 itself or anything less than one encoding step (8.4e-8 degrees) above it, since encodeLongitude rounds down (GeoEncodingUtils.java:90-96); likewise latitude -90. In probes on main: 161 of 2,000 seeded lines of this shape failed; one probed family needed the last vertex 1 to 10 encoding steps east of -180 with a latitude step near 1e-7 to 1e-6 degrees (this repro's 1e-5 also fails); 0 of 10,000 random lines with 4-decimal coordinates failed. The tested polygon controls, the +180 and +90 mirrors and the same shape at longitude -170 built (polygons were not ruled out exhaustively). Rare, but round-number inputs hit it.
  • A silent variant away from the bounds. Elsewhere the same rounding can store a centroid one encoding step outside the line's own box, with no exception: in the same seeded family at longitude -170, 186 of 2,000 lines. Callers see that value through LatLonShapeDocValuesField.getCentroid() (LatLonShapeDocValuesField.java:67-68); the shape doc-values query traversal skips the stored centroid (ShapeDocValues.java:769). Fix A cures this too.
  • Age. In every release since the API was added in 9.4.0; not new in 10.6.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The input is valid: Line checks every vertex, -180 is an inclusive bound, and createIndexableFields indexes the same line.
  • The rejected value is Lucene's own: a weighted mean of the line's points, which belongs inside the box writeTree just wrote.
  • No javadoc, test, CHANGES entry or #1017 review comment that was read treats it as intended.
The full argument170 words
  • Valid input. Line validates each vertex with checkLatitude / checkLongitude (Line.java:72-74). GeoUtils.MIN_LON_INCL = -180.0D (GeoUtils.java:53); the geo package doc gives "Longitude: -180° to +180°" (geo/package-info.java:34). Lucene's GeoTestUtil picks the range ends on purpose ("explicitly targeting edges", GeoTestUtil.java:66, :90-95).
  • Caller's precondition. encodeLongitude: "@param longitude longitude value: must be within standard +/-180 coordinate bounds", "@throws IllegalArgumentException if longitude is out of bounds" (GeoEncodingUtils.java:86-88). Here the caller is ShapeDocValues, encoding a value it derived from in-range vertices.
  • No recorded intent found in the javadocs, CHANGES and MIGRATE entries, tests, commit messages and #1017 review material that were read. On #1017, iverase pointed out that centroid sums over encoded ints could overflow, and nknize moved the centroid into decoded double space; jpountz suggested reusing Elasticsearch's implementation, and nknize preferred a fresh Lucene implementation. No comment discusses rounding past the coordinate bound or a clamp.
  • Precedent. #11973 / #11974, an edge-flag mistake in the same experimental class, was fixed as an ordinary bug.
The devil's-advocate check87 words

Strongest case for "intended": the API is @lucene.experimental, its javadoc warns that "vertex values are indexed with some loss of precision" (LatLonShapeDocValuesField.java:47-48), and encodeLongitude documents that it throws on out-of-range input.

Why it fails: experimental allows API and format changes, not exceptions on valid shapes; the precision warning is about quantized vertices, not rejecting a shape; and the out-of-range value is computed by ShapeDocValues itself. Reviewers from another model family (OpenAI GPT-5.6) rated the argument weak; an intended-behaviour check and the adjudication rejected it.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A: clamp the centroid into the bounding box before encoding

Clamp the centroid into the decoded bounding box in Writer.writeTree before encoding it. Validated: the repro passes with assertions on and off, and the nine shape suites pass (67 tests, 7 skipped). It changes the stored centroid only when the computed centroid lies outside the box: by rounding, or the zero-weight (0, 0) fallback of LFV-366. No format change.

// write centroid; it is a weighted mean of the shape's coordinates, so clamp the rounding
// error of the double sums back into the bounding box before encoding
double centroidX =
    Math.min(Math.max(root.midX, encoder.decodeX(root.minX)), encoder.decodeX(root.maxX));
double centroidY =
    Math.min(Math.max(root.midY, encoder.decodeY(root.minY)), encoder.decodeY(root.maxY));
output.writeVLong((long) encoder.encodeX(centroidX) - Integer.MIN_VALUE);
output.writeVLong((long) encoder.encodeY(centroidY) - Integer.MIN_VALUE);
All options and trade-offs3 options · 374 words

Fix A was validated with gradle in a scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168; B and C were not run. A separate bug in the same centroid code (zero total length leaves the centroid at (0, 0), LFV-366) needs its own fix in buildTree; fix A would only move that wrong value into the box.

A: clamp the centroid into the bounding box before encoding (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

Eight lines in Writer.writeTree (ShapeDocValues.java:491-493), shared by LatLon and XY shapes.

// write centroid; it is a weighted mean of the shape's coordinates, so clamp the rounding
// error of the double sums back into the bounding box before encoding
double centroidX =
    Math.min(Math.max(root.midX, encoder.decodeX(root.minX)), encoder.decodeX(root.maxX));
double centroidY =
    Math.min(Math.max(root.midY, encoder.decodeY(root.minY)), encoder.decodeY(root.maxY));
output.writeVLong((long) encoder.encodeX(centroidX) - Integer.MIN_VALUE);
output.writeVLong((long) encoder.encodeY(centroidY) - Integer.MIN_VALUE);

Trade-offs

Keeps the centroid inside the shape's box, where it belongs. A no-op whenever the computed centroid is already inside the box, so ordinary shapes store the same bytes; it also moves into the box the silent one-step overshoot described under "Who is affected" and the zero-weight (0, 0) fallback of LFV-366. No format change. Shared with XY shapes, whose encoder casts to float and rejects only non-finite values (XYShapeDocValues.java:78-85, XYEncodingUtils.java:35-52), so no XY failure is claimed.

B: compute the mean relative to the box minimum

#

minX + sum(w * (mid - minX)) / sum(w): every term is >= 0, so the result cannot fall below the minimum.

Trade-offs

Not run. Still needs a guard at the maximum, and it changes the sums in buildTree for every shape.

C: wrap the value, as Elasticsearch does

#

Elasticsearch's own geo_shape doc-values writer normalizes its centroid (normalizeLon / normalizeLat, elastic/elasticsearch#50297, #64619).

Trade-offs

Not run. Avoids the exception, but wrapping turns -180.00000000000003 into a longitude near +180, outside the line's box. Not recommended for Lucene.

Downstream note

#

For a service that indexes user-supplied line geometry, the effect is one rejected document with a misleading error: no hang, no unbounded resource use, no index change, no wrong indexed results.

How it was found

#

A proof-obligation audit of the shape doc-values centroid-encoding contract: the encoded centroid of a valid shape must be encodable, since it is a weighted mean of in-range coordinates. The audit found the unclamped encode, and an executable JUnit test on main then witnessed the exception.

The method in full82 words

Method: semi-formal (2026-11-04, main 5adff78bb168).

  • The obligation: for every shape that the public factories accept, encodeX(root.midX) and encodeY(root.midY) in Writer.writeTree get a value inside [-180, 180] / [-90, 90]. Mathematically the weighted mean lies in the shape's bounding box, but the double computation does not guarantee it, and nothing in writeTree clamps it.
  • Witness: a public-API JUnit test with a line on the lowest grid longitude and a last vertex one encoding step east, reduced to the round-number test shown here.
