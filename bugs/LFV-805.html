Intervals.ordered(a, a) reports a minimum width of 1, so an Intervals.or under phrase or maxgaps silently drops the two-word match

The bug in 30 seconds

#

Interval queries match runs of positions. minExtent() is the smallest width a source can match. Under phrase and maxgaps, Lucene splits an or() whose alternatives have different widths into separate phrases, because inside one OR a wider match that contains a narrower one is dropped. Alternatives of width 1 stay together.

  1. You index x a a c (positions 0 to 3) and search phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), a), c): x, then either a a or a, then c. You expect 1 hit: phrase(x, ordered(a, a), c) alone finds this document, and or() is documented to rewrite itself under phrase so that alternatives of different lengths are not lost.
  2. You get 0 hits, with no error. With two different words it works: phrase(x, or(ordered(a, b), a), c) finds x a b c, because Lucene rewrites it into or(phrase(x, ordered(a, b), c), phrase(x, a, c)).
  3. Why: the rewrite splits out only alternatives whose minExtent(), the smallest width they can match, is above 1. ordered(a, a) is built as one repeated source that reports the width of a single a, 1, although it spans 2 positions here. So the OR stays whole.
  4. Inside the OR, the one-word matches [1,1] and [2,2] lie inside the two-word match [1,2], and minimal-interval semantics drops [1,2]. The phrase needs exactly [1,2] between x (0) and c (3), so it finds nothing.
  5. The same with unordered(a, a), under maxgaps(0, ...), containedBy and containing, with any other width-1 alternative inside the copies' span (or(ordered(a, a), b) misses x a b a c; or(ordered(a, a, a), ordered(a, a)) misses x a a a c) and from the query string fn:phrase(x fn:or(fn:ordered(a a) a) c). Since 8.5.0.

Who is affected

#

Anyone whose interval query (the Intervals factories, the query parser's fn: functions, or Intervals.analyzedText over text that repeats a word) puts a clause made only of copies of one word inside the default or() under phrase, maxgaps, containedBy or containing, next to a one-word or shorter repeated alternative. Default settings, any index size, no error: matches disappear.

Mechanism, terms and numbers970 words

Under a gap-sensitive parent (phrase, maxgaps), Intervals.or is documented to rewrite itself so that alternatives of different lengths are not minimized away. The rewrite decides "one position or longer" with minExtent() == 1. Since LUCENE-9099 (8.5.0), Intervals.ordered(a, a) and Intervals.unordered(a, a) are a RepeatingIntervalsSource whose minExtent() returns the inner source's value, 1 for a term, although on ordinary text each interval it returns spans at least 2 positions. So or(ordered(a, a), a) stays one OR; on x a a c that OR returns [1,1] and [2,2] and drops [1,2], and phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), a), c) finds nothing, while phrase(x, ordered(a, a), c) alone and phrase(x, or(ordered(a, b), a), c) on x a b c match. The same happens with unordered(a, a), under maxgaps(0, ...) and on the big side of containedBy/containing, with any other alternative that also reports minExtent() 1 and falls inside the copies' span (a word between them, or a shorter repeat such as ordered(a, a) next to ordered(a, a, a)), through the flexible query parser's fn: functions and through Intervals.analyzedText("a a", ...) with gaps. Silent wrong results with the default writer, codec and searcher, in every release from 8.5.0 to 10.5.2 and on main. Counting the copies in minExtent() (in.minExtent() + childCount - 1, saturated) makes the repro pass with the whole lucene/queries module green; applied alone it widens two separate bugs (LFV-291, LFV-810), so it should land with or after their fixes.

Terms

#
  • Interval query: matches runs of positions. Intervals.term("a") gives one-position intervals such as [1,1]; ordered(a, b) gives [start, end] of an a followed by a b; phrase(x, s, c) needs x, then s, then c back to back.
  • Minimal intervals: most operators, and or() in particular, keep only intervals that contain no other interval they found (the repeated source below is documented as "avoiding minimization" of its own copies). If one alternative matches [2,2] and another [1,2], the OR keeps [2,2] and drops [1,2].
  • The disjunction rewrite (LUCENE-8477, 8.1.0): because of that, phrase and maxgaps rewrite an inner or() whose alternatives have different widths into an OR of separate queries, so phrase(x, or(ordered(a, b), a), c) becomes or(phrase(x, ordered(a, b), c), phrase(x, a, c)). or(false, ...) turns this off and is documented to "miss valid hits due to minimization".
  • minExtent(): "the minimum possible width of an interval returned by this source". The rewrite keeps every alternative with minExtent() == 1 together in one OR, as one-position alternatives.
  • Repeated source (LUCENE-9099, 8.5.0): ordered(a, a) and unordered(a, a) are built as one RepeatingIntervalsSource(a, 2), which joins two consecutive a intervals into one.

The bug

#
  1. Intervals.ordered and unordered merge equal arguments into a RepeatingIntervalsSource and return it as the whole query when nothing else is left (OrderedIntervalsSource.java:29-57, UnorderedIntervalsSource.java:33-57).
  2. Its minExtent() is return in.minExtent(); (RepeatingIntervalsSource.java:93-96): 1 for a term. On distinct positions its intervals are never narrower than 2 (all 510 documents of 1 to 8 positions over {a, b}, executed).
  3. Disjunctions.splitDisjunctions (Disjunctions.java:75-95) groups every alternative with minExtent() == 1 into one OR, citing "we know that all combinatorial sources have a minExtent > 1". or(ordered(a, a), a) therefore stays one OR inside the phrase. phrase, maxgaps, containedBy, containing, notContainedBy and notContaining run this rewrite when they are built.
  4. The OR's iterator orders intervals by end, then by start descending, and drops every interval that contains the one it just returned (DisjunctionIntervalsSource.java:171-175, :230-247). On x a a c it returns [1,1] and [2,2] and drops [1,2] of ordered(a, a). The phrase needs [1,2] between x (0) and c (3): 0 hits.
  5. ordered(a, b) sums its parts (OrderedIntervalsSource.java:69-75), 2, so it is split out and matches. A clause that merely contains a repeat, ordered(a, a, b), reports 2 (true minimum 3), so it is split out too; there only the score is affected.
  6. Side effect: IntervalScorer computes the sloppy frequency as 1 / max(length - minExtent + 1, 1) (IntervalScorer.java:66), so an exact a a match of ordered(a, a) scores 0.333 where ordered(a, b) on a b scores 0.5.

Exposure and likelihood282 words
  • Who. Applications that build interval queries with the Intervals factories, the flexible StandardQueryParser's fn: functions, or Intervals.analyzedText over query text that repeats a word, where a clause made only of copies of one source (ordered(a, a), unordered(a, a), ordered(a, a, a), or a filter over one such as maxgaps(1, ordered(a, a))) is an alternative of the default or() under phrase or maxgaps (or on the big side of containedBy/containing), next to another alternative that reports minExtent() 1 (a word, or a shorter repeat). A natural shape is "the word doubled, or the word once".
  • Effect. Documents in which that other alternative falls inside the copies' span are missed, silently and every time, with assertions on or off. Exact repeated matches also score lower than comparable two-word matches (0.333 against 0.5). Nothing is written to the index.
  • Default vs opt-in. Default writer, codec, analyzer and searcher; the rewrite that should protect the match is the default (or() means or(true, ...)). The intervals package is documented as experimental.
  • Scale. None: one tiny document triggers it, deterministically, at any index size.
  • How likely. Uncommon: it needs a repeated-only clause as an OR alternative under a parent that rewrites. A clause that merely contains a repeat (ordered(a, a, b)) matches correctly. No report was found in the six and a half years since 8.5.0.
  • Related, separate reports in the same class (each with its own fix line): the width() sign of the same iterator (LFV-306), a repeated disjunction that is never pulled up (LFV-809), overlapping copies in ordered(X, X) (LFV-807), nested repeats leaking inner gaps (LFV-810), and notContainedBy over the same rewrite (LFV-291).

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • minExtent() is "the minimum possible width"; ordered(a, a) is never 1 wide on distinct positions.
  • The rewrite exists "to ensure that documents ... are matched"; only or(false, ...) may "miss valid hits", and the bug makes or() act like it.
  • The rewrite assumes "all combinatorial sources have a minExtent > 1"; LUCENE-9099 broke that ten months later, unremarked.
The full argument268 words
  • The contract is a minimum width. IntervalsSource#minExtent: "Return the minimum possible width of an interval returned by this source" (IntervalsSource.java:63).
  • The rewrite promises this match. The Intervals class javadoc: gap-sensitive sources "will rewrite their sub-sources so that disjunctions of different lengths are pulled up to the top of the interval tree ... to ensure that documents containing "b c" are matched" (Intervals.java:47-54); or(): "Automatically rewrites if wrapped by an interval source that is sensitive to internal gaps" (:118-125). Only or(false, ...) "can miss valid hits due to minimization" (:127-136). LUCENE-8477, a bug fix in 8.1.0: "Interval disjunctions could miss valid hits if some of the clauses of the disjunction are minimized away. We now rewrite ..." (lucene/CHANGES.txt:6502-6506).
  • The code states the invariant the repeated source breaks: "we know that all combinatorial sources have a minExtent > 1" (Disjunctions.java:76-78).
  • Two changes that never met. LUCENE-8477 (2019, 8.1.0) added the grouping when it was sound: ordered(a, a) summed to 2, as executed on 8.2.0 to 8.4.1. LUCENE-9099 (2020, 8.5.0) added the repeated source with return in.minExtent();; its issue, commit message, CHANGES entry (lucene/CHANGES.txt:5996-6001) and its one approving review never mention minExtent() or the rewrite.
  • No upstream test asserts the current value. TestIntervals asserts minExtent() 20 times (for example 2 for unordered(pease, hot), TestIntervals.java:380-402); none of its repeat tests checks it, and TestDisjunctionRewrites has no repeated shape.
  • Legal input. Repeated arguments are supported since LUCENE-9099, analyzedText builds them from plain query text, and or() under phrase is the canonical LUCENE-8477 shape.
The devil's-advocate check287 words

The strongest case for "works as designed" (the cross-model review, reading only, rated it moderate; the intended-behaviour check built it and rejected it): minExtent() is only a lower bound and 1 is a valid one; for identical tokens stacked at one position (position increment 0) ordered(a, a) really returns [p, p], so 1 is then exact and the fix over-reports; and minimal-interval semantics drop containing intervals by design, in an experimental package.

Two more points a maintainer may raise: in the LUCENE-8477 issue discussion (a JIRA comment, now on #9523; not in the PR review) jimczi warned that minimization would keep producing pathological cases; and the flexible query parser's docs say, for fn:ordered/fn:unordered over an fn:or, that "the algorithm is not required to return or highlight all matching interval alternatives" (intervalfn/package-info.java:347-349, :381-383).

Why it fails: the only consumer that affects matching, splitDisjunctions, reads minExtent() == 1 as "every interval is one position", and the documented default promises the rewrite. On the stacked document every query keeps its hit with and without the fix, and the fix leaves its score unchanged (0.5), because the scorer clamps at 1 (executed). The sibling UnorderedIntervalsSource already over-reports the same way (unordered(a, b) returns [0,0] on stacked a/b, minExtent() 2, the value upstream asserts). LUCENE-8477 chose to rewrite rather than document such misses. The query parser note is about which intervals of a matching document are returned or highlighted (in its examples fn:ordered/fn:unordered do not rewrite and the document still matches); here the whole document is missed under a parent whose rewrite the Intervals note promises "to ensure that documents ... are matched". File the claim for distinct positions.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Count the copies in RepeatingIntervalsSource.minExtent()

Count the copies in RepeatingIntervalsSource.minExtent(): in.minExtent() + childCount - 1, saturated (+4/-1). The OR is then split as for two different words. The repro, a new rewrite test and the whole lucene/queries module pass. Applied alone it widens two separate bugs, so land it with or after the LFV-291 and LFV-810 fixes.

  @Override
  public int minExtent() {
-    return in.minExtent();
+    // On distinct positions each copy ends after the previous one, so the copies together span at
+    // least in.minExtent() + childCount - 1 positions (stacked tokens, or an `in` whose own
+    // minExtent() over-reports, can be narrower). Saturate: extend() can report MAX_VALUE.
+    return (int) Math.min((long) in.minExtent() + childCount - 1, Integer.MAX_VALUE);
  }

One fix was applied in a scratch worktree at a9779ef3c193 and run through gradle; the alternative was not run.

Two couplings, to state in the PR. Land the fix with or after the fixes of LFV-291 and LFV-810, or say so.

  • LFV-291 (notContainedBy over the same rewrite): applied alone, the fix lets more queries reach it. notContainedBy(x, or(ordered(a, a), b)) on a x a b goes from 0 hits (correct) to 1 (wrong), because the OR is now split the way or(ordered(a, b), b) already is (that control gives 1 on stock). Executed by the devil's advocate and re-run with this fix.
  • LFV-810 (a nested repeat passes its inner gap up to its parent): applied alone, the fix pulls the repeat out of the OR, so it then sits directly inside an ordered/unordered under maxgaps(n >= 1), where LFV-810 loses correct hits. maxgaps(1, unordered(x, or(unordered(a, a), b))) on x a c c a x and maxgaps(1, ordered(x, or(ordered(a, a), b), c)) on x a x x a c give 1 hit on stock and 0 with the fix; stock is right, because IntervalIterator#gaps counts only "the gaps between the immediate sub-intervals" (IntervalIterator.java:62-71). Over all 5,460 documents of 1 to 6 positions over {x, a, b, c} and 26 query shapes, these two queries lose 87 and 10 hits and 30 documents score lower; with the LFV-810 candidate fix applied as well, 0 hits are lost, no score falls and every gain is kept (the fact check's probe; the two queries re-run for this doc).

Workarounds today (executed, also on 10.5.2): build the rewritten form by hand, or(phrase(x, ordered(a, a), c), phrase(x, a, c)) (1 hit on x a a c and on x a c); or, when the copies must be adjacent anyway, write the repeat as phrase(a, a), whose minExtent() is 2, so phrase(x, or(phrase(a, a), a), c) is rewritten and finds x a a c.

Count the copies in RepeatingIntervalsSource.minExtent() (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In RepeatingIntervalsSource.minExtent() (RepeatingIntervalsSource.java:93-96) return in.minExtent() + childCount - 1, computed in long and saturated at Integer.MAX_VALUE (+4/-1 in one file). The iterator joins childCount consecutive intervals of in; on distinct positions each copy ends after the previous one, so the joined interval spans at least in.minExtent() + childCount - 1 positions when the first copy is at least in.minExtent() wide. Tight for unordered, whose copies may overlap. Not a universal bound (executed): stacked identical tokens give [p, p], and extend() clamps starts at 0, so ordered(extend(a, 2, 0), extend(a, 2, 0)) returns [0,1] on a a while reporting 3 on stock and 4 with the fix. The saturation is local: ExtendedIntervalsSource.minExtent() returns Integer.MAX_VALUE on overflow (ExtendedIntervalsSource.java:126-132) and ordered(extend(a, 0, MAX), extend(a, 0, MAX)).minExtent() stays Integer.MAX_VALUE, but the enclosing sums in OrderedIntervalsSource, UnorderedIntervalsSource and BlockIntervalsSource are unchecked: ordered(extend(a, 0, MAX), b).minExtent() already wraps to Integer.MIN_VALUE on stock, and with the fix so does ordered(ordered(h, h), b) for h = extend(a, 0, MAX - 2) (stock: MAX_VALUE). Add the structural test to TestDisjunctionRewrites (no upstream test covers a repeated shape).

  @Override
  public int minExtent() {
-    return in.minExtent();
+    // On distinct positions each copy ends after the previous one, so the copies together span at
+    // least in.minExtent() + childCount - 1 positions (stacked tokens, or an `in` whose own
+    // minExtent() over-reports, can be narrower). Saturate: extend() can report MAX_VALUE.
+    return (int) Math.min((long) in.minExtent() + childCount - 1, Integer.MAX_VALUE);
  }

Trade-offs

  • Query size and the clause limit: the rewrite now splits alternatives it grouped before, and Disjunctions.pullUp expands them as a Cartesian product, as it already does for different words (the cost the Intervals class note describes). A phrase of 10 clauses or(ordered(ti, ti), ti) grows from 1 BLOCK to 1,024; 11 such clauses build on stock but throw IllegalArgumentException: Too many disjunctions to expand with the fix (Disjunctions.java:43-45, default max clause count 1024), as 11 clauses or(ordered(ti, ui), ti) already do on stock (executed). The rewritten query's toString(), equals/hashCode (so query-cache keys) and the number of iterators change too. minExtent() itself is one addition, called when the query is built and once per segment when the scorer is created (IntervalQuery.java:189-196). No benchmark was run.
  • Hits and scores: the queries shown here start matching. Where the rewrite does not change the query's shape, scores can only rise, because the sloppy frequency uses minExtent() (ordered(a, a) on a a: 0.333 to 0.5). Queries that the rewrite now splits can lose hits and score lower through LFV-810 until that fix lands, and LFV-291 gains wrong hits (above). Broader monotonicity was not established.
  • For identical tokens stacked at one position the new value over-reports, as UnorderedIntervalsSource already does; in every executed stacked case the hits and scores stayed the same.
  • Not covered by any validation: custom IntervalsSource implementations, payload-filtered terms at one position, and extend() near position 0 or Integer.MAX_VALUE beyond the executed cases. A CHANGES entry under Bug Fixes is the owner's call.

Fix the grouping in splitDisjunctions instead (not run)

#

Preferred by the intended-lens cross-model reviewer, who places the defect here: group only alternatives known to return one-position intervals, rather than every alternative with minExtent() == 1 (Disjunctions.java:83).

Trade-offs

Keeps minExtent() exact for stacked tokens. A conservative "always a single position" property that defaults to false is correct without any propagation: every source not known to be single-position is split, as different words are today (with the same expansion cost). Propagating it through wrappers that keep it (maxgaps, maxwidth, fixField, ...) only keeps more of today's grouping. It leaves the low score. A comment in splitDisjunctions that its grouping relies on minExtent() being a true minimum would help with either option.

Workaround: write the rewritten query by hand (executed)

#

or(phrase(x, ordered(a, a), c), phrase(x, a, c)) instead of phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), a), c); 1 hit on x a a c and on x a c on stock main and 10.5.2.

Trade-offs

Needs the application to know about the bug and to expand every such OR itself; does not fix the score.

Downstream note

#

Downstream relevance: used as an exclusion (for example a MUST_NOT clause), such a query silently keeps the documents it fails to match.

How it was found

#

A check of Lucene's default disjunction rewrites flagged repeated sources inside an OR, and a probe on the stock jar showed the missed hit. A bounded-exhaustive comparison with an independent reference semantics (1.1 million checks on documents of up to 4 positions) then tied the missing matches to minExtent(), and a public-API JUnit reproduced them.

The method in full189 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation on the real jar (semi-formal tier).

  • First signal (2026-10-26): a hunt unit on spans and intervals checked whether Lucene's default disjunction rewrites keep their documented intent. It was clean except for repeated-source leads, and a probe on the stock jar showed phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), a), c) missing x a a c while the ordered(a, b) control matched.
  • A follow-up unit (2026-10-27, main a9779ef3c193) wrote a reference semantics from the javadoc and compared it with the real classes on every document of 1 to 4 positions over three letters and 6,856 expressions of depth up to 3 (1,100,295 checks; 23,911 failures on stock, spread over several separate bugs). Bug record

#

LFV-805, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
Intervals.ordered(a, a) and Intervals.unordered(a, a) report minExtent() 1, so the default disjunction rewrite under phrase/maxgaps leaves or(ordered(a, a), a) unsplit and phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), a), c) misses 'x a c'
Area
queries / intervals (RepeatingIntervalsSource.minExtent for ordered/unordered of equal sub-sources; Disjunctions rewrite under phrase/maxgaps)
Priority
Low, rank 294: Needs the unusual ordered(a, a) or unordered(a, a) shape as an or() alternative under phrase or maxgaps.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications whose interval queries (Intervals factories, StandardQueryParser fn: functions such as fn:phrase(x fn:or(fn:ordered(a a) a) c), or Intervals.analyzedText over text such as 'a a') put a clause made only of copies of one source, ordered(a, a) or unordered(a, a), as an alternative of a default rewriting Intervals.or under a gap-sensitive parent (phrase or maxgaps) silently miss documents in which a single-position alternative falls on or between the copies (phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), a), c) misses 'x a c'; phrase(x, or(ordered(a, a), b), c) misses 'x a b a c'), with the default writer, codec, analyzer and searcher, -ea and -da, at any index size, in every release from 8.5.0 to 10.5.2 and on main; nothing is written to the index, and as a side effect an exact repeated match scores lower (observed 0.33 for ordered(a, a) on 'x a c' against 0.5 for ordered(a, b) on 'a b').
Summary
You search with Intervals.phrase(x, Intervals.or(Intervals.ordered(a, a), a), c), meaning 'x, then either a or a, then c', on the text 'x a c' and expect a hit, because phrase(x, ordered(a, a), c) alone matches it and Intervals.or is documented to rewrite itself under gap-sensitive parents so that alternatives of different lengths are not lost. Instead it finds nothing: ordered(a, a) and unordered(a, a) are built as a RepeatingIntervalsSource whose minExtent() returns the minExtent of a single 'a' (1) instead of 2, the rewrite assumes that only single-position sources report 1 and so keeps or(ordered(a, a), a) as one OR, and inside that OR the interval of 'a a' contains the interval of a single 'a' and is minimized away. The same query with two different words, phrase(x, or(ordered(a, b), a), c) on 'x a b c', matches as expected.
