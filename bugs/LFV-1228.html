Block tree stats: the "term suffix bytes before compression" line averages the compressed bytes per block

Terms.getStats() and CheckIndex -level 2 -verbose print N term suffix bytes before compression (A suffix-bytes/block), but since 8.5.0 A is the compressed (on-disk) suffix bytes per block, the number on the next line. A one-block field prints 30 ... (24.0 suffix-bytes/block); only this diagnostic figure is wrong.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

Stats.startBlock adds each block's on-disk suffix span to totalBlockSuffixBytes and its decoded size to totalUncompressedBlockSuffixBytes. toString prints the second as the line's total but divides the first by the block count. The next line prints the first as compressed term suffix bytes, so the average belongs there.

Background: block tree statistics

#
  1. The default terms dictionary, the block tree, stores each field's terms in blocks (by default 25 to 48 entries; a field with fewer terms, like the repro's 10, is a single smaller block). A block keeps its terms' suffixes after a shared prefix, compressed when that pays off.
  2. Terms.getStats(), an expert debugging call, walks the whole dictionary and returns a Stats object; CheckIndex -level 2 -verbose prints it for every field.
  3. Two of its lines describe suffixes: N term suffix bytes before compression (A suffix-bytes/block) and, right below, M compressed term suffix bytes (ratio ...).

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Index one document with 10 StringField terms t0 to t9 in field f, default codec. The field's terms dictionary is a single block.
  2. Print terms("f").getStats(), or run CheckIndex -level 2 -verbose. With one block the per-block average is the total, so you expect 30 term suffix bytes before compression (30.0 suffix-bytes/block).
  3. You get (24.0 suffix-bytes/block), and the next line reads 24 compressed term suffix bytes: the average divides the compressed total, not its own line's total. Same with assertions on and off.
  4. At realistic size the gap is larger: 2,000 terms in 80 blocks print 128.9 per block (10314 compressed bytes / 80), where the line's own average is 34376 / 80 = 429.7.
  5. Why: LUCENE-4702 (8.5.0) moved the line's total to a new uncompressed counter and relabelled it, but left the average on the old counter, which now holds on-disk bytes. Totals, ratio, the two suffix counters, indexing and search are unaffected.

Who is affected

#

Anyone reading block tree statistics (CheckIndex -level 2|3 -verbose or Terms.getStats()) on a default-codec index since 8.5.0. Wrong whenever the on-disk and decoded suffix sizes differ, as for any field of realistic size: the printed figure is the true one times the next line's ratio (3.3 times too small for 2,000 terms). Indexing and search are unaffected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers549 words

The block tree terms dictionary's Stats.toString, which Terms.getStats() returns and CheckIndex -level 2 -verbose prints per field, shows the line N term suffix bytes before compression (A suffix-bytes/block). N is totalUncompressedBlockSuffixBytes, the decoded suffix bytes, but A is totalBlockSuffixBytes / totalBlockCount, where totalBlockSuffixBytes is the on-disk (possibly compressed) size that the next line prints as compressed term suffix bytes (Stats.java:205-214, :212). Every other average in the output divides its own line's total.

A one-block field of 10 terms prints 30 term suffix bytes before compression (24.0 suffix-bytes/block); a 2,000-term field with 80 blocks prints 128.9 where its own average is 429.7. The mix came in with LUCENE-4702 (b283b8df628, released in 8.5.0), is in every release since, through 10.6.0 and main, and is in both block tree copies on main (core lucene103 and backward-codecs lucene90; the released backward-codecs jars 9.0.0 to 10.6.0 also carry a lucene40 copy with it). Only this figure is wrong: the totals, the compression ratio, the two suffix counters, indexing, search and CheckIndex verdicts are unaffected. The fix is one word in each copy. Stats is @lucene.internal debug output with no documented format, so this is a small diagnostic defect.

Why 24 and not 30 in the minimal case

#

The block is NO_COMPRESSION, yet the two counters differ. Decoded: 20 suffix bytes (t0 to t9) plus 10 suffix-length bytes = 30. On disk: a 2-byte header, the 20 suffix bytes, a 1-byte length count and the 10 equal lengths written as one byte = 24 (Lucene103BlockTreeTermsWriter.java:920-942, expanded again on read at SegmentTermsEnumFrame.java:209-220). That storage trick is not part of this bug; it only explains why the minimal case already shows it.

Reach

#

Terms.getStats() is public: "Expert: returns additional information about this Terms instance for debugging purposes" (Terms.java:215-216); the block tree's FieldReader.getStats() is "For debugging -- used by CheckIndex too" (FieldReader.java:110-114). CheckIndex collects the stats in its postings check (CheckIndex.java:2254-2259), which runs only at -level 2 or higher (:1114-1125, :4754; the default level is 1, :4742, :4748), and prints them only with -verbose (:2488-2492).

Exposure and likelihood199 words
  • Default: the default codec and an ordinary IndexWriter; no special input or setting.
  • Effect: one misleading figure in debugging output, for someone judging block sizes or compression. The two suffix counters are right (another Stats field, indexNumBytes, is separately wrong: LFV-162), and the right average can be computed from the line's total and the block count.
  • Scale: the printed figure is the true one times the ratio printed on the next line: 128.9 against 429.7 for the 2,000-term field (80 blocks), and 196.4 against 238.8 in an output with 1,507,239 blocks that the change's author posted on #5767.
  • Not every field: a 3-term one-block field happened to print the right figure, because there the on-disk span equals the decoded size; hence "whenever the two sizes differ, as for any field of realistic size" (shown on 10-, 1,000- and 2,000-term fields).
  • People read this output: #5767, #8476 and #7254 have CheckIndex block tree statistics pasted into them.
  • Likelihood: deterministic (a given index always prints the same figure), but these are occasional debugging runs, nothing acts on the number, and no remark on it was found in almost seven years although it shows in the outputs posted on #5767.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The label says "before compression" and the total is the counter documented as "Total number of suffix bytes before compression"; the on-disk total has its own line below.
  • Every other average in the output divides its own line's total.
  • In the same PR the author fixed this mix on the parallel "term stats bytes" line, not on this one.
The full argument241 words
  • Contract: no format is documented (Stats is @lucene.internal, Stats.java:28-32), so the obligation comes from the line itself: its label, the javadoc of the counter it prints (:82-83), and the convention of the sibling lines (:188-196, :241-260).
  • History: before LUCENE-4702 (8.4.1) one counter served both numbers: totalBlockSuffixBytes term suffix bytes (totalBlockSuffixBytes / totalBlockCount suffix-bytes/block), the stored size, which was then the decoded size. b283b8df628 (jpountz, 2020-01-24) redefined totalBlockSuffixBytes as the on-disk span, added totalUncompressedBlockSuffixBytes, moved the line's total to it and relabelled the line, but kept the average on the old counter. In the same commit the parallel term stats bytes before compression line averaged its own uncompressed counter.
  • The author's own correction: in PR apache/lucene-solr#1126, c6c5649178d5 (2020-01-17) added the stats line with an uncompressed total and a compressed average, and 98daeb97302f (2020-01-20) changed only that line's average. The outputs posted on #5767 show it: 6647577 term stats bytes before compression (11.7 stats-bytes/block) on 2020-01-17 (11.7 = 2226414 compressed / 189932 blocks), (35.0 stats-bytes/block) on 2020-01-20 (6647577 / 189932), while the suffix line in the same posts keeps the compressed average (14057717 ... (43.1 suffix-bytes/block), 43.1 = 8180420 / 189932). The suffix line, written that way in 1007b92c0496 (2019-12-18), never got the matching edit.
  • No sign of intent: no test, CHANGES or MIGRATE entry describes the output, and no review comment on #1126 or its follow-up #1216 discusses the averages.
The devil's-advocate check134 words

The strongest case for "intended": Stats is @lucene.internal debug output with no stable format, and the parenthetical may have stayed "on-disk suffix bytes per block" on purpose, for continuity with the pre-8.5 figure. On that side, too: the parallel stats line the author corrected was dropped about a week later (#1216, 2020-01-28), the on-disk counter's javadoc is still meaningful, and maintainers could reasonably decline the change as churn in debug output. An independent reviewer from another model family rated this moderate.

Why it fails (whether it is intended is for maintainers to decide): the label next to the figure says "before compression", the on-disk total already has its own line, and the author made exactly this correction on the parallel stats line in the same PR. Internal status lowers the severity, not the correctness.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Average the line's own counter, in both copies

Divide the line's own counter: at Stats.java:212, totalBlockSuffixBytes becomes totalUncompressedBlockSuffixBytes, in core lucene103 and in backward-codecs lucene90. Validated with gradle: the test fails before and passes after, assertions on and off; the postings-format, CheckIndex and lucene90 suites pass (76 tests, 0 failures) and the format check is clean.
