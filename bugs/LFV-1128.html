LatLonShape circle queries miss a line or polygon whose edge crosses the circle with no vertex inside

A LatLonShape distance query can miss a line or polygon edge that crosses the circle when neither end is inside: Lucene tests one point of the edge, chosen in raw lon/lat degrees, which off the equator is not the nearest point. A line 8.95 km from the centre of a 10 km circle at latitude 60 gets 0 hits.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

When neither end of an edge is inside the circle, Circle2D tests only the edge point nearest the centre in raw degrees. Haversine distance shrinks east-west steps by cos(lat), so for a diagonal edge that is the wrong point, and an edge crossing only the circle's rim band can be reported as not touching it.

Background: how a shape edge is tested against a circle

#
  1. LatLonShape indexes a line or polygon as small triangles; their edges are straight lines in (lon, lat) degrees.
  2. A distance query (LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery) asks of every indexed edge whether it touches the circle. The circle is measured in metres with the haversine (great-circle) distance.
  3. On the ground one degree of longitude is cos(lat) times one degree of latitude: the same length at the equator, half as long at latitude 60.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Index a two-point LatLonShape line from (lat 60.38, lon 9.8) to (lat 59.78, lon 10.4) and query LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery with INTERSECTS and a 10 km circle around (60, 10).
  2. Both ends are outside the circle (43.7 km and 33.1 km away), but the line passes 8.95 km from the centre: about 12% of it lies inside. You expect 1 hit.
  3. You get 0 hits and no error, with assertions on and off. A DISJOINT query returns the line instead.
  4. Why: with no vertex inside, Circle2D tests one point of the edge: the centre's foot computed in raw degrees, as if a degree of longitude were as long as a degree of latitude. At latitude 60 it is half as long, so the tested point is 11.19 km away, outside, and nothing else is tested.
  5. So an edge that dips only into an outer band of the circle can be missed, depending on its direction. For ordinary radii the band's worst depth depends on latitude alone: 0 at the equator, 5.7% of the radius at 45°, 20% at 60°.

Who is affected

#

Anyone who queries LatLonShape lines or polygons with a circle: INTERSECTS can miss them, DISJOINT then over-matches, CONTAINS can over-match. No special configuration, ordinary radii, any latitude off the equator. It needs an edge long enough to cross the circle's rim band with both ends outside (two-point lines, coarse polygons), so it is rare in practice.

Mechanism, terms and numbers1,019 words

LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery decides for each indexed edge whether it touches the query circle. When neither end point of an edge is inside the circle, Circle2D tests one more point of the edge and nothing else: the foot of the circle's centre on the edge, computed with a Euclidean dot product on raw (lon, lat) degrees. On the ground a degree of longitude is only cos(lat) times a degree of latitude, so for a diagonal edge away from the equator that foot is not the point nearest the centre, and an edge that enters only an outer band of the circle can be silently missed by INTERSECTS and matched by DISJOINT, depending on its direction; through the same test a polygon can also be reported to CONTAIN a circle that crosses one of its edges. In the repro, a line passes 8.95 km from the centre of a 10 km circle at latitude 60, the tested point is 11.19 km away, and the query returns 0 hits. For circles up to about 100 km the band's worst depth, 1 - 2c/(1+c²) of the radius with c = cos(lat), does not depend on the radius (at 1,000 km a scan found slightly deeper misses): the Circle javadoc's own 1 km example circle misses a line 953 m from its centre. Point documents and XYShape circles are not affected. The defect is as old as the API (8.5.0, LUCENE-8707) and present in 10.6.0 and main. In #10279 (2020) Ignacio Vera already named this mechanism for a CONTAINS test failure; only withinTriangle was changed then. Scaling longitude differences by cos(centre latitude) in the projection fixes the repro and keeps the geo and shape test suites green.

The mechanism (main 5adff78bb168, lucene/core/src/java/org/apache/lucene/)

#
  1. Contracts. LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery: "create a query to find all polygons that intersect a provided circle" (document/LatLonShape.java:315-319). Component2D.intersectsLine: "return true if this component2D intersects the provided line" (geo/Component2D.java:49).
  2. The query. For an indexed LINE the INTERSECTS predicate calls component2D.intersectsLine (document/LatLonShapeQuery.java:101-108), for a TRIANGLE intersectsTriangle (:109-118). DISJOINT is intersects().negate() (document/SpatialQuery.java:129-130), so each INTERSECTS miss is a DISJOINT match.
  3. Vertices first. Circle2D.intersectsLine returns contains(a) || contains(b) || calculator.intersectsLine(a, b) (geo/Circle2D.java:81); intersectsTriangle checks the vertices, the centre in the triangle, then the three edges the same way (:99-106). withinLine (:165) and withinTriangle (:198-205, used by CONTAINS) use the same edge test.
  4. The haversine calculator calls the shared static helper with the centre, and again with the centre shifted by 360° for circles that cross the dateline (geo/Circle2D.java:449-459).
  5. The defect (fix site). The static intersectsLine (geo/Circle2D.java:219-256) projects the centre onto the segment with the Euclidean dot product on raw (lon, lat) degrees (:229-238), rejects a foot outside the segment (:240-242), builds that one point (:244-245) and returns calculator.contains(pX, pY) (:253), the haversine test (:427-447: a bounding-rectangle pre-check, then SloppyMath.haversinSortKey compared with the radius's sort key). No other point of the edge is tested.
  6. Why that point is wrong. The raw-degree foot minimises lon² + lat² differences, so it counts east-west steps as 1/cos(lat) times too long and slides along a diagonal edge towards the part that is closer to the centre in longitude (and farther in latitude): in the repro (60.09, 10.09) is 0.09° off the centre in both coordinates, the nearest point (60.036, 10.144) 0.036° in latitude and 0.144° in longitude. The Cartesian calculator (XYShape circles, :356-357) uses the same helper in its own matching metric and is correct. Point documents use contains only (document/LatLonShapeQuery.java:95-100).

The numbers (the repro)

#

Circle(lat 60, lon 10, 10 km); line A = (60.38, 9.8), B = (59.78, 10.4), 74.6 km long; A is 43.7 km and B 33.1 km from the centre.

  • Raw-degree foot: t = 0.483, (60.09, 10.09), 11,186 m from the centre: outside, so intersectsLine is false.
  • Nearest point of the line: t = 0.573, (60.036, 10.144), 8,947 m: inside. The test's witness (60.04, 10.14) is on the line at t = 17/30, 8,961 m away. About 12% of the line lies inside the circle.
  • Through LatLonGeometry.create(circle) the same component answers contains(10.14, 60.04) = true and intersectsLine = false.
  • The index stores quantized vertices (about 4.2e-8° latitude, 8.4e-8° longitude); with margins of about a kilometre this does not matter here.

How deep a missed edge can reach

#

In local metric coordinates the tested distance is at most (1 + c²)/(2c) times the true minimum, c = cos(lat), so an edge whose true minimum lies between 2c/(1 + c²)·R and R can be missed: a band of depth 1 - 2c/(1 + c²) of the radius R. Latitude 0: 0%; 30°: 1.0%; 45°: 5.7%; 48.86° (the javadoc example): 8.2%; 60°: 20%; 75°: 51.5%. In this small-circle approximation it does not depend on R: a numerical scan of random crossing segments found worst depths just below the bound for radii from 100 m to 100 km, and slightly deeper misses at 1,000 km (6.5% at 45°, 21.5% at 60°). Whether an edge in the band is missed depends on its direction: in that scan the stock code missed about half of the band crossings (53.4% at 45° and at 60°, R = 10 km).

Exposure and likelihood286 words
  • Who is hit. Users of LatLonShape line and polygon fields queried with newDistanceQuery, or with newGeometryQuery and a Circle: INTERSECTS misses the document, DISJOINT matches it, and CONTAINS can match a polygon whose edge cuts the circle (through withinTriangle). No special configuration: the repro uses a plain IndexWriterConfig and searcher and fails under every seed run (the test framework randomizes the codec), with assertions on or off; no error, and the index itself is fine.
  • Ordinary parameters. For ordinary radii (up to about 100 km) the band depends on latitude only: 5.7% of the radius at 45°, 20% at 60°. The Circle javadoc's own 1 km example circle misses a line 953 m from its centre.
  • What it takes. Both end points of the edge outside the circle while the edge passes through the outer band, so the edge must be long relative to the circle and run diagonally in lon/lat: two-point lines, coarse polygons, long road or pipeline segments. Densely sampled shapes likely have a vertex inside first (not measured). The finding is rated rare; no user report was found in the recorded searches.
  • How often on random crossings (a synthetic scan: end points uniform in a box of ±3 radii around the centre, only segments with both ends outside that truly cross, 5,071-5,286 per cell): the stock code misses 0% at the equator, 0.5-0.8% at 30°, 3.5-3.8% at 45°, 10.7-12.1% at 60° and 24.7-26.2% at 75°, for every radius from 100 m to 1,000 km (at 75° only up to 100 km: the 1,000 km cell leaves the lat/lon range).
  • Not affected. Point documents (they use contains), XYShape circles (matching Cartesian metric), and edges with a vertex inside the circle.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The same component says a point of the edge is inside the circle but the edge does not touch it.
  • In #10279 the maintainer described this mechanism and called its effect an error; #10909 fixed another missed match of this method as a bug.
  • Not a large-circle limit: the javadoc's own 1 km example misses a line 953 m away.
The full argument220 words
  • Self-contradiction. Through LatLonGeometry.create(circle), contains(10.14, 60.04) is true for a point of the edge while intersectsLine is false for the edge. Lucene's edges are straight lines in lon/lat by design, and the expected answer holds in that model; as a cross-check only, the great-circle arc between the same end points also comes within the radius (9.10 km in the repro, 953.6 m in the javadoc example).
  • Contracts are unqualified: "find all polygons that intersect a provided circle" (LatLonShape.java:315), "return true if this component2D intersects the provided line" (Component2D.java:49).
  • Maintainer's words. In #10279 (LUCENE-9239), explaining a CONTAINS test failure, Ignacio Vera wrote: "The current implementation uses cartesian geometry to find the closest point from a line to a point (our edges are cartesian!). Then it uses the spherical geometry to calculate the distance between this two points. This error is a side effect of this approach." The fix (b732ce70025) changed only withinTriangle; the projection stayed.
  • Precedent. #10909 (LUCENE-9870, PR #41) fixed another way this method missed matches along indexed lines, as a bug, in 8.8.2.
  • Why the tests are silent. The random shape-query tests use the query's own Circle2D as the oracle (BaseSpatialTestCase.java:641, :664), and TestCircle2D's fixed line cases use circles at latitude 0, where the band is empty.
The devil's-advocate check139 words

Strongest case for "intended": Circle is @lucene.experimental; its javadoc says "The circle is approximated on the spherical Earth model" and "For very large circles or circles near poles, consider using polygons instead" (geo/Circle.java:29-47); Component2D is @lucene.internal; and LUCENE-9239 knowingly kept the Cartesian closest point.

Why it fails: the miss happens inside Lucene's own spherical model (the same component says the point is inside); for ordinary radii the band depends on latitude, not on size, so a 1 km circle at 48.86° is neither "very large" nor "near poles"; @lucene.experimental allows API changes, not wrong answers; the maintainer called the effect an error, and #10909 treated a sibling miss as a bug. Cross-model reviews (OpenAI GPT-5.6) rated the argument moderate and rejected it, as did the devil's-advocate verifier and the adjudication. Verdict: not intended, a defect.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Project in a cos(latitude)-scaled metric (fix A)

In Circle2D's static intersectsLine, multiply longitude differences by cos(centre latitude) before the dot product (1 for the Cartesian calculator), so the tested foot is close to the true nearest point. The repro then passes, 54 geo and shape test suites stay green, and :lucene:core:check -x test passes.

    final double vectorAPX = (centerX - aX) * xScale; // xScale: 1 cartesian, cos(centerLat) haversine
    final double vectorAPY = centerY - aY;
    final double vectorABX = (bX - aX) * xScale;
    final double vectorABY = bY - aY;
    ...
    final double pX = aX + (bX - aX) * distance; // the foot itself stays in degrees

    // HaversinDistance: this.lonScale = Math.cos(Math.toRadians(centerLat));
    // Circle2D.intersectsLine(centerLon, centerLat, aX, aY, bX, bY, this, lonScale)
All options and trade-offs4 options · 606 words

Every point the helper tests lies on the edge, so within Lucene's model (lon/lat-straight edges and the existing contains predicate) a positive answer is right; a fix only has to test a better point, or more points, and options A-C add no geometric false match beyond that predicate's existing boundary behaviour. One fix was built and tested (not proven) in a scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168; the alternatives are listed with their trade-offs.

Project in a cos(latitude)-scaled metric (fix A) (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

Add an xScale parameter to the static helper and scale both x differences by it; build the foot from the unscaled difference. HaversinDistance passes cos(toRadians(centerLat)), computed once in its constructor, for both the normal and the dateline-shifted call; CartesianDistance passes 1 (unchanged behaviour). Full patch: 13 insertions, 7 deletions in geo/Circle2D.java.

    final double vectorAPX = (centerX - aX) * xScale; // xScale: 1 cartesian, cos(centerLat) haversine
    final double vectorAPY = centerY - aY;
    final double vectorABX = (bX - aX) * xScale;
    final double vectorABY = bY - aY;
    ...
    final double pX = aX + (bX - aX) * distance; // the foot itself stays in degrees

    // HaversinDistance: this.lonScale = Math.cos(Math.toRadians(centerLat));
    // Circle2D.intersectsLine(centerLon, centerLat, aX, aY, bX, bY, this, lonScale)

Trade-offs

Approximate, not exact: cos(lat) is taken at the centre. On the random crossings of the scan it misses none for radii up to 100 km at latitudes 0-75°; at 1,000 km it misses 0.02-0.15% (1, 7 and 8 of about 5,160 at 30°, 45° and 60°), all within 0.74% of the radius of the rim, and in one cell it missed one crossing the stock code caught. Cost: one multiplication per difference and one Math.cos per circle. At a pole the projection becomes a latitude-only projection. Not run separately at the poles, on dateline-crossing segments or circles, on zero-length segments, or for long edges of large circles beyond the scan, which models the helper only (no quantization, rectangle pre-check or dateline path).

Minimise the haversine distance along the edge (exact if the search finds the global minimum)

#

Search t in [0, 1] for the minimum of haversinSortKey along the edge (for example golden-section or Newton steps seeded by fix A's foot, on the part of the edge inside the circle's bounding box) and compare it with the radius.

Trade-offs

Removes fix A's residual, including for large circles, but costs several distance evaluations per edge on a hot leaf path, and its convergence needs an argument (the distance along a lon/lat-straight edge is not guaranteed to be unimodal for very long edges). Not implemented or run.

Test both feet

#

Keep today's raw-degree foot and also test fix A's foot.

Trade-offs

Never worse than today on any input (it only adds a test of a point on the edge); still approximate; two contains calls per edge. Not run.

Accept within a tolerance

#

Accept the edge when the tested point is within the radius plus an error bound.

Trade-offs

Not recommended: it adds false matches for edges that pass just outside the circle, trading one wrong answer for another. Not run.

How it was found

#

A proof-obligation audit of Circle2D's contract asked whether intersectsLine is true whenever an edge shares a point with the circle. The closest-point step failed that obligation for haversine circles off the equator, and an executable public-API repro witnessed it. An independent reproducer, cross-model reviews, a devil's-advocate verifier and an adjudication confirmed it.

The method in full170 words

Method: semi-formal (a proof-obligation audit of a class's contract, witnessed by an executable repro).

  • The obligation: Component2D.intersectsLine must be true when the segment shares a point with the circle. For the haversine calculator the proof step "the tested foot is the nearest point of the segment" does not hold, because the foot is computed in raw degrees; a public-API JUnit (circle at 45°, a diagonal two-point line) witnessed the miss on main, 10.6.0 and 9.12.3, and a cos-latitude fix sketch made it pass.
  • Then an independent reproducer wrote its own test from the claim (the 60° repro here), cross-model reviews (OpenAI GPT-5.6, intended and refute lenses, executed) wrote and ran their own tests, a devil's-advocate verifier argued the intended-behaviour case and found the javadoc-example miss, and an adjudication re-ran the tests and checked the geometry. An audit and a final repro check followed.
  • Earlier hunt coverage had not reached it: its Circle2D checks used XY circles only, and its distance-query checks used point documents, which this bug does not affect.

LFV-1128, snapshot of 2026-11-08

Registry title
LatLonShape circle (distance) queries miss lines and polygons whose edge crosses the circle with no vertex inside, because Circle2D tests only the edge point closest to the centre in raw lon/lat degrees, not the point closest by haversine distance (DISJOINT matches them instead)
Area
core / geo (Circle2D haversine segment test behind LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery)
Priority
Medium, rank 42: LatLonShape distance queries silently miss lines and polygons under INTERSECTS, and match them under DISJOINT, at ordinary radii away from the equator, with default settings in every release since 8.5.0. It needs a long edge that crosses only the circle's outer band with both ends outside, which densely sampled shapes rarely have, so the trigger is rare.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
LatLonShape line and polygon documents queried with LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery are hit when an edge crosses the circle with both ends outside and runs diagonally in lon/lat away from the equator: an edge that passes only through an outer band of the circle is silently missed by INTERSECTS and matched by DISJOINT (and, through the same test, a polygon can be reported to CONTAIN a circle that crosses one of its edges), with the default codec, IndexWriterConfig and searcher, -ea and -da alike; the band's worst-case depth depends only on latitude, 1-2c/(1+c^2) of the radius with c = cos(lat) (0 at the equator, 5.7% at 45 deg, 20% at 60 deg), so ordinary radii are affected (the Circle javadoc's own 1 km example circle misses a line 953 m from its centre), but the edge must be long compared with the band's chord, so densely sampled shapes rarely meet it; point documents and XYShape circles are not affected.
Summary
You index a two-point LatLonShape line from (lat 60.38, lon 9.8) to (lat 59.78, lon 10.4) and run LatLonShape.newDistanceQuery with INTERSECTS and a 10 km circle around (lat 60, lon 10). The line passes 8.95 km from the centre, so you expect 1 hit, and the circle's own contains() accepts a point of the line; you get 0 hits and no error, and DISJOINT matches the line instead. When neither end is inside, Circle2D tests only the point of the segment closest to the centre in raw lon/lat degrees (here 11.19 km away), not the point closest by haversine distance, so diagonal edges that cross the outer part of the circle are missed.
