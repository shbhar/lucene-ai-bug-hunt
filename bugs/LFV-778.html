`makeRecipDistanceValueSource` measures geo boxes wider than 180 degrees the short way round, so edge scores fall below ~0.1 (0.0082 at 340 degrees wide)

For a geo query box wider than 180 degrees, SpatialStrategy.makeRecipDistanceValueSource measures the box the short way round the globe, so its edges score far too low: 0.0082 instead of the documented ~0.091 for rect(-170, 170, -10, 10). Affects every strategy with a distance source since 4.0.0; the hits are unchanged.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

zScaling should follow the query shape's size. Lucene estimates it from the distance between two opposite bounding-box corners: exact on a plane and inside the tested tolerance for geo boxes up to 180 degrees wide, but on a globe a wider box's corners are closer the other way round, through the longitudes outside the box.

  1. You score a geo query by distance with strategy.makeRecipDistanceValueSource(queryShape). Its javadoc: score = zScaling / (d + zScaling), where zScaling is one tenth of the distance from the centre to the farthest edge, so 1 at the centre and ~0.1 at the edge.
  2. Your query box is rect(-170, 170, -10, 10): 340 degrees of longitude around (0, 0). The point (170, 0) on its east edge is 170 degrees away, so you expect zScaling 17.0 and a score of 17 / 187 = 0.091 there.
  3. You get zScaling 1.41 and a score of 0.0082, on all four strategies. Lucene takes half the distance between the corners (-170, -10) and (170, 10), and on the globe those are only 28.2 degrees apart, the short way across the dateline.
  4. So a box W degrees wide is scaled like a box 360 - W wide. The band of all longitudes rect(-180, 180, -5, 5) gets zScaling 0.5 instead of 17.5: its edge point (180, 5) scores 0.0028.
  5. No error and the same hits, but distance swamps any combined score: with FunctionScoreQuery.boostByValue on the band, a hit with inner score 10 at 170 degrees ranks below a hit with inner score 1 at 10 degrees (0.029 against 0.048).

Who is affected

#

Applications that boost or rank by makeRecipDistanceValueSource in a geo context with a query shape wider than 180 degrees (wide boxes, latitude bands, hemispheres; mildly, pole circles), on every strategy with a distance source. Scores leave the tested tolerance for thin boxes from about 327 degrees wide; the hits and sorting by the value alone do not change.

Mechanism, terms and numbers821 words

SpatialStrategy.makeRecipDistanceValueSource(queryShape) returns zScaling / (d + zScaling), and its javadoc says zScaling is "one tenth the distance to the farthest edge from the center", so scores are 1 at the centre and "as low as ~0.1 at its furthest edges". The code takes one tenth of half the distance between the bounding box's corners (minX, minY) and (maxX, maxY). In a geo context that is a great-circle distance, which always takes the shorter way round the globe; for a box wider than 180 degrees the shorter way runs outside the box, across the dateline, so a box W degrees wide gets the zScaling of a box 360 - W wide.

For rect(-170, 170, -10, 10) zScaling is 1.41 instead of 17.0 and the edge point (170, 0) scores 0.0082 instead of 0.091; for the band rect(-180, 180, -5, 5) it is 0.5 and 0.0028, on every strategy with a distance source (PointVector, BBox, SerializedDV and both prefix-tree strategies; the method is final, so they share the zScaling). Nothing fails and the hits do not change, but any combined score over-weights distance. The code is unchanged since the method was added in 4.0.0 (2012); LUCENE-4550 (4.2.0) fixed the identical computation in SpatialArgs.calcDistanceFromErrPct and left this copy. A guarded fix that changes only geo shapes wider than 180 degrees passes the repro and the module's tests.

How the score is computed

#
  • SpatialStrategy.java:126-132 (javadoc): "The formula is zScaling/(d + zScaling) where 'd' is the distance and 'zScaling' is one tenth the distance to the farthest edge from the center. Thus the scores will be 1 for indexed points at the center of the query shape and as low as ~0.1 at its furthest edges."
  • :133 the method is public final, so every strategy shares its zScaling; the distance comes from each strategy's makeDistanceValueSource (:143), which CompositeSpatialStrategy and NumberRangePrefixTreeStrategy do not support (they throw). The class is @lucene.experimental (:53; :51: "however it is quite stable").
  • :134-141 diagonalDist = distance from corner A = (minX, minY) to corner B = (maxX, maxY) of queryShape.getBoundingBox(); distToEdge = diagonalDist * 0.5.
  • :142-144 c = distToEdge * 0.1, wrapped as ReciprocalDoubleValuesSource(c, distance from the centre), which returns c / (d + c) (util/ReciprocalDoubleValuesSource.java:61-63).

Where it goes wrong

#
  • Plane (non-geo context): half the diagonal is the centre-to-corner distance, the farthest point of a box: the javadoc holds.
  • Geo box up to 180 degrees wide: the great circle from A to B stays within the box's longitudes; half of it is 0.45 to 1.00 of the distance to the farthest edge point: 1.00 near the equator, lower for high-latitude boxes, whose corner geodesic bulges towards the pole (10x10 at latitude 60: 0.983; 60x20 at latitude 60: 0.863; rect(-60, 60, 40, 80): 0.668; lowest 0.447 in a grid of 13,319 boxes 10 to 180 degrees wide). Every edge score in that grid stays inside the upstream tolerance (lowest 0.043).
  • Geo box wider than 180 degrees: the great circle from A to B takes the shorter way, through the longitudes outside the box: the 360 - W degree gap. For rect(-170, 170, -10, 10), A = (-170, -10) and B = (170, 10) are 28.2 degrees apart, so zScaling = 0.1 x 28.2 / 2 = 1.41: exactly the value of the 20-degree box rect(-10, 10, -10, 10). For a full-longitude band A and B are on the same meridian and only the height counts (10 degrees for 5 S to 5 N; about 7e-16 for the equator line rect(-180, 180, 0, 0)).

Exposure and likelihood272 words
  • Who. Opt-in spatial-extras scoring API; no other Lucene code calls it. Downstream, Solr's spatial field types expose it as score=recipDistance (read, not run).
  • What changes. Only the size of the recip values. The value stays monotone in distance, so the hit set and a sort by the value alone are unchanged. A combined score changes: with FunctionScoreQuery.boostByValue on the band, "far" (inner score 10, at 170 degrees) scores 0.0293 and "near" (inner score 1, at 10 degrees) 0.0476, so near ranks first; with the documented zScaling far ranks first (0.933 against 0.636).
  • How far off. Outside the upstream tolerance (0.01 to 0.19) for thin boxes from about 327 degrees wide up to 360: the first width out of it is 327 to 333 at the equator (heights 0 to 20), 333 to 341 at latitude 30 (heights 0 to 20) and 336 to 343 at latitude 60 (heights 0 to 10); boxes 40 degrees tall, or 20 at latitude 60, never left it in that sweep. Below those widths, low but inside it (200: 0.074; 270: 0.033); rect(-165, 165, -5, 5), 330 wide, already scores 0.0095. Circles whose bounding box spans all longitudes (they contain a pole) get 0.50 to 0.96 of the documented zScaling, inside the tolerance.
  • Not affected in practice. Non-geo contexts. Geo shapes whose bounding box is at most 180 degrees wide stay inside the tolerance, although high-latitude ones get as little as 0.45 of the documented zScaling (edge scores down to 0.043 in a 13,319-box grid); the fix does not change them.
  • Likelihood. Rare, since most geo queries are much narrower; when it happens it is silent.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The javadoc gives the formula and the ~0.1 edge score with no width limit; the 340-degree box's edges are 166-170 degrees away, yet zScaling is 1.41.
  • LUCENE-4550 fixed the identical computation in SpatialArgs.calcDistanceFromErrPct as "a bug nonetheless" (4.2.0).
  • Up to 180 degrees wide the code stays inside the upstream tolerance; past 180 it collapses (12 times too small at 340).
The full argument269 words
  • Explicit, unqualified contract. SpatialStrategy.java:126-132 states the formula, what zScaling is and the resulting ~0.1 edge score; it has no exception for geo contexts or wide shapes, and the method accepts any Shape.
  • Stated intent. LUCENE-4208 (#5280), which added the method: "'c' is roughly 1/10th of the distance to the query shape edge", and "how quickly the score curve slides ... doesn't vary based on the overall query shape; I think it should". The shape-relative scale is the point of the method.
  • The half-diagonal is a proxy. On a plane it is the centre-to-corner distance; for geo boxes up to 180 degrees wide it is 0.45-1.00 of the distance to the farthest edge, always inside the upstream tolerance in a 13,319-box grid. It collapses once the box is wider than 180 degrees, because the shorter corner geodesic then runs through the 360 - W degrees of longitude outside the box instead of through the box's own W.
  • Precedent. LUCENE-4550 (#5616, 6ba9d0b6418, 4.2.0, by the same author) replaced this exact computation in SpatialArgs.calcDistanceFromErrPct: "If the shape presented is extremely wide, > 180 degrees, then the internal calculations in SpatialArgs.calcDistanceFromErrPct(...) will wrongly measure the shape's size as having width < 180 degrees ... I am flagging this as "minor", but a bug nonetheless." Its issue and patch never mention this method.
  • Tests. TestSpatialArgs asserts that makeRectangle(-180, 180, 0, 0) is 180 degrees from its centre to its corner: a full-longitude rectangle is a normal shape for the module. The only test of this method, TestDistanceStrategy.testRecipScore, uses a circle of radius 4.27 degrees, too small to reach the problem.
The devil's-advocate check223 words

The strongest case for "intended": the API is @lucene.experimental, "~0.1" signals a heuristic and the upstream tolerance is wide; in LUCENE-4550 the same maintainer called shapes wider than 180 degrees "unrealistic shape sizes" and said the report came from "someone using lucene-spatial incorrectly"; and for the full-longitude band one can argue that its edges are the 5 S and 5 N lines, 5 degrees from the centre, which gives exactly the code's 0.5. And in LUCENE-4208 the author first proposed c as "1/10th the distance to the nearest query shape edge" (2012-09-12); under that reading the 340-degree box would get about 1.0, close to the code's 1.41.

Why it fails: the 340-degree box has real east and west edges 165.9 to 170 degrees from its centre, so the documented zScaling is 16.6 to 17.0 under any reading, against 1.41, a factor of 12. Even for the band the javadoc's second sentence fails: (180, 5) is on its north edge and scores 0.0028. Two days after the "nearest edge" comment the author proposed the "farthest edge" javadoc that was committed (2012-09-14), and under it both shapes score far below "~0.1 at its furthest edges". And the same maintainer called the identical computation "a bug nonetheless" and fixed it. A committer may still rate it minor, as LUCENE-4550 was, or prefer a javadoc qualification.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Measure geo shapes wider than 180 degrees from the centre; leave everything else alone

When the context is geo and the bounding box is wider than 180 degrees, take zScaling from the circle's radius, or else from the centre's distance to the farthest bounding-box corner; keep the old formula otherwise (+30/-8 in SpatialStrategy.java). The repro and all 121 spatial-extras tests pass; no probed shape up to 180 degrees wide changes.

Measure geo shapes wider than 180 degrees from the centre; leave everything else alone (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In makeRecipDistanceValueSource, when ctx.isGeo() and the bounding box is wider than 180 degrees, take distToEdge from the circle's radius, or else from the centre's distance to the farthest bounding-box corner; keep the half-diagonal otherwise. For a box wider than 180 degrees the corners lie on its edges, and a full-longitude box's corners lie on its two latitude lines, its only edges; a circle's farthest edge is its radius. The guard is exactly the condition under which the shorter corner geodesic runs through the 360 - W degrees of longitude outside the box instead of through the box's own W; on the 21 probe shapes the result matches the intended scale (0.976-1.000), which is not a proof for every shape.

Trade-offs

  • No change for non-geo contexts or for any shape whose bounding box is at most 180 degrees wide: identical zScaling for all 7 such probe shapes (small circles, high-latitude boxes) and identical output for all 13,319 boxes of a fact-check grid, including high-latitude boxes whose corner geodesic bulges out of the box.
  • Wide geo shapes change, by design: wide boxes go from 0 to 0.99 of the documented zScaling to 0.976-1.000; circles that contain a pole from 0.50-0.96 to 1.000.
  • A step at 180 degrees: the 180-degree rect(-90, 90, 80, 90) keeps zScaling 0.5 (about 0.45 of the documented value), while the 180.0001-degree rect(-90.00005, 90.00005, 80, 90) gets 1.117.
  • Full-longitude boxes are measured to their latitude edges, so a point beyond the farthest edge (for example (180, 0) inside rect(-180, 180, -85, 85), 180 degrees away while the edges are at most 95) scores below 0.1 (0.050), as "farthest edge" implies.
  • Other wide shapes (polygons, collections) are approximated by their bounding-box corners, which can lie far outside the shape; no polygon was tested, and neither was the Geo3d context. Cost: up to four distance computations once per query, only for affected non-circle shapes (none for circles); no API, index or format change.

The hunt's sketch: farthest bounding-box corner or edge midpoint, for every shape (not recommended)

#

zScaling = 0.1 x the largest distance from the centre to the bounding box's corners and edge midpoints, with no width guard.

Trade-offs

  • Fixes every box (the hunt's 1016 shapes: 0 out of tolerance, not independently re-run; its pole circles came close: r = 45 at (0, 60) scores 0.189 with it), but changes ordinary shapes too (10x10 at latitude 60: +1.75%; 60x20 at latitude 60: +16%); a downstream test pins recip-distance scores at 1e-3 (read, not run).
  • Over-corrects circles that contain a pole: their bounding box reaches round the far side of the pole, so the circle r = 6 at (0, 85) gets zScaling 1.6 instead of 0.6 and edge score 0.21, and r = 1.1 at (0, 89) 0.22, both above the tolerance; this doc's pole control fails with it (1 of 4).

Reuse LUCENE-4550's centre-to-corner rule for every shape (not run)

#

Consistent with SpatialArgs.calcDistanceFromErrPct.

Trade-offs

That rule deliberately takes the nearer corner (for precision), so it would still under-measure some wide shapes (by hand: the northern hemisphere rect(-180, 180, 0, 90) would stay at 4.5 instead of 13.5), and it shifts ordinary geo shapes slightly too.

Document the limitation

#

Say that zScaling is estimated from the bounding-box diagonal and is too small for geo shapes wider than 180 degrees.

Trade-offs

Cheapest and defensible for an experimental API, but the scores of those queries stay wrong when a small guarded fix exists; LUCENE-4550 chose the code fix for the same computation.

Downstream note

#

Solr's spatial field types expose this method as score=recipDistance, so their users get the same scores for very wide geo boxes (read, not run).

How it was found

#

Code reading while planning a bug hunt over spatial-extras' scoring code flagged the half corner-to-corner distance as wrong on a sphere past 180 degrees. A grid of 1016 generated query shapes on four strategies, checked against an independent great-circle reference, then found every out-of-tolerance case at its sampled widths 340-360 degrees.

The method in full160 words
  • Lead (code reading). The hunt's planning step read SpatialStrategy.java:127-144 and named the lead: zScaling from half the corner-to-corner distance, the short way round on a sphere, too small for boxes wider than 180 degrees. So it is not a blind discovery.
  • Quantified (translation validation on a bounded grid). The finder generated 1016 query shapes (rectangles with centres at latitudes 0, +-60, +-89 and longitudes 0, +-179.5, 180, widths 1, 10, 90, 179, 180, 181, 200, 270, 340, 359 and 360 including dateline-crossing ones, heights 0 to 180; circles with radii 0.5 to 179), indexed the centre and the farthest boundary point with PointVector, BBox, SerializedDV and RecursivePrefixTree strategies on the stock jar, and compared the scores against an independent great-circle reference (haversine and atan2 forms cross-checked). 124 generated shapes (91 distinct rectangles) were outside the upstream tolerance on every strategy, all at the sampled widths 340, 359 and 360.
  • Witnessed by a public-API JUnit, then reproduced independently (see the checks).

LFV-778, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
SpatialStrategy.makeRecipDistanceValueSource takes zScaling from half the corner-to-corner great-circle distance of the query's bounding box, which goes the short way round the globe once a geo box is wider than 180 degrees of longitude, so edge scores fall far below the documented ~0.1 (0.0082 for rect(-170,170,-10,10), 0.0028 for a 5S-5N band of all longitudes; released since 4.0.0)
Area
spatial-extras / SpatialStrategy recip-distance scoring (every strategy with a distance value source)
Priority
Low, rank 370: Only recip-distance scores change, and only for geo boxes wider than 180 degrees, which is the scoring-only profile of LFV-675 and LFV-369, and it affects every strategy, so it goes between them.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that score or boost by SpatialStrategy.makeRecipDistanceValueSource in a geo context with a query box wider than 180 degrees of longitude get edge scores below the documented ~0.1 with no error, on every strategy (PointVector, BBox, SerializedDV, RecursivePrefixTree); the scores leave the upstream test tolerance (0.10 +- 0.09) only for thin boxes about 340-360 degrees wide and are low but inside it from 181 to 339 degrees, the hit set never changes and sorting by the value alone keeps its order, so only rankings that combine it with another score (for example FunctionScoreQuery.boostByValue) change; geo boxes under 180 degrees and non-geo contexts keep the documented approximation, and circles stay inside the tolerance.
Summary
You score a geo spatial query with makeRecipDistanceValueSource, whose javadoc says zScaling is one tenth of the distance from the centre to the farthest edge, so scores run from 1 at the centre down to about 0.1 at the furthest edge. For a box wider than 180 degrees of longitude the method instead takes half the great-circle distance between the box's two opposite corners, and that distance goes the short way round the globe: for rect(-170,170,-10,10) zScaling is 1.41 instead of 17.0, and the edge point (170,0) scores 0.0082 instead of about 0.091 (for the band rect(-180,180,-5,5), 0.0028). Nothing fails; distance is over-weighted, so with FunctionScoreQuery.boostByValue a hit with inner score 10 at 170 degrees ranks below a hit with inner score 1 at 10 degrees on the band query, the reverse of the documented scaling.
