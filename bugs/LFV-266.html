SpanWithinQuery's Matches report the contained span's position but the enclosing span's offsets and terms, so the default UnifiedHighlighter marks the enclosing terms

For ac inside ab … bc, a SpanWithinQuery match says position 1 but offsets 0-8 and the terms ab, ac, bc, so the default UnifiedHighlighter returns <b>ab</b> <b>ac</b> <b>bc</b> instead of ab <b>ac</b> bc. The query's own hits and scores are right; every release since 7.5.0 has it.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

SpanWithinQuery(big, little) matches the little spans inside a big span. Weight#matches, which highlighters read, takes a match's positions from the little span but its offsets and term list from a collector that, for this query, also walks the big span.

  1. You index "ab ac bc" (ab at offsets 0-2, ac 3-5, bc 6-8) and search SpanWithinQuery(big = SpanNear([ab, bc], slop 1), little = ac): "ac inside ab … bc". The document is a hit, correctly.
  2. You ask Weight#matches where it matched, as highlighters do. You expect ac only: position 1, offsets 3-5, one term. The intervals equivalent, Intervals.containedBy(ac, ordered(ab, bc)), reports exactly that.
  3. Instead you get position 1 but offsets 0-8 and the terms ab, ac, bc. The position comes from the contained span; the offsets and terms come from a collector that, for this query, also walks the enclosing span.
  4. So the default UnifiedHighlighter returns <b>ab</b> <b>ac</b> <b>bc</b>, on a field with offsets and on a plain positions-only field alike, and MatchRegionRetriever returns 0-8 with offsets but 3-5 for the same text without them.
  5. Nested queries are hit too: SpanNear([that query, zz]) on "ab ac bc zz" reports offsets 0-11 instead of 3-11. Which documents match and the query's own scores never change.

Who is affected

#

Applications that highlight SpanWithinQuery hits, directly or nested in another span query, with the default UnifiedHighlighter, MatchHighlighter or MatchRegionRetriever, or that read its Matches. It happens on every such match, with default settings and at any index size. The query's own hits and scores are unaffected; the query is little used and the recorded searches found no report since 2018.

Mechanism, terms and numbers517 words

SpanWithinQuery(big, little) matches the little spans that lie inside a big span. For each match, SpanWeight#matches takes the positions from the spans, which for this query are the little spans, but the offsets and the sub-matches from Spans#collect, and ContainSpans#collect collects the big span's terms as well. So one MatchesIterator reports the contained term's position with the enclosing span's offsets and terms: for ac inside ab … bc, positions [1,1] with offsets [0,8) and the sub-matches ab@0, ac@1, bc@2. The default UnifiedHighlighter (weight matches on since 9.0.0) therefore marks the enclosing terms, on fields with and without offsets, and MatchRegionRetriever returns [0,8) on a field with offsets but [3,5) on the same text indexed with positions only. Nested span queries are affected the same way. Which documents the query matches and its own scores are unaffected. The interval counterpart had the same inconsistency and was fixed in 9.1.0 (LUCENE-10229); spans were never changed. Present since span queries got Matches in 7.5.0.

Terms

#
  • SpanWithinQuery(big, little): "Keep matches that are contained within another Spans"; it matches "spans from little that are inside of big" (SpanWithinQuery.java:28, :31-35). SpanContainingQuery(big, little) is the mirror image: it keeps the big spans that contain a little one.
  • Weight#matches(context, doc) returns Matches; getMatches(field) gives a MatchesIterator with, per match, its start and end position, its start and end offset, and getSubMatches(), "the positions and offsets of individual terms within the current match" (MatchesIterator.java:47-84). Highlighters use it: the UnifiedHighlighter by default since 9.0.0, MatchRegionRetriever and MatchHighlighter since 9.0.0.
  • Spans#collect(SpanCollector) visits the leaf terms (postings) of the current span; payload scoring and the UnifiedHighlighter's older phrase path use it too.

Mechanism (main a9779ef3c193)

#
  1. SpanWeight#matches (SpanWeight.java:209) returns spans.startPosition() and spans.endPosition() - 1 as the match positions (:252-260).
  2. Its startOffset() and endOffset() are the first and last entries of innerTerms (:262-276), and getSubMatches() iterates innerTerms (:278-323). collectInnerTerms() fills innerTerms with spans.collect(termCollector) and sorts them by position (:330-334).
  3. For SpanWithinQuery the spans are new ContainSpans(big, little, little) (SpanWithinQuery.java:90): positions come from the third argument, the little spans (ContainSpans.java:28-47).
  4. But ContainSpans#collect calls bigSpans.collect(collector); littleSpans.collect(collector); (ContainSpans.java:54-58). At a match the big spans sit on the enclosing span, so ab@0 and bc@2 join ac@1; after the sort the offsets run from ab's 0 to bc's 8.
  5. The UnifiedHighlighter marks every leaf sub-match (OffsetsEnum.java:171-240) and re-analyzes a positions-only field into a MemoryIndex that stores offsets (MemoryIndexOffsetStrategy.java:48), so it is wrong on both fields. MatchRegionRetriever reads the match offsets on a field with offsets (OffsetsFromMatchIterator.java:53-54) but maps the match positions to offsets on a field without them (OffsetsFromPositions), so the two fields disagree.

SpanContainingQuery uses new ContainSpans(big, little, big) (SpanContainingQuery.java:91): its positions are the big span's, so its offsets and terms agree with them. The interval counterpart was fixed by keeping only the small source: "the only sub source we care is the \"small\" source" (ContainedByIntervalsSource.java:69-75, #521).

Exposure and likelihood359 words
  • Highlighting. The UnifiedHighlighter uses Weight#matches by default since 9.0.0 (LUCENE-9431; opt-in from 7.5.0, LUCENE-8286): it marks <b>ab</b> <b>ac</b> <b>bc</b> on a field with offsets and on a plain positions-only TextField (executed). MatchRegionRetriever and MatchHighlighter (9.0.0, LUCENE-9463) return [0,8) with offsets and [3,5) without (executed). Custom code that reads MatchesIterator offsets or sub-matches gets the wide values (executed).
  • Nested queries. SpanNear([SpanWithin(...), zz]) reports offsets [0,11) instead of [3,11) and the terms ab, bc besides ac, zz (executed). SpanOrQuery, FilterSpans and the other composite spans forward collect the same way (read).
  • No special settings. Nothing beyond using SpanWithinQuery and highlighting: the mechanism depends only on the span tree and the indexed positions and offsets, not on codec or IndexWriterConfig settings (read). The JUnits ran under the test framework's randomized codec, IndexWriterConfig and similarity, and the highlighter with all its defaults (executed); no field with offsets is needed for the UnifiedHighlighter.
  • How often. On every SpanWithinQuery match whose enclosing span has leaves outside the contained one, those leaves become sub-matches, and with ordinary (non-stacked) tokens the match offsets widen to them; that is typical when the big clause is wider than the little one, though no usage data was gathered. Deterministic.
  • How visible. The extra marks are query terms, so they look plausible; SpanWithinQuery is a little-used legacy query (intervals are the modern replacement, and #16820 proposes deprecating spans). Registry likelihood: rare; priority: medium (both qualitative; maintainers could rate it lower).
  • Downstream. Elasticsearch exposes the query as span_within; a maintainer closed elastic/elasticsearch#42715 (2024) as fixed with output that highlights the enclosing terms, shown as evidence that highlighting works; the extra marks were not discussed (read, not executed).
  • What stays right. Which documents a SpanWithinQuery matches, counts and its own scores: span scoring never calls Spans#collect, whose only callers are Matches, the payload queries, the UnifiedHighlighter's PhraseHelper, the sandbox PayloadSpanUtil and the composite spans that forward it (read). Executed: identical hits and bit-identical scores for top-level SpanWithinQuerys with and without fix option B; fix option A changes payload-function scores over a SpanWithinQuery (see the fixes). The SpanContainingQuery and Intervals.containedBy controls on the same input pass (executed).

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • MatchesIterator: positions and offsets are "of the current match", sub-matches are "terms within the current match"; positions [1,1] with offsets [0,8) fit no reading.
  • Lucene fixed the same split for intervals in 9.1.0 (LUCENE-10229): containedBy reports only the contained term.
  • Lucene disagrees with itself: [0,8) with offsets, [3,5) without; the classic Highlighter marks only ac.
The full argument307 words
  • The contract. MatchesIterator.java:47-52 "The start position of the current match"; :61-66 "The starting offset of the current match"; :68-73 "The ending offset of the current match"; :75-84 getSubMatches "iterates over the positions and offsets of individual terms within the current match". If ab and bc were part of the match, its positions would be [0,2]; if they were not, its offsets would be [3,5) and they would not be sub-matches.
  • What the query says it matches. SpanWithinQuery.java:28 "Keep matches that are contained within another Spans"; :31-35 "matching spans from little that are inside of big"; :75-78 "Return spans from little ... The payload is from the spans of little".
  • Precedent. LUCENE-10229 (#11265, by @dweiss) "Match offsets should be consistent for fields with positions and fields with offsets" quotes @romseygeek, who led the Matches API work, from #11259: "It's because it returns the position of the inner match, but the offsets of the outer ... the current behaviour is kind of the worst of both worlds". #521 (@zhaih, 9.1.0, CHANGES.txt:4469-4470) made containedBy report only the small source; #803 (9.2.0) followed up. Spans were not mentioned or changed.
  • Design intent. LUCENE-8306 described getSubMatches() as "another MatchesIterator over the individual term positions within the current match"; LUCENE-8286's CHANGES entry says weight-matches highlighting "will highlight more accurately than any other highlighter" (CHANGES.txt:7161-7162).
  • Internal disagreement (executed): MatchRegionRetriever [0,8) with offsets vs [3,5) without; the classic Highlighter gives ab <B>ac</B> bc; Intervals.containedBy gives [3,5) and ac only.
  • No test or discussion endorses it. No upstream test checks the positions, offsets, sub-matches or highlights of SpanWithinQuery or SpanContainingQuery Matches: TestSpanWithinQuery and TestSpanContainQuery reach the test framework's CheckHits.checkMatches, which asserts only that Matches is non-null for every hit (CheckHits.java:353-359). The LUCENE-6083, -6706, -8306, -8404 and -10229 threads say nothing in favour of reporting the enclosing span.
The devil's-advocate check241 words

The strongest works-as-designed case (the intended-behaviour verifier and a reading-only review by a model from another family): a containment match depends on both clauses, and ContainSpans#collect has visited both on purpose since LUCENE-6706 (2015, payload scoring), so "every leaf that caused the match" is one consistent reading of the sub-matches, and the Matches class javadoc says it reports "all matching terms in a query" (Matches.java:24-25); Matches and MatchesIterator are @lucene.experimental and SpanWeight is "Expert-only"; the UnifiedHighlighter's older phrase path (PhraseHelper, its own Spans#collect call) has highlighted the enclosing terms for years; and an Elasticsearch maintainer closed #42715 as fixed with such output. The #11259 comment quoted above also says "It's not obvious to me what the correct response is here" and goes on: "perhaps we should change it so that you get offsets of the inner match as standard, and then the outer match is returned as part of the sub matches"; the expectation that the sub-matches are ac only follows what #521 implemented, not that comment.

Why it fails: it explains the collector, not one MatchesIterator whose positions and offsets name different regions (even the #11259 suggestion gives the match the inner offsets, [3,5) here); "experimental" lets the API change, not contradict its own javadoc; and Lucene already ruled on the identical inconsistency for intervals. The verifiers rejected it (moderate strength). For the issue: lead with the internal inconsistency, not with which words should light up.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A SpanWithinQuery match collects only the contained span's leaves

In SpanWithinQuery, let the match's collect visit only the contained span (+6 lines), so offsets and sub-matches describe ac alone, nested cases included. Both JUnits and the whole queries and highlighter suites pass. Trade-off: payload scoring and payload checks over a SpanWithinQuery then see only the contained span's payloads, as its javadoc already says.

        @Override
        public void collect(SpanCollector collector) throws IOException {
          // the match is the little span: collect its leaves only, not the big span's
          littleSpans.collect(collector);
        }

A SpanWithinQuery match collects only the contained span's leaves (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

Override collect in the anonymous ContainSpans that SpanWithinWeight#getSpans returns (+6 lines in SpanWithinQuery.java), so that Spans#collect visits only littleSpans. This fixes the top-level case, nested span queries (composite spans forward collect), big-span terms stacked on the little span's position, and also the UnifiedHighlighter's non-Matches phrase path (PhraseHelper calls Spans#collect itself). It gives the same small-source-only result as #521, which made containedBy report only the small source, but unlike #521 (an intervals Matches change only) it also changes the other Spans#collect consumers. SpanContainingQuery is unchanged.

        @Override
        public void collect(SpanCollector collector) throws IOException {
          // the match is the little span: collect its leaves only, not the big span's
          littleSpans.collect(collector);
        }

Trade-offs

  • Payloads change for SpanWithinQuery. Every Spans#collect consumer sees only the contained span: PayloadScoreQuery over a SpanWithinQuery scores only its payloads (probe, payloads ab=1, ac=2, bc=4: min/avg/max 1.0/2.33/4.0 before, 2.0/2.0/2.0 after), SpanPayloadCheckQuery checks only those, so it can accept or reject different spans and therefore match different documents (SpanPayloadCheckQuery.java:164-169), and the sandbox PayloadSpanUtil returns only those (read).
  • That matches the existing SpanWithinWeight#getSpans javadoc ("The payload is from the spans of little", SpanWithinQuery.java:76-77, LUCENE-6083) but reverses what LUCENE-6706 (5.3.0) made collect do; that change targeted payload scoring in general, its thread never discusses SpanWithinQuery, and no upstream test covers SpanWithinQuery payloads.
  • A direct SpanContainingQuery is unchanged, but a SpanContainingQuery or other composite with a SpanWithinQuery clause changes the same way.
  • It would need a CHANGES entry that names the payload change.
  • The adjudication and the registry notes describe the complete fix as a Matches-only leaf collection that keeps payload behaviour (option C). A is recommended here because it is the only option built and validated that fixes the nested and stacked cases and the PhraseHelper path, and because it matches the getSpans javadoc; if payload behaviour must not change, C is the choice, and maintainers may prefer it.

Filter the collected terms to the match positions (Matches path only) (validated by the checks)

#

In SpanWeight#matches collectInnerTerms(), keep only collected terms whose position lies in [startPosition, endPosition) (+14 lines; the finder's sketch). No change to payloads or scoring.

Trade-offs

  • Leaves nested queries wrong: big-span terms inside the outer match positions survive (bc@2 in SpanNear([SpanWithin, zz])), so the default UnifiedHighlighter still marks bc there: ab <b>ac</b> <b>bc</b> <b>zz</b> on "ab ac bc zz", where option A gives ab <b>ac</b> bc <b>zz</b> (executed by the fact check).
  • Leaves stacked tokens wrong: a big-span term at the little span's position survives (xy@1 in the probe).
  • The UnifiedHighlighter's non-Matches phrase path is unchanged.

What the checks validated

  • Same worktree and seed, assertions on: TestSpanWithinHighlighting passes 4 of 4, but TestSpanWithinMatches still fails 1 of 4: the nested test, "Unexpected term match: 2[6->8]" (bc@2).
  • Probe: the stacked case keeps the sub-match xy@1 [3,8) (offsets become [3,5)); the UnifiedHighlighter without weight matches still marks all three words; payload scores unchanged.
  • In the hunt, the same diff passed the finder's and the independent reproduction's tests and changed no other result in a run of 437 highlighter and span tests (435 upstream tests plus the finder's 2-test repro; only the repro changed).

A separate, Matches-only leaf collection (not built)

#

Give Spans a second collection path used only by SpanWeight#matches (for example a method that defaults to collect, overridden by the within-ContainSpans and forwarded by every composite Spans). It would fix nested and stacked cases without touching payloads.

Trade-offs

Touches every composite Spans class and adds API surface to a module that may be deprecated (#16820). Not built or run.

Downstream note

#

Downstream servers expose this query (Elasticsearch's span_within), so their highlights of it can mark the enclosing terms too; only highlight extents and Matches offsets change, never which documents the query matches, its scores or any filtering decision.

How it was found

#

Bounded-exhaustive translation validation of the highlighter offset sources on the real jars: every document of a small enumerated language and 121 queries. The check "same text, same highlight with and without offsets" failed on the multi-valued tiers only for this SpanWithinQuery; code reading found the cause.

The method in full155 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation on the stock jars (the hunt's semi-formal tier), hunt unit "highlighter offset sources".

  • The harness ran the real UnifiedHighlighter offset sources, MatchRegionRetriever and the Matches API of the stock main jars over every document of a small enumerated document language (holes, stacked tokens, multi-valued fields with position gaps 0 and 1) and a 121-query catalogue, and compared them with an exact reference semantics and with each other.
  • Relation R7b (MatchRegionRetriever on a field with offsets equals the same on a positions-only field) failed on the multi-valued tiers, which have no stacked tokens with distinct offsets, only for SpanWithinQuery(SpanNear([ab, bc], 1, ordered), ac): 44 of 1,815 span checks at gap 1 and 56 at gap 0 locally, 3,280 in the larger gap-0 batch job.
  • The root cause was then found by reading SpanWeight#matches and ContainSpans#collect, and reduced to a public-API JUnit.
  • No model checker, SMT solver or prover was involved.

History

#

Introduced in 7.5.0 by 028c86b1fa7 (LUCENE-8306 / LUCENE-8404, 2018), which gave span queries Matches with offsets and terms from Spans#collect; it relies on a 2015 change that made the within-collector visit both spans. Executed: fails on nine releases from 7.5.0 to 10.5.2 and on main; not fixed upstream.

Versions tested8 rows

Introduced by 028c86b1fa7 (master) and its branch_7x copy 2376d7dded3, 2018-07-22, "LUCENE-8306: Allow iteration over submatches. Also includes LUCENE-8404, adding match iteration to SpanQuery" (@romseygeek; JIRA patch workflow, issues #9353 and #9451, no CHANGES entry). It added SpanWeight#matches with offsets and sub-matches from Spans#collect. First release 7.5.0 (git tag --contains); first main-line release 8.0.0.

Precondition, not the cause: 8914ec5f750 (trunk, first release tag 6.0.0) and its branch_5x copy eeae87518e3 (first release tag 5.3.0), LUCENE-6706, #7764, 2015-08-03, changed ContainSpans#collect from sourceSpans.collect to big and little, for payload scoring. Reverting only that method body makes the repro pass on 7.5.0 and on main (a causal experiment, not a fix).

Exposure grew: UnifiedHighlighter weight matches opt-in from 7.5.0 (LUCENE-8286) and default from 9.0.0 (LUCENE-9431); MatchRegionRetriever from 9.0.0 (LUCENE-9463). Every release tag from 7.5.0 to 10.5.2 in the history clone has the same three ingredients (tag scan; 8.8.2 and 8.9.0 to 8.11.4 are not tagged there, 8.11.4 was executed); branch_10x and branch_10_6 have the same relevant code (their SpanWeight.java and highlighter files differ only outside the Matches path).

Bug record

LFV-266, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
SpanWithinQuery's Weight#matches reports the contained (little) span's positions but the enclosing (big) span's offsets and sub-matches, so Matches-based highlighting (the default UnifiedHighlighter, MatchRegionRetriever) marks the enclosing terms and a field with offsets highlights differently from the same text indexed with positions only
Area
queries / span queries Matches API (SpanWeight#matches takes offsets and sub-matches from ContainSpans#collect for SpanWithinQuery; seen through the UnifiedHighlighter and MatchRegionRetriever)
Priority
Low, rank 305: Highlighting-only (hits and scores are correct) in the little-used legacy SpanWithinQuery.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that read Weight#matches of a SpanWithinQuery, alone or nested inside another span query, get the little span's positions together with the enclosing big span's offsets and leaf terms on every such match, with the default codec, IndexWriterConfig and searcher, -ea and -da, at any index size, in every release from 7.5.0 through 10.5.2 and on main: the UnifiedHighlighter with weight matches (on by default since 9.0.0) marks the enclosing span's terms, also on a default positions-only field through its re-analysis offset source, and MatchRegionRetriever returns the whole big span on a field with offsets but only the contained match on a positions-only field; which documents match and their scores are unaffected (executed: identical hits and bit-identical scores with and without the fix), and SpanContainingQuery and Intervals.containedBy report consistent matches.
Summary
You index 'ab ac bc' in a field with offsets and ask Weight#matches for SpanWithinQuery(big = SpanNear([ab, bc], slop 1, in order), little = ac); you expect one match, 'ac' at position 1 with offsets [3,5) and sub-matches only inside it, which is what the equivalent Intervals.containedBy(ac, ordered(ab, bc)) reports. Instead the match says positions [1,1] but offsets [0,8) and lists ab@0 and bc@2 as sub-matches, because the offsets and sub-matches come from Spans#collect, which for SpanWithinQuery also collects the enclosing span. So the default UnifiedHighlighter marks '<b>ab</b> <b>ac</b> <b>bc</b>' instead of 'ab <b>ac</b> bc', and MatchRegionRetriever returns [0,8) on a field with offsets but [3,5) on the same text indexed with positions only; hits and scores are unaffected.
