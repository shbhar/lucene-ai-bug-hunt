DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS: the field length counts terms, but sumTotalTermFreq sums the custom frequencies

On a field indexed with IndexOptions.DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (10.5.0 and later), Lucene stores each document's length as a term count but sums the custom frequencies into sumTotalTermFreq, contrary to the FieldInfo#isTermDocField and FieldStats javadocs. BM25 then compares dl = 2 with avgdl = 15, not 3, so length normalisation is much weaker.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

Lucene keeps two numbers that BM25 divides: each document's field length dl, stored in the norm, and the field's sumTotalTermFreq, from which it computes the average length avgdl. On a DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS field the first counts terms and the second adds up the custom values.

Background

#
  1. Custom term frequencies. An analysis chain can attach an integer to each term: DelimitedTermFrequencyTokenFilter turns a|5 into the term a with frequency 5. Lucene stores that number in the postings, and scorers read it as the term's frequency in the document.
  2. DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (new in 10.5.0) marks a field whose custom values are opaque scores, not counts: a term may occur only once per document, and the field length counts 1 per term, so that large values cannot overflow it.
  3. BM25 length normalisation favours shorter documents: it compares each document's length dl (from the norm) with the field's average length avgdl = sumTotalTermFreq / number of documents with the field.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. You index "a|5 b|5" and "a|5 b|5 c|5 d|5" into a TextField-type field (norms on) set to DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS, so every term carries the custom frequency 5. Lucene records the field lengths as 2 and 4: one per term.
  2. The javadocs say such frequencies count as 1 "for statistical and scoring purposes" (FieldInfo#isTermDocField) and that sumTotalTermFreq "is also the sum of each document's field length" (FieldStats). So you expect sumTotalTermFreq = 2 + 4 = 6, and BM25's average length avgdl = 6 / 2 = 3.
  3. Instead sumTotalTermFreq is 30, the sum of the 5s, and BM25 explains dl = 2.0 and 4.0 against avgdl = 15.0: the lengths are in terms, the average is in custom-value units.
  4. Length normalisation is much weaker: query a scores the short document 0.1682 and the long one 0.1646, 2% apart. The same data on a legacy DOCS_AND_FREQS field, where both sides sum the custom values, scores 0.1545 and 0.1402, 10% apart. No error, and the same documents match.

Who is affected

#

Only fields indexed with the opt-in DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (10.5.0 and later). Anyone relying on the FieldInfo or FieldStats javadocs gets a wrong contract; scores change only with norms on and a length-normalising similarity such as the default BM25. In the recorded probes, matches, deletions and CheckIndex are unaffected, and nothing throws at normal scale.

Mechanism, terms and numbers575 words

Since 10.5.0 a field can be indexed with IndexOptions.DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS: each term occurs at most once per document and carries an opaque custom value. For such fields IndexingChain adds 1 per term to the field length, so the norm stores a term count (minus overlapping tokens, by default), while the postings keep the custom value, so totalTermFreq and sumTotalTermFreq sum the custom values. FieldInfo#isTermDocField says the frequency "for statistical and scoring purposes is treated as 1" and FieldStats says sumTotalTermFreq "is also the sum of each document's field length"; neither holds on this option, and nothing documents the exception.

The measured consequence: BM25 and every SimilarityBase model take avgdl from sumTotalTermFreq / docCount and compare it with dl from the norm, so on such fields dl counts terms while avgdl is inflated by the mean custom value per posting (without overlapping tokens). For two documents with custom frequency 5 everywhere, dl = 2 and 4 meet avgdl = 15 instead of 3, and the short document's lead for query a drops from 10.2% to 2.2%. In the recorded default-BM25 scope probes, hit sets, deletions, CheckIndex and pruned top-k matched the controls, and nothing throws at normal scale. Both halves are deliberate and tested upstream, so this is a documentation and API-contract defect, with the BM25 effect as an open question: the #15584 author asked about BM25 on such fields, and nobody discussed dl against avgdl.

  1. Length. On DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS the length grows by 1 per term; on every other option by the custom value. Similarity#computeNorm encodes it (minus overlaps by default) into the norm (Similarity.java:151-161). This is the #15584 change, made so that large custom values no longer overflow the per-document length ("too many tokens", the #11086 / LUCENE-10048 use case).
  2. Postings and term statistics. The posting freq is still the custom value (FreqProxTermsWriterPerField.java:125, :177, via getTermFreq() at :208-209); a second occurrence of a term in one document is rejected (:193-194). The postings writer sums freq() into totalTermFreq (PushPostingsWriterBase.java:139-140), as the TermsEnum and Terms javadocs define it and CheckIndex enforces.
  3. Search-time statistics. IndexSearcher#fieldStats sums Terms#getSumTotalTermFreq over the leaves (IndexSearcher.java:1144-1159); termStats passes totalTermFreq through (:1130-1133).
  4. Consumers. BM25Similarity#avgFieldLength is sumTotalTermFreq / docCount (BM25Similarity.java:143-145); SimilarityBase sets the field's token count and average length from sumTotalTermFreq (SimilarityBase.java:85-86). The Similarity class javadoc recommends exactly that (Similarity.java:54-55).

So only on this option is dl in terms while avgdl is in custom-value units: without overlapping tokens, avgdl is multiplied by sumTotalTermFreq / sumDocFreq, the mean custom value per posting (30 / 6 = 5 in the example). On legacy DOCS_AND_FREQS custom frequencies both sides sum the custom values; on DOCS fields both count unique terms.

Exposure and likelihood334 words
  • Who. Applications that index a field with IndexOptions.DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (released in 10.5.0; the IndexOptions enum is @lucene.experimental).
    • Code that reads FieldInfo#isTermDocField, FieldStats or the Similarity javadoc to decide what the statistics mean, for example a custom similarity or a distributed-statistics layer, gets a wrong contract.
    • Scores change with norms on (a TextField-derived type keeps them) and a length-normalising similarity: the default BM25Similarity or any SimilarityBase model (DFR, IB, LM). Without overlapping tokens, dl / avgdl shrinks by the mean custom value per posting: 5 in the example, where the short document's lead drops from 10.2% to 2.2%. Custom values may go up to Integer.MAX_VALUE (upstream's testNoOverflowInt), and larger values can weaken length normalisation much further; how much depends on b, k1 and how the values are spread. One document with large values raises avgdl for the whole field.
  • Not affected. Fields without norms (every document then has dl = 1), BM25 with b = 0, similarities that ignore length (upstream's tests score this option with pass-through similarities, and BaseSimilarityTestCase runs it with norms omitted), legacy DOCS_AND_FREQS custom frequencies, and, in the recorded default-BM25 scope probes, everything except scoring: on an index with deletions, 120 queries gave the same hit sets as a legacy custom-frequency index and CheckIndex was clean on both; on 150 queries pruned top-1/top-10 equalled exhaustive scoring; scores stayed finite. The probes cover the tested cases, not every query, codec or similarity.
  • Silent. At normal scale, no exception and no log; only explain() shows the mismatch between dl and avgdl.
  • No scale needed. One document shows it. Nothing wrong is written to the index: IndexSearcher combines the statistics at search time, so a search-time fix (option B) changes the statistics it gives the scorers without a reindex; the raw postings statistics (TermsEnum, Terms) stay in custom-value units.
  • How likely: rare. The option is new (April 2026) and meant for opaque per-term scores with custom scoring; code search found no downstream user beyond an Elasticsearch back-compat test.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • FieldInfo#isTermDocField says the frequency "for statistical and scoring purposes is treated as 1"; upstream's own tests expect totalTermFreq 92 = 42 + 50 and a score of 17.
  • FieldStats: sumTotalTermFreq "is also the sum of each document's field length", true for custom frequencies until 10.5.0.
  • Nothing documents the split, and nobody on #15584 or #11086 discussed dl against avgdl.
The full argument392 words
  • The javadoc says the opposite of what the index does. FieldInfo.java:717-720: "Returns true if the field has custom term frequencies indexed that are to be treated as opaque values, while the term frequency for statistical and scoring purposes is treated as 1." Upstream's tests contradict both halves: TestCustomTermFreq#testTotalTermFreqTermDoc (:314-344) expects totalTermFreq 92 = 42 + 50 and 188 = 128 + 60, and TestSimilarity#testTermDocSimilarity (:207-248) expects a TermQuery score of 17, the custom value, under a similarity whose tf(freq) is freq (:48).
  • FieldStats documents an identity that held until 10.5.0. FieldStats.java:55-58: sumTotalTermFreq "is the sum of TermStats#totalTermFreq() across all terms. It is also the sum of each document's field length across all documents." The sentence dates from LUCENE-8007 (2017); the same repro on DOCS_AND_FREQS passes on 9.12.3, 10.4.0, 10.5.0, 10.5.1, 10.5.2, 10.6.0 and main. Similarity.java:54-55 tells similarity authors to compute the average length from it.
  • Nothing documents the exception. No javadoc, MIGRATE.md or CHANGES text mentions termdoc statistics; the CHANGES entry covers the length half and ends mid-sentence: "IndexWriter counts their terms rather than summing their freqs. Use" (lucene/CHANGES.txt:919-920). The TermFrequencyAttribute and DelimitedTermFrequencyTokenFilter javadocs still say the field "must be indexed with IndexOptions#DOCS_AND_FREQS" (2017), although upstream's tests feed custom frequencies into DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS fields.
  • The question was asked; the units were never discussed. On #15584 (2026-01-17) msokolov wrote "I guess someone might try using BM25 simlarity with this field, or some scoring that assumes that totalTermFrequency > termFrequency(doc)?", and rmuir answered "Searching and pruning would be my concerns. Look at BaseSimilarityTestCase for the former." The PR then ran BaseSimilarityTestCase on the option with norms omitted and synthetic statistics, and added a pass-through-similarity score test, so nothing exercises dl against avgdl. On #11086 (2026-01-16) msokolov asked what would happen "if someone were to, say, compute the default Similarity over such a field (we don't plan to do that, but someone could, and it shouldn't break in a horrible way)"; nobody replied. No comment on either thread mentions the length / sumTotalTermFreq split.
  • Precedent for one unit. LUCENE-8025 (#9073) and LUCENE-8031 (#9079), the same kind of mismatch on DOCS fields, were fixed as bugs in 2017 and 2018; on one test collection rmuir measured BM25 MAP 0.0531 before and 0.1390 after. LUCENE-7854 (#8905) asked for custom frequencies to give lengths "same as if i stacked".
The devil's-advocate check290 words

The strongest case for "intended" (the intended-behaviour check, the study's cross-model lenses and the adjudication rated it moderate; the 2026-10-31 refute re-run and the cross-model fact check rated it strong for the code behaviour, the fact check "weak for the documentation as written"): each half is deliberate and tested. testFieldInvertState pins the length at 2 for the freqs {42, 128} (TestCustomTermFreq.java:612-631) and testTotalTermFreqTermDoc pins totalTermFreq at 92. Terms and TermsEnum define the totals as sums of freq(), and CheckIndex enforces it. The custom values are "scores", not counts; the author called BM25 on such fields a non-goal that "shouldn't break in a horrible way"; on the first, attribute-based draft of #15584 rmuir wrote that it "won't fix the issue of all kinds of crazy/undefined behavior at search-time. It is just allowing it to happen at index-time."; and rmuir called avgdl "just a pivot" on LUCENE-8000 (#9048, open). BM25Similarity#avgFieldLength is protected and documented as sumTotalTermFreq / docCount, so an application can override it. And the FieldStats sentence is already loose: on a DOCS field "a a" the length is 2 while sumTotalTermFreq is 1, and with a stacked synonym BM25 sees dl 1.0 against avgdl 2.0.

Why it fails: it defeats a claim that the statistics code is wrong, which this report does not make. It does not defeat the documentation defect: the isTermDocField javadoc is false for statistics and for scoring whichever side upstream keeps, and nothing documents the split. On DOCS fields BM25 still compares dl and avgdl in one unit, and synonyms follow the documented discountOverlaps rule; only the termdoc option fails both readings (length vs sumTotalTermFreq, dl vs avgdl). "Not horrible" is why the impact is minor, not why it is correct.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Correct the javadocs (the minimum)

At minimum, correct the javadocs: FieldInfo#isTermDocField, IndexOptions, FieldStats and Similarity should say that the field length counts terms while statistics and scorers see the custom values, and the feeder javadocs should allow this option. To keep the old promise instead, count each term once in IndexSearcher's statistics: the repro and 2,355 tests pass.

Count each term once in IndexSearcher's statistics (validated by the checks)

#

+22/-1 lines in IndexSearcher.java: for a field whose FieldInfo#isTermDocField() is true, termStats reports totalTermFreq = docFreq and fieldStats reports sumTotalTermFreq = sumDocFreq, the term count that the field length uses. The raw index statistics are unchanged.

public TermStats termStats(Term term, int docFreq, long totalTermFreq) throws IOException {
  if (isTermDocField(term.field())) {
    // custom freqs are opaque values, not counts: each term counts once per document
    totalTermFreq = docFreq;
  }
  // This constructor will throw an exception if docFreq <= 0.
  return new TermStats(term.bytes(), docFreq, totalTermFreq);
}
// fieldStats(): sumTotalTermFreq += termDoc ? terms.getSumDocFreq() : terms.getSumTotalTermFreq();
// isTermDocField(field): the FieldInfo of the first leaf that has the field

Trade-offs

  • Makes the TermStats and FieldStats that IndexSearcher supplies count-based for termdoc fields, so "for statistical purposes treated as 1" and both FieldStats identities hold for those statistics, and puts dl and avgdl in one unit: the example then scores exactly like the legacy DOCS_AND_FREQS field (0.1545 and 0.1402). The public TermsEnum#totalTermFreq() and Terms#getSumTotalTermFreq() stay in custom-value units; CheckIndex and testTotalTermFreqTermDoc are untouched; no reindex.
  • Behaviour change: BM25 and SimilarityBase scores on existing termdoc fields with norms change (LM, DFR and IB models also see count-based collection statistics), so it may suit main better than a 10.x bugfix release.
  • Both methods must change together: changing only fieldStats would break the documented totalTermFreq <= sumTotalTermFreq and trip the assertion in SimilarityBase.java:80.
  • Subclasses that override termStats/fieldStats (distributed statistics) keep the raw values, and the "scoring purposes" half of the javadoc still needs option A's wording.
  • isTermDocField(field) takes the FieldInfo of the first leaf that has the field. Within one index a field's index options cannot change (FieldInfo#verifySameIndexOptions), but an IndexSearcher over a MultiReader that combines a termdoc leaf and a legacy leaf of the same field name would apply the first leaf's rule to every leaf; a real patch should decide per leaf or reject the mix.
  • One FieldInfos lookup over the leaves per call (it stops at the first leaf with the field); not benchmarked.

How it was found

#

A metamorphic test indexed the same weighted terms three ways (repeated terms, legacy custom frequencies, DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS) and expected equal scores. The termdoc field with norms needed its own score class; checking why against the FieldStats javadoc exposed the split, which was then minimised by hand into a public-API JUnit.

The method in full123 words

Method: non-formal (a randomized differential and metamorphic harness, the hunt's tier for executed testing without a model checker or prover).

  • A hunt unit tests index-options invariance: family W indexes the same weighted terms as natural repetitions, as legacy custom frequencies on DOCS_AND_FREQS, and as DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS, and relation R2 expects bit-identical scores within a score class.
  • R2 had to put DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS with norms in a class of its own, because its field length counts terms; with norms off R2 held bit-exactly over 440 seeds. Checking that class split against the FieldStats javadoc exposed the inconsistency.
  • The finder minimised it into a 4-test public-API JUnit on the stock jars of main a9779ef3c193; the registry later reduced it to the one-test repro above.

LFV-608, snapshot of 2026-11-06

Registry title
DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS fields (10.5.0+): the norm length counts terms but sumTotalTermFreq and totalTermFreq sum the custom frequencies, contradicting the FieldInfo#isTermDocField, IndexOptions and FieldStats javadocs and putting BM25's dl and avgdl in different units
Area
core index / DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (termdoc) field length vs term statistics; search similarities (BM25, SimilarityBase)
Priority
Low, rank 728: Experimental DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS fields (10.5) put BM25's dl and avgdl in different units, which contradicts the javadocs but only weakens length normalization for a niche option.
Impact class
minor
Impact note
This hits applications that index a field with the opt-in, experimental IndexOptions.DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (released in 10.5.0). It matters if they rely on the FieldInfo#isTermDocField, IndexOptions or FieldStats javadocs, which promise count-based statistics that the index does not provide. It also matters if they score the field with norms on (TextField keeps them) using the default BM25Similarity or a SimilarityBase model. Length normalisation is then weakened by a factor equal to the mean custom value (5 in the repro). Matches are never wrong, nothing throws, nothing is corrupted, and no scale is needed. Fields without norms, frequency-only similarities and BM25 with b=0 are unaffected.
Summary
You index two documents, 'a|5 b|5' and 'a|5 b|5 c|5 d|5', into a field with IndexOptions.DOCS_AND_CUSTOM_FREQS (DelimitedTermFrequencyTokenFilter, a TextField type with norms). Lucene stores their lengths as term counts, 2 and 4. FieldInfo#isTermDocField says such frequencies count as 1 'for statistical and scoring purposes', and FieldStats says sumTotalTermFreq 'is also the sum of each document's field length'. So you expect sumTotalTermFreq to be 6 and BM25's average length to be 3. Instead, sumTotalTermFreq and totalTermFreq sum the custom values (30), and BM25 explains dl=2.0 against avgdl=15.0. Each document's length is compared with an average inflated by the mean custom value, which quietly weakens length normalisation.
