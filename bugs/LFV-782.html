Geo3D outside distance to a lat/lon box that reaches a pole ignores the pole, so newOutsideBoxSort sorts documents beyond the pole after nearer ones

Sorting with Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort by a box that reaches a pole, say latitude 60..90 x longitude -10..10, gives a document across the pole at (80, 180) the distance to a far corner, 39.88 degrees, instead of 10, so it sorts after a document 25 degrees away. In every release since 5.3.0 (the sort since 6.1.0).

The bug in 30 seconds

#

Geo3D sorts documents by their distance from a lat/lon box with newOutsideBoxSort. For a box that touches a pole and spans under 180 degrees of longitude, Lucene builds a GeoNorthRectangle (or GeoSouthRectangle): a wedge whose two side edges meet at the pole. Its distance code checks the bottom edge, the sides and the bottom corners, but not the pole.

  1. You index documents with Geo3DDocValuesField (WGS84) at (80, 180), just across the North Pole, and at (35, 0), and sort them with newOutsideBoxSort("loc", 60, 90, -10, 10, WGS84): the box latitude 60..90 x longitude -10..10, which contains the pole.
  2. You expect (80, 180) first: the pole is inside the box and only 10 degrees (about 1,112 km) away, while (35, 0) is 25 degrees (about 2,780 km) south of the box.
  3. You get (35, 0) first: (80, 180) gets 4,434,716 m, 39.88 degrees, the distance to a bottom corner of the box, (60, 10) or (60, -10). No error; the hits are the same, only the sort values and the order are wrong.
  4. Why: for a box that reaches one pole and spans under 180 degrees of longitude (and is not a line or a point), Lucene builds a GeoNorthRectangle, whose outsideDistance takes the minimum over the bottom edge, the two side edges and the two bottom corners, never the pole where both side edges end. From (80, 180) neither side edge offers a point on the box, so a corner wins.
  5. Move the top to 89.9999 and the same point gets 10.0001 degrees (a plain rectangle, correct); at 89.999999 it is 39.88 again. Boxes that reach the South Pole (GeoSouthRectangle) have the mirror bug; boxes 180 degrees wide or more are fine.

Who is affected

#

Applications that sort with newOutsideBoxSort (or call computeOutsideDistance on a GeoBBoxFactory box) where the box reaches latitude 90 or -90 and spans under 180 degrees of longitude. Documents beyond the pole (26-37% of outside grid points in the boxes measured) get values too large, mostly by tens of degrees, and sort late or leave the top N.

Mechanism, terms and numbers929 words

Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort orders documents "by ascending outside distance from the box". For a box whose top latitude is the North Pole (or whose bottom latitude is the South Pole) and whose longitude extent is under 180 degrees, GeoBBoxFactory builds a GeoNorthRectangle (GeoSouthRectangle): a wedge whose two side edges meet at the pole, which is inside the box. Its outsideDistance takes the minimum over the bottom parallel, the two side meridians and the two bottom corners, never the pole. For a point on the far side of the pole neither side meridian offers a point on the box, so the result is the distance to a corner: for the box latitude 60..90 x longitude -10..10 and the point (80, 180), 39.88 degrees instead of 10, and in the WGS84 sort 4,434,716 m, so the document sorts after one at (35, 0), 25 degrees south of the box (sort value 2,779,879 m). In the three boxes measured, 26% to 37% of the outside grid points get values that are too large, most by tens of degrees; the hit set never changes. The same box with a top latitude of 89.9999 is a plain rectangle and gives 10.0001 degrees. The bug dates from the first outside-distance code (LUCENE-6578, 5.3.0, 2015) and is in every release since (the sort since 6.1.0); adding the pole distance to the minimum, as GeoLongitudeSlice already does, fixes it, with all of lucene/spatial3d passing.

Terms

#
  • Outside distance: the arc distance from a point to the nearest point of a shape, 0 inside it. Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort sorts by it and reports it in meters (the arc distance times the planet's mean radius).
  • Polar rectangle: GeoBBoxFactory.makeGeoBBox builds a GeoNorthRectangle when the top latitude is the pole, the bottom latitude is not the other pole, the box has nonzero width and height, and its longitude extent is under 180 degrees (strictly, under pi minus pi x 1e-12 rad), a GeoSouthRectangle for the South Pole (GeoBBoxFactory.java:58-131). Its boundary is the bottom (top) parallel between two corners and two meridian segments from the corners to the pole.
  • Pole tolerance: the factory treats a latitude as the pole when it is within pi x 1e-12 rad of it or its sine is within 1e-12 of 1 (GeoBBoxFactory.java:137-150). The sine test reaches about 0.00008 degrees (9 m) from the pole, so 89.999999 and 89.99992 build a GeoNorthRectangle and 89.9999 a plain GeoRectangle. The sine tolerance dates from LUCENE-10673 (first released in 9.4.0); in older releases only latitudes essentially at the pole count.

The bug (main a9779ef3c193)

#
  1. newOutsideBoxSort (Geo3DDocValuesField.java:281-291) calls Geo3DUtil.fromBox (Geo3DUtil.java:173-189), which checks the latitudes against +/-90 and calls GeoBBoxFactory.makeGeoBBox; the factory returns GeoNorthRectangle / GeoSouthRectangle for a pole-reaching box narrower than GeoWideRectangle.MIN_WIDE_EXTENT (GeoBBoxFactory.java:101, :128-131; GeoWideRectangle.java:31).
  2. The sort's comparator calls computeOutsideDistance(DistanceStyle.ARC, x, y, z) on each decoded doc value (Geo3DPointOutsideDistanceComparator.java:101, :143), which returns 0.0 inside the box and outsideDistance otherwise (GeoBaseMembershipShape.java:47-53).
  3. GeoNorthRectangle.outsideDistance (GeoNorthRectangle.java:283-298) returns min(bottom, left, right, LRHC, LLHC); GeoSouthRectangle.outsideDistance (GeoSouthRectangle.java:254-269) is the mirror. The pole is never a candidate.
  4. Each side term measures only to the perpendicular foot on the meridian plane and its antipode and drops those outside the box (Plane.java:600-636). For (80, 180) and the side at longitude -10 the foot is (80.1489, 170), on the far half of the meridian, and the antipode (-80.1489, -10), below the box: the term is +infinity, and the same for the other side.
  5. What is left: the bottom parallel's point (60, 0) at 40 degrees and the corners (60, +-10) at 39.882280 degrees. The pole, 10 degrees away, is never asked.
  6. The class itself lists the pole as the end point of both side edges (leftPlanePoints, rightPlanePoints, edgePoints, GeoNorthRectangle.java:156-159) and adds it to its bounds (:279). GeoLongitudeSlice.outsideDistance adds both poles (GeoLongitudeSlice.java:196-207); the wide polar rectangles search the whole side meridians, so the pole lies on their arc (GeoWideNorthRectangle.java:258-274).

Exposure and likelihood464 words
  • Who. Users of the optional lucene/spatial3d module who sort with Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort, or call computeOutsideDistance on a GeoBBoxFactory box (alone or inside a composite shape), where the factory builds a GeoNorthRectangle or GeoSouthRectangle: a box of nonzero size that reaches latitude 90 or -90 (or lies within the factory's pole tolerance, such as 89.999999), not both, and spans under 180 degrees of longitude, for example a polar sector such as lat 60..90 x lon -10..10, and the index holds documents on the far side of the pole.
  • Effect. Those documents get sort values that are too large (meters in the FieldDoc), mostly by tens of degrees, so they sort after farther documents or fall out of the top N. Every returned hit still matches the query. No exception, with assertions on or off.
  • Default vs opt-in. An optional module and an apparently niche sort: no in-tree caller or test uses it and no matching public report was found (there is no usage data). The caller must pass a PlanetModel; the repro passes WGS84, with new IndexWriterConfig() and the codec the test framework picks (Asserting(Lucene104) in the logged run). The bug is in the shape geometry and does not depend on the codec or the writer settings.
  • Scale. None: one document triggers it, deterministically, at any index size. The share depends on the box: in the independent reproducer's grid sweeps 28% (north box) and 26% (south box) of the outside points were wrong, by up to about 44 and 49 degrees; for the headline box lat 60..90 x lon -10..10 a 1-degree grid in the fact check found 37% wrong, by up to 30 degrees. On the fact check's grid, 81-87% of the wrong values in these three boxes were off by 10 degrees or more.
  • Not affected. Boxes whose top (bottom) latitude is outside the factory's pole tolerance, about 0.00008 degrees from the pole (89.9999 is already correct; 89.99992 and 89.999999 are not), boxes spanning 180 degrees or more, boxes away from the poles, and the hit set of any query.
  • Workaround. Until a fix ships, a top latitude just outside the tolerance, such as 89.9999, gives correct values; it leaves out a cap of about 11 m around the pole, so points in that cap get a small positive distance instead of 0.
  • How likely. Rare: it needs both a pole-reaching narrow box and documents beyond the pole. No public report was found in the 11 years since 5.3.0.
  • Related findings (separate reports, other fix sites): LFV-737 (GeoExactCircle outside distance skips the slice end points) is the same missing-end-point pattern; LFV-735 (GeoStandardPath measures its end caps to the end point instead of the cap edge) is a related outside-distance error at the ends of a shape.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • newOutsideBoxSort promises "ascending outside distance from the box" for any latitude within +/-90; the pole is inside the box.
  • The class lists the pole as an end point of both side edges and measures the other end points, the corners; GeoLongitudeSlice, from the same commit, includes the poles.
  • Not an approximation: top 89.9999 gives 10.0001 degrees, 90 gives 39.88.
The full argument253 words
  • The contract. newOutsideBoxSort: "This sort orders documents by ascending outside distance from the box. Points within the box have distance 0.0." Latitude bounds "must be within standard +/-90 coordinate bounds" (Geo3DDocValuesField.java:260-280). GeoOutsideDistance: shapes "that can compute the distance from a point to the closest outside edge" (GeoOutsideDistance.java:19-21).
  • The stated intent. LUCENE-6578 (now #7636), which introduced outside distances, asked for the arc distance "from a GeoPoint to the nearest edge/point of a GeoShape".
  • The class's own geometry. The pole is inside the box (the repro asserts isWithin(NORTH_POLE)) and is the end point of both side edges (GeoNorthRectangle.java:156-159); the method already measures the other end points, the corners.
  • The same commit did it right elsewhere. 688c67f8ff2 gave GeoLongitudeSlice.outsideDistance its NORTH_POLE / SOUTH_POLE terms in the same diff.
  • Not an approximation. On the same point the value jumps from 10.000098 degrees (top 89.9999) to 39.882280 (top 90): about 3,300 km for an 11 m change of the box. spatial3d's documented accuracy caveats are orders of magnitude smaller: the doc-value precision loss (4.19e-8 for the latitude component and 8.38e-8 for longitude, Geo3DDocValuesField.java:45-47), "up to 0.5% error" for the WGS84 distance query (Geo3DPoint.java:112-113) and the accuracy setting of exact circles (GeoCircleFactory.java:50-57).
  • Tests and history. No upstream test calls newOutsideBoxSort; the only box outside-distance assertions use a plain rectangle (lucene/spatial3d/src/test/org/apache/lucene/spatial3d/geom/TestGeoBBox.java:75-77). The two methods are unchanged since 2015 except for a reformat (LUCENE-9570), and no CHANGES entry or issue documents a polar limitation.
The devil's-advocate check228 words

The strongest case for "intended" (the intended-behaviour checker built it; cross-model reviews rated it weak): GeoNorthRectangle and GeoSouthRectangle are @lucene.internal, and GeoOutsideDistance, GeoBBox and GeoBBoxFactory are @lucene.experimental, as are PlanetModel, which the sort requires, and Geo3DPoint; the Geo3DDocValuesField class javadoc still lists its factory methods as "TBD" and warns about precision loss; "the closest outside edge" does not say that edge end points count; LUCENE-7212 (#8267), which added the sort, is still open and its last maintainer comment lists tests as still needed; and a fix changes existing sort values and top-N results.

Why it fails: newOutsideBoxSort itself carries no stability tag and promises the ascending outside distance for any latitude in +/-90; "edge" must include its end points, because the method already measures the corners; neither an open feature issue nor a precision warning in the 1e-8 range documents that values may be off by tens of degrees; and the jump between 89.9999 and 90 shows a missing case, not a design. Upstream has treated geo3d distance errors as bugs before (for example LUCENE-7139, "Fix bugs in geo3d's Vincenty surface distance"). The compatibility effect is real; it bears on how the fix is announced (a CHANGES entry), not on whether the current values are correct. Expect a maintainer to say the sort is rarely used: that bears on priority, not on validity.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Add the pole distance to the minimum

Add the pole to the minimum in GeoNorthRectangle.outsideDistance and GeoSouthRectangle.outsideDistance, as GeoLongitudeSlice does: two added lines and one changed line per class. The repro passes with assertions on and off, and all of lucene/spatial3d passes (185 tests including the repro).

Add the pole distance to the minimum (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In GeoNorthRectangle.outsideDistance (:283-298) add distanceStyle.computeDistance(planetModel.NORTH_POLE, x, y, z) to the minimum; in GeoSouthRectangle.outsideDistance (:254-269) the same with SOUTH_POLE. The boundary is the bottom (top) parallel and two meridian segments ending at the corners and the pole; the existing terms cover the parallel, the segments' interiors and the corners, so the pole is the only missing end point. For a point whose perpendicular feet fall off both segments, the nearest point of each segment is an end point, so this completes the minimum.

Trade-offs

  • Cost: one extra point distance per call, only in the two narrow polar classes. No format, API or index change.
  • Compatibility: existing users of the sort get smaller values for documents beyond the pole, a different order and possibly a different top N; worth stating in the CHANGES entry.
  • Distance styles: the pole is a point of the box for every DistanceStyle, and the term uses the same computeDistance(GeoPoint, x, y, z) as the corners; only ARC, the style the sort uses, was validated.
  • With the PR: add the repro (or a polar case in TestGeoBBox plus a first newOutsideBoxSort test in TestGeo3DDocValues) and a CHANGES entry.

  • In the hunt: the upstream spatial3d suite gives the same result with and without the change (181 tests: 178 pass, 3 assumption-aborted, on ea57360912ed), and the bounded sweep drops from 81,742 to 0 violations in 17,068,396 checks (not re-run independently).

Document the limitation (a compatibility choice)

#

Say in the newOutsideBoxSort javadoc that boxes reaching a pole give too large values beyond the pole, and keep the current values. Users can work around it with a top latitude such as 89.9999.

Trade-offs

Keeps existing sort values and top-N results stable and is cheaper to review, but leaves errors of tens of degrees (up to about 49 in the boxes measured) for a legal input when a two-line fix exists, and the factory's pole tolerance (89.999999 counts as 90) makes the trigger hard to describe.

Not a fix: search the whole side meridians, as the wide classes do

#

GeoWideNorthRectangle bounds its side planes only by the latitude plane, which is safe because, for an extent of 180 degrees or more, the far half of each side meridian lies inside the box (GeoWideNorthRectangle.java:262-267).

Trade-offs

For a narrow box the far half of each side meridian is outside the box, so the same change would make distances too small: for (80, 180) the left-side candidate (80.1489, 170), 1.727941 degrees away, is rejected only by the right side plane, so dropping that bound would return 1.73 degrees instead of 10 (the diagram probe trace; the change itself was not run).

Downstream note

#

Downstream relevance: an application that takes the first hit of this sort as the document nearest to a polar box can get a farther one; hit sets and filters are not affected.

How it was found

#

A bounded-exhaustive comparison of the real jar against an exact reference: 540 lat/lon boxes and about 600 polygons, each checked at about 3,700 to 5,400 points on the sphere and on WGS84, 17.07 million checks. All 81,742 mismatches were in GeoNorthRectangle and GeoSouthRectangle, always too large; the cause was then read off the code and reduced to a JUnit.

The method in full234 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive translation validation on the stock jar (semi-formal tier), in a hunt unit on the geo3d distance sort fields.

  • The harness built 540 GeoBBoxFactory boxes (9 latitudes including +-90 and +-89.5 x 12 longitude pairs including dateline-crossing, full, wide and degenerate boxes) and 600 simple star polygons (599 built on WGS84), and compared each one's outside distance at about 3,700 to 5,400 decoded doc-value points with an independent exact reference of the arc distance to the boundary. Geo3D parallels are horizontal planes and its meridians planes through the origin, so the same direction-based reference is exact on SPHERE and WGS84 for Lucene's ARC distance (the angle between the points' direction vectors, not an ellipsoidal surface distance). Only ARC was checked, and 27,398 checks within 3e-6 rad of an edge were skipped.
  • Of 17,068,396 checks, 81,742 failed, all in two classes: GeoNorthRectangle (20,963 per planet model) and GeoSouthRectangle (19,908 per planet model), always with Lucene's value too large. With the pole term added, 0.
  • The unit's plan targeted the sort comparators; it did not name this lead. The cause was read off the two outsideDistance methods and reduced to a public-API JUnit on the stock main jars.
  • Found 2026-10-27 on main a9779ef3c193; then checked by an independent reproducer, an intended-behaviour devil's advocate, reviews by a model from another family (OpenAI GPT-5.6), an adjudication, a history, prior-report and security follow-up, and a credibility audit.

History

#

There from the start: GeoNorthRectangle and GeoSouthRectangle.outsideDistance were first written without a pole term in LUCENE-6578 (688c67f8ff2, 2015-07-01, released in 5.3.0); the sort came in 6.1.0 (LUCENE-7212). Executed on 14 releases from 5.3.0 to 10.5.2 and main; not fixed upstream.

Versions tested7 rows

Introduced by LUCENE-6578 (now #7636, "Geo3d: arcDistanceToShape() method may be useful"): trunk 688c67f8ff2 (svn r1688545, 2015-07-01, "LUCENE-6578: Geo3D: compute the distance from a point to a shape.", committed by @dsmiley; CHANGES credits Karl Wright and David Smiley; JIRA patches, no PR) and the branch_5x backport 780e4040238, first released in 5.3.0. The same diff gave GeoLongitudeSlice.outsideDistance its pole terms.

Later changes to the two methods: only the LUCENE-9570 reformat (2cbf261032d, 2021); the files moved with LUCENE-6607 and LUCENE-7056. The sort newOutsideBoxSort came with LUCENE-7212 (cc263413457, branch_6x aa339a2f075, 2016-05-18, #8267, still open) and had the bug from its first release, 6.1.0. Release-level bracketing only; no commit-level build.

The near-pole trigger is younger: latitudes such as 89.999999 count as the pole only through the sine tolerance of LUCENE-10673 (bd0718f0716, branch_9x 17e8a42e1aa, 2022-08-04, first released in 9.4.0); before that, the pole test was within pi x 1e-12 rad (in 9.2-9.3 also a sine of exactly 1), so older releases are affected only at the exact pole latitudes.

LFV-782, snapshot of 2026-10-30

Registry title
Geo3D outside distance to a lat/lon box that reaches a pole (GeoNorthRectangle / GeoSouthRectangle) ignores the pole, so Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort gives documents beyond the pole the distance to a far corner (39.88 instead of 10 degrees for the box lat 60..90 x lon -10..10 and the point (80, 180)) and sorts them after nearer documents
Area
spatial3d / geom polar rectangles outside distance (GeoNorthRectangle, GeoSouthRectangle) and the Geo3DDocValuesField outside-box sort
Priority
Low, rank 363: Geo3D outside-distance sorts are wrong only for pole-reaching boxes narrower than 180 degrees in the little-used spatial3d sort, so it follows the similar outside-distance rows LFV-274 and LFV-735.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Applications that sort by Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort (or call computeOutsideDistance on a GeoBBoxFactory box) with a box whose maxLatitude is 90 or minLatitude -90 (also latitudes within the factory's pole tolerance, such as 89.999999) and whose longitude extent is under 180 degrees get distances tens of degrees too large for documents on the far side of the pole, so those documents sort after farther ones or drop out of the top N, with no error, on the default planet model, codec and config and with -ea and -da alike; the hit set never changes, and boxes that stop short of the pole (89.9999 already gives the right value) or span 180 degrees or more of longitude are not affected.
Summary
You sort documents by their outside distance from a lat/lon box that reaches the North Pole, for example latitude 60..90 x longitude -10..10, with Geo3DDocValuesField.newOutsideBoxSort. A document at (80, 180) is only 10 degrees (about 1,112 km) from that box, because the box contains the pole, so it should sort before a document at (35, 0), which is 25 degrees south of the box. Instead GeoNorthRectangle.outsideDistance never considers the pole, where both side edges of the box end, and returns the distance to a far corner (39.88 degrees on the sphere; 4,434,716 m against 2,779,879 m in the WGS84 sort), so the nearer document sorts last; GeoSouthRectangle has the mirror bug at the South Pole.
