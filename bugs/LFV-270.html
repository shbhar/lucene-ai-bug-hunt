A late message from the old primary makes an NRT replica delete the new primary's pre-copied merged segment

After a clean NRT fail-over, a late notification from the old primary makes a replica delete the new primary's pre-copied merged segment: its only check that the generation went up, an assert, comes after the deletion. The next cut-over then fails ("_3.si ... may be missing or corrupt") or copies the segment again.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

newNRTPoint calls maybeNewPrimary before any version check. maybeNewPrimary treats any generation different from the current one as a new primary and first deletes the pre-copied merged files; its only check that the generation went up is an assert after that loop. So an older generation deletes them, with assertions on and off.

Background: primary generations and merge pre-copy

#
  1. In lucene-replicator's NRT segment replication (opt-in, experimental) one PrimaryNode indexes and ReplicaNodes copy its files. After each refresh the primary tells each replica newNRTPoint(primaryGen, version).
  2. Each newly promoted primary gets a higher primary generation, so replicas can tell which primary they follow. Versions keep growing across a promotion; a replica skips a notification whose version is older than its own.
  3. Before a merged segment becomes visible, the primary pre-copies it to the replicas. A replica keeps those files as pending until a point uses them, and then does not copy them again.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. You fail over cleanly: primary P1 (generation 1) is closed, then replica A is promoted to P2 (generation 2), and replica B follows P2 at version 11.
  2. P2 merges, and B pre-copies the merged segment _3 (3 files, 2167 bytes) so that its next refresh does not have to copy it.
  3. P1's last notification, delayed in transit, now reaches B: newNRTPoint(1, v) with a version below B's. You expect B to ignore it, as it already ignores older versions.
  4. Instead B treats generation 1 as a new primary and deletes the 3 pre-copied files before its only check that the generation went up, an assert. With assertions off it also sets its generation back to 1.
  5. B's cut-over to P2's merged point (version 13) then fails with CorruptSegmentInfoException: segment info file: _3.si cannot be read - it may be missing or corrupt and B stays at version 11, or B copies the 3 files again. It recovers at P2's next point (version 15).

Who is affected

#

Users of the opt-in, experimental NRT segment replication, in every release since 6.0.0, with assertions on or off. It takes one notification or pre-copy request from the old primary that arrives after a replica moved to the new primary, while a merged segment is pre-copied; Lucene's reference transport does not order them. Rare, and it recovers at the next point.

Mechanism, terms and numbers608 words

In lucene-replicator's NRT segment replication every promoted primary gets a higher primary generation. ReplicaNode.newNRTPoint calls maybeNewPrimary before any version check, and maybeNewPrimary treats any generation different from lastPrimaryGen as a new primary: it deletes the merged segment files pre-copied from the current primary (pendingMergeFiles), and only then asserts that the generation went up. So after a clean fail-over (the old primary closed, then a replica promoted), one delayed notification or merge pre-copy request from the old primary deletes the new primary's pre-copied merged segment, with assertions on and off; with assertions off the replica also goes back to the old generation. The replica's next cut-over to the merged point then fails with a misleading "may be missing or corrupt" exception, or the merged segment is copied again. It recovers at the primary's next point, and nothing wrong is searched. Present since 6.0.0 (LUCENE-5438); ignoring an older generation before anything is touched fixes the repro and passes the module's nightly suites.

Step by step, on the minimal repro

#
  1. Primary and replica at generation 2. The primary starts with generation 2 and forcePrimaryVersion 11, past every version (up to 10) the generation-1 primary published, as a promotion does; the replica starts following it.
  2. Two segments, one sync. Two empty documents in two segments (_0, _1), flushAndRefresh(), and the replica copies that point.
  3. Pre-copy. forceMerge(1) makes _2; the primary's merged-segment warmer pre-copies [_2.cfe, _2.cfs, _2.si] to the replica through launchPreCopyMerge, which records them in pendingMergeFiles. They have no reference yet: one is taken only when a point that uses them is installed (:446, :450).
  4. The late call. newNRTPoint(1, 10) arrives. maybeNewPrimary(1) sees 1 != 2 and runs deleteIfNoRef on the three files, which deletes them.
  5. Assertions on: the assert throws newPrimaryGen=1 vs lastPrimaryGen=2, after the deletion, and also skips pendingMergeFiles.clear(), so close() later tries to delete the files again (NoSuchFileException: _2.si, suppressed). Assertions off: lastPrimaryGen becomes 1 and the call returns at the older-version skip.

What follows (the study's consequence probe)

#
  • Late call while B copies P2's merged point: the copy listed the pre-copied files as identical (CopyJob.java:85), so it fetches nothing; the cut-over reads the new SegmentInfos (:433-434), which references the deleted _3.si, and throws RuntimeException: CorruptSegmentInfoException: segment info file: _3.si cannot be read - it may be missing or corrupt before the searcher is switched (:440). B keeps serving version 11.
  • Late call before that point: the merged point's copy fetches all three files again, 2167 bytes in the probe.
  • Assertions off: a commit in the window records __primaryGen=1 (:368); by reading, a restart from it does a full, safe re-sync (:189-191).
  • In every case a commit right after succeeds, and B recovers at P2's next point (version 15, generation 2).
Exposure and likelihood318 words
  • Who is hit. Applications that use lucene-replicator's NRT segment replication (PrimaryNode / ReplicaNode / CopyJob subclasses, all marked @lucene.experimental). Every release since 6.0.0, any index size, assertions on or off.
  • What it takes. A fail-over during which one notification (or merge pre-copy request) from the old primary reaches a replica after it started following the new primary, plus a merged segment pre-copied from the new primary that no published point uses yet. Lucene's reference transport does not rule this out: the primary sends each notification on its own connection (SimplePrimaryNode.java:358-366, test code), the server runs one handler thread per connection (TestSimpleServer.java:344-356), and the replica hands the received generation to newNRTPoint unchecked (SimpleReplicaNode.java:205-218; :331-339 for pre-copy). Only timing keeps it rare. Likelihood: rare; nothing has been reported since 2016.
  • What goes wrong. (1) The cut-over to the new primary's merged point fails with a misleading "may be missing or corrupt" exception and the replica keeps serving its previous point until the primary publishes again; the reference job runner logs and ignores it (Jobs.java:105-111, test code), so it shows as a failed refresh. (2) Or the merged segment is copied again, the cost the pre-copy exists to avoid ("NRT reopen time on replicas is only in proportion to flushed segment sizes, not merged segments", PreCopyMergedSegmentWarmer.java:33-36); for a large merge that is a full extra transfer and a slow refresh. (3) With assertions off the replica reports the old generation, and a commit in that window is tagged with it.
  • What does not go wrong. No wrong documents are searched: the replica serves its previous point or the new primary's, the failure comes before the searcher switch, a commit right after succeeds, and nothing hangs.
  • Impact class: crash (an unexpected exception on a state the API accepts); priority low (opt-in experimental module, rare trigger, recovers at the next point).

Minimal repro

#

One JUnit test in the replicator module's test package: a primary and a replica in memory, two empty documents, one sync, forceMerge(1), then the late call newNRTPoint(1, 10). Single thread, deterministic, no sockets or reflection. It accepts the late call being ignored or rejected and checks only that the pre-copied files still exist. Run it with gradle (command below).

More files, environment, full output444 words

The ~120 lines below the test method are the minimum PrimaryNode, ReplicaNode and CopyJob subclasses that run the protocol on one thread, copying files straight from the primary's directory. Why it looks unintended

#
  • The generation exists "so replicas can detect when the primary they were talking to is changed" (Node.java:50-54); an older one is never a new primary.
  • The same method skips older versions because reordering "can happen"; a late older generation is the same reordering.
  • The assert shows an older generation was not expected, but it runs after the deletion.
The full argument345 words
  • The generation exists for this comparison. Node.java:50-54: the primary gen "increments every time we promote a new primary, so replicas can detect when the primary they were talking to is changed"; PrimaryNode.java:136-139: "incremented each time a new primary is started for this index"; ReplicaNode.java:85: lastPrimaryGen is the "Primary gen last time we successfully replicated".
  • The discard rationale covers only a new primary. The comment above the call (ReplicaNode.java:536-544) explains discarding pre-copied segments for "the same primary (just e.g. rebooted), or a promoted replica that had a newer NRT point". Nothing about an older generation.
  • Reordering is already expected. An older version "can happen, if two syncs happen close together, and due to thread scheduling, the incoming older version runs after the newer version" (:564-566), and is skipped.
  • The pre-copied files are the current primary's. :82-83: "Merged segment files that we pre-copied, but have not yet made visible in a new NRT point".
  • No caller precondition is documented in the newNRTPoint javadoc (:525-529), maybeNewPrimary (:705), the package docs, CHANGES.txt or MIGRATE.md. The module author's 2017 design post: "once you pick the new primary, Lucene will take care of having all replicas cutover to it, removing stale partially copied files from the old primary"; it leaves leader election, failure detection, load balancing, cluster state and "the low level mechanics of how to copy bytes from primary to replica" to the application, and says nothing about late or reordered messages.
  • History. Before 9afa560054aa (2016-02-08) maybeNewPrimary ran after the copy job was created, with the generation of the primary actually serving the copy, and deleted nothing. That commit moved the call to the top and added the deletion ahead of the older assert-only check, with a comment about new primaries only. No module test sends an older generation (the three explicit newNRTPoint calls in TestNRTReplication carry the generation-0 primary's own generation).
  • The control. The same test with generation 2 in the late call passes, with assertions on and off: only the older-generation path deletes the files.
The devil's-advocate check247 words

Strongest case for "intended" (an independent reviewer from another model family, OpenAI GPT-5.6, refute lens, reading only): the newNRTPoint javadoc speaks of a new infos "on the primary", and a deposed generation-1 sender is no longer the primary; the assert states that callers present generations in increasing order; the module is @lucene.experimental; the design post leaves tracking which node is the primary to the application, so filtering stale senders is its job.

Why it fails:

  1. No javadoc states the precondition; an assert that fires after the damage is no contract, and with assertions off it is not checked at all.
  2. The trigger is a clean fail-over: the old primary is already closed, so there is no live sender to fence, only a message in flight. Nothing documents that the caller must filter it: the application's ReplicaNode subclass could compare against the protected lastPrimaryGen (ReplicaNode.java:85-86), but no javadoc asks it to, the module's own test transport does not (SimpleReplicaNode.java:205-218 passes the received generation on unchecked), and the replica already holds that value and drops older versions the same way.
  3. @lucene.experimental is about API stability, not about which inputs may damage the replica's state.
  4. The reviewer agreed with the root cause, could not run Java, and rated its argument moderate; the tie-breaker, which ran the test, the control and the fix, confirmed the bug. The split-brain reading applies only to the study's dropped first test (a deposed primary that keeps publishing), which is not claimed.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended A. Ignore an older generation in newNRTPoint, reject it in maybeNewPrimary before deleting

In newNRTPoint, ignore and log a notification whose generation is older than lastPrimaryGen, before maybeNewPrimary, like an older version; in maybeNewPrimary, throw IllegalStateException for an older generation before deleting anything, which also covers launchPreCopyMerge. The repro passes with assertions on and off; with assertions on, the module's nightly TestNRTReplication (13 tests) and one TestStressNRTReplication run pass too.

Trade-offs

  • A stale launchPreCopyMerge caller now gets an exception instead of a started job. Returning silently there instead would leave the reference transport's handler waiting on its finished flag until it notices the dead connection (SimpleReplicaNode.java:339-362), so the exception is the cleaner signal (read, not run).

B. Only move the check to the top of maybeNewPrimary (not run)

#

Throw for newPrimaryGen < lastPrimaryGen at the top of maybeNewPrimary and leave newNRTPoint alone.

Trade-offs

Smaller, and it protects the pre-copied files on both paths, but a late notification then throws to the transport instead of being skipped like an older version.

C. Also turn start()'s assert into a check (optional, separate case)

Trade-offs

How it was found

#

TLA+ model checking of the NRT replication protocol: TLC showed an older generation getting past the assert-only check with assertions off, in a split-brain trace that is not claimed. JUnit probes then narrowed it to a late message after a clean fail-over and showed the deletion of the pre-copied merge with assertions on and off.

The method in full164 words
  • Method (semi-formal). The hunt plan for the replicator listed primary-generation fencing ("no copy accepted from a deposed primary") as a property to model. The finder wrote a TLA+ model of the NRT replication protocol with maybeNewPrimary in both assertion modes. TLC found the fencing property violated with assertions off (2076 distinct states); with assertions on the "no assert fires" property is violated instead (23 states). That trace needs a deposed primary that keeps publishing, and the property holds under the module's controller discipline (220 states), so the finder's first JUnit (a split brain) was dropped from the claim.
  • Narrowing (non-formal). The devil's-advocate verifier ran a probe with the old primary closed before the promotion: with assertions off the generation went back to 1 and the next commit recorded it. The tie-breaker's probe added the merge pre-copy and showed the deletion, the failed cut-over and the re-copy, with assertions on and off.
  • Reduction. The one-test repro used here was then written from the narrowed scenario.

LFV-270, snapshot of 2026-11-03

Registry title
An NRT replica acts on a late notification from an older primary generation: ReplicaNode.maybeNewPrimary deletes the current primary's pre-copied merged segment before its only check, an assert, so the replica's cut-over to the merged point fails or the segment is copied again (with or without assertions), and with assertions off lastPrimaryGen also moves back
Area
replicator / NRT segment replication (ReplicaNode.newNRTPoint, maybeNewPrimary, launchPreCopyMerge: primaryGen fencing)
Priority
Low, rank 825: In experimental NRT replication, a late notification from the old primary gives a loud, transient failed cut-over or a redundant copy.
Impact class
crash
Impact note
Applications that use lucene-replicator's NRT segment replication (PrimaryNode/ReplicaNode/CopyJob subclasses, opt-in and @lucene.experimental), in every release since 6.0.0 and at any index size, are hit when, during a clean fail-over (old primary closed before the new one is promoted, no split brain), a notification from the old primary reaches a replica after it has started following the new primary: with -ea and with -da the replica deletes the new primary's pre-copied merged segment, so its cut-over to that merged point fails with a misleading RuntimeException wrapping CorruptSegmentInfoException 'segment info file: _3.si cannot be read - it may be missing or corrupt' (NoSuchFileException for _3.si on the executed releases) and it keeps serving its previous point until the primary publishes again, or, if the late call lands before the merged point, the whole merged segment is copied again (defeating the pre-copy); with -da lastPrimaryGen and the next commit's __primaryGen also go back to the dead primary's generation until the next point; no wrong search results, no corrupt commit and no hang were observed.
Summary
You run Lucene's NRT segment replication and fail over cleanly: the old primary (generation 1) is closed, then replica A is promoted to primary (generation 2), and replica B follows A and pre-copies A's merged segment. A last notification from the old primary, delayed in transit, then reaches B; you expect B to ignore it, as it already ignores out-of-order older NRT versions, because the primary generation only ever grows. Instead ReplicaNode.maybeNewPrimary treats the older generation as a primary change and deletes A's pre-copied merged files before its only check (an assert), so B's cut-over to A's merged point fails with '_3.si cannot be read - it may be missing or corrupt' or B copies the merged segment again, and with assertions off B also sets lastPrimaryGen back to 1.
