addIndexes(CodecReader) with a term-vector field that has zero terms writes a chunk the default codec cannot read back, and every later merge that re-encodes the segment closes the IndexWriter

If addIndexes(CodecReader) copies a document whose term-vector field has no terms left, the default codec writes a chunk it cannot read back, and on current releases every later merge that re-encodes that segment closes the IndexWriter. Confirmed on 11 versions from 4.2.0 to main; CheckIndex at its default level reports clean.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

The copier behind addIndexes(CodecReader) hands the codec a field with zero terms. The default term-vector writer then stores per-field floats that its reader does not expect when no term of the chunk has offsets, and the reader lists the field but returns no terms for it, so the next merge counts one field too many.

  1. Copy a 2-document index with addIndexes(SlowCodecReaderWrapper.wrap(r)), where r removes the term "secret" from field a in both the postings and the term vectors. Doc 0's a held only "secret" and stores offsets, so its term vectors now list a with 0 terms.
  2. You expect doc 1's term vectors to read back as body=[here, public, text]. Instead the read throws IOException: offset 0 is invalid: the writer stored 8 bytes (one float per field of the chunk) that the reader does not expect, so it decodes the term bytes from the wrong place. A chunk holds up to 128 documents; its other documents with term vectors fail the same way.
  3. Copy again with a not storing offsets, plus a doc 2. Delete doc 2 and commit(). You expect the merge to succeed. Instead it throws Wrong number of values added, expected: 2, got: 1 and the IndexWriter closes; every fresh writer whose merge re-encodes the segment fails the same way.
  4. Why the merge fails: the copied segment lists a for doc 0 but returns no terms for it, and the merge copier counts the field before it skips it. Normal indexing never writes a zero-term field, so ordinary indexes are not affected.
  5. No warning: addIndexes and commit succeed, and CheckIndex at its default level reports the index clean (here levels 2 and 3 report the segment broken; without offsets only level 3 does).

Who is affected

#

Applications that copy or rewrite an index through addIndexes(CodecReader) with their own reader that removes terms from term-vector fields: default codec and config, any size, 4.2.0 to 10.5.2 and main. One document whose term-vector field holds only removed terms is enough. Normal indexing never produces it.

Mechanism, terms and numbers805 words

IndexWriter.addIndexes(CodecReader...) copies the term vectors of a reader that is not a same-format segment through the codec-agnostic copier TermVectorsWriter.addAllDocVectors. If the reader removes terms (a FilterLeafReader that drops a term from the postings and, consistently, from the term vectors), a document whose term-vector field held only removed terms reaches the codec as a field with numTerms = 0. The default Lucene90 term-vector writer then writes one 4-byte AvgCharsPerTerm float per field of the chunk because the field has the OFFSETS flag, while its reader reads those floats only when the chunk has at least one offset, so the chunk's other documents with term vectors (a chunk holds up to 128 documents) cannot be read. The same reader lists the zero-term field but returns null from terms(), so the next merge that re-encodes the segment (a deletion rules out the bulk copy once the merge policy picks the segment) declares a field it never writes and, on 9.0.0 and later, closes the IndexWriter, again for every new writer. CheckIndex at its default level reports the index clean. Normal indexing never produces a zero-term term-vector field; the trigger is an application-written term-removing reader. Confirmed on 11 versions from 4.2.0 (2013) to 10.5.2 and main. Before 9.0.0 (merges) and 10.4.0 (chunk reads), runs without assertions could fail silently: a merge wrote one document's term vectors under another, and chunk reads returned wrong bytes or threw ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException.

Terms

#
  • Term vectors: an optional per-document copy of a field's terms (with positions, offsets, payloads), stored by the default codec in compressed chunks of up to 128 documents, flushed once their term bytes reach 4 KB (Lucene90TermVectorsFormat.java:68, :170).
  • The copier: TermVectorsWriter.addAllDocVectors, the codec-agnostic path that writes one document's term vectors from any Fields. addIndexes(CodecReader...) through SlowCodecReaderWrapper uses it, and so does any merge of a segment with deletions (Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsWriter.java:915-931, bulk-merge test :953-967).
  • A zero-term field: a document's term vectors list field a, but its Terms has no terms. A term-removing FilterLeafReader produces it when a document's field held only removed terms. The indexing chain never does: TermVectorsConsumerPerField.finish() skips fields with no terms (TermVectorsConsumerPerField.java:69-71).

The bug

#
  1. The copier declares before it looks. It calls startDocument(vectors.size()) (TermVectorsWriter.java:242-251; the javadoc at :64-69 promises startField will then be called that many times), forwards an empty Terms as startField(numTerms = 0) (:278-289), and skips a null Terms with continue ("FieldsEnum shouldn't lie...", :267-271).
  2. Writer and reader disagree about one chunk section. The writer's flushOffsets sets hasOffsets |= fd.hasOffsets for every field instance (Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsWriter.java:629) and, if set, writes one AvgCharsPerTerm float per distinct field (:643-657). The reader computes totalOffsets from the term frequencies of OFFSETS fields, to which a zero-term field adds nothing (Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsReader.java:551-561), and reads the floats only if (totalOffsets > 0) (:587-591).
  3. So the chunk is shifted. With a zero-term OFFSETS field and no other offset in the chunk, the reader starts decoding the LZ4-compressed term bytes 4 bytes per field too early. It sees a zero match offset and throws IOException("offset 0 is invalid") (LZ4.java:113-116, since #15570 in 10.4.0). The zero-term document itself reads back; the other documents of the chunk that have term vectors do not.
  4. And the next merge miscounts. The same reader lists the zero-term field in iterator() and size() (Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsReader.java:879-902, :948-950) but returns null from terms() ("no term", :918-921). A merge that re-encodes the segment gives that Fields to the copier, which declares the field and skips it. The writer sizes its packed field table for the declared total (Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsWriter.java:436-450, :483-498), and DirectWriter.finish() throws Wrong number of values added (:495); with no other field in the chunk, flushFieldNums fails first (:465-466). IndexWriter treats it as tragic.

The numbers

#

The independent reproduction's documents, default codec, main a9779ef3c193: doc 0 a = "secret" (term vectors with offsets), doc 1 body = "here public text" (no offsets). After the filtered addIndexes, the chunk's sections end at byte 21; the writer put 2 floats (fields a and body) as 8 zero bytes at [21, 29) and the compressed terms at 29; the reader starts decoding at 21. Decoding from 29 gives "herepublictext"; from 21 it throws. Without offsets and with a doc 2 that is then deleted, the commit's merge sees 2 declared fields (doc 0 a, doc 1 body) and 1 written: expected: 2, got: 1. CheckIndex: level 1 (default) clean; level 2 test: term vectors ERROR [offset 0 is invalid]; level 3 a NullPointerException on the null Terms; both report the whole segment as broken. Without offsets (the intended-behaviour check's probe), level 2 is clean and only level 3 fails.

Exposure and likelihood456 words
  • Who is exposed. Applications that copy, prune or rewrite an index through IndexWriter.addIndexes(CodecReader...) with their own reader that removes terms from fields that store term vectors (for example stripping a blocked vocabulary while copying an index without re-analysing the source). By reading, the same copier serves merges whose readers the application wraps (MergePolicy.OneMerge#wrapForMerge) and custom codec merges (not executed). Default codec and IndexWriterConfig, any index size.
  • The pattern is used. Lucene's own TestFilterLeafReader passes a term-filtering FilterLeafReader (only terms containing 'e') to addIndexes(SlowCodecReaderWrapper.wrap(reader)); it filters postings only. A 2016 java-user thread passes a filtering reader to addIndexes to archive part of an index. No Lucene-shipped reader produces the zero-term shape from an ordinarily written segment, and no downstream engine checked does.
  • What it takes. Once such a copy runs, ordinary content triggers it. The merge failure needs only the emptied field and a later merge that re-encodes the segment; a deletion rules out the bulk copy once the merge policy picks the segment; in the test, one deletion of three and a commit() with the default merge policy were enough. The unreadable chunk also needs the emptied field to store offsets and no other offset in its chunk.
  • What breaks. Up to 127 other documents of the chunk (those with term vectors) have unreadable term vectors (term-vector highlighting, term-vector "more like this" and term-vector APIs fail for them). On 9.0.0 and later every merge that re-encodes the segment closes the IndexWriter, and a fresh writer fails again, so indexing stops and the segment's partial deletions cannot be reclaimed by such a merge (a segment whose documents are all deleted is still dropped without one). A merge without deletions copies the bad chunk unchanged into the merged segment.
  • Silent until then. addIndexes and commit succeed; CheckIndex at its default level reports clean. With offsets, levels 2 and 3 report the whole segment broken; without offsets, level 2 is clean and level 3 fails. When it reports the segment broken, -exorcise would remove all its documents.
  • Older releases, without assertions. Before 9.0.0 the merge "succeeds" and writes one document's term vectors under another document; before 10.4.0 some chunk reads return wrong bytes and others throw ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException (on 9.12.3 the security probe counted 30,572 exceptions and 10,388 silent wrong reads). With assertions on, these releases throw AssertionError.
  • What stays intact. The bug adds no damage to postings, stored fields or doc values beyond what the filter itself does (it removes the term from the postings on purpose; the postings writer drops the emptied field). One side effect: when the merge fails inside commit(), that commit's deletion is not committed.
  • How likely. Rare: an expert pattern, and the searches found no report since 2013.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • TermVectorsWriter.startDocument promises startField will be called numVectorFields times; the copier in the same class declares a field, then skips it.
  • No javadoc forbids an empty Terms; CheckIndex's slow-level postings and term-vector checks report no error on the filtered reader, and the postings writer simply drops the emptied field.
  • The default codec writes a segment it cannot read.
The full argument341 words
  • Lucene's own contract. startDocument: "startField(...) will be called numVectorFields times" (TermVectorsWriter.java:64-69). addAllDocVectors declares vectors.size() (:251) and skips a null Terms (:268-271).
  • No numTerms >= 1 anywhere. startField: "startTerm will be called numTerms times" (:74-80). Fields, Terms, TermVectors, addIndexes(CodecReader...) (only "empty segments are dropped", IndexWriter.java:3254-3275) and SlowCodecReaderWrapper ("Wraps arbitrary readers for merging", SlowCodecReaderWrapper.java:37-40) state no such rule. The test framework's AssertingTermVectorsFormat checks that the declared counts match, not that terms exist.
  • Empty Terms from filtering are anticipated. Terms.getMax(): "empty: only possible from a FilteredTermsEnum..." (Terms.java:157, :174, both in getMax()); getMin() "returns null when there are no terms" (:139-141). CheckIndex tolerates the postings "ghost field" (CheckIndex.java:2244-2249) and has the TODO "really the codec should not return a field from FieldsEnum if it has no Terms... but we do this today" (:1519-1522).
  • The format expected zero-term fields. The reader's numTerms == 0 branch ("no term", Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsReader.java:918-921) dates from the format's first commit in 2013, yet the reader keeps listing the field.
  • Lucene's own checks accept the input. CheckIndex.testPostings and testTermVectors at MIN_LEVEL_FOR_SLOW_CHECKS report no error on the filtered reader (they are not a complete validator of arbitrary readers); the postings path drops the same emptied field and stays clean.
  • Input versus output. The filter lists the field and returns a non-null, empty Terms, which nothing forbids. After the copy, the Lucene90 reader lists the field and returns null from terms(), which conflicts with Fields.terms: "This will return null if the field does not exist" (Fields.java:38). The shape the merge copier then mishandles is Lucene's own output.
  • Tests and precedent. TestParallelReaderEmptyIndex.testEmptyIndexWithVectors: "Adding this index to another index should not throw any exception" (weak precedent: ordinary indexing has already omitted the empty field there). LUCENE-1918 (#2993, 2009) fixed an addAllDocVectors crash on unusual addIndexes input as a bug. 4.1.0, and 4.2.0 with the older Lucene40 term-vector format, handle the same input.
The devil's-advocate check212 words

The strongest case for "not supported" (built by the devil's-advocate checker and the cross-model reviewer): the codec SPI and Fields are @lucene.experimental; the indexing chain and MemoryIndex enforce numTerms > 0 on purpose (TermVectorsConsumerPerField.java:69-71; MemoryIndex filters numTokens > 0 in its Fields iterator and size(), MemoryIndex.java:1834, :1898; a comment there, "ensure infos.numTokens > 0 invariant; needed for correct operation of terms()", stood from 2005 until LUCENE-7091 removed it in 2016); the copier's comment "FieldsEnum shouldn't lie..." shows its author assumed a listed field has terms; and in #9450 / LUCENE-8403 maintainers declined "filtered" term vectors. So "your reader should hide a field once it has no terms left". The cross-model reviewer rated this argument moderate.

Why it fails: the restriction is written nowhere; Lucene's own Lucene90 reader produces exactly such a listing, which its own copier and CheckIndex (level 3) then cannot handle; LUCENE-8403 was about term vectors that disagree with the postings, while this filter keeps them identical; and the result is not a rejected input but a segment the default codec cannot fully read and that closes every writer whose merge re-encodes it. An IllegalArgumentException at addIndexes time would be a defensible answer; silent corruption is not. Verdict: a defect (adjudication: confirmed, high confidence).

Suggested fix

#

Recommended The copier forwards only fields with at least one term, and declares exactly that many

In TermVectorsWriter.addAllDocVectors, first collect the fields that have at least one term, call startDocument with that count, and write only those (+32/-26, one file). The codec never sees a zero-term field and the declared count always matches. The test and the closest suites pass; a chunk already written with offsets stays unreadable.

final List<String> fieldNames = new ArrayList<>();
final List<Terms> fieldTerms = new ArrayList<>();
final List<Integer> fieldNumTerms = new ArrayList<>();
for (String fieldName : vectors) {
  final Terms terms = vectors.terms(fieldName);
  if (terms == null) continue;
  int numTerms = (int) terms.size();   // counted with the iterator when -1
  if (numTerms == 0) continue;
  fieldNames.add(fieldName); fieldTerms.add(terms); fieldNumTerms.add(numTerms);
}
final int numFields = fieldNames.size();
startDocument(numFields);
// then write exactly these fields

The copier forwards only fields with at least one term, and declares exactly that many (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In TermVectorsWriter.addAllDocVectors (TermVectorsWriter.java:235-328), first collect the fields whose Terms is non-null and has at least one term (counting with the iterator when size() is -1, as the code already does), call startDocument with that count, then write those fields. This is what the indexing chain already guarantees (TermVectorsConsumerPerField.java:69-71) and what the postings writer does with an emptied field. It is the common copier of Lucene's shipped non-bulk term-vector merge paths (another contributor's codec that overrides merge(MergeState) would need its own check). It removes both symptoms: no zero-term instance is written, and a later merge of a segment whose reader lists a null-Terms field no longer miscounts. The finder's patch, with its now-unused java.util.Iterator import removed.

final List<String> fieldNames = new ArrayList<>();
final List<Terms> fieldTerms = new ArrayList<>();
final List<Integer> fieldNumTerms = new ArrayList<>();
for (String fieldName : vectors) {
  final Terms terms = vectors.terms(fieldName);
  if (terms == null) continue;
  int numTerms = (int) terms.size();   // counted with the iterator when -1
  if (numTerms == 0) continue;
  fieldNames.add(fieldName); fieldTerms.add(terms); fieldNumTerms.add(numTerms);
}
final int numFields = fieldNames.size();
startDocument(numFields);
// then write exactly these fields

Trade-offs

  • Terms are counted in the first loop instead of the second, so the number of passes is unchanged; it keeps one document's field names, Terms objects and term counts in three small lists. Not benchmarked; this is already the slow per-document path.
  • Already-written segments (two-phase probe: written by stock main, merged with the fix): without offsets the merge now succeeds and CheckIndex at level 3 is clean; with offsets the chunk is already unreadable, so the merge still fails reading it (offset 0 is invalid, tragic). The fix cannot repair those bytes; rebuilding the segment from the source documents avoids it, and salvage with a tolerant reader was not investigated.

Write AvgCharsPerTerm under the reader's condition (executed: not enough alone)

#

In Lucene90CompressingTermVectorsWriter.flushOffsets, hasOffsets |= fd.hasOffsets && fd.numTerms > 0; (+2/-1), so the floats are written only when the reader will read them (totalOffsets > 0).

// the reader reads AvgCharsPerTerm only if the chunk has an offset (totalOffsets > 0)
hasOffsets |= fd.hasOffsets && fd.numTerms > 0;

Trade-offs

Run with the new test (-ea and -da): doc 1 now reads back as [here, public, text], but CheckIndex (level 3) still fails on the listed zero-term field and both merge tests fail as before. It only changes what the writer emits for zero-term OFFSETS instances, which normal indexing never produces. Useful next to the recommended fix, so that no other caller of the codec API can produce the mismatched layout.

Reject the input

#

Throw IllegalArgumentException from addAllDocVectors (or the Lucene90 startField) on numTerms == 0 or on a listed field with null Terms.

Trade-offs

Defensible, but it turns a valid filtered copy into an error the application must work around, and it leaves an already-written segment with a null-Terms listing permanently unmergeable. Not run.

Make the Lucene90 reader stop listing zero-term fields

#

Skip numTerms == 0 instances in TVFields.iterator() and size().

Trade-offs

Fixes the merge miscount for existing segments too, but not the layout mismatch, and touches the read path. Not run. Separately, CheckIndex.testTermVectors could report a listed field with null Terms instead of throwing a NullPointerException (CheckIndex.java:4360-4362).

Downstream note

#

Only an application's own term-removing reader can trigger it (no Lucene-shipped reader, and none of the Elasticsearch, OpenSearch or Solr merge and addIndexes paths read for this check, removes term-vector terms), and on releases before 10.4.0 run without assertions the misreads are silent and can carry fragments of neighbouring documents' terms.

How it was found

#

An AI tool's SMT model (Z3) of which optional sections the Lucene90 term-vector writer emits and its reader expects found one disagreement: an OFFSETS field with zero terms. The AI tool then found a public-API path that produces such a field, addIndexes with a term-removing reader, and found the merge failure by running it.

The method in full190 words

Method: SMT model checking with Z3 4.12.2 (the hunt's formal tier), by an AI tool working the hunt's unit on the compressing term-vector format, on main ea57360912ed.

  • The model encodes, per field instance of one chunk, the flags (positions, offsets, payloads), the term count and the term frequencies, and for each optional section of the chunk when the writer emits it and when the reader reads it, each mapped to file:line.
  • Obligation L1, "AvgCharsPerTerm presence agrees for every chunk the codec API accepts", is satisfiable for 1 to 4 field instances; the counterexample is an OFFSETS instance with numTerms == 0. L2, the same under numTerms >= 1 (what the indexing chain guarantees), and L3, the block value counts, are unsatisfiable.
  • The AI tool then searched for a public-API path that delivers numTerms == 0 to the writer, found addIndexes(CodecReader) with a term-filtering reader that CheckIndex accepts, and found the second symptom, the poisoned merge, by running it.
  • Independent checks followed: a reproducer with its own filter and tests, a cross-model review, a devil's-advocate checker, an adjudication, a history sweep and a security probe (see the checks below).

Introduced in 4.2.0 (2013) by LUCENE-4599, the compressing term-vector format, which LUCENE-4733 made the default: 4.1.0 passes, 4.2.0 fails, and 4.2.0 with the older Lucene40 format passes. Every executed release from 4.2.0 to 10.5.2 and main fails. Not fixed upstream.

Introduced by 5837fe83bd8 (2013-01-21, @jpountz, "LUCENE-4599: New compressed TVF impl: CompressingTermVectorsFormat", issue #5664; Subversion era, no pull request; branch_4x backport 4a62a579a70, the commit in the 4.2.0 tag): the AvgCharsPerTerm write and read conditions and the reader's numTerms == 0 -> null branch all date from it. Made the default by 94b17a28de5 (2013-02-02, LUCENE-4733, #5798; branch_4x e3710747d87). The copier's declare-first, skip-null behaviour is older (873f1999244, 2011, LUCENE-2621) and was harmless with the Lucene40 format, whose reader returns an empty Terms for such a field.

Only the versions below were run. Git pickaxe shows the same code fragments (hasOffsets |= fd.hasOffsets, totalOffsets > 0, charsPerTerm) carried through the format's copies and refactors (LUCENE-5969, LUCENE-9705, #15712), so the releases in between appear affected.

Symptom changes, not fixes: 873ac5f162a (LUCENE-9907, #72, 9.0.0) moved the field tables to DirectWriter, whose count check makes the poisoned merge throw instead of silently writing one document's term vectors under another; #15570 (473dd7b5865, 10.4.0) made LZ4 reject offset 0, so the shifted read throws instead of returning wrong bytes.

Bug record

#

LFV-244, snapshot of 2026-10-27

Registry title
addIndexes(CodecReader) from a reader whose term vectors list a field with zero terms writes a term-vector chunk that the default codec cannot read back, and every later merge that re-encodes the segment closes the IndexWriter
Area
core / codecs: term vectors (TermVectorsWriter.addAllDocVectors merge copier; Lucene90 compressing term-vector writer and reader), reached through IndexWriter.addIndexes(CodecReader)
Priority
Medium, rank 155: A term-removing filter reader fed to addIndexes writes an unreadable term-vector chunk and every later merge closes the writer, but that expert pattern makes it rarer than LFV-249.
Impact class
data corruption
Impact note
Applications that pass their own term-removing reader (a FilterLeafReader or FilterCodecReader that drops terms from the postings and term vectors, an expert pattern) to IndexWriter.addIndexes(CodecReader...) or to a merge whose readers they wrap are hit, with the default codec and IndexWriterConfig, at any index size and in every release since 4.2.0, as soon as one document's term-vector field is left listed with zero terms: every later merge that re-encodes that segment (a plain delete plus commit is enough) closes the IndexWriter tragically, on every retry (before 9.0.0 with assertions off it instead silently writes one document's term vectors under another document), and when that field stores offsets and nothing else in its chunk of up to 128 documents has offsets, the other documents' term vectors in the chunk are unreadable, while CheckIndex at its default level reports the index clean; normal indexing never produces such a field.
Summary
You copy an index with IndexWriter.addIndexes(CodecReader...) through a reader that removes some terms consistently from the postings and the term vectors (CheckIndex at its strictest level accepts this reader), so one document's term-vector field is left with zero terms. You expect the new segment to read back and merge like any other, as the postings do (the postings writer simply drops the emptied field). Instead the default term-vector format writes per-field AvgCharsPerTerm floats that its own reader does not expect, so when the emptied field stores offsets the other documents of the chunk throw 'offset 0 is invalid' on read, and because the reader lists the emptied field but returns null for it, the next merge that re-encodes the segment fails with 'Wrong number of values added' and closes the IndexWriter, on every retry.
