SimpleQueryParser skips the operator typed directly after a phrase slop, so "a b"~2+c means phrase OR c

In SimpleQueryParser + means AND, but typed directly after a phrase slop it is skipped: "new york"~2+pizza becomes "new york"~2 OR pizza and matches 3 documents instead of 1, with default settings, no error, in every release since 4.7.0.

The bug in 30 seconds

#

SimpleQueryParser lets you glue operators to tokens, as in token1+token2. After a phrase slop such as ~2, the parser skips the next character, so a glued +, |, quote or ( loses its meaning.

  1. Index three documents: "new york pizza", "new york bagels" and "chicago pizza". Parse "new york"~2+pizza with SimpleQueryParser and its default settings: the phrase new york with up to 2 positions of slop, AND pizza.
  2. You expect 1 hit, "new york pizza". With a space, "new york"~2 +pizza, or without the slop, "new york"+pizza, that is exactly what you get.
  3. Instead you get 3 hits. The parser reads the slop digit 2 and stops on the +; then a leftover step, meant to skip the phrase's closing quote, skips the +. The query is built as phrase OR pizza.
  4. The same happens to | (with default operator AND, an OR becomes an AND), to a quote (the next phrase becomes loose words) and to ( (the group is lost). No error, with assertions on or off.
  5. A space after the slop digits avoids it. A fuzzy term (a~1+b) and a ) that closes a group are not affected.

Who is affected

#

Every SimpleQueryParser user with the default flags (the near operator is on by default) who types a sloppy phrase followed, with no space, by +, |, a quote or (. No scale is needed. By code reading, parsers built on this class downstream inherit it. The shape is rare, and the searches found no prior report.

Mechanism, terms and numbers762 words

SimpleQueryParser's javadoc says that whitespace and ( ) + | " delimit tokens, that a phrase is a token, that token1+token2 is AND and token1|token2 is OR (written without spaces), and that "term1 term2"~5 is a near query. When a phrase with a slop is followed directly by one of those delimiters, the parser skips the delimiter: "a b"~2+c becomes f:"a b"~2 f:c (OR) instead of +f:"a b"~2 +f:c, although "a b"~2 +c and "a b"+c both give the AND. On the documents "new york pizza", "new york bagels" and "chicago pizza", "new york"~2+pizza matches 3 documents instead of 1.

The cause is an off-by-one in consumePhrase: parseFuzziness reads the slop digits and stops on the delimiter after them, and the unconditional ++state.index that follows, written in 2013 to step over the closing quote, then steps over that delimiter. It needs the default flags only, raises no error, and is in every release since 4.7.0 (2014), the first with the ~ operator. The fix is one condition: skip the closing quote only when there was no slop.

Terms

#
  • Slop (near query). "a b"~2 is a phrase whose words may be up to 2 positions apart (PhraseQuery(2, "f", "a", "b")). An empty ~ followed by another character means slop 2 (since 6.5.0); a ~ at the very end of the input means no slop.
  • Default operator. What two tokens with nothing between them combine with: OR, unless setDefaultOperator(MUST).
  • Delimiter. A character that ends a token: whitespace, ", |, +, ( or ) (javadoc SimpleQueryParser.java:41-42, tokenFinished :536-550).

Further cases (executed on main, 10.5.2 and with the fix):

  • A glued + under default AND, or | under default OR, is skipped too. When a token follows, the default operator then gives the same query; when a second operator follows, it does not, because the parser keeps the first explicit operator: under default AND "a b"~2+|c gives OR on main and AND with a space or the fix.
  • The skip also follows slop text that does not parse: "a b"~x+c gives slop 0 and loses the + (f:"a b" f:c), and so do "a b"~1a+c and "a b"~2\+c.
  • An empty phrase: a""~+a gives f:a f:a instead of +f:a +f:a.

The mechanism (main ea57360912ed)

#
  1. consumePhrase (SimpleQueryParser.java:296-364) reads the phrase up to its closing quote. If ~ and more characters follow, it moves onto the ~ and sets hasSlop (:317-324).
  2. parseFuzziness (:495-533, called at :356) advances from the ~ and stops at the first delimiter (:505-507), so the index is left on the +.
  3. consumePhrase then always runs ++state.index (:362). That line dates from 2013, when the index was always on the closing quote there. After a slop it skips the +, and the parse loop never sees it.
  4. The lost + or | leaves the next token to the default operator; a lost quote turns the next phrase into separate terms; a lost ( merges the group into the main sequence.
  5. consumeToken calls the same parseFuzziness for fuzzy terms (:410) with no trailing increment, so a~1+b keeps its AND. A group's ) is safe because consumeSubQuery parses the group's inner text as its own substring (:288-289).
// SimpleQueryParser.java:355-362
if (hasSlop) {

} else {
  branch = newPhraseQuery(phrase, 0);
}

Exposure and likelihood298 words
  • Who is exposed. Every caller of SimpleQueryParser with the default flags: the two-argument and the weights constructors pass -1 (SimpleQueryParser.java:141-148), which includes NEAR_OPERATOR. Nothing has to be configured. There is no other in-tree caller outside the parser's own package.
  • What it takes. A +, |, " or ( typed directly after the slop digits, after an empty ~, or after slop text that does not parse (~x). Glued operators are the documented style of this syntax (token1+token2), so users who write "new york"~2+pizza, and code that builds query strings by concatenation, can produce it.
  • What goes wrong. A query of the wrong structure, silently. Under default OR it over-matches: AND becomes OR, AND NOT becomes OR NOT, a phrase becomes loose terms. Under default AND a glued | under-matches: OR becomes AND, while a lost quote still widens the query (the second phrase becomes required loose terms: 2 hits instead of 1 on the documents "a b c d" and "a b d c"). A lost ( can go either way. No exception, crash or index change.
  • Downstream (by code reading only, not run). Elasticsearch's and OpenSearch's simple_query_string parsers and Solr's simple query parser extend SimpleQueryParser; consumePhrase is private, so they inherit the parse loop.
  • How likely. Rare: slop is an advanced feature, the upstream test writes "foo bar"~12 baz with a space, the most natural glued case (a ) closing a group) is unaffected, and the searches found no prior report (see Prior reports).
  • Opt-out. The targeted opt-out is turning NEAR_OPERATOR off, which disables phrase slop altogether; turning off the phrase operator or the operator that follows also removes the path, but disables more syntax. The practical workaround is a space after the slop digits.

Why it is a bug, not intended

#
  • The javadoc defines token1+token2 as AND and "term1 term2"~5 as a near query, so "a b"~2+c is valid syntax.
  • The parser itself stops the slop at the +, and keeps it after a space, after a phrase without slop and after a~1+b.
  • The skipping step is older than the slop feature: it was written to skip the closing quote.
The full argument249 words
  • Contract. SimpleQueryParser.java:40-42: "Tokens are considered to be any of a term, phrase, or subquery ... Whitespace ... and certain operators may be used to delimit tokens + | \" ." :50-51: "'+' specifies AND operation: token1+token2", "'|' specifies OR operation: token1|token2". :56: "'~N' at the end of phrases specifies near query: \"term1 term2\"~5".
  • Upstream tests. testORPhrase requires "foo bar"|"star wars" to be an OR although the test helper's default operator is AND, so an operator glued to a phrase must be honoured (testANDPhrase agrees, but under that default it cannot tell a kept + from a dropped one). No test covers an operator glued to a slop; testPhraseWithSlop checks "foo bar"~12 baz with a space, which the skip does not harm, so it is not evidence either way. With the fix all 46 TestSimpleQueryParser tests pass.
  • The parser's own consistency. parseFuzziness ends the slop exactly at the + because tokenFinished says it is a delimiter, and then the delimiter is discarded. The fuzzy-term path calls the same helper without the extra step and keeps the operator.
  • History. The ++state.index came with the class (LUCENE-5336, 2013), when the index was always on the closing quote at that point. LUCENE-5410 (2014) put the slop branch in front of it without changing it. Its review noted that the term and phrase paths differ "in how they consume/increment state.data and state.index", but no comment discusses an operator after a slop. No CHANGES, MIGRATE or javadoc text describes the skip.
The devil's-advocate check246 words

The strongest "works as designed" case (the GPT-5.6 reviews, the devil's advocate and the adjudication looked for it): the javadoc promises only a best effort, "Any errors in query syntax will be ignored and the parser will attempt to decipher what it can; however, this may mean odd or unexpected results", and slop parsing is lenient on purpose ("foo bar"~1a and "foo bar"~-1 silently give slop 0 in an upstream test). A maintainer could call an operator glued to a slop badly composed input. They could also point out that no upstream example or test puts an operator right after a slop, that - is not a delimiter and is swallowed into the slop text, and that the behaviour has not changed in twelve years, so a stored query could depend on it.

Why it fails: the input is not a syntax error, since the javadoc defines every piece of it; the parser itself recognizes the + as the end of the slop and then throws it away; the same delimiter is honoured after a space, after a phrase without slop and after a fuzzy term; the lenient tests are about the slop text itself, while the + after a valid ~2 is outside it; and the cause is a leftover increment from before the slop feature, not a decision. Twelve years of the same behaviour argue for a CHANGES entry, not for keeping a skip that no documentation describes. Verdict: not intended, a defect.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Run the trailing increment only when there was no slop

Skip the closing quote only when there was no slop: wrap the trailing ++state.index in consumePhrase in if (hasSlop == false). Then the new test passes 7 of 7, the existing 46 TestSimpleQueryParser tests still pass, and so does the whole queryparser module (540 tests, 0 failures).

      buildQueryTree(state, branch);

-      ++state.index;
+      if (hasSlop == false) {
+        // skip the closing quote (or a trailing '~'); after a slop, parseFuzziness already left the
+        // index on the delimiter that ended it, which must still be parsed, or at the end of input
+        ++state.index;
+      }
    }
  }

Run the trailing increment only when there was no slop (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

fix.diff, +5/-1 in SimpleQueryParser.java. Without a slop the index is on the closing quote (or a trailing ~ at the end of the input) and must be skipped as before. With a slop, parseFuzziness has already consumed the ~ and the digits and stands on the delimiter or at the end of the input, so nothing is left to skip; the parse loop then handles the delimiter as it does after a space.

      buildQueryTree(state, branch);

-      ++state.index;
+      if (hasSlop == false) {
+        // skip the closing quote (or a trailing '~'); after a slop, parseFuzziness already left the
+        // index on the delimiter that ended it, which must still be parsed, or at the end of input
+        ++state.index;
+      }
    }
  }

Trade-offs

  • The resulting query changes only when a +, |, " or ( follows the slop text: after a valid or empty slop (the bug), and also after slop text that does not parse ("a b"~x+c), which then parses like its spaced form. Whitespace, an extraneous ) and the end of input give the same query as before (executed).
  • A stored query written in the glued shape and tuned to today's result would now parse as documented, so a CHANGES entry under bug fixes is worth adding.
  • Not reviewed by a committer.

Change the shared helper instead of the phrase path.

Workaround in every affected release

#

Put a space after the slop digits ("a b"~2 +c); applications that build query strings should do the same, and keep access restrictions in a separate FILTER clause.

Trade-offs

Avoids the symptom; does not fix the parser.

Downstream note

#

The parser returns the whole query, so a restriction written in this syntax inherits the misparse. A glued + turns its AND into OR and it fails open: in the probe 7 of 8 documents match instead of 3. Only the rule's author creates the shape; a separate FILTER clause and the documented escaping contain it. No exploit known.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal. While building exhaustive checks of SimpleQueryParser, an AI tool spotted the extra step by reading the code. The checks then found it as the only mismatch: over all 2.4 million strings of up to 7 characters from an 8-symbol alphabet, a space next to an operator changed the query only after a phrase slop.

The method in full179 words

Method: bounded-exhaustive checking on the stock jar (the hunt's semi-formal tier; no model checker, SMT solver or prover).

  • In a hunt unit (query parsers, 2026-10-26) the finder, an AI tool, saw the off-by-one by reading consumePhrase while building two harnesses. Its first run, every string of up to 5 characters, found nothing: the bug needs at least 6.
  • Metamorphic check. "A space next to an operator does not change the query", over every string of up to 7 characters over a + | - " ~ 1 and space: 2,396,745 strings, 10,047,388 checks, 819 mismatches per default operator, every one a phrase followed by ~.
  • Translation validation. An independent reference semantics of the documented grammar, evaluated as match sets on all 40 documents of up to 3 tokens over {a, b, c}: 11,413,636 checks per default operator, 354,538 (OR) and 349,990 (AND) mismatches, every one an operator directly after phrase~N.
  • With the fix both harnesses report 0 mismatches. The finder then wrote a deterministic public-API JUnit; the independent checks, history sweep, prior-report search and security assessment followed.

History

#

Introduced in 4.7.0 by LUCENE-5410 (2014), which added ~ to the parser and put the slop branch in front of an older step that skips the closing quote. Wrong on all 13 releases run, from 4.7.0 to 10.5.2, and on main; never fixed. 4.7.0 is the first release with the class at all.

Versions tested7 rows

Introduced by LUCENE-5410 "add fuzzy and near to SimpleQueryParser" (issue #6473; patch by @dakrone, committed by @rmuir on 2014-02-02 as 584fda3bdf0 on trunk and b18fdadedec on branch_4x, an SVN-era patch with no pull request). The skipping ++state.index is older: LUCENE-5336 (3c9e753df64, 2013-11-12) added it when the index was always on the closing quote at that point.

consumePhrase is unchanged in all 110 release tags of the history clone (4.7.0 to 10.5.2; the clone lacks 8.8.2 and 8.9.0 to 8.11.4, of which 8.8.2 and 8.11.4 were executed); parseFuzziness changed in 6.5.0 (an empty slop means 2), 9.0.0 and 9.10.0 without moving where the index stops. Not a regression. Upstream main (head f4cc6ac2097c, 2026-10-26) has no change to the file since df6f71a3729, a formatting commit already in the tested tree.

LFV-359, snapshot of 2026-10-29

Registry title
SimpleQueryParser drops the '+', '|', quote or '(' typed directly after a phrase slop, so "a b"~2+c parses as phrase OR c and "a b"~2|c (default operator AND) as phrase AND c
Area
queryparser / SimpleQueryParser (near operator: consumePhrase phrase slop)
Priority
Medium, rank 35: It has the same profile as LFV-019 (default syntax, released since 4.7, silent wrong matches, reachable through parsers built on SimpleQueryParser), but it needs the rare glued input shape of a slop followed directly by an operator.
Impact class
silent wrong results
Impact note
Every SimpleQueryParser user with the default flags (NEAR_OPERATOR is on by default) who types a sloppy phrase immediately followed by '+', '|', a quote or '(' with no space gets a query of the wrong structure and wrong hits with no error (AND becomes OR under the default OR, OR becomes AND under default MUST, a following phrase becomes loose terms, a following group loses its grouping); no scale is needed, every release since 4.7.0 has it, a space after the slop digits avoids it, and a ')' that closes a group directly after the slop is not affected.
Summary
You parse "a b"~2+c with SimpleQueryParser and expect what its javadoc defines, the near query AND c, just as "a b"~2 +c and "a b"+c parse. Instead the parser skips the character right after the slop digits, so the '+' is lost and you get phrase OR c (on an index with the documents 'a b' and 'c' it matches 2 documents instead of 0); the same happens to '|' ("a b"~2|c becomes AND under default MUST), to a quote ("a b"~2"c d" becomes the phrase OR c OR d) and to '(' ("a b"~2(c+d) loses its group). The cause is an off-by-one in consumePhrase: parseFuzziness already stops on the delimiter that ends the slop, and the unconditional '++state.index' that was written to step over the closing quote then skips that delimiter.
