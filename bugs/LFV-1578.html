The finding in 30 seconds

#

The limits first. A search through the stock FacetsCollectorManager is not affected, and one FacetsCollector that visited every segment counted right. The defect needs an application that counts a stock collector that visited exactly one segment of a reader with several. It is silent, and whether the counts come out wrong depends on the data.

Background: three pieces

#
  1. Sorted-set facets store each label as an ordinal, a small number. Each segment numbers its own labels from 0 in sorted order; the reader numbers the labels of all its segments together. An ordinal map translates a segment's ordinals into the reader's.
  2. A FacetsCollector gathers the hits of a search and keeps one record per segment it visited (MatchingDocs). A counter such as SortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts then counts the labels of those hits into an array with one slot per reader ordinal.
  3. An IndexSearcher with an executor splits the segments into slices and asks its CollectorManager for one collector a slice. The stock FacetsCollectorManager merges those collectors into one before anything is counted.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Index six one-document segments: a=x in the first five, a=z in the sixth. Search them, with a query that matches every document, through an IndexSearcher that has an executor: default slicing makes two slices here, segments 1 to 5 and segment 6 alone.
  2. Give each slice its own stock FacetsCollector through your own CollectorManager, count each collector with SortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts and add the counts. You expect x=5 z=1, which the merged collector of the stock FacetsCollectorManager gives on the same searcher.
  3. Instead the sum is x=6 z=0: the first slice counts x=5, the second counts its one z document as x=1. No exception, the same with assertions on and off. The search itself is right (6 hits) and nothing is stored.
  4. Why: the second slice's collector holds one per-segment record. For a list of exactly one record the counter leaves out the reader's ordinal map (matchingDocs.size() > 1) and uses the segment's own ordinal 0 (z, its only label) as the reader's ordinal 0 (x).
  5. It depends on the data. With the documents in the other order (z first) the same search counts right, x=5 z=1: the one-segment slice then holds an x document, and x is ordinal 0 in that segment and in the reader. A small test index can hide it.

Who is affected

#

Applications that count, with either sorted-set counter, a stock FacetsCollector that visited exactly one segment of a reader with several: their own CollectorManager that counts each slice collector of a concurrent search by itself, or their own collector that declines later segments (run on 10.6.0, branch_11x and main). A search through the stock FacetsCollectorManager is not affected.

(b) Comments in the code read one record as an index of one segment (// No ord mapping (e.g., single segment index):, SortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts.java:256). (c) Both counters carry @lucene.experimental (SortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts.java:55, ConcurrentSortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts.java:50). (d) CollectorManager's class javadoc puts per-segment work on the manager: "Any computation or logic that needs to happen once per segment requires specific handling in the collector manager implementation, because the collection of an entire segment may be split across threads." (lucene/core/src/java/org/apache/lucene/search/CollectorManager.java:35-39). (e) Facets has no operation that adds two results (Facets.java:27-69), so the sum of the test is the application's own arithmetic. At main b5a4546a5964 the one public search method of IndexSearcher that takes a collector is search(Query, CollectorManager) (IndexSearcher.java:730), and the migration guide there says: "The manager will generally need to merge results from multiple Collectors in its reduce() method." (lucene/MIGRATE.md:863).

Why it does not hold. The sentences of (a) describe the usual flow; none of them states that a collector must have visited every segment of the reader, and getMatchingDocs itself speaks of visited segments. (b) is an assumption inside the code that the counter does not check, and collectors that visited five of six, five of seven and two of seven segments counted right in the runs. (c) is about the stability of the API. (d) is about one segment that is split into partitions among several collectors; default slicing does not split a segment (IndexSearcher.java:323-325), and in the test each segment is collected by one collector. (e) The sum is the application's, but the wrong count is not: the one-segment collector counted by itself lists x (1) where z (1) is due, before anything is added; and the test's reduce does merge the results of its collectors, by adding their counts.

What the argument does carry is the rank. The merged collector of the stock manager counted right in the runs, and the guide and the demo show that flow, so the application has to count a one-segment collector itself to meet the defect. That is why the priority is Low.

Summary
You search a six-segment index (one document a segment: a=x in the first five, a=z in the sixth) with an IndexSearcher that has an executor, give each slice of the search its own stock FacetsCollector through your own CollectorManager, count each collector with SortedSetDocValuesFacetCounts and add the counts. You expect x=5 z=1, which the merged collector of the stock FacetsCollectorManager gives on the same searcher. Instead the sum is x=6 z=0, with no error, with and without assertions. The collector of the second slice visited one segment, and for a list of exactly one per-segment record the counter leaves out the reader's ordinal map ('matchingDocs.size() > 1') and uses the segment's own ordinal 0 (z, its only label) as the reader's ordinal 0 (x); with the documents in the other order (z first) the same search counts right, because the segment's ordinals then agree with the reader's.
