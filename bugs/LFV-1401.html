IndexWriter.getDocStats() under-counts while a flush has written segments it has not yet published

While commit(), flush() or an NRT reader open writes two or more in-memory segments, getDocStats() misses the ones already written: 2 documents read as maxDoc 1 until the flush publishes them. Each written segment leaves the RAM count at once but joins the segment list only at the end.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

IndexWriter counts documents in two places: the RAM count and its segment list. Writing a segment removes its documents from the RAM count at once, but the segment enters the list only when the flush publishes it. In between, getDocStats(), which adds the two, misses those documents.

Background: in-memory segments and flush publication

#
  1. Adds that run at the same time each take their own in-memory segment, so two overlapping adds leave two (a later add reuses a free one). commit(), flush() and an NRT reader open run a full flush, which writes every in-memory segment to the directory, one after another.
  2. A written segment waits on a flush ticket. IndexWriter publishes tickets in order, adding each segment to its segment list (segmentInfos).
  3. getDocStats() returns the RAM count plus the maxDoc of every segment in that list.

The finding, step by step

#
  1. Default config. Add 2 documents so the writer holds 2 in-memory segments of 1 document each (two adds that overlap in time, for example on two threads, do this). Delete nothing: getDocStats().maxDoc is 2.
  2. Call commit(). Its flush writes segment 1, then segment 2.
  3. You expect getDocStats() to report maxDoc = numDocs = 2 throughout: it counts every document, flushed or still in RAM.
  4. You get 1 while segment 2 is written. Segment 1's document has already left the RAM count, but the segment joins the segment list only when the flush publishes, after it has written every segment.
  5. After the commit the count is 2 again; the commit was right and getPendingNumDocs() stayed right. With 8 indexing threads held at a barrier (each fills its own in-memory segment) on a plain FSDirectory, maxDoc fell from 8 to 0 during commit().

Who is affected

#

Default config: applications that index on two or more threads at once and call getDocStats() (in practice from another thread) while a commit, flush() or NRT refresh runs (since 9.9.0), or while two auto flushes finish out of order (since 7.7.0). Stats only: the count heals when the flush publishes, and nothing persisted or visible to readers is wrong.

Mechanism, terms and numbers724 words

IndexWriter.getDocStats() adds the documents buffered in RAM (numDocsInRAM) to the maxDoc of every segment in segmentInfos. When a flush writes an in-memory segment, DocumentsWriter.doFlush subtracts that segment's documents from numDocsInRAM right after writing it (DocumentsWriter.java:545), but the segment joins segmentInfos only when its flush ticket is published (IndexWriter.java:2990). Since #12647 (9.9.0) the full-flush loop publishes after it has written every segment (DocumentsWriter.java:579-580), so while commit(), flush() or an NRT open writes its second and later in-memory segments, the earlier ones are counted nowhere: 2 acknowledged documents read as maxDoc 1, and 8 as 0 in a polling test.

The count heals when the flush publishes; nothing persisted is wrong, the commit is correct, and getPendingNumDocs() stays right. The same gap opens in every release since 7.7.0 when a later flush finishes before an earlier one, because flush tickets are published strictly in order. A fix that keeps a written segment's documents in the RAM count until it is published closes both forms.

The two forms

#
  • Full flush (every multi-segment one since 9.9.0). commit() (IndexWriter.java:3809), flush() (:4402) and an NRT open (:586) all call docWriter.flushAllThreads(), so all three run this loop; only commit() was executed. In the repro, segment 1 is written and its 1 document subtracted (RAM count 2 to 1); while segment 2 is written, getDocStats() = 1 (segment 2's document, still in RAM) + 0 (empty segment list) = 1. After the last segment is written and before the publish at :580 the RAM count is 0, which is how a polling test with 8 indexing threads saw maxDoc 0.
  • Out of order (since 7.7.0). DocumentsWriterFlushQueue.innerPurge publishes only the head ticket, and only when it is ready (DocumentsWriterFlushQueue.java:82-114, canPublish() at :153). If auto flush B finishes while an earlier flush A is still writing, B's documents have left the RAM count but wait behind A. A probe with two indexing threads and maxBufferedDocs=2, no commit, read maxDoc 2 instead of 4 on 7.7.0, 9.8.0, 10.6.0 and main.
  • Why the lock does not help. getDocStats() and publishFlushedSegment hold the IndexWriter lock, but doFlush writes segments and subtracts their documents without it.

Why 9.9.0 made it common

#

Up to 9.8.0, doFlush had onTicketBacklog(); break; under the check ticketQueue.getTicketCount() >= perThreadPool.size() (9.8.0 DocumentsWriter.java:522-528). A full flush checks its in-memory segments out of the pool, so unless two or more new in-memory segments started during the commit (each joins the pool, 9.8.0 DocumentsWriterPerThreadPool.java:85-125), the check held, the loop broke out and the written segment was published before the next one was written. #12647 "Cleanup flushing logic in DocumentsWriter" removed the break, so every multi-segment full flush now writes all its segments before the single publish at :580.

With indexing during the commit, 9.8.0 has the window too: a fact-check probe (one test, assertions on, checkPendingFlushUpdate off so that the indexing thread does not help with the flush) adds 2 documents into 2 new in-memory segments while the commit writes its first segment; while the commit writes its second, maxDoc reads 3 instead of 4 on 9.8.0, 9.9.0 and main, and 4 with the recommended fix.

One exception shortens the window: onTicketBacklog() queues a publish event (IndexWriter.java:470-472). An indexing call on another thread that itself flushes or applies deletes while the loop runs processes that event (maybeProcessEvents, IndexWriter.java:6344) and publishes the segments written so far early (code reading).

Exposure and likelihood267 words
  • Who is hit: the default IndexWriterConfig and codec, two or more in-memory segments (in practice two or more threads indexing at once), and a getDocStats() call during a full flush or an out-of-order auto flush. With one in-memory segment the gap is only the few statements between DocumentsWriter.java:545 and :580 (not measured).
  • Size and duration: the dip is the documents of every segment the flush has written so far, up to all of them (8 to 0 in an executed polling test: 8 indexing threads held at a barrier, a 512 MB RAM buffer, no merges, FSDirectory; 5 of 5 runs), and lasts while the flush writes the remaining segments. 2 documents are enough to show it.
  • What stays correct: everything persisted; the commit (2 documents after it); readers, which count only the segments they hold (code reading); and getPendingNumDocs() (2 throughout in the model-based run).
  • Consumers: in Lucene, the Monitor's WritableQueryIndex.numDocs() (WritableQueryIndex.java:329) is not reached in practice, because its adds and its commit run in one block under commitLock (:109-132), one thread at a time; DirectoryTaxonomyWriter reads maxDoc only in its constructor and replaceTaxonomy (:174, :879). Downstream, a code search (2026-11-08) found no production use: Elasticsearch's only caller is a test helper in its searchable-snapshot cache (PersistentCache.getNumDocs(), commented "package private for tests"), and OpenSearch and Solr call the method only in tests (reach only, not a report).
  • Likelihood: rare by accident: no report of it was found, and servers usually take document counts from readers. With a poller running during commits it is easy to hit.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The javadoc says "Returns accurate DocStats", counting docs "still in the RAM buffer". Nothing was deleted, yet 2 becomes 1.
  • getDocStats() was added (LUCENE-8609) for stats "not subject to concurrent changes"; IndexWriter is documented as completely thread safe.
  • A temporary maxDoc drop was fixed as a Blocker before (LUCENE-7894); #12647, a cleanup, widened this window in 9.9.0 without discussing it.
The full argument234 words
  • Contract: "Returns accurate DocStats for this writer" (IndexWriter.java:6819); DocStats.maxDoc: "The total number of docs in this index, counting docs not yet flushed (still in the RAM buffer), and also counting deleted docs" (:6838-6839); numDocs the same without deleted docs (:6845-6846). Class javadoc: IndexWriter instances "are completely thread safe" (:167).
  • Why the method exists: CHANGES 7.7.0, LUCENE-8609: getDocStats() "allows to get consistent numDocs and maxDoc stats that are not subject to concurrent changes" (lucene/CHANGES.txt:7064-7065); MIGRATE: "a consistent view on document stats" (lucene/MIGRATE.md:1556-1558).
  • No index state matches the value: 2 documents acknowledged, none deleted, and the getter reports 1, then 2 again.
  • Precedent: LUCENE-7894 (#8944, Blocker, fixed in 7.0 by ee1edd9d467, "make sure IW finishes segment flush so we don't see a temporary drop in IW.maxDoc()"). LUCENE-3551 (#4625, 2011) named this very window ("we decrement the numDocsInRam ... but before we checkpoint") as a race and fixed it only for nrtIsCurrent.
  • The widening was not chosen: #12647 was a refactoring with one approval, no review comments and no CHANGES entry; its body is about removing duplicate loops and says nothing about publication timing or doc counts. No test or doc accepts a dip, and no located upstream test asserts a value inside the written-but-unpublished window (TestConcurrentMergeScheduler polls numDocs while another thread flushes, but only waits for it to reach 8).
The devil's-advocate check170 words

The strongest case for "intended" (an intended-behaviour review, and a cross-model review that rated it moderate): LUCENE-8609 aimed only at maxDoc and numDocs being consistent with each other, and maxDoc >= numDocs still holds during the dip; a written-but-unpublished segment is literally neither "in the index" nor "still in the RAM buffer"; in-order ticket publication is deliberate. The javadoc's own example (IndexWriter.java:6819-6821) is that pairwise problem, numDocs greater than maxDoc, and the 1/1 read is pairwise consistent.

Why it fails: the javadoc counts all documents, including the RAM buffer, and calls the result accurate; CHANGES and MIGRATE promise stats "not subject to concurrent changes"; the dip breaks read-your-own-writes (in the out-of-order probe the thread that has just added two documents gets a count without them); LUCENE-3551 treated this window as a race to fix. The case rests mainly on how the javadoc defines the counts, less on LUCENE-8609's motivation. Verdict: not intended, a defect. In-order publication can stay: only the counting has to cover the waiting segments.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Keep a written segment in the RAM count until it is published

Count until publish: keep a written segment's documents in the RAM count until publishFlushedSegment adds the segment under the IndexWriter lock (or deleteAll drops its ticket); a failed flush subtracts as before. 2 files, +12/-1. Validated with gradle: the test passes with assertions on and off, and so do 12 neighbouring suites (277 tests, 17 skipped).

// DocumentsWriter.doFlush
boolean countedUntilPublished = false;
try {
  final FlushedSegment newSegment = flushingDWPT.flush(flushNotifications);
  countedUntilPublished = newSegment != null;
  ticketQueue.addSegment(ticket, newSegment);
  dwptSuccess = true;
} finally {
  if (dwptSuccess == false || countedUntilPublished == false) {
    subtractFlushedNumDocs(flushingDocsInRam);
  }

// IndexWriter.publishFlushedSegment (synchronized), first statement; also in lockAndAbortAll's purge
docWriter.subtractFlushedNumDocs(newSegment.info.maxDoc());
All options and trade-offs4 options · 517 words

Four options; the recommended one was applied in a scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168 and validated with gradle.

Keep a written segment in the RAM count until it is published (validated by the checks) (recommended)

#

In DocumentsWriter.doFlush, skip the subtraction when the segment was written and parked on its ticket; subtract instead in IndexWriter.publishFlushedSegment, in the same IndexWriter-locked step that adds the segment to segmentInfos, and in lockAndAbortAll's purge, which drops tickets for deleteAll. A failed flush (no segment) subtracts in doFlush as before.

// DocumentsWriter.doFlush
boolean countedUntilPublished = false;
try {
  final FlushedSegment newSegment = flushingDWPT.flush(flushNotifications);
  countedUntilPublished = newSegment != null;
  ticketQueue.addSegment(ticket, newSegment);
  dwptSuccess = true;
} finally {
  if (dwptSuccess == false || countedUntilPublished == false) {
    subtractFlushedNumDocs(flushingDocsInRam);
  }

// IndexWriter.publishFlushedSegment (synchronized), first statement; also in lockAndAbortAll's purge
docWriter.subtractFlushedNumDocs(newSegment.info.maxDoc());

Trade-offs

  • numRamDocs() ("Expert: Return the number of documents currently buffered in RAM", IndexWriter.java:4458-4462) then also counts written-but-unpublished documents; the separate-counter option keeps its meaning.
  • DocumentsWriter.anyChanges() also reads the RAM count, but it already returns true while any ticket is queued (DocumentsWriter.java:353-356), so its result does not change (code reading).
  • The subtraction runs before ensureOpen(false), so a writer closed by a tragic event still releases the count.

  • Earlier runs: the out-of-order probe passes with the same fix (maxDoc 4), and the model-based test is clean.

A separate counter for written, not yet published documents

#

Move a written segment's count from the RAM count to a new counter, decrement it in publishFlushedSegment, and add both in getDocStats(). Not tried.

Trade-offs

Keeps numRamDocs() meaning RAM only. The move happens outside the IndexWriter lock, so unless both updates are atomic (one packed AtomicLong, or one lock) getDocStats() can briefly double-count or under-count.

Publish after every written segment inside the full-flush loop (an unconditional publish)

#

Call the publish after every segment the full-flush loop writes, unconditionally. Restoring only 9.8.0's conditional break is not enough: 9.8.0 still opens the window when two or more new in-memory segments start during the commit. A history run on 9.9.0 with the unconditional publish passes the test.

Trade-offs

Fixes only the full-flush form: the out-of-order form still fails (it fails on 9.8.0).

Document the dip

#

Say in the getDocStats() javadoc that the stats can be low while a flush runs.

Trade-offs

Cheap, but contradicts "Returns accurate DocStats" and leaves callers without a correct concurrent count.

Downstream note

#

Products that read IndexWriter.getDocStats() while indexing, for example for metrics or capacity checks, can see a temporarily low count during flushes; counts taken from readers are not affected.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal: in a model of IndexWriter's flush control, the step "each document is counted in exactly one of the RAM count and the segment list" did not hold against DocumentsWriter.java:545 and :580. A model-based test on the real jar (oracle: getDocStats().maxDoc == getPendingNumDocs()) then saw maxDoc 1, numDocs 1, pending 2; it was cut to the one-method test.

The method in full110 words
  • Model: a component model of IndexWriter's flush control and sequence numbers. Its step "a document is counted in exactly one of numDocsInRAM and segmentInfos" fails when read against the code: the subtraction at DocumentsWriter.java:545 runs before the publish at :580.
  • Witness on the real code: a model-based test drove the stock main jar with the oracle getDocStats().maxDoc == getPendingNumDocs() (when no add is mid-document) and reported maxDoc=1 numDocs=1 pending=2 at the pause point; with the count-until-publish fix it found no failure.
  • Minimal repro: first a latch-driven JUnit test with a second indexing thread, then the single-thread, one-method test shown here; fails on main with assertions on and off.

LFV-1401, snapshot of 2026-11-08

Registry title
IndexWriter.getDocStats() under-counts acknowledged documents whose segment is already written but not yet published: possible with out-of-order concurrent flushes since getDocStats was added (7.7.0), and since 9.9.0 (#12647) open during every commit(), flush() or NRT open that writes two or more in-memory segments
Area
index / IndexWriter.getDocStats vs. flush-ticket publication (DocumentsWriter.doFlush, DocumentsWriterFlushQueue, IndexWriter.publishFlushedSegment)
Priority
Low, rank 764: With the default configuration and indexing on two or more threads, getDocStats() under-counts acknowledged documents while a commit, flush or NRT open writes two or more in-memory segments (since 9.9.0, and since 7.7.0 when two flushes finish out of order), and a caller that polls it from another thread hits that window easily. It is Low because the count heals once the flush publishes, nothing persisted or seen by readers is wrong, in-tree callers are not affected in practice, and the major servers take document counts from readers.
Impact class
minor
Impact note
Applications with the default IndexWriterConfig and codec that index on two or more threads (so the writer holds two or more in-memory segments) and call getDocStats() from another thread while a commit(), flush() or NRT open/refresh writes those segments (since 9.9.0), or while two concurrent flushes finish out of order (since 7.7.0): maxDoc and numDocs drop below the number of acknowledged, never-deleted documents (judge runs: 1 instead of 2 during a commit, 2 instead of 4 during out-of-order auto flushes; a reviewer's FSDirectory polling test saw 8 -> 0) until the flush publishes the waiting segments, then heal; nothing persisted is wrong, getPendingNumDocs() and all readers stay correct, and no in-tree consumer is affected in practice (the Monitor serialises adds and commits; the taxonomy writer reads maxDoc only at quiescent points); single-threaded indexing is not affected; no scale needed.
Summary
You add two documents from two threads at the same time (so the writer holds two in-memory segments), delete nothing, and call getDocStats() while another thread runs commit(). You expect maxDoc = numDocs = 2 throughout, because DocStats counts every document, flushed or still in the RAM buffer. Instead, while the commit writes the second segment, getDocStats() returns 1 and 1: DocumentsWriter has already removed the first segment's documents from its RAM count, but the full flush adds that segment to SegmentInfos only after its whole loop, so for that time the segment is counted nowhere; the count returns to 2 once the flush publishes.
