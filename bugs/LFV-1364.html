QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer's percentage threshold leaves out deleted documents that docFreq() still counts, so updates create extra stop words

QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer turns its percentage (default 40%) into a document count with numDocs(), which leaves out deleted documents, but compares it with docFreq(), which still counts them. While deleted copies left by updateDocument are not merged away yet, terms below the percentage can become stop words and silently vanish from analyzed queries; maxDoc(), which MoreLikeThis uses since 8.0.0, fixes it.

The finding in 30 seconds

#

QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer wraps a query-time analyzer and drops terms that occur in "too many" documents of an index, by default more than 40% of them, to keep very common terms out of queries.

  1. Index three documents whose body is w, x and y, then re-index each one unchanged with updateDocument. The old copies are now deleted but not merged away yet: 3 live documents, 6 in total.
  2. Build new QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer(analyzer, reader) with its default 40%. Each term is in 1 of 3 live documents, or 2 of 6 counting the deleted copies: 33% either way, so you expect no stop words.
  3. The threshold is (int) (numDocs() * 0.4) = (int) (3 * 0.4) = 1 document, but each term's docFreq() is 2, because it still counts the deleted copy. 2 > 1, so getStopWords("body") returns [w, x, y].
  4. The wrapper now removes those terms: the query w analyzes to no token at all. With the default writer settings it can happen on a bigger index too: of 20 documents with 3 re-indexed, a term in 7 (35%) is dropped at 40%.

Who is affected

#

Applications that build the analyzer with a percentage (the 2-argument constructor's default 40% or a maxPercentDocs constructor) over an index whose deletions are not merged away yet, which ordinary updates can leave with default settings. The threshold shrinks by about the deleted share, so mostly terms near it are dropped. The int maxDocFreq constructors are not affected.

Mechanism, terms and numbers507 words

The percentage constructors of QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer (the 2-argument one with its default 40%, and both float maxPercentDocs ones) turn the percentage into an absolute count with (int) (indexReader.numDocs() * maxPercentDocs) and then make every term with docFreq() > count a stop word. numDocs() counts live documents only, while docFreq() still counts deleted documents that are not merged away yet. So on an index with unmerged deletions, which ordinary updateDocument calls can leave behind with the default settings, terms below the configured share of documents can become stop words, and the wrapper silently drops them from analyzed queries. The tested ratio, docFreq / numDocs, can exceed 100%. The drift is bounded by the share of unmerged deletions, so mostly terms near the threshold flip. Every release tested from 2.4.0 to 10.6.0 fails, and main still has it; MoreLikeThis.setMaxDocFreqPct had the same mix and was switched to maxDoc() in 8.0.0 (LUCENE-8333); the same fix here makes the percentage a share of all documents, deleted copies included.

How far it drifts

#

If a share d of all documents is deleted and the deleted copies contain a term as often as the live ones, docFreq is about liveDocFreq * maxDoc / numDocs, so the analyzer acts as if maxPercentDocs were p * (1 - d) (arithmetic, not run). At the default 40% and the default TieredMergePolicy target of at most 20% deleted documents that is about 32%: terms between about 32% and 40% of the live documents flip. When updates concentrate on documents that contain the term the drift is larger: with maxPercentDocs = 1.0, which no share can exceed, one document re-indexed once with NoMergePolicy (numDocs=1, maxDoc=2, docFreq=2) is still a stop word (a supporting probe, not the repro). With the default IndexWriterConfig that one-document case does not show it (the reader sees maxDoc=1, no stop word): whether deleted copies are still there depends on segment layout and merges, which is why the repro pins NoMergePolicy.

Exposure and likelihood259 words
  • Who is hit. Users of QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer(Analyzer, IndexReader) (default 40%), (Analyzer, IndexReader, float) and (Analyzer, IndexReader, Collection, float) on an index with deletions that are not merged away yet. Ordinary updateDocument calls with the default IndexWriterConfig can leave such deletions (20 documents with 3 re-indexed already shows it); whether they are still there depends on segment layout, merges and commits, and a segment whose documents are all deleted is dropped unless the merge policy keeps it. deleteDocuments leaves the same kind of deleted documents; it was not run separately. The int maxDocFreq constructors compare docFreq with the caller's own count and are not affected.
  • What happens. The stop-word set holds terms below the configured percentage, and the wrapper's StopFilter removes them from every text it analyzes, with no error. A query made only of such terms analyzes to no token at all; in a larger query those terms are left out (what a query parser then builds is up to the parser).
  • Scale. Bounded by the share of unmerged deletions (the default TieredMergePolicy aims to keep it at or below 20%): mostly terms near the threshold, which are already fairly frequent, flip. Larger effects need many deletions concentrated on documents with the term, or tiny indexes, where the (int) truncation also counts. The 3-document repro, where every term of the field becomes a stop word, is such a tiny index.
  • Age and use. Unchanged since the class was added in 2008 (first release 2.4.0); the recorded searches found no public report; how widely the class is used was not measured.

Why it looks unintended

#
  • The javadoc calls maxPercentDocs "the maximum percentage (between 0.0 and 1.0) of index documents which contain a term"; the repro's terms are at 33% either way.
  • The tested ratio, docFreq / numDocs, can exceed 100%: at maxPercentDocs = 1.0 one update can still make a stop word.
  • MoreLikeThis.setMaxDocFreqPct had the same mix and was switched to maxDoc() in 8.0.0 (LUCENE-8333).
The full argument213 words
  • The contract. "maxPercentDocs The maximum percentage (between 0.0 and 1.0) of index documents which contain a term, after which the word is considered to be a stop word" (QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer.java:90-91, :106-107); the default is "(40%) of index documents" (:54-56). In the repro each term is in 1 of 3 live documents and 2 of 6 documents counting deleted copies.
  • The sibling was fixed. On LUCENE-8221 (#9268) Robert Muir wrote of MoreLikeThis.setMaxDocFreqPct: "Its also confusing and inconsistent that it uses numDocs() in the percentage calculation, which excludes deleted documents, when docFreq() does not. It should really use maxDoc() here", and later that "percentage can be larger than 100% with the broken numDocs formula". Dawid Weiss switched it to maxDoc() in LUCENE-8333 (#9380, 7a5d9ca5e88, Lucene 8.0.0, "Changes in Runtime Behavior"). CommonTermsQuery also divides by reader.maxDoc().
  • Core's convention. The javadoc of BM25Similarity.idfExplain says it uses docCount instead of numDocs "because also TermStats#docFreq() is used, and when the latter is inaccurate, so is FieldStats#docCount(), and in the same direction".
  • Nothing pins the current behaviour. TestQueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer indexes 200 documents with addDocument only and passes all 7 tests with the fix; no CHANGES or MIGRATE entry describes the choice; the LUCENE-494 discussion that added the class (#1572) never mentions deleted documents.
The devil's-advocate check130 words

The case: numDocs() is "the number of documents in this index" (IndexReader.java:293), so "percentage of index documents" means live documents, and counting deleted postings makes a stricter guard for a class whose purpose is query cost. Dawid Weiss first made this argument for MoreLikeThis on LUCENE-8221 ("a stricter estimate").

Why it fails: the repro's terms are below 40% under the live reading too (1 of 3); a ratio that can pass 100% is not a percentage of anything; nothing documents or tests the stricter intent; and upstream settled the same question for the sibling in favour of maxDoc(), with Dawid Weiss making the change. What remains: the fix changes results (fewer stop words on indexes with deletions), so, like LUCENE-8333, it is a change in runtime behaviour.

Suggested fix

#

Recommended Divide by maxDoc() (validated)

Use indexReader.maxDoc() instead of numDocs() at line 113, so both sides of the comparison count deleted documents, as MoreLikeThis.setMaxDocFreqPct does since 8.0.0. The repro then passes, as do the 7 tests of TestQueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer and the module's static checks.

-    this(delegate, indexReader, fields, (int) (indexReader.numDocs() * maxPercentDocs));
+    this(delegate, indexReader, fields, (int) (indexReader.maxDoc() * maxPercentDocs));
All options and trade-offs3 options · 456 words

One fix site, QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer.java:113; every percentage constructor reaches it. Only the recommended option was run (repro, the query package's tests and the module's static checks, in a scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168).

Divide by maxDoc() (validated) (recommended)

#

Count deleted documents on both sides: the denominator then counts deletions the way MoreLikeThis.setMaxDocFreqPct (LUCENE-8333) and CommonTermsQuery do (their APIs differ otherwise: an integer percent; rounding up with Math.ceil).

-    this(delegate, indexReader, fields, (int) (indexReader.numDocs() * maxPercentDocs));
+    this(delegate, indexReader, fields, (int) (indexReader.maxDoc() * maxPercentDocs));

Trade-offs

  • The tested ratio becomes a real percentage (never above 100%) of all documents, deleted copies included, not of live documents only; core scoring statistics count deletions the same way.
  • Deleted documents still shift a term's share a little (a term whose documents were updated more often weighs a little more); that approximation is Lucene-wide and is not claimed here.
  • Behaviour change: indexes with unmerged deletions get fewer stop words. LUCENE-8333 put the same change under "Changes in Runtime Behavior" in a major release; a CHANGES entry there fits here too.
  • Open PR #16847 (long global doc ids) rewrites line 113 as Math.toIntExact((long) ((double) indexReader.numDocs() * maxPercentDocs)), keeping numDocs(): the two changes conflict textually, one should rebase on the other.

What the checks validated

  • Scratch worktree of 5adff78bb168, gradle, OpenJDK 25.0.3, seed DEADBEEF. Stock: the repro fails with -Ptests.asserts=true and =false.
  • With the fix: the repro and TestQueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer (1 + 7 tests, the only test class of the package) pass with assertions on, 0 failures; the repro passes with assertions off; :lucene:analysis:common:check -x test (checkGoogleJavaFormat, ecjLint, forbiddenApis, license and checksum checks) passes with the fix and the repro test in place.

Keep the live-document reading and count live postings for candidate terms (not run)

#

Keep the numDocs() threshold, and for a term whose docFreq() exceeds it, count its live documents (a PostingsEnum filtered by MultiBits.getLiveDocs) before calling it a stop word.

Trade-offs

  • Exact share of live documents, no drift.
  • One pass over the postings of every candidate term when the analyzer is built, and the candidates are the most frequent terms, with the longest postings lists.
  • Needs the percentage to reach the per-term loop: today the percentage constructor reduces it to an int first.

Divide by the field's Terms.getDocCount() (not run)

#

A per-field denominator. LUCENE-8984 made a similar switch in MoreLikeThis, for its idf (numDocs to the field's doc count), not for its max-doc-frequency threshold.

Trade-offs

  • Consistent with docFreq (it counts deleted documents too).
  • Changes the meaning to "share of the documents that have the field", a larger share for sparse fields, which the javadoc's "of index documents" does not say; needs a per-field threshold.

Downstream note

#

Products that build this analyzer from a frequently updated index can get a somewhat stricter stop-word list than configured until merges remove the deleted documents.

How it was found

#

Semi-formal contract checking: the documented promises of QueryAutoStopWordAnalyzer were written as proof obligations and checked against the code. The float constructors promise a percentage of index documents, while the code divides a deletion-counting docFreq by live documents; an executable public-API test on stock main showed the extra stop words.

The method in full149 words

Method: semi-formal (proof obligations stated from the documented contracts and checked against the code, witnessed by executable tests).

  • Obligation. For the percentage constructors, a term is a stop word only if it is in more than maxPercentDocs of the index documents, under one consistent count of documents.
  • Check. The threshold (int) (numDocs() * p) counts live documents; docFreq() counts deleted documents too (IndexReader.java:401-408), so the obligation fails as soon as deleted copies are not merged away.
  • Witness. A 20-document updateDocument test on main 5adff78bb168, then the one-test repro (3 documents), and a bounded-exhaustive differential check over 16,380 small indexes (1 to 6 live documents, every subset containing the term, every subset re-indexed once, maxPercentDocs 0.25, 0.4 and 0.5): the real class matches the formula in every case, 2,197 cases make a stop word that is below the percentage under both readings, and the maxDoc() formula never does.
